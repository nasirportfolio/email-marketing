How to Write an Abandoned Cart Email to Customers

An abandoned cart email reminds a shopper about products they added to their cart but did not purchase. A useful message should make returning to the cart easy while addressing the hesitation that may have stopped the customer from completing checkout.

Quick Answer: A good abandoned cart email should show the product the customer left behind, explain the next step clearly, include one primary call to action, and make it easy to return to checkout. Start with a helpful reminder before automatically offering a discount, because not every shopper needs an incentive to complete a purchase.

Start With This Abandoned Cart Email Template

Use the following template as a starting point. Replace the placeholders with the customer's actual cart information before sending the email.

Subject: You left something in your cart

Hi [First Name],

You were close to completing your order. We saved the items you added to your cart.

[Product Name]
[Short product benefit or description]
[Price]

If you are still interested, you can return to your cart and complete your order.

Return to My Cart

If you have a question about the product, shipping, returns, or payment options, please contact us.

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

The link above is an example placeholder for the template only. Your actual email should contain the customer's real cart or checkout URL generated by your ecommerce or email platform.

What Is an Abandoned Cart Email?

An abandoned cart email is a message sent to a shopper who added one or more products to a shopping cart but left before completing the purchase.

The purpose is not simply to say, "You forgot something." The email should help the shopper understand what is waiting in the cart and provide a simple path back to checkout.

For ecommerce businesses, this makes abandoned cart email one part of a broader customer journey. The message should complement other transactional and lifecycle emails rather than replace them.

Why Send an Abandoned Cart Email?

Customers abandon carts for many reasons. They may want more time to decide, have questions about shipping, become distracted, encounter unexpected costs, or simply stop browsing.

A reminder gives the shopper another opportunity to continue the purchase without requiring them to search for the product again.

Current ecommerce guidance commonly recommends showing the abandoned products and providing a direct route back to the cart. Shopify also recommends testing timing, copy, incentives, and other elements against store-specific results rather than assuming one approach works for every audience. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

When Should You Send It?

There is no universal sending time that works for every store. Product price, purchase consideration time, audience behavior, and the checkout experience can all affect the appropriate timing.

A practical starting point is to test a first reminder relatively soon after abandonment and then use a later follow-up if the shopper does not purchase. Current Shopify guidance describes two to four hours as one possible starting point for a first reminder and 24 to 48 hours for a follow-up, while other Shopify guidance describes different testing schedules. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

The important point is to treat these times as testing starting points, not fixed rules.

A Simple Test Sequence

  1. Email 1: A simple reminder about the abandoned cart.
  2. Email 2: Address common buying concerns or reinforce product value.
  3. Email 3: Provide a final reminder or carefully tested incentive when appropriate.

Stop the sequence when the customer completes the purchase. There is little value in continuing to send abandoned-cart reminders after the intended action has already happened.

What to Include in an Abandoned Cart Email

A useful abandoned cart email normally contains the information the shopper needs to recognize the cart and decide whether to return.

  • The customer's name when appropriate.
  • The product or products left in the cart.
  • Product images when they improve recognition.
  • Price and quantity where useful.
  • A short reminder of the product's value.
  • A clear return-to-cart CTA.
  • Relevant shipping or return information when it addresses likely concerns.
  • An unsubscribe option when required for the type of marketing message being sent.

Avoid filling the email with unrelated products, excessive promotional copy, or multiple competing CTAs. The main action should remain obvious.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Remind the Customer What They Left Behind

Start with the cart rather than a long brand introduction.

For example:

You left something in your cart.

Your selected items are still waiting for you. If you are ready to continue, you can return to your cart below.

2. Show the Relevant Product

If your email platform supports dynamic product data, display the actual product from the customer's cart. This makes the message more useful than a generic reminder.

3. Reinforce the Main Benefit

Give the customer a reason to continue. Focus on a genuine benefit rather than exaggerated sales language.

For example, a clothing store might remind the customer about the material, fit, or intended use of the product. A software company might highlight the feature that originally attracted the customer.

4. Make the Next Step Obvious

The customer should not have to search your website for the cart. Link directly to the appropriate cart or checkout destination.

Address Customer Concerns

Sometimes the best abandoned cart email does more than remind the shopper. It answers a question that may have prevented the purchase.

Depending on the product, useful information may include:

  • Shipping options and expected delivery information.
  • Return or exchange policies.
  • Product sizing or specifications.
  • Payment options.
  • Warranty information.
  • Customer support contact information.

Do not invent reassurance. Only mention guarantees, delivery promises, inventory levels, or policies that your business actually provides.

If shipping uncertainty is a common issue, related post-purchase and delivery communication can also help you build a more complete customer journey. For example, see How to Write a Shipping Confirmation Email.

Should You Offer a Discount?

Not necessarily.

A discount can remove a price objection, but giving every shopper a discount may also train customers to wait for an offer before purchasing.

A practical sequence is to begin with a useful reminder and test whether an incentive is actually needed. If you use a discount, make the terms clear and ensure the offer is genuine.

Important: Never create artificial urgency such as "Only 2 left!" unless your inventory system confirms that statement. Similarly, do not claim that a cart is reserved unless your store actually reserves the items.

Abandoned Cart Subject Lines

The subject line should make the purpose of the email clear without relying on misleading curiosity.

  • You left something in your cart
  • Your cart is waiting for you
  • Still thinking it over?
  • Ready to finish your order?
  • Your selected items are still in your cart
  • Complete your order when you're ready
  • Did you forget something?
  • Your cart is still open

Test subject lines against your own audience rather than assuming that a particular phrase will always perform best.

Create a Clear Call to Action

The CTA should tell the customer exactly what happens when they click.

Useful CTA examples include:

  • Return to My Cart
  • Complete My Order
  • Continue Checkout
  • Review My Cart
  • Finish Your Order

Avoid vague CTA text such as "Click Here" when you can clearly describe the action.

Make It Mobile-Friendly

Many shoppers read email on smartphones, so the message should remain easy to understand and use on a small screen.

  • Use a simple single-column layout.
  • Keep paragraphs short.
  • Use readable text.
  • Make the main CTA easy to tap.
  • Compress large product images.
  • Use descriptive alt text.
  • Test the actual links.
  • Check the email on multiple email clients and devices.

Mobile usability is especially important when the CTA takes the customer directly back to a shopping cart. Shopify's current guidance also recommends testing rendering across common devices and email clients. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a U.S. online store selling running shoes. A customer adds a pair priced at $89.99 to the cart but leaves without purchasing.

Subject: Your running shoes are still in your cart

Hi Sarah,

You were checking out a pair of our running shoes, and they are still in your cart.

Trail Runner Pro — $89.99

If you're still interested, you can return to your cart and complete your order.

Return to My Cart

Have a question about sizing, shipping, or returns? Our support team is happy to help.

Thanks,
The [Brand Name] Team

This is a hypothetical example. The price, product, customer name, and wording should be replaced with actual store information.

Automate the Sequence

Abandoned cart emails are particularly suitable for automation because the trigger can be based on a customer's cart activity.

A basic automation might work like this:

  1. A shopper adds a product to the cart.
  2. The shopper leaves without completing the purchase.
  3. The platform waits for the configured period.
  4. The first reminder is sent.
  5. If the customer still has not purchased, a follow-up may be sent.
  6. The workflow stops when the purchase is completed.

Shopify currently provides abandoned-cart automation capabilities through Shopify Messaging, while third-party platforms can provide additional segmentation and automation options. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Common Abandoned Cart Email Mistakes

  • Sending a generic message: Show the actual product whenever possible.
  • Using too many CTAs: Keep the primary action obvious.
  • Offering discounts too quickly: Test whether a reminder works without an incentive.
  • Using fake urgency: Only use accurate inventory or reservation information.
  • Sending after purchase: Make sure the automation stops when the customer converts.
  • Ignoring mobile users: Test the complete email experience on smaller screens.
  • Broken cart links: Test the actual destination before activating the automation.
  • Too much copy: Give the customer the information needed to make the next decision.
  • Ignoring customer concerns: Address relevant shipping, returns, payment, or product questions.

Abandoned Cart Email Checklist

  • ☐ The email clearly identifies the abandoned cart.
  • ☐ The actual abandoned product is shown when supported.
  • ☐ Product name and price are accurate.
  • ☐ The main CTA returns the shopper to the correct cart or checkout.
  • ☐ The email has a clear subject line.
  • ☐ The copy addresses relevant purchase concerns.
  • ☐ Any discount or promotion is genuine and clearly explained.
  • ☐ Any urgency statement is factually accurate.
  • ☐ The sequence stops after purchase.
  • ☐ The email works on mobile devices.
  • ☐ Product images have useful alt text.
  • ☐ Links have been tested.
  • ☐ Required unsubscribe and business information is included for applicable marketing messages.

Final Thoughts

A good abandoned cart email does not need to be complicated. Remind the customer what they selected, provide useful information, and make returning to the cart simple.

Start with a straightforward reminder before relying on discounts or aggressive urgency. Then test your timing, subject lines, offers, and copy using your own customer and conversion data.

The most useful abandoned cart emails are the ones that reduce friction and help the shopper make a clear decision.
