How to Write a Wrong Item Received Email to Customers

When a customer receives the wrong item, the problem is usually simple to describe but frustrating to experience. They ordered one product and received something different. A good customer service email should acknowledge the mistake, apologize clearly, explain what will happen next, and make the resolution easy to understand.

Quick Answer: A wrong item received email should confirm what went wrong, apologize without making excuses, explain the replacement or refund process, provide clear return instructions when required, and tell the customer what happens next. Keep the message specific to the customer's order.

Wrong Item Received Email Template

Subject: We’re Sorry About the Wrong Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that you received the wrong item in your order. I understand how frustrating it is to receive something different from what you purchased.

We checked your order and confirmed that you ordered [Correct Product], but received [Incorrect Product].

We’ll arrange for the correct item to be sent to you [explain the actual replacement timing or next step].

Regarding the incorrect item, [explain your actual return instructions]. Please follow those instructions so we can process the return correctly.

We’re sorry for the inconvenience, and thank you for giving us the opportunity to correct the mistake.

Best,
[Customer Service Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is a Wrong Item Received Email?

A wrong item received email is a customer service message sent after an order contains an item different from what the customer purchased.

The mistake could involve the wrong product, size, color, model, quantity, or another order detail. The email should focus on correcting the problem rather than explaining internal fulfillment issues in excessive detail.

A useful customer service response should personalize the message, acknowledge the customer's experience, summarize the problem, and explain the available next step. These are also recommended practices in current ecommerce customer-service guidance. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

When to Send the Email

Send the response as soon as the wrong-item issue has been confirmed and you know what action your business can actually take.

If you need information from the customer first, explain exactly what you need. For example, you might ask for a photo of the item received if your store's process requires it.

Avoid promising a replacement, refund, shipping upgrade, or return-label arrangement until you have confirmed that the option is available under your actual store policy.

What to Include in the Email

A useful wrong-item email normally includes:

  • The customer's name
  • The order number
  • The item they ordered
  • The item they received, when known
  • A brief apology
  • The action your business will take
  • Replacement, exchange, or refund information when applicable
  • Return instructions if the incorrect item must be returned
  • Expected timing only when you can support it
  • A clear way to contact customer service if the customer has questions

The goal is to remove uncertainty. The customer should not have to send several additional emails just to understand what happens next.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Start With a Clear Apology

Begin by acknowledging the mistake directly.

For example:

“I’m sorry that you received the wrong item in your order. I understand how frustrating it is to receive something different from what you purchased.”

Avoid blaming the customer, warehouse, delivery company, or another team before you have established the facts.

2. State What Happened

If your records confirm the problem, briefly state what the customer ordered and what they received. This shows that you understand the specific issue.

For example:

You ordered the Black Canvas Backpack, but the package contained the Blue Travel Backpack.

3. Explain the Solution

The most important part of the email is the next step. Tell the customer what you are going to do, rather than making them figure out the process themselves.

Depending on your actual policy, the solution might involve sending the correct product, arranging an exchange, issuing a refund, or providing another approved resolution.

Confirm the Order Details

Before sending the email, check the order record carefully. Confirm the product name, variant, quantity, and order number.

If the customer reports receiving the wrong size or color, verify the original order rather than assuming that the fulfillment team made the mistake.

Practical tip: Keep the customer's original order information visible when writing the response. A specific response is usually more useful than a generic “sorry for the inconvenience” message.

Explain Replacement or Refund Options

If you are sending the correct item, explain what the customer can expect next. If you are offering a refund instead, explain the applicable process and timing according to your actual policy.

Replacement example

We’ll arrange to send the correct item to you. Once the replacement shipment is processed, we’ll provide the updated tracking information.

Refund example

We can process a refund for the incorrect item according to our refund policy. We’ll confirm once the refund has been submitted.

Do not promise an exact refund date or shipping date unless your business can reliably meet it.

Explain Return Instructions Clearly

If the incorrect item needs to be returned, make the instructions easy to follow. Tell the customer what they need to do, where to find the return instructions, and whether your business provides a return label when that is part of your actual policy.

For example:

Please use the return instructions provided with this email to send the incorrect item back. If you have any difficulty with the return process, reply to this message and our support team will help you.

Do not invent a return address, label link, deadline, or shipping arrangement in a reusable template. Replace the placeholders with your store's real policy before sending the message.

Wrong Item Email Subject Lines

Here are practical subject-line options:

  • We’re Sorry About Your Order
  • We Received Your Wrong Item Report
  • We’re Fixing the Issue With Your Order
  • About the Item You Received
  • Your Replacement Order Update
  • Next Steps for Your Order

Choose a subject that accurately describes the purpose of the email. Avoid exaggerated language or urgency that does not reflect the situation.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a customer in the United States ordered a $59.99 black backpack but received a blue backpack. The business has confirmed the fulfillment mistake and will send the correct product.

Subject: We’re Fixing the Issue With Your Order

Hi Sarah,

I’m sorry that your order arrived with the wrong backpack. You ordered the black backpack, but the package contained the blue version instead.

We’ve confirmed the order details and will arrange to send the correct black backpack. We’ll provide the replacement tracking information once the shipment is processed.

For the incorrect backpack, please follow the return instructions provided by our support team.

We apologize for the mistake and appreciate your patience while we correct it.

Best,
Customer Support
[Company Name]

This is a hypothetical example. A real business should replace the details with its actual order, fulfillment, return, and refund information.

Using Automation for Order Problems

Customer service templates can be useful when a business handles the same type of order problem repeatedly. Automation can help organize these responses, but the message should still contain the customer's actual order information and the correct resolution.

For example, a workflow could identify a support request about an incorrect shipment and route it to a customer service process where the order details are checked before the response is sent.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Giving a generic apology

“Sorry for the inconvenience” alone does not tell the customer what will happen next. Acknowledge the actual problem and explain the resolution.

2. Blaming another department

Customers generally need a solution rather than an internal explanation of who made the mistake. Keep internal fulfillment details out of the response unless they are genuinely useful.

3. Making unsupported promises

Do not promise free express shipping, an immediate refund, or a specific delivery date unless the business has confirmed that commitment.

4. Making the customer repeat information

If the order number and product details are already available, use them. Asking customers to repeat information unnecessarily adds friction.

5. Forgetting the return process

If the incorrect item needs to be returned, explain the actual return process clearly. Do not leave the customer wondering what to do with the item they received.

6. Sending the same generic message to every customer

Templates save time, but personalization still matters. Include the customer's name, relevant order information, and the actual solution whenever possible. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Wrong Item Email Checklist

  • Confirm the customer's order before responding.
  • Use the customer's name.
  • Clearly acknowledge the wrong item.
  • Apologize without making excuses.
  • State what the customer ordered and what they received when confirmed.
  • Explain the replacement, exchange, or refund option.
  • Provide return instructions when required.
  • Use only real shipping and refund information.
  • Give a realistic timeframe when one is known.
  • Tell the customer how to get further help.
  • Follow up after the issue has been resolved when appropriate.

Final Thoughts

A wrong-item email should make the customer's next step obvious. Start by acknowledging the mistake, confirm the order details, explain the solution, and provide clear return instructions when necessary.

The strongest customer service templates are not simply copied messages. They provide a consistent structure while leaving enough room to personalize the response around the customer's actual order and problem. Current ecommerce guidance similarly emphasizes empathy, personalization, clear next steps, and follow-up. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Before sending the email, verify every promise about replacement, refund, return shipping, and delivery timing against your actual store policy.
