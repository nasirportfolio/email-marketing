How to Write an Out-of-Stock After Order Email to Customers

Telling a customer that an item they already ordered is suddenly out of stock is one of the more difficult ecommerce service messages to write. The customer has already made a purchase, so the email needs to be honest, specific, and focused on what can happen next.

Quick Answer: An out-of-stock after order email should clearly explain that the purchased item is unavailable, apologize for the problem, explain the available options, and give the customer a clear next step. Depending on the actual inventory and store policy, the options may include waiting for restock, choosing an alternative, receiving a refund, or canceling the affected item.

Out-of-Stock After Order Email Template

Subject: An Update About Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry to let you know that the [Product Name] you ordered is currently unavailable.

We discovered an inventory issue while processing your order, and we apologize that we were unable to fulfill this item as originally expected.

We can offer the following option: [insert the actual available option].

If you would prefer a different resolution, please [explain the actual process for selecting another option].

We’re sorry for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

Best,
[Customer Service Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is an Out-of-Stock After Order Email?

An out-of-stock after order email is a customer service message sent when a product becomes unavailable after the customer has already placed an order.

This situation is different from a normal out-of-stock product page. The customer has already purchased the item, so the business needs to explain how the existing order will be handled.

Inventory problems can occur because of inaccurate stock counts, fulfillment issues, unexpected demand, damaged inventory, or inventory being unavailable at the location that fulfills online orders. Shopify notes that inventory levels and fulfillment locations can affect whether products are available for online orders. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The email should focus on the customer's order rather than giving a long internal explanation. The important information is what happened, what options are available, and what the customer needs to do next.

When to Send the Email

Send the email as soon as the stock problem has been confirmed and the business knows which resolution options are actually available.

Do not wait until the original promised shipping date if you already know that the item cannot be fulfilled. Earlier communication gives the customer more information and allows them to decide whether to wait, choose another product, or request another available resolution.

If the item is temporarily unavailable but additional stock has been confirmed, you can explain the expected availability without presenting an uncertain date as a guarantee.

What to Check Before Sending It

Before contacting the customer, verify the inventory and order information carefully.

  • Order number
  • Customer name
  • Product name and variant
  • Quantity ordered
  • Current available inventory
  • Whether inventory exists at another fulfillment location
  • Whether the item is expected to be restocked
  • Whether another approved product can be offered
  • Actual refund or cancellation options
  • Any previous communication with the customer
Important: Do not send an out-of-stock notice simply because one inventory location shows zero. First confirm whether another location can fulfill the order and whether the order can actually be completed. Inventory and fulfillment-location settings can affect online availability. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

What to Include in the Email

A useful out-of-stock email should normally include:

  • The customer's name
  • The order number
  • The affected product and variant
  • A clear explanation that the item is unavailable
  • A brief apology
  • The reason, if it is useful and confirmed
  • The actual available options
  • Any relevant timing information
  • Instructions for choosing an option, when necessary
  • A clear way to contact customer support

Avoid filling the message with internal inventory terminology that the customer does not need. The customer mainly needs an accurate explanation and a practical path forward.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Start With the Important Information

Tell the customer immediately that the product they ordered is unavailable. Do not hide the main issue after several paragraphs.

I’m sorry to let you know that the Classic Leather Backpack you ordered is currently unavailable.

2. Acknowledge the Customer's Disappointment

The customer has already decided to buy the product, so an unexpected stock problem can be frustrating. A short, sincere apology is more useful than a long explanation.

We understand that you ordered this item expecting it to be available, and we’re sorry that we’re unable to fulfill it as originally expected.

3. Explain the Situation Honestly

If you know what caused the problem, explain it briefly. If you do not know the exact cause, do not invent one.

For example, if an inventory discrepancy has been confirmed, you can say:

We discovered an inventory discrepancy while processing your order and confirmed that the item is not currently available.

4. Present the Available Options

The resolution is the most important part of the email. Tell the customer what your business can actually offer.

Depending on the circumstances and store policy, the options may include waiting for a confirmed restock, choosing an available alternative, receiving a refund for the affected item, or canceling the affected portion of the order.

Explain the Backorder Option

A backorder allows a customer to purchase or keep an order for an item that is currently unavailable but expected to become available later. Shopify describes a backorder as an item purchased while out of stock and fulfilled at a later date. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

If your business offers a backorder option, explain the expected timing as accurately as possible. If the date is uncertain, give a realistic estimate rather than presenting an unconfirmed date as guaranteed.

Backorder example:

The item is currently out of stock, but we expect additional inventory to become available around [actual estimated timeframe]. If you would like to keep the item on your order, please [explain the actual confirmation process].

If you cannot provide a reliable restock estimate, say so clearly rather than giving the customer a date that may change.

Offer an Alternative Product Carefully

An alternative product can be useful when the original item is unavailable, but it should not be presented as an automatic replacement unless the customer has agreed to it or your actual policy allows that process.

Explain why the alternative is being suggested and provide enough information for the customer to make an informed choice.

If you prefer not to wait for the original item, we can help you choose from the currently available products in our [relevant category]. Please reply to this email and our support team can help you with the available options.

Do not describe an alternative as “equivalent” or “better” unless the business can substantiate that claim.

Explain Refund Options

If the product cannot be fulfilled and a refund is available, explain what part of the order will be refunded and how the customer can receive it according to the actual store process.

Shopify supports refunds for an entire order or selected items, including partial refunds, depending on the order and payment situation. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Refund example:

Since the [Product Name] is unavailable, we can refund the amount paid for that item according to our refund policy. We’ll confirm once the refund has been submitted.

Do not promise an exact bank-processing time unless your payment process supports that timeframe. The time required for funds to appear can depend on the payment method and provider.

When the Order Contains Multiple Items

An out-of-stock problem becomes more complicated when the order contains several products. The customer needs to know whether the available items will still ship and how the unavailable item affects the rest of the order.

If the other items can ship

The remaining items in your order can continue through fulfillment. The [Product Name] is currently unavailable, so we’d like to confirm how you would like us to handle that item.

If the unavailable item affects the entire order

The [Product Name] is currently unavailable, and this affects the fulfillment of your order. We’re sorry for the inconvenience. We can offer [actual available options].

Be precise about which items are affected. Do not make the customer guess whether the entire order has been delayed or only one product.

Out-of-Stock Email Subject Lines

Here are practical subject-line options:

  • An Update About Your Order
  • Important Update About Your Order
  • We’re Sorry — An Item in Your Order Is Unavailable
  • Update About [Product Name]
  • We Need to Update Your Order
  • Your Order — Next Steps
  • Availability Update for Your Order

Choose a subject that accurately reflects the message. Avoid creating unnecessary urgency or making the email look like a promotional campaign.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a customer in the United States ordered a $79.99 backpack. During fulfillment, the store discovers that the selected color is unavailable. The business has confirmed that more stock is expected, but it cannot guarantee the exact arrival date.

Subject: An Update About Your Backpack Order

Hi Emily,

I’m sorry to let you know that the black version of the backpack you ordered is currently unavailable.

We discovered an inventory issue while processing your order and confirmed that we cannot ship the selected color right now.

We expect additional inventory around [actual estimated timeframe]. If you would like to wait for the black backpack, we can keep you updated as inventory becomes available.

If you prefer another option, please reply to this email and our support team can help you review the available alternatives.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

Best,
Customer Support
[Company Name]

This is a hypothetical example. Replace the product, price, availability estimate, and resolution with information confirmed by the actual business.

Follow up after the customer chooses an option

If the customer chooses a replacement, backorder, alternative product, or refund, send the appropriate confirmation after the action has actually been completed.

Follow-up example:

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re following up to confirm that your selected resolution for [Product Name] has been processed. [Insert the actual next step or confirmation.]

Thank you for your patience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Waiting too long to tell the customer

If you already know the product cannot be fulfilled, delaying communication can create more frustration. Contact the customer once the situation and available options have been confirmed.

2. Blaming the inventory team

Internal explanations rarely solve the customer's problem. Keep the message focused on the customer's order and the available resolution.

3. Promising an uncertain restock date

A tentative supplier or inventory estimate should not be presented as a guaranteed delivery date. If the timing is uncertain, say so.

4. Automatically replacing the product

Do not substitute another product without following the business's actual policy and customer-agreement process.

5. Forgetting partial-order details

If only one product is unavailable, clearly explain what happens to the other products in the order.

6. Offering an alternative without enough information

If you suggest another product, give the customer enough information to understand the choice. Avoid unsupported claims that an alternative is “better” or “equivalent.”

7. Giving a refund promise without checking the actual order

Confirm the amount and applicable refund process before making a customer-facing promise. Shopify allows full or partial refunds depending on the order circumstances. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Out-of-Stock Email Checklist

  • Confirm the order number.
  • Verify the exact product and variant.
  • Check inventory across relevant fulfillment locations.
  • Confirm whether the item can be restocked.
  • Determine the actual available customer options.
  • Explain the problem clearly.
  • Apologize without making excuses.
  • Provide a realistic timeframe when one is known.
  • Explain backorder options when actually available.
  • Explain replacement or alternative-product options accurately.
  • Explain refund options according to the actual policy.
  • Clarify what happens to other items in a multi-item order.
  • Tell the customer what action they need to take.
  • Follow up after the selected resolution has been processed.

Final Thoughts

An out-of-stock-after-order email should be honest, specific, and solution-focused. Tell the customer what happened, explain the actual options, and make the next step easy to understand.

If the item can be restocked, provide a realistic estimate. If an alternative is available, let the customer understand the choice. If the item cannot be fulfilled, explain the applicable refund or cancellation process clearly.

Most importantly, verify the inventory and order details before sending the message. Good customer-service email templates save time, but accuracy and personalization should come first.
