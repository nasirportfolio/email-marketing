How to Write a Product Usage Tips Email After Purchase

A product usage tips email helps customers understand how to get more value from something they have already purchased. Instead of sending another generic promotional message, the email gives practical guidance that helps the customer use the product with more confidence.

Quick Answer: A useful product usage tips email should focus on a few practical actions the customer can take with the product. Show the customer what to do, explain the most important tips clearly, and provide a relevant guide or support option when more information is needed. Keep the advice specific to the product purchased.

Start With This Product Usage Tips Email Template

Use this template as a starting point and replace the placeholders with verified information about the customer's actual product.

Subject: 3 tips to get more from your [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

Now that you have your [Product Name], here are a few simple tips to help you get started.

Tip 1: [Useful action]

[Brief explanation of how and why to do it.]

Tip 2: [Useful action]

[Brief explanation of how and why to do it.]

Tip 3: [Useful action]

[Brief explanation of how and why to do it.]

Want more help? Visit our [Product Guide / Help Center / Tutorial].

If you have a question, contact our support team.

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

The template should be adapted to the actual product. Do not publish generic instructions if the product requires specific setup, safety, maintenance, or operating guidance.

What Is a Product Usage Tips Email?

A product usage tips email is a post-purchase message that teaches customers practical ways to use a product they have already purchased.

The message might explain setup, common features, recommended workflows, useful shortcuts, or simple ways to get better results.

This type of email is different from a shipping or delivery notification. Those messages primarily tell the customer where an order is. A usage email focuses on what the customer can do with the product after receiving it.

Why Send Product Usage Tips?

A customer may buy a product because they understand its main benefit but still need help using it properly. A short educational email can bridge that gap.

Useful product education can:

  • Help customers get started.
  • Explain useful features or functions.
  • Reduce confusion after delivery.
  • Direct customers to appropriate support resources.
  • Give customers practical reasons to keep engaging with the product.
  • Create a more helpful post-purchase experience.

Shopify's current post-purchase guidance includes product education among the key customer touchpoints after delivery, with examples such as usage guides, setup tips, and maintenance information. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

When Should You Send It?

The best timing depends on when the customer can actually use the information.

For a simple product, tips may be useful shortly after delivery. For a product that requires setup or learning, the first message may need to arrive as soon as the customer receives it, followed by additional educational messages.

Current Shopify guidance gives one to three days after purchase or delivery as an example timing window for product education, but the right timing depends on the product and customer journey. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

A Practical Timing Framework

  • Immediately: Essential setup or access instructions.
  • After delivery: Basic usage guidance.
  • A few days later: Advanced tips or useful features.
  • Later: Maintenance, replenishment, or related education where appropriate.

Do not send advanced tips before the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to understand the basics.

What to Include

A product usage email does not need to contain the entire product manual. Select the information that is most useful at the current stage of the customer journey.

  • Product name.
  • A short explanation of the email's purpose.
  • Three to five useful tips where appropriate.
  • Short explanations or examples.
  • A link to detailed documentation if needed.
  • Customer support information.
  • A relevant next step.

If the product has important safety or operating requirements, those should be presented accurately and prominently rather than buried among marketing content.

How to Write It Step by Step

1. Start With the Customer's Purchase

Tell the customer why they are receiving the email.

Now that you've received your [Product Name], here are three simple ways to get more from it.

2. Choose a Small Number of Useful Tips

Avoid turning the email into a complete manual. Select the tips that are most likely to help the customer right now.

3. Explain the Benefit

Don't only tell the customer what button to press or what action to take. Explain what the action helps them accomplish when that explanation is useful.

4. Make Each Tip Actionable

A useful tip should tell the customer what to do rather than simply describe a feature.

5. Provide a Deeper Resource

If customers need more detail, link to the appropriate guide, knowledge base, tutorial, or support page.

How to Choose Useful Tips

The quality of a usage email depends heavily on choosing the right tips.

Good sources for selecting tips include:

  • Frequently asked customer questions.
  • Common setup problems.
  • Features customers often overlook.
  • Common usage mistakes.
  • Support tickets and customer service conversations.
  • Product documentation.
  • Questions asked by new customers.

If customers repeatedly ask the same question after purchasing a product, that question may be a strong candidate for a product usage email.

Use a Simple Format

A simple structure makes the email easier to scan, especially on mobile devices.

Subject: 3 ways to get more from your [Product]

Opening: One short paragraph.

Tip 1: Action + explanation.

Tip 2: Action + explanation.

Tip 3: Action + explanation.

Resource: Link to a detailed guide.

Support: Tell the customer where to ask questions.

This structure gives customers the answer quickly while still providing a path to deeper information.

Examples for Different Products

Fitness Equipment

Tips might explain basic setup, safe use, recommended starting settings, or where to find training instructions.

Kitchen Equipment

Tips might cover first-time setup, recommended settings, cleaning, or simple ways to use an important feature.

Clothing

Tips might explain fit, styling, washing, storage, or material-specific care when those instructions are verified.

Consumer Electronics

Tips might cover initial setup, important features, charging practices, connectivity, or links to the official support documentation.

These examples describe possible content categories. The actual instructions should always match the product's verified documentation.

Include a Support Path

Educational email does not eliminate the need for customer support. Some customers will still have questions.

Give them a clear next step such as:

  • Read the complete product guide.
  • Visit the help center.
  • Watch an official tutorial.
  • Contact customer support.
  • Read the product FAQ.

Keep the support path relevant to the product. Avoid sending customers to a generic homepage when a specific help article would answer their question.

This is particularly useful after delivery. Your delivery confirmation email can mark the point when the customer receives the product, while the usage email can provide the next layer of practical help.

Product Usage Subject Lines

Keep the subject line specific enough that the customer understands the value of opening the message.

  • 3 tips to get more from your [Product]
  • How to get started with your [Product]
  • A few useful tips for your new [Product]
  • Get more from your [Product]
  • Have you tried these [Product] features?
  • Simple ways to use your new [Product]
  • Your [Product] guide: 3 useful tips
  • Make the most of your new [Product]

Avoid making the subject line promise something the email does not actually provide.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a U.S. ecommerce store that sells a countertop espresso machine. After delivery, the store wants to help a new customer get started without overwhelming them with the complete manual.

Subject: 3 tips to get more from your espresso machine

Hi Alex,

Now that your espresso machine has arrived, here are three simple tips to help you get started.

1. Start with the recommended setup

Follow the setup instructions supplied with your specific model before making your first drink.

2. Use the recommended settings

Start with the settings provided in the official product guide and adjust them as you become familiar with the machine.

3. Learn the cleaning routine

Follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions and recommended maintenance schedule.

For detailed instructions, visit our [Espresso Machine Guide].

Questions? Our support team is here to help.

Thanks,
The [Brand Name] Team

This is a hypothetical example. The actual setup, operating, and maintenance instructions must come from the product's verified documentation.

Automate the Email

Product usage emails are well suited to automation because the customer's purchase can determine which educational message they receive.

A basic workflow can look like this:

  1. Customer purchases a product.
  2. The order is fulfilled and delivered.
  3. The system identifies the product purchased.
  4. The customer enters the appropriate education flow.
  5. The usage tips email is sent.
  6. A later message can provide advanced guidance if appropriate.

Shopify's current automation guidance describes post-purchase emails as useful for teaching customers how to use or care for products, and recommends reviewing automated flows rather than treating them as permanently finished. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

If you are building a broader post-purchase sequence, your post-purchase follow-up email can come before more specific educational messages.

Use Purchase-Based Segmentation

Product usage emails become more useful when customers receive information related to what they actually bought.

Useful segmentation data may include:

  • Product purchased.
  • Product category.
  • Product model or version.
  • Customer type.
  • First-time versus repeat purchase.
  • Purchase date.
  • Previous product ownership.

For example, a customer purchasing a first-time-use product may need introductory tips, while a repeat customer may benefit more from advanced features.

Current Shopify guidance similarly describes using purchase history and customer behavior to tailor post-purchase flows. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Measure and Improve

After launching the email, review whether customers are actually engaging with the content and whether the flow is contributing to a better customer experience.

Useful measurements may include:

  • Delivery rate.
  • Click-through rate.
  • Clicks on the product guide.
  • Support interactions.
  • Unsubscribe rate.
  • Conversion to a relevant next action.

Do not judge the email using one metric alone. For an educational message, a click to a detailed product guide may be more meaningful than a direct purchase.

Review the flow periodically and update the content when products, documentation, or customer questions change. Shopify's current automation guidance recommends regular review of automated flows rather than a "set and forget" approach. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Common Product Usage Email Mistakes

  • Sending generic advice: Match the content to the purchased product.
  • Including too many tips: Start with the most useful information.
  • Using unverified instructions: Confirm product-specific information before publication.
  • Making the email too promotional: The main purpose should be useful customer education.
  • Sending the wrong product guide: Test segmentation and dynamic content carefully.
  • Ignoring mobile readers: Use short paragraphs, bullets, and clear links.
  • Sending too early: Make sure the customer can reasonably use the information.
  • Never updating the flow: Review instructions when products or documentation change.

Product Usage Email Checklist

  • ☐ The email identifies the purchased product.
  • ☐ The tips are relevant to that specific product.
  • ☐ The information is accurate and verified.
  • ☐ The most useful information appears early.
  • ☐ The tips are actionable.
  • ☐ The email is easy to scan on mobile.
  • ☐ A detailed guide is provided when necessary.
  • ☐ Customer support is easy to find.
  • ☐ Product-based segmentation has been tested.
  • ☐ The timing fits the customer's product journey.
  • ☐ The automation stops or changes when appropriate.
  • ☐ Links have been tested.
  • ☐ The email provides useful information rather than unnecessary promotion.

Final Thoughts

A product usage tips email is a practical way to stay useful after a customer has purchased from you. Instead of immediately asking for another sale, help the customer understand how to use the product well.

Keep the message focused, product-specific, and actionable. Start with the most useful tips, provide a deeper resource when necessary, and make customer support easy to reach.

When automated correctly, product education can become a useful part of the post-purchase journey without turning every customer interaction into a promotion.
