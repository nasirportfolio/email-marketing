What Is Email Marketing Segmentation? A Complete Beginner's Guide

Email marketing segmentation is the process of dividing an email list into smaller groups of subscribers based on shared characteristics, interests, actions, or other relevant information.

Instead of sending exactly the same email to every subscriber, businesses can use segmentation to deliver messages that are more relevant to different groups of people.

Quick Answer: Email marketing segmentation means organizing subscribers into relevant groups so that businesses can send more targeted and useful email communications. Segments can be based on interests, customer status, engagement, behavior, location, or other appropriate data.

What Is Email Marketing Segmentation?

Email marketing segmentation is the practice of dividing an email subscriber list into smaller groups according to information that is useful for marketing communication.

These groups are commonly called segments.

For example, an online store might have separate segments for new subscribers, existing customers, frequent buyers, and subscribers who have shown interest in a particular product category.

Each segment can receive communication that is appropriate for its situation or interests.

Why Is Email Segmentation Important?

Subscribers are not all the same. They can have different needs, interests, levels of engagement, and relationships with a business.

Segmentation helps marketers recognize these differences and create more relevant communication.

1. It Can Improve Relevance

A segmented email campaign can focus on information that is more closely related to the recipient's interests.

2. It Supports Better Customer Communication

New subscribers may need educational information, while existing customers may need product updates, support information, or recommendations.

3. It Supports Personalization

Segmentation provides a foundation for personalized email experiences. Marketers can tailor content based on information they have appropriately collected about subscribers.

4. It Works Well With Automation

Segments can be used as conditions or audiences in automated email workflows.

You can learn more about automation in What Is Email Marketing Automation? .

5. It Can Reduce Irrelevant Messages

Sending every campaign to every subscriber can result in unnecessary messages. Segmentation can help businesses send appropriate campaigns to appropriate audiences.

How Does Email Segmentation Work?

Email segmentation generally follows a simple process:

  1. Collect appropriate subscriber information.
  2. Identify meaningful differences between subscribers.
  3. Create groups based on those differences.
  4. Prepare content for each relevant group.
  5. Send the appropriate campaign to each segment.
  6. Measure engagement and improve the strategy.

The exact process depends on the email marketing platform and the data available to the business.

Common Types of Email Segmentation

There are many ways to segment an email list. The best method depends on the business model, audience, available data, and marketing objective.

Segmentation Type Example Possible Use
Demographic Age group or job role Tailor content to audience characteristics
Interest Interested in email automation Send topic-specific educational content
Behavior Clicked a product link Follow up with related information
Customer status Prospect or customer Use different communication journeys
Engagement Highly active subscriber Adjust campaigns based on engagement
Location Subscribers in a particular region Deliver regionally relevant communication

Demographic Segmentation

Demographic segmentation groups subscribers according to relevant demographic information.

Depending on the business and the information legitimately collected, this might include factors such as:

  • Age group
  • Job role
  • Industry
  • Company size
  • Language preference

Demographic information should only be collected and used when it has a clear and legitimate purpose for the communication.

Interest-Based Segmentation

Interest-based segmentation groups subscribers according to topics, products, services, or subjects they have indicated an interest in.

Example:

Suppose an email marketing website covers three main topics:

  • Email automation
  • Email design
  • Email analytics

Subscribers can be grouped according to the topics they choose during signup or the content they intentionally interact with.

Behavioral Segmentation

Behavioral segmentation uses subscriber actions to create meaningful groups.

Examples include subscribers who:

  • Opened a particular email.
  • Clicked a specific link.
  • Downloaded a resource.
  • Visited a particular page.
  • Purchased a product.
  • Started but did not complete an action.

Behavioral data can be particularly useful because it reflects what a subscriber actually did rather than only what they reported about themselves.

Customer Status Segmentation

Businesses can separate subscribers according to their relationship with the company.

Group Example Communication
New subscriber Welcome and educational content
Prospect Helpful information and product education
New customer Onboarding and product guidance
Existing customer Useful updates and relevant offers
Former customer Appropriate re-engagement communication

Engagement-Based Segmentation

Engagement segmentation groups subscribers according to how actively they interact with emails.

A business might distinguish between highly engaged, moderately engaged, and inactive subscribers.

This can help marketers avoid treating an inactive subscriber exactly like someone who regularly interacts with campaigns.

Location-Based Segmentation

Location-based segmentation groups subscribers according to relevant geographic information.

It can be useful when businesses operate across different regions, countries, or time zones.

Possible uses include:

  • Regional promotions
  • Local events
  • Time-zone appropriate sending
  • Region-specific information
  • Language-specific communication

Email Segmentation Examples

Example 1: Online Clothing Store

An online clothing store could create segments based on product categories that customers have shown interest in.

Customers interested in men's running shoes could receive relevant product education or new-product information instead of campaigns focused on unrelated categories.

Example 2: Software Company

A software company might separate free users, trial users, and paying customers.

Each group can receive communication appropriate to its stage in the customer journey.

Example 3: Educational Website

An educational website might allow subscribers to choose subjects they want to learn.

Subscribers can then receive articles and resources related to their selected subjects.

How to Create Email Segments

Step 1: Define Your Goal

Start by deciding why you need segmentation.

For example, you might want to improve educational content relevance, create different onboarding journeys, or communicate with customers based on their interests.

Step 2: Identify Useful Data

Determine which information can meaningfully support your goal.

Avoid collecting large amounts of information simply because it is technically possible.

Step 3: Create Meaningful Segments

Create groups that have a clear purpose.

A segment should help you make a meaningful communication decision.

Step 4: Prepare Relevant Content

Create content that matches the needs or interests of the segment.

Step 5: Test Your Campaign

Check that the correct subscribers are included and that the message matches the intended audience.

Step 6: Measure Results

Review appropriate email marketing metrics and use the results to improve future campaigns.

Segmentation and Email Automation

Segmentation and automation work well together.

A segment can determine who receives an automated workflow, while subscriber actions can also cause people to enter or leave particular groups.

Simple Automation Example:
  1. A visitor subscribes to an email list.
  2. The subscriber selects "Email Automation" as an interest.
  3. The subscriber enters an automation segment.
  4. A welcome email is sent.
  5. Relevant automation articles are recommended.
  6. Future messages are adjusted according to engagement.

Segmentation and Personalization

Segmentation and personalization are related but they are not exactly the same thing.

Segmentation groups subscribers into categories.

Personalization adapts content or messaging for an individual subscriber or a particular audience using available information.

For example, a business could create a segment for customers interested in running shoes and then personalize an email using the customer's name or relevant product information.

Common Email Segmentation Mistakes

1. Creating Too Many Segments

Excessive segmentation can make campaigns difficult to manage and may provide little additional value.

2. Using Irrelevant Data

Information should have a meaningful connection to the communication strategy.

3. Ignoring Data Quality

Incorrect or outdated subscriber information can lead to poor targeting.

4. Sending the Same Message to Every Segment

If every segment receives exactly the same communication, the benefit of creating the segments may be limited.

5. Over-Personalizing

Personalization should be useful and appropriate. Using information in ways subscribers do not expect can reduce trust.

6. Forgetting Inactive Subscribers

Engagement should be monitored over time. Inactive subscribers may need a different communication strategy from highly engaged subscribers.

Email Segmentation Best Practices

  • Start with a clear business or communication objective.
  • Collect only useful and appropriate information.
  • Keep segments simple enough to manage.
  • Use behavior and interests when they are relevant.
  • Review and update segments regularly.
  • Match content to each segment.
  • Respect subscriber expectations.
  • Test campaigns before sending them.
  • Monitor engagement and campaign performance.
  • Protect subscriber information and follow applicable privacy requirements.

Email Segmentation Checklist

Use this checklist when creating an email segmentation strategy:

  • ☐ Define the purpose of segmentation.
  • ☐ Identify useful subscriber information.
  • ☐ Create clear and meaningful segments.
  • ☐ Check the quality of your subscriber data.
  • ☐ Prepare relevant content for each segment.
  • ☐ Test the audience before sending.
  • ☐ Connect segments to automation where appropriate.
  • ☐ Monitor engagement.
  • ☐ Review segments regularly.
  • ☐ Respect subscriber privacy and expectations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is email marketing segmentation?

Email marketing segmentation is the process of dividing an email list into smaller groups based on relevant characteristics, interests, behaviors, or customer relationships.

Why is email segmentation important?

Segmentation helps businesses make email communication more relevant by allowing different groups of subscribers to receive content that better matches their needs or interests.

What are the main types of email segmentation?

Common types include demographic, interest-based, behavioral, customer-status, engagement-based, and location-based segmentation.

What is behavioral segmentation in email marketing?

Behavioral segmentation groups subscribers according to actions such as opening emails, clicking links, downloading resources, visiting pages, or purchasing products.

What is the difference between segmentation and personalization?

Segmentation divides subscribers into groups, while personalization adapts content or communication using information about an individual subscriber or a particular audience.

Can email segmentation be automated?

Yes. Many email marketing platforms allow segments to be created or updated based on subscriber information and behavior, and those segments can be connected to automated workflows.

Should a small business use email segmentation?

Yes. A small business does not need a complicated segmentation system. Starting with a few meaningful groups based on customer status, interests, or engagement can be useful.

Conclusion

Email marketing segmentation is a practical way to organize subscribers into meaningful groups and deliver more relevant communication.

Effective segmentation begins with a clear purpose. Businesses should identify useful information, create manageable groups, provide relevant content, and regularly review campaign performance.

Segmentation can also become more powerful when combined with email automation and personalization.
