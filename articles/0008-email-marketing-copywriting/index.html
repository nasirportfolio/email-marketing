What Is Email Marketing Copywriting? A Complete Beginner's Guide

Email marketing copywriting is the process of writing the words used in marketing emails to inform, engage, persuade, or guide subscribers toward a specific action.

Quick Answer: Email marketing copywriting is the practice of writing clear, relevant, and persuasive email content that helps a subscriber understand a message and take an intended action.

Email copy can include the subject line, preview text, greeting, body content, product descriptions, calls to action, and other words that appear in an email. Good email copy focuses on the reader's needs rather than simply promoting a product or service.

What Is Email Marketing Copywriting?

Email marketing copywriting is the writing of marketing-focused email content. The purpose of the copy depends on the campaign. An email might educate subscribers, introduce a product, encourage a purchase, announce an event, build a relationship, or bring inactive customers back.

Email copywriting is different from simply writing an email. Marketing copy has a specific communication goal and is usually written with a particular audience and desired action in mind.

Effective email copy should be understandable, useful, relevant, and appropriate for the audience.

Why Is Email Copywriting Important?

Email marketing depends heavily on communication. Even a technically well-designed email can perform poorly if the message is confusing, irrelevant, or difficult to act on.

Good copywriting can help a business:

  • Explain a product or service clearly.
  • Communicate the value of an offer.
  • Capture attention with a relevant subject line.
  • Guide readers toward a specific action.
  • Build trust with subscribers.
  • Educate an audience.
  • Maintain relationships with customers.

Copywriting is only one part of email marketing. Audience targeting, segmentation, personalization, deliverability, design, timing, and the quality of the offer can also influence campaign performance.

How to Write Better Email Subject Lines

The subject line is one of the first pieces of email copy a recipient sees. It should accurately communicate what the email is about while giving the reader a reason to consider opening it.

Keep the Message Clear

A clear subject line is generally more useful than a vague subject that makes the reader guess what the email contains.

Vague: Something special for you

Clear: A beginner's guide to email segmentation

Focus on the Reader

Consider what the subscriber wants to learn, solve, achieve, or discover.

Avoid Misleading Claims

The subject line should accurately represent the email. Misleading subject lines can reduce trust and create a poor subscriber experience.

How to Write Preview Text

Preview text provides additional context that can support the subject line. Instead of repeating the subject line, use the available space to explain what the reader can expect.

Subject: How to build an email list

Preview text: Learn practical ways to attract subscribers and grow a permission-based list.

How to Write Effective Email Body Copy

1. Start With the Reader's Problem or Goal

Begin by addressing something relevant to the audience. This helps readers understand why the message matters to them.

2. Explain the Value

Tell the reader what they can gain from reading the email, using the product, or following the recommended action.

3. Keep the Message Focused

Avoid putting several unrelated ideas into one email. A focused message is easier to understand.

4. Use Simple Language

Write in a way that your intended audience can easily understand. Short sentences and familiar words can make marketing emails easier to read.

5. Use Short Paragraphs

Large blocks of text can be difficult to read, particularly on mobile devices. Break longer content into smaller sections.

6. Give the Reader a Clear Next Step

Once the message has been explained, make the intended action clear.

How to Write Effective Calls to Action

A call to action, often called a CTA, tells the reader what to do next.

Common actions include:

  • Read an article.
  • Download a resource.
  • Register for an event.
  • View a product.
  • Start a trial.
  • Complete a purchase.
  • Contact the business.

A good CTA should make the intended action understandable.

Weak: Click Here

More descriptive: Download the Email Checklist

A Simple Email Copy Structure

Beginners can use a simple structure when writing a marketing email.

  1. Opening: Introduce the topic or problem.
  2. Context: Explain why it matters to the reader.
  3. Value: Provide useful information, benefits, or an appropriate offer.
  4. Action: Tell the reader what to do next.
  5. Closing: End naturally and maintain the relationship with the subscriber.
Simple Example:

Problem: Your email campaigns are getting clicks, but subscribers are not completing the desired action.

Value: A clearer call to action and more focused email copy may make the next step easier to understand.

Action: Review your CTA and simplify the message.

Email Marketing Copywriting Best Practices

1. Know Your Audience

Before writing an email, understand who will receive it and what matters to them.

2. Write With One Main Goal

Give the email a clear purpose. Multiple unrelated CTAs can make the message harder to follow.

3. Lead With Value

Give subscribers a useful reason to read the email. Helpful content can build trust over time.

4. Make the Copy Scannable

Use short paragraphs, headings, lists, and clear CTAs where appropriate.

5. Match the Copy With the Landing Page

When an email sends a subscriber to another page, the destination should clearly match the promise and topic of the email.

6. Personalize When It Adds Value

Personalization can make messages more relevant when accurate subscriber information is available.

7. Use Segmentation

Different audiences may have different needs. Segmentation can help you send more relevant messages to different groups.

8. Test Your Copy

Test appropriate elements such as subject lines, CTAs, content, or offers to understand how your audience responds.

Common Email Copywriting Mistakes

  • Writing without a clear audience in mind.
  • Using complicated language.
  • Making the email about the business instead of the reader.
  • Using misleading subject lines.
  • Including too many unrelated messages.
  • Using vague calls to action.
  • Writing long blocks of text.
  • Making unsupported claims.
  • Forgetting to test the email.
  • Ignoring the mobile reading experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is email marketing copywriting?

Email marketing copywriting is the process of writing marketing email content to inform, engage, persuade, or guide subscribers toward a specific action.

What does an email copywriter write?

An email copywriter may write subject lines, preview text, greetings, email body content, product messaging, promotional copy, and calls to action.

How is email copywriting different from content writing?

Both involve writing, but email copywriting is usually designed for direct communication with subscribers and often has a specific action or campaign goal. Content writing may focus more broadly on education, information, or organic search visibility.

How long should a marketing email be?

There is no single ideal length for every email. The email should be long enough to communicate its purpose clearly without unnecessary information. The appropriate length depends on the audience, message, and goal.

What makes an email subject line effective?

An effective subject line is clear, relevant to the recipient, and consistent with the content of the email. It should avoid misleading claims.

What is a CTA in email marketing?

CTA stands for call to action. It tells the reader what action to take next, such as reading an article, downloading a resource, registering for an event, or viewing a product.

Should email copy be personalized?

Personalization can be useful when it makes the message more relevant. It should be based on accurate information and used responsibly.

Conclusion

Email marketing copywriting is an important part of creating effective email campaigns. It helps businesses communicate ideas, explain value, educate subscribers, promote products, and guide readers toward useful actions.

Beginners should focus on understanding the audience, choosing one clear goal, writing useful and readable content, creating descriptive calls to action, and testing important elements.

As your email marketing skills develop, combine copywriting with segmentation, personalization, automation, and a strong overall email marketing strategy.
