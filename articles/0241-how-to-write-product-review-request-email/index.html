How to Write a Product Review Request Email

Quick Answer: A product review request email should arrive after the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to experience the product. Keep the message short, explain why you are asking, make the review action easy to find, and avoid pressuring the customer to leave a positive review. If the customer has a problem, provide a clear support option instead of treating the review request as the only next step.

A customer has purchased your product and had an opportunity to use it. Now you would like to know what they think.

Asking for a review sounds simple, but the wording and timing can make a major difference to the customer experience. A request that is too early may arrive before the customer can reasonably evaluate the product. A request that is too complicated may make the customer ignore it.

The goal of a product review request email is not to tell customers what they should say. The goal is to make it easy for customers to share an honest opinion about their experience.

What Is a Product Review Request Email?

A product review request email is a message sent to a customer asking them to share their experience with a product they purchased.

The request may ask the customer to leave a written review, provide a rating, or visit a review page where they can share their experience.

A review request is different from a general feedback email. A review request normally asks for an opinion that can be submitted as a product review, while general feedback may be intended for internal improvement, support, or research.

For a broader look at customer feedback, see Article 0048 — Referral Customer Feedback .

Decide What the Email Should Accomplish

Before writing the email, identify the single action you want the customer to understand.

For a review request, that action might be:

  • Open the review page.
  • Rate the purchased product.
  • Write a short review.
  • Share an honest experience with the product.

Keeping the purpose clear helps prevent the email from becoming a mixture of review requests, promotions, surveys, social-media requests, and unrelated announcements.

When Should You Send a Product Review Request?

Timing should depend on when the customer can reasonably evaluate the product.

A customer who has just placed an order may not have received the product yet. A customer who received it yesterday may not have used it enough to form a useful opinion.

Before sending a review request, consider:

  1. Has the customer received the product?
  2. Has the customer had a reasonable opportunity to use it?
  3. Does the product require setup or preparation?
  4. Would the customer reasonably be able to evaluate it at this stage?

The appropriate timing can therefore differ between products. A simple item may be evaluated quickly, while a product that requires repeated use may need more time.

How to Write the Subject Line

A good subject line should make the purpose of the email understandable without creating misleading urgency or exaggerated claims.

Example subject lines:
  • How are you enjoying your new product?
  • Could you share your experience?
  • Tell us what you think
  • How did your recent purchase work for you?
  • Would you share a quick product review?

Choose a subject line that accurately reflects the email. If the message asks for a review, the subject should not disguise the purpose as something unrelated.

How to Start the Email

The opening should connect naturally to the customer's recent purchase.

You can acknowledge the purchase or the customer's experience before making the review request.

Avoid starting with a long explanation about why reviews are important to your company. The customer is more likely to understand the message when the reason for contacting them is clear.

Simple opening:

We hope you have had a chance to try your recent purchase. When you have a moment, we would appreciate hearing about your experience.

How to Ask for the Review

Make the request direct and easy to understand.

The customer should not have to search through several paragraphs to discover what you want them to do.

A simple request might explain:

  • What you are asking for.
  • Why the customer's experience is useful.
  • Where they can submit the review.
  • What they should do if they need assistance instead.

The wording should leave room for an honest response. A review request should not imply that the customer must leave a positive review.

Create One Clear Call to Action

The review action should be easy to find.

If the main purpose is collecting a product review, make the review link or button the primary action.

Avoid surrounding the request with many competing links such as unrelated products, blog posts, social-media profiles, discount offers, and newsletters.

Primary action:

Share your product review

If customers may need help with an order or product problem, a secondary support option can be included without competing with the main review action.

Ask for an Honest Review

A review request should allow the customer to describe their actual experience.

Avoid wording that tells the customer what rating or type of review you want them to provide.

For example, instead of asking customers to "leave us a five-star review," you can simply invite them to share their experience.

This keeps the request focused on customer feedback rather than attempting to control the customer's response.

Give Customers a Support Option

Not every customer will have a positive experience. Some may have a question, encounter a problem, or need assistance with the product.

A thoughtful review request can make it easy for those customers to contact support.

Example:

If something is not working as expected, please contact our support team before leaving your review. We will be happy to help.

The purpose is not to prevent negative feedback. It is to make sure customers have a clear path to assistance when they need it.

A Simple Product Review Request Structure

A practical review request can follow this structure:

  1. Acknowledge the purchase. Connect the email to the customer's recent experience.
  2. Give the customer context. Make sure the timing makes sense for the product.
  3. Ask for the review. State the requested action clearly.
  4. Provide the review link. Make the action easy to complete.
  5. Offer support. Give customers a way to get help if they have a problem.

This structure is enough for many review requests. The email does not need to become a long marketing message.

A Simple Hypothetical Example

Imagine an online store that sells a reusable water bottle. After the customer has had enough time to use the product, the store could send a short review request.

Subject: How are you enjoying your new bottle?

We hope you have had a chance to use your new bottle.

When you have a moment, we would appreciate hearing about your experience. Your feedback can help other customers understand the product and help us improve the customer experience.

Share your product review

If you have any questions or have experienced a problem with your order, please contact our support team so we can help.

This example is intentionally simple. The actual wording should reflect the product, brand voice, review process, and customer relationship.

Use Relevant Personalization

Personalization can make a review request more relevant when the information is accurate and genuinely useful.

Depending on the email system, the message might refer to:

  • The customer's first name.
  • The purchased product.
  • The purchase date or relevant customer event.
  • The appropriate review page.

Do not add personalization simply because a platform allows it. Every personalized element should improve clarity or relevance.

First-Time and Repeat Customers

A customer's previous relationship with your business can affect how you write the request.

First-time customers

A first-time customer may benefit from a little more context about why you are asking for feedback and where the review can be submitted.

Repeat customers

A repeat customer may already understand your review process. The request can therefore be shorter and more directly connected to the recent purchase.

The important point is to use customer history only when it genuinely helps make the message more relevant.

Can You Automate Review Request Emails?

Yes. Review requests can be connected to purchase and customer events through an email automation workflow.

A simple workflow might look like this:

  1. Customer completes a purchase.
  2. The order is delivered or another relevant event occurs.
  3. The system waits an appropriate amount of time.
  4. The review request is sent.
  5. The customer can submit a review or contact support.

Automation should be tested carefully. The timing should make sense, the review link should work, and customers should not receive unnecessary repeated requests.

Common Product Review Request Mistakes

1. Asking before the customer can use the product

If the customer has not had a reasonable opportunity to experience the product, the request may be premature.

2. Asking for a positive review

The request should invite an honest experience rather than instructing the customer what opinion to provide.

3. Hiding the review action

If the customer cannot quickly find where to leave the review, the request becomes unnecessarily difficult.

4. Adding too many competing actions

Multiple promotions and unrelated links can distract from the review request.

5. Ignoring customers who need help

Customers with problems should have a clear support path.

6. Sending repeated requests

An automated workflow should account for whether a customer has already completed the requested action.

Product Review vs. General Feedback

A product review and general customer feedback can overlap, but they can serve different purposes.

Aspect Product Review General Feedback
Primary purpose Share an experience with a product Provide broader feedback about an experience
Typical destination Product or review page Survey, form, support channel, or internal system
Potential audience Often other prospective customers Often the business or service team
Main question How was your experience with the product? What could we improve or understand better?

Choosing the right type of request makes it easier to write an email with a clear purpose.

Product Review Request Email Checklist

Before sending the email, check:

  • ☐ The customer has had a reasonable opportunity to use the product.
  • ☐ The reason for the email is clear.
  • ☐ The subject line accurately describes the message.
  • ☐ The review request is direct and easy to understand.
  • ☐ The customer is invited to share an honest experience.
  • ☐ The review link works correctly.
  • ☐ There is one clear primary call to action.
  • ☐ Customers who need help have a support option.
  • ☐ The email does not contain unnecessary promotions.
  • ☐ Customers who already reviewed are handled appropriately.
  • ☐ The email has been checked on mobile.
  • ☐ The automated timing has been tested.

How to Measure a Review Request Email

The measurement should reflect the purpose of the campaign.

If the primary goal is to collect product reviews, you can examine how many recipients complete the requested review action.

You may also examine supporting email metrics to understand whether customers are opening the message and interacting with the review link. These metrics should be interpreted in the context of the campaign rather than treated as the only measure of success.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I ask a customer for a product review?

Ask after the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to receive and experience the product. The appropriate timing depends on the type of product and the experience required to evaluate it.

What should a product review request email say?

It should explain why you are contacting the customer, invite them to share their experience, provide an easy way to submit the review, and offer support if they have a problem.

Should I ask customers for a five-star review?

A review request should allow customers to provide their honest experience rather than instructing them what rating to give.

How long should a review request email be?

It should be long enough to explain the request and provide the necessary action, but it does not need to contain unrelated information. A short, focused message is often easier to act on.

Can I automate product review requests?

Yes. A review request can be triggered by relevant customer or purchase events. The workflow should be tested to make sure the timing, links, and follow-up logic work correctly.

What if the customer had a bad experience?

Make it easy for the customer to contact support when appropriate. A review request should not prevent customers from communicating problems directly with the business.

Conclusion

A product review request email does not need to be complicated.

Start by choosing an appropriate time, connect the message to the customer's recent purchase, make the request clear, provide an easy review action, and give customers a support option when needed.

Most importantly, ask customers to share their genuine experience rather than trying to control what they say. A focused and respectful request is more useful to both the customer and the business.
