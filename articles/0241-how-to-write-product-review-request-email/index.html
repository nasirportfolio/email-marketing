How to Write a Product Review Request Email

Quick answer: Send a product review request after the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to use the product. Explain the request briefly, provide one clear review link, invite an honest opinion, and offer customer support when needed. Use a tested process to avoid sending unnecessary reminders or requesting a review before the product can reasonably be evaluated.

A customer has purchased your product. After delivery and enough time to experience it, you want to learn what worked well, what could be improved, and how the product met the customer's expectations.

A product review request email gives the customer a convenient way to share that experience. However, a request can be ineffective when it arrives too early, hides the review link, or asks the customer to provide a particular rating.

This guide explains how to plan, write, personalize, automate, and measure review request emails. It includes subject lines, a reusable email template, examples for different situations, and a pre-send checklist.

1. What Is a Product Review Request Email?

A product review request email is a message asking a customer to describe their experience with a product they purchased. Depending on the review system, the customer may be asked to submit a written review, select a rating, or answer a few product-related questions.

A product review is not exactly the same as general customer feedback. A public review is usually intended to help other people understand the product, while private feedback may help a business investigate a problem or improve its processes.

Decide which outcome you need before writing the message. If you want a public product review, link directly to the appropriate review page. If you want detailed internal feedback, use a suitable feedback form instead.

2. Define the Goal Before Writing

A good review request has one main purpose. Decide exactly what you want the customer to do and make that action clear.

  • Open the product review page.
  • Share an honest written review.
  • Rate the product using the available review system.
  • Describe a specific part of the product experience.

Avoid combining a review request with several unrelated promotions, newsletter invitations, product recommendations, and social-media requests. Those additional actions can distract from the main purpose.

3. Choose the Right Time to Send the Email

The right timing depends on the product, delivery method, and amount of experience required to evaluate it. There is no single delay that works for every business.

A customer may be able to evaluate a simple household item soon after delivery. A product that requires installation, repeated use, or a longer learning period may need more time.

Product or situation Timing consideration What to check
Simple physical product After delivery and an opportunity to use it Has the customer received and tried the item?
Product requiring setup After reasonable setup time Can the customer evaluate the setup and basic use?
Consumable product After an appropriate period of use Has the customer experienced enough of the product?
Digital product or software After access and initial use Has the customer been able to test the relevant features?
Delayed or incomplete order After the issue is resolved and evaluation is reasonable Is the review request appropriate at this stage?

These are planning considerations, not fixed timing rules. Use your delivery data, product characteristics, and customer support patterns to decide when a request is appropriate.

Check the order status first

Before an automated email is sent, confirm that the relevant event has occurred. A purchase confirmation does not prove that a physical product has arrived. Where possible, use a reliable delivery event or another suitable trigger.

Also consider whether a customer has an unresolved order issue. The workflow should respond appropriately rather than sending repeated, poorly timed messages.

4. Write a Clear Subject Line

The subject line should explain the purpose of the message without misleading the recipient or creating artificial urgency.

Subject line examples
  • How are you enjoying your recent purchase?
  • Could you share your product experience?
  • We would value your honest feedback
  • Would you write a quick product review?
  • How has your new product worked for you?

Choose a subject that accurately describes the email. Test different wording when you have enough recipients to make the comparison useful. Change one major element at a time so that the result is easier to interpret.

5. Start With a Relevant Opening

Connect the message to the customer's recent purchase. A brief opening helps the recipient understand why the email arrived.

Example opening

We hope you have had a chance to try your recent purchase. When you have a moment, we would appreciate hearing about your experience.

Avoid a long introduction about how important reviews are to the company. The customer's experience should remain the focus of the message.

6. Ask Directly for an Honest Review

State what you want the customer to do and explain where the review will be submitted. Do not ask customers to provide a particular star rating or suggest that only positive experiences are welcome.

A clear request can include three elements:

  1. The request: Invite the customer to share an honest experience.
  2. The destination: Provide the relevant review page or form.
  3. The choice: Let the customer decide what to say without pressure.

For example, write, "Would you share your experience with this product?" rather than, "Please give us five stars." The first version asks for an opinion; the second attempts to influence the result.

7. Use One Clear Call to Action

Make the review action easy to identify. Use descriptive link text such as "Share your product review" rather than a vague label that gives no indication of what happens next.

Suggested call to action:

Share your product review

This is an illustrative example. Replace the destination with the actual review page when preparing your campaign.

Before sending, test the real link on a phone and desktop computer. Confirm that it opens the correct product or review page and does not require customers to search for the item again.

A secondary support link can be useful, but avoid surrounding the main request with competing promotions and unrelated calls to action.

8. Offer Help Without Suppressing Negative Reviews

Some customers may have questions, receive a damaged item, or encounter a product problem. A support option gives them a direct way to request assistance.

Example support message

If you have a question or something is not working as expected, please contact our support team. We will be happy to help.

Support should not be used to discourage dissatisfied customers from posting honest reviews. Avoid sending only satisfied customers to public review sites while diverting dissatisfied customers away from them. Apply a fair, consistent review-request process and let customers express their genuine experiences.

9. Copyable Product Review Request Email Template

Use the following template as a starting point. Replace the placeholders with accurate information before sending.

Subject: How has your recent purchase worked for you?

Hello [Customer First Name],

Thank you for purchasing [Product Name] from [Business Name]. We hope you have had a chance to use it.

When you have a moment, would you share your honest experience with the product? Your review can help other customers make informed decisions and help us understand what we can improve.

Share your product review

If you have any questions or need help with your purchase, please contact us at [Support Email Address].

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Sender Name]
[Business Name]

Before sending: replace every placeholder and the example.com link. Do not send this template with an untested or incorrect destination.

10. Adapt the Email to Different Situations

For a first-time customer

Include enough context to explain why you are contacting the customer. Keep the request straightforward and make the review process easy to understand.

Example

Thank you for choosing us for your recent purchase. If you have had enough time to try [Product Name], we would appreciate hearing about your experience. You can share your honest review here: [Review Link].

For a repeat customer

If the customer is familiar with your business, the message can be shorter. Refer to the recent purchase rather than asking for a general review of every previous order.

Example

Thanks for shopping with us again. When you have had a chance to try [Product Name], would you share your thoughts? [Review Link]

For a digital product

Make sure the customer has received access and had a reasonable opportunity to try the relevant features.

Example

We hope you have had an opportunity to explore [Product Name]. If you would like to share your experience, you can leave an honest review here: [Review Link].

These are illustrative templates. Adjust them to match the actual product, customer relationship, and review process.

11. Personalize the Request Carefully

Personalization can make a message more relevant when the information is accurate and useful. Depending on your email platform and available data, you may include:

  • The customer's first name.
  • The product name.
  • A relevant purchase or delivery event.
  • A direct link to the correct product review page.

Test personalization fields before launching the campaign. If the customer's name is missing, the product field is incorrect, or the link points to another item, the message can become confusing.

Segment customers when there is a meaningful reason to change the message or timing. For an introduction to this concept, read Article 0006 — Email Marketing Segmentation.

12. Build a Reliable Review Request Automation

Automation can reduce manual work, but it should follow customer events accurately. A basic workflow can be designed as follows:

  1. A customer completes a purchase.
  2. The system waits for the appropriate delivery, access, or usage event.
  3. A suitable delay allows the customer to experience the product.
  4. The system checks whether the request is still appropriate.
  5. The review email is sent with the correct product link.
  6. The workflow records relevant outcomes and prevents unnecessary repeat messages.

Use safeguards in the workflow

  • Exclude canceled orders and orders that were never fulfilled when relevant.
  • Handle returns, replacements, and unresolved delivery problems appropriately.
  • Check whether a review has already been submitted when that information is available.
  • Limit reminders and respect unsubscribe requests and communication preferences.
  • Test the trigger, timing, personalization, and review link before launch.

A workflow should not assume that every purchase means the customer is ready to review the product. Build conditions around the events your system can reliably detect.

For more background, read Article 0004 — Email Marketing Automation.

13. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Sending the request too early

Customers need enough time to receive, access, or use the product. Choose the timing according to the product rather than using the purchase date alone.

Requesting a specific rating

Invite honest feedback instead of asking for a five-star rating or a positive comment.

Using a broken or generic review link

A customer should be able to reach the correct review destination without unnecessary steps. Test the link before sending.

Adding too many promotional messages

Extra offers and unrelated links may distract the customer from the primary action. Keep the email focused.

Repeatedly contacting the same customer

Uncontrolled automation can send multiple requests for one purchase. Set frequency limits and account for completed review actions when the relevant data is available.

Requesting reviews only from selected happy customers

A review process should not be designed to hide unfavorable experiences. Use consistent eligibility criteria and allow customers to express their genuine opinions.

14. Product Reviews vs. General Feedback

Both can help a business learn about customer experiences, but their destinations and purposes may differ.

Aspect Product review General feedback
Main purpose Describe an experience with a product Understand a broader experience or identify improvements
Typical destination Product page or review platform Survey, form, or support channel
Potential audience Other prospective customers and the business Usually the business or its internal teams
Example question How was your experience with this product? What could we improve about your experience?

Choose the right request for the information you need. A public review request should not be presented as a private survey if the response will be published publicly.

15. Measure the Results

The main performance measure should reflect the purpose of the campaign. For a review request, focus on completed reviews rather than relying solely on email opens or link clicks.

  • Delivery rate: How many messages were successfully delivered?
  • Click rate: How many recipients clicked the review link?
  • Review completion rate: How many eligible recipients completed a review?
  • Unsubscribe rate: How many recipients opted out after receiving the message?
  • Complaint rate: Did the campaign generate spam complaints or other warning signs?

If you can reliably connect review submissions to campaign recipients, calculate the completion rate using a clearly defined denominator. For example, completed reviews divided by successfully delivered requests can be useful when both counts refer to the same campaign and measurement period.

Do not assume every click becomes a review. Customers may open the destination, decide not to participate, or encounter a problem. Use the available data to identify where the process can be improved.

For more measurement guidance, see Article 0011 — Email Marketing Analytics.

16. Product Review Request Pre-Send Checklist

Check every item before activating the campaign:

  • ☐ The customer has had a reasonable opportunity to experience the product.
  • ☐ The subject line accurately describes the email.
  • ☐ The message explains why the customer is receiving it.
  • ☐ The request invites an honest opinion without demanding a positive rating.
  • ☐ The main review link opens the correct destination.
  • ☐ The email has a clear primary call to action.
  • ☐ Customers can contact support if they need assistance.
  • ☐ The workflow does not selectively suppress negative reviews.
  • ☐ Repeated requests are limited and handled appropriately.
  • ☐ Unsubscribe requests and applicable communication rules are respected.
  • ☐ Personalization fields and mobile formatting have been tested.
  • ☐ The campaign has a defined method for measuring completed reviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I send a product review request email?

Send it after the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to receive and experience the product. The appropriate delay depends on the product and the experience needed to evaluate it.

What should a product review request email include?

Include a relevant opening, a clear and respectful request, a working review link, and a support option where appropriate.

Should I ask customers for a five-star review?

No. Invite customers to share their honest experience instead of requesting a particular rating or attempting to control their opinion.

Can product review request emails be automated?

Yes. You can trigger requests using suitable purchase, delivery, or product-access events. Test the timing, links, and repeat-send safeguards before activating the workflow.

What if a customer has had a bad experience?

Provide a clear way to contact support, but do not use support as a reason to prevent or discourage honest public reviews. Customers should remain free to describe their experience.

How can I tell whether the campaign is successful?

Track completed reviews where possible, alongside delivery, clicks, unsubscribes, and complaints. Interpret each measure in context rather than judging success from opens alone.

Conclusion

A useful product review request email makes it easy for customers to describe their experience without pressure. Start with appropriate timing, write a clear subject line, ask directly for an honest review, and provide a working link.

If you automate the process, test its triggers and safeguards. Prevent unnecessary repeated requests, offer help when customers need it, and measure completed reviews rather than treating clicks as the final outcome.

The objective is to make the review process straightforward and respectful for customers while collecting feedback that can help people make informed decisions.
