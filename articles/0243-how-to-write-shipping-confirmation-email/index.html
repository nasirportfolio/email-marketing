How to Write a Shipping Confirmation Email That Customers Can Understand

When an online order leaves the seller, customers want to know what has shipped, how to follow its progress, and when it may arrive. A well-written shipping confirmation email answers these questions without forcing the customer to search through a long message.

Quick answer

Confirm that the order has shipped, display the order number and relevant items, provide a working tracking link, explain any available delivery estimate, and make customer support easy to reach. Keep the shipment information more prominent than optional promotional content.

1. What Is a Shipping Confirmation Email?

A shipping confirmation email is a transactional message sent when an order reaches the shipping status defined by the seller's fulfillment process. It tells the customer that the order has moved beyond the initial purchase stage.

An order confirmation usually acknowledges that the purchase was received. A shipping confirmation provides an update about dispatch and, when available, tracking and delivery information.

The exact trigger matters. Some systems mark an order as shipped when a shipping label is created, while others use a carrier handoff or fulfillment event. Configure the workflow so the email accurately describes the order's actual status.

2. Choose a Clear Subject Line

Customers should understand the purpose of the email before opening it. Use direct wording rather than vague phrases that could describe a newsletter or sales promotion.

  • Your order has shipped
  • Your order #[Order Number] is on the way
  • Shipping update for order #[Order Number]
  • Your package is ready to track
  • An update about your recent order

Use the order number in the subject when it helps customers distinguish between multiple purchases. Test the subject on mobile devices to ensure the important words appear early.

3. Put the Shipping Status First

Start with the main update. Avoid lengthy introductions, unrelated offers, or a brand story before telling the customer what happened.

Example opening

Good news! Your order #[Order Number] has shipped. You can use the tracking information below to check its progress.

Only use this wording when the order has genuinely reached the shipping status described. If the package has not been handed to the carrier, explain the actual status instead of implying that it is already in transit.

4. Include the Right Order Details

Give customers enough information to identify the shipment and check that it matches their purchase.

  • Order number: the identifier used by the store.
  • Items shipped: product names and quantities.
  • Variants: relevant sizes, colors, or product options.
  • Shipping method: the service selected, when available.
  • Carrier: the delivery company handling the package.
  • Delivery estimate: a date or window, if reliable data exists.

Do not expose unnecessary personal information. Display only the order and delivery details needed by the customer, and use the store's authenticated order page for information that requires additional protection.

5. Make the Tracking Link Easy to Find

Tracking is often the main reason a customer opens this message. Place the tracking number and primary tracking action near the order details instead of burying them at the bottom.

Tracking information example

Carrier: [Carrier Name]

Tracking number: [Tracking Number]

Primary action: Track your package

In your live email, link the words “Track your package” to the verified tracking URL generated for that shipment. The text above is a template, not a working tracking link.

Before sending, verify that:

  • The tracking URL belongs to the intended carrier or trusted order system.
  • The URL contains the correct shipment identifier.
  • The link works on desktop and mobile devices.
  • The link does not expose another customer's order information.
  • The tracking number is displayed accurately.

If tracking is not available yet, say so clearly. Do not create a fake tracking link or imply that a tracking number is already active.

6. Explain Delivery Estimates Honestly

Include a delivery estimate when your store or carrier can provide one. Label it as an estimate unless the service genuinely guarantees the date under its terms.

For example:

Estimated delivery: [Estimated Date]

This date is an estimate. Delivery timing may change as the carrier processes the shipment.

Weather, holidays, operational delays, incorrect addresses, and other circumstances can affect delivery. Avoid making promises that your business cannot reliably fulfill.

7. Explain Delayed Tracking Updates

A carrier's tracking system may not show movement immediately after dispatch. If this is normal for your shipping process, a brief explanation can reduce confusion.

Example message

Tracking information may take some time to appear or update after dispatch. Please check again later. If the information remains unavailable or looks incorrect, contact our support team.

Do not use this explanation to dismiss a genuine delivery problem. Provide a support route when tracking remains unavailable beyond the period your fulfillment process considers normal.

8. Handle Split Shipments and Partial Orders

Some orders are dispatched in more than one package. Products may ship from different warehouses, become available at different times, or require separate carriers.

When an order is split into multiple shipments:

  • Identify which items are included in the current package.
  • Provide the correct tracking details for each shipment.
  • Explain that remaining items will arrive separately, if known.
  • Avoid implying that every item has shipped when only part of the order has.
  • Link to the order page when it provides a clearer consolidated view.

Example wording

Part of your order has shipped. This package contains [Shipped Items]. The remaining items will be sent separately. You can review the current status of your order using [Verified Order Status Link].

Use this template only when the order system confirms that the order is being shipped in multiple packages.

9. Include a Useful Customer Support Option

Customers need to know what to do if the tracking number is incorrect, the delivery estimate changes, or the package appears to be missing.

Depending on your business, offer one or more relevant options:

  • A working customer support email address.
  • A contact page or help center.
  • An authenticated order-status page.
  • Instructions for reporting a delivery problem.

Ask customers to include their order number when contacting support, but do not ask them to send passwords or other sensitive authentication details.

10. Use Personalization That Improves Clarity

Personalization is valuable when it helps the customer recognize the purchase or understand the next step. Useful fields include the customer's name, order number, shipped items, carrier, and delivery estimate.

Test every dynamic field. If a customer's name is unavailable, the email should still read naturally. If the tracking number is missing, the template should not display a broken button or an empty label.

11. Shipping Confirmation Email Template

The following hypothetical template can be adapted to a store's actual order system. Replace all bracketed fields and test the links before using it.

Subject: Your order #[Order Number] has shipped

Hello [Customer Name],

Your order #[Order Number] has shipped. Here are the details for your shipment.

Items in this shipment

  • [Product Name] — Quantity: [Quantity]

Carrier: [Carrier Name]

Tracking number: [Tracking Number]

Estimated delivery: [Estimated Date, if available]

Track your package

Tracking details may take some time to update. If you have questions about your shipment, contact [Support Contact] and include your order number.

Thank you for your order,
[Business Name]

The bracketed tracking URL is an instructional placeholder. Replace it with a valid, shipment-specific URL before sending. Never publish a placeholder as if it were a functional customer link.

12. Shipping Confirmation vs. Order Confirmation

Order confirmation Shipping confirmation
Confirms that the order was received. Reports that the order or shipment has reached the stated shipping stage.
Focuses on purchase details and order acknowledgement. Focuses on dispatched items, tracking, and delivery information.
May include a purchase summary. May include carrier information and shipment-specific tracking.
Answers: “Was my order received?” Answers: “What has shipped, and how can I track it?”

13. Automate the Email Carefully

Shipping confirmation messages are often triggered by an ecommerce platform, order-management system, or fulfillment integration. The correct setup depends on how your store records shipment events.

  1. Identify the exact event that means an order has shipped.
  2. Map the order number, items, carrier, and tracking URL to the email fields.
  3. Set appropriate behavior for split shipments and missing tracking data.
  4. Prevent duplicate messages for the same shipment event.
  5. Test the workflow with sample or test orders.
  6. Check that the email content reflects the latest shipment status.

Do not assume that creating a shipping label always means a package has entered the carrier's network. Use status wording that accurately reflects the event your system records.

14. Common Shipping Confirmation Mistakes

Hiding the tracking information

Make tracking easy to find because it is often the customer's main reason for opening the message.

Sending the message too early

Confirm the meaning of the shipping event in your system. Do not state that a package is in transit when it has only been prepared for dispatch.

Making unsupported delivery promises

Use accurate delivery estimates and explain that dates can change when circumstances affect the carrier.

Including too many promotions

Keep the order update primary. Optional promotional content should not obscure the tracking link, shipment details, or support information.

Using incorrect dynamic data

Incorrect order numbers, product names, quantities, and tracking URLs can undermine customer trust. Test these fields before enabling the workflow.

Ignoring mobile usability

Customers may read the email on a phone. Use readable text, a clear layout, descriptive links, and buttons that are easy to tap.

15. How to Test the Email Before Launch

Review the email itself and the data supplied by the order system. A visually attractive template is not enough if its tracking link or shipment information is wrong.

  • Does the subject clearly identify the shipping update?
  • Is the shipping status accurate?
  • Is the order number correct?
  • Do the item names and quantities match the shipment?
  • Does the tracking number belong to the correct package?
  • Does the tracking link open the intended destination?
  • Are delivery dates described accurately?
  • Are partial shipments handled correctly?
  • Does the support link or contact information work?
  • Do missing dynamic fields fail gracefully?
  • Does the message display correctly on mobile and desktop?
  • Are duplicate messages prevented where appropriate?

Use test orders and test recipients where possible. Verify the complete customer journey instead of checking only the email preview.

16. Measure Performance and Improve the Experience

Review whether customers receive and use the information provided. Depending on your platform, useful measures may include:

  • Successful email delivery and bounce rates.
  • Tracking-link clicks.
  • Support requests caused by unclear shipment information.
  • Incorrect or missing tracking data.
  • Duplicate notifications or incorrect shipment-status messages.

Interpret these measures in context. Many tracking clicks can mean that customers are using the feature as intended; the number alone does not prove that the email is effective or ineffective. Also remember that open tracking can be affected by privacy features and should not be treated as a perfect measure of customer attention.

Shipping Confirmation Email Checklist

  • The subject line clearly communicates the update.
  • The opening confirms the correct shipping status.
  • The order number and shipped items are accurate.
  • The tracking information is visible and functional.
  • Delivery estimates are realistic and clearly labeled.
  • Split shipments are explained when applicable.
  • Customers can easily contact support.
  • Promotional content does not obscure essential details.
  • Dynamic fields and links have been tested.
  • The email works on mobile and desktop.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a shipping confirmation email be sent?

Send it when the order reaches the shipping status defined by your fulfillment process. Make sure the wording accurately describes that status.

What should a shipping confirmation email include?

It should generally include the shipping status, order number, relevant shipped items, available tracking information, delivery estimates when available, and a clear support option.

Should I include the tracking number?

Yes, when a valid tracking number is available. Pair it with a verified tracking link when possible. If tracking is not yet available, explain that clearly instead of displaying an empty or broken link.

What if an order ships in multiple packages?

Identify which items are in each shipment and provide the corresponding tracking details. Explain that remaining items will arrive separately when that information is confirmed.

Should shipping confirmation emails contain promotions?

Optional secondary content may be appropriate for some businesses, but the transactional information should remain prominent. Do not let promotions distract from shipment details or support.

What should I do if tracking does not update?

Explain that carrier data may take time to appear when that applies to your shipping process. Provide a support option if the tracking information remains unavailable or appears incorrect.

Final Takeaway

A useful shipping confirmation email answers the customer's immediate questions: what has shipped, how to track it, when it may arrive, and where to get help. Start with an accurate status update, show the relevant order details, and make the tracking action easy to find.

Test the links and dynamic data, account for partial shipments, and avoid unsupported delivery promises. These practical steps make the email a more reliable part of the customer experience.
