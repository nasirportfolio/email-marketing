How to Write a Shipping Confirmation Email That Customers Can Understand

Quick Answer

A shipping confirmation email should quickly tell the customer that the order has shipped, what was shipped, where it is going, how to track it, and what to do if something looks wrong. Keep the message focused on useful information rather than turning a transactional update into a promotional email.

What Is a Shipping Confirmation Email?

A shipping confirmation email is sent after an order has been handed over to a carrier or otherwise marked as shipped. Its main purpose is to keep the customer informed about the next stage of the order.

Unlike a promotional email, the customer is usually expecting this message. They want to know whether the package has left the seller, how they can track it, and when they might receive it.

That makes clarity more important than clever copy. A good shipping confirmation email helps the customer find the information they need without searching through the entire message.

What Should a Shipping Confirmation Email Accomplish?

Before writing the email, decide what information the customer needs at this particular stage of the order.

A useful shipping confirmation normally helps the customer:

  • Understand that the order has shipped.
  • Identify the order connected to the message.
  • See the items included in the shipment.
  • Find the tracking information.
  • Understand the expected delivery information.
  • Know where to get help if there is a problem.

These goals should determine the structure of the email. Extra promotional content should not make the important shipping information harder to find.

Write a Clear Shipping Confirmation Subject Line

The subject line should immediately communicate why the customer is receiving the email.

Examples include:

  • Your order has shipped
  • Your order is on the way
  • Your shipment is on its way
  • Shipping update for your order
  • Your order #[Order Number] has shipped

A customer should not have to open the email to discover whether it is an order update, a promotion, or a newsletter.

Start With the Most Important Information

The opening should confirm the shipping status as directly as possible.

Example:

Good news — your order has shipped and is now on its way.

The exact wording can vary according to the brand voice, but the customer should understand the main update immediately.

Include the Order Number

The order number gives the customer a simple way to identify the transaction associated with the email.

This is particularly useful when a customer has placed multiple orders or when several people use the same household email address.

If the platform supports it, make the order number easy to locate near the top of the message.

Show What Has Been Shipped

Customers should be able to confirm what the shipment contains. Depending on the store and email system, this can include:

  • Product name
  • Quantity
  • Variant such as size or color
  • Product image when useful
  • Order number

You do not necessarily need to reproduce every detail from the original order confirmation. Include enough information for the customer to recognize the shipment.

Make the Tracking Information Easy to Find

Tracking information is often one of the most important parts of the email. Customers should not have to scroll through several paragraphs to find it.

A clear tracking button can be useful when the destination is a legitimate tracking page.

Example:

Tracking number: [TRACKING NUMBER]

Track your package

Use descriptive link text rather than unexplained URLs. Also make sure the tracking destination actually works before sending the message.

Explain the Expected Delivery Information

If reliable delivery information is available, include it in a noticeable place.

For example:

  • Estimated delivery date
  • Expected delivery window
  • Carrier name
  • Shipping method

Avoid presenting an estimate as a guarantee. Delivery dates can change because of carrier delays, weather, holidays, address problems, or other circumstances outside the store's direct control.

Tell Customers What to Do If Tracking Does Not Update

Tracking information does not always update immediately. A customer may click the tracking link and see that the carrier has not yet recorded a new scan.

A short explanation can prevent unnecessary confusion.

Example:

Tracking information may take some time to appear after the package leaves our facility. If you do not see an update immediately, please check again later.

Keep this explanation short. The purpose is to set a reasonable expectation, not to overwhelm the customer with possible shipping scenarios.

Include a Simple Support Option

A shipping confirmation should make it clear how customers can get help if something is wrong.

Depending on the business, the support option could point to:

  • A customer support email address
  • A contact page
  • A help center
  • An order support page

For example:

Need help with your shipment? Contact our support team and include your order number so we can help you more quickly.

Use One Primary Call to Action

The most useful primary action in a shipping confirmation email is usually related to the shipment itself.

Depending on the customer journey, that could be:

  • Track your package
  • View order details
  • Get shipping help

A large collection of unrelated buttons can distract from the reason the customer received the email.

Do Not Turn the Shipping Confirmation Into a Sales Pitch

A customer who is looking for tracking information has a different immediate need from someone reading a promotional campaign.

If you include promotional content, keep the essential shipping information prominent and easy to access.

The message should still function as a useful order update even if the customer ignores every promotional element.

Personalize the Email With Useful Information

Personalization is most useful when it makes the message easier to understand.

Useful details can include:

  • The customer's name
  • Order number
  • Products purchased
  • Shipping method
  • Relevant delivery information

Personalization should not create unnecessary complexity. The goal is to make the customer feel that the message is connected to their actual order.

If you want to explore broader personalization techniques, see email personalization .

Use the Customer's Order Context

Not every customer needs the same shipping message.

For example, an order containing several products may need slightly different information from a simple one-product order. Similarly, customers using different shipping methods may receive different delivery information.

Your email system can use available order data to make the message relevant while keeping the basic structure consistent.

Shipping Confirmation Email Structure

A simple structure can make these emails easier to build and maintain.

  1. Clear shipping confirmation
  2. Order number
  3. Items shipped
  4. Tracking number or tracking button
  5. Expected delivery information
  6. Short shipping guidance
  7. Support option
  8. Optional secondary content

This structure follows the customer's immediate information needs rather than forcing the customer to read a long introduction first.

Shipping Confirmation Email Example

The following is a hypothetical example that demonstrates the structure without presenting a fabricated customer result or testimonial.

Subject: Your order has shipped

Hello [Customer Name],

Good news — your order #[Order Number] has shipped and is now on its way.

Your order
[Product Name]
Quantity: [Quantity]

Shipping method: [Shipping Method]

Estimated delivery: [Estimated Date]

Tracking number: [Tracking Number]

Track your package

Tracking information may take some time to update after the package enters the carrier's system.

If you need help with your shipment, please contact [Support Information] and include your order number.

Thank you,
[Business Name]

Shipping Confirmation vs. Order Confirmation

These two emails communicate different stages of the same purchase.

Order Confirmation Shipping Confirmation
Confirms that the order was received. Confirms that the order has shipped.
Usually focuses on the purchase details. Focuses on shipment and delivery information.
May include payment and order information. Usually includes carrier and tracking information.
Answers: “Did you receive my order?” Answers: “Has my order been shipped, and where is it?”

Keeping these purposes separate can make each message more useful and easier for customers to understand.

How Automation Fits Into Shipping Confirmation Emails

Shipping confirmation emails are commonly connected to an order or fulfillment event. The email should be triggered by the appropriate status in the ecommerce or order-management system.

The exact setup depends on the platform, store configuration, and email system.

For a broader introduction to automated customer communication, see email marketing automation .

The important principle is to make sure the email reflects the actual order state. A shipping confirmation should not be sent before the order has genuinely reached the relevant shipping stage.

Common Shipping Confirmation Email Mistakes

1. Hiding the Tracking Link

Customers often open the message specifically to find tracking information. Make the tracking option easy to locate.

2. Using Vague Subject Lines

A subject such as “An update for you” does not immediately tell the customer what changed.

3. Overloading the Email With Promotions

Too much promotional content can distract from the shipment details.

4. Making Unsupported Delivery Promises

If the delivery date is only an estimate, describe it as an estimate. Avoid creating expectations that the business or carrier cannot guarantee.

5. Forgetting the Support Path

Customers need a clear next step if their tracking information is incorrect or their package appears delayed.

6. Sending Incorrect Order Data

Dynamic fields should be tested carefully. Incorrect order numbers, tracking numbers, names, or product details can create confusion and reduce customer trust.

How to Test a Shipping Confirmation Email

Before using the email in a live workflow, test both the message and the underlying data.

  • Check the subject line.
  • Confirm the order number displays correctly.
  • Check product names and quantities.
  • Test the tracking link.
  • Confirm the carrier information is correct.
  • Check estimated delivery wording.
  • Test the support link or contact information.
  • Review the message on a mobile device.
  • Check that dynamic fields do not appear blank.

A test order can help identify problems that are difficult to notice when reviewing only the template.

How to Improve the Email After Launch

Once the email is active, review whether customers can successfully use the information it provides.

Depending on the email platform and business setup, useful measures may include:

  • Email delivery
  • Open activity
  • Tracking-link clicks
  • Support requests related to shipping
  • Customer questions caused by unclear information

These measurements should be interpreted in context. For example, a high number of tracking clicks is not automatically a problem; it may simply mean customers are using the tracking feature as intended.

For a broader understanding of email measurement, see email marketing analytics .

Shipping Confirmation Email Checklist

  • Does the subject line clearly indicate that the order has shipped?
  • Is the order number easy to find?
  • Are the shipped products identified clearly?
  • Is the tracking information visible?
  • Does the tracking link work?
  • Is delivery information presented accurately?
  • Are estimates clearly described as estimates?
  • Can the customer easily contact support?
  • Are unnecessary promotional elements limited?
  • Have dynamic order fields been tested?
  • Does the email work well on mobile devices?
  • Can the customer understand the next step quickly?

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a shipping confirmation email be sent?

It should be sent when the order reaches the shipping or fulfillment stage that your business uses to indicate that the package has been shipped. The exact trigger depends on your ecommerce and fulfillment system.

What should a shipping confirmation email contain?

It should generally contain the shipping status, order number, relevant product information, tracking details, available delivery information, and a clear way to get support.

Should a shipping confirmation email include promotions?

It can include secondary content when appropriate, but the shipping information should remain the primary focus. Promotional content should not make tracking or order information difficult to find.

Should I include the customer's name?

A customer's name can make the message more personal, but useful order information is usually more important. Personalization should support clarity rather than add unnecessary complexity.

What if the tracking number does not work yet?

Explain that carrier tracking information may take some time to appear or update, when that is applicable to your shipping process. Give the customer a clear support option if the information remains unavailable or appears incorrect.

Final Takeaway

A shipping confirmation email does not need complicated copy. Its job is to give customers the information they need after an order has shipped.

Start with a clear shipping confirmation, identify the order, show what was shipped, make tracking easy to find, provide useful delivery information, and give customers a simple support path.

When the message is organized around the customer's immediate question — “Where is my order?” — the email becomes a practical part of the customer experience rather than just another automated message.
