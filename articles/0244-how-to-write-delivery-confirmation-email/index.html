How to Write a Delivery Confirmation Email After an Order Arrives

Quick Answer

A delivery confirmation email tells the customer that their order has been marked as delivered and gives them useful next steps. A clear message should identify the order, confirm the delivery status, provide relevant delivery details, explain what to do if the package cannot be found, and make customer support easy to reach.

What Is a Delivery Confirmation Email?

A delivery confirmation email is a customer communication sent after an order has been reported as delivered. It marks another stage in the customer's post-purchase journey: the package has moved from shipping to delivery.

The customer's needs at this point are different from those in a shipping confirmation email. Instead of primarily asking where the package is, the customer may want to know whether the order was successfully delivered and what to do if they cannot locate it.

The email can therefore serve as a simple bridge between delivery and the customer's next interaction with the business.

When Should You Send a Delivery Confirmation Email?

The message should normally be triggered when the order reaches the delivery status used by the business or its fulfillment system.

The exact timing depends on the ecommerce platform, carrier information, fulfillment process, and automation setup.

The important principle is accuracy. A delivery confirmation should reflect the actual status available to the business rather than being sent based on an assumption about when the package should arrive.

What Should a Delivery Confirmation Email Accomplish?

Before writing the message, identify the customer's most important questions after delivery.

A useful delivery confirmation should help answer:

  • Was my order delivered?
  • Which order was delivered?
  • When was it marked as delivered?
  • Where should I look if delivery details are available?
  • What should I do if I cannot find the package?
  • How can I contact the business if there is a problem?

Keeping these questions in mind prevents the email from becoming a generic promotional message.

Write a Clear Subject Line

The subject line should tell the customer what happened without making them open the email simply to discover its purpose.

Possible subject lines include:

  • Your order has been delivered
  • Your order has arrived
  • Your package was delivered
  • Delivery confirmation for order #[Order Number]
  • Your order #[Order Number] has arrived

Choose wording that accurately reflects the delivery information available to your business.

Start With the Delivery Confirmation

Put the main update near the beginning of the email.

Example:

Your order has been marked as delivered. We hope everything arrived safely.

The opening does not need to be complicated. The customer should understand the purpose of the message within a few seconds.

Include the Order Number

The order number connects the delivery notification to a specific purchase. This is especially helpful for customers who have placed multiple orders.

Place the order number somewhere easy to identify rather than hiding it in a long paragraph.

Order: #[Order Number]

Include Relevant Delivery Details

The amount of delivery information you provide should depend on what your fulfillment system makes available.

Useful details may include:

  • Delivery date
  • Order number
  • Products included in the order
  • Carrier name
  • Delivery status
  • Available delivery location information
  • Tracking or shipment reference

Do not add details that your system cannot reliably provide. Accuracy is more useful than a long list of fields.

Tell Customers What to Do If They Cannot Find the Package

A delivery status does not necessarily mean the customer has physically located the package. A customer may see a delivered status and still be unable to find the order.

A short guidance section can tell them what to check before contacting support.

Example:

If you cannot find your package, please check the delivery area around your address and with other members of your household or building. If you still cannot locate it, contact our support team with your order number.

Adjust this guidance to match the actual delivery process used by the business. Do not promise that a particular delivery location or procedure was used unless the available delivery information supports it.

Make Customer Support Easy to Find

A delivery confirmation is also an opportunity to give customers a clear path to help.

Include an appropriate support method, such as:

  • A customer support email address
  • A contact page
  • A help center
  • An order-support page

Ask customers to include their order number when contacting support if that information helps the business locate the order more efficiently.

Need help with your delivery? Contact our support team and include your order number so we can look into the issue.

Decide Whether the Customer Needs Another Action

Not every delivery confirmation needs a complicated call to action.

The appropriate action depends on what the customer needs at this stage. Possible actions include:

  • View order details
  • Get delivery help
  • Contact support
  • View the customer's account

If there is no important action for the customer to take, the message can remain informational.

Keep Promotional Content Secondary

A delivery confirmation can be part of a larger customer relationship, but its primary purpose is to communicate delivery information.

If you include product recommendations, discounts, or other promotional content, make sure customers can still find the delivery information and support instructions easily.

A useful transactional message should not require the customer to navigate through several promotional sections before finding the information they need.

Use Personalization Carefully

Personalization can make the message more relevant when it is based on reliable order information.

Useful personalization may include:

  • Customer name
  • Order number
  • Products purchased
  • Delivery date
  • Relevant account or order information

The purpose of personalization is not simply to insert more customer data. It should make the delivery confirmation easier to understand.

For broader personalization techniques, see Email Personalization .

Structure the Email Around the Customer's Immediate Need

A practical delivery confirmation can follow a simple structure:

  1. Clear delivery confirmation
  2. Customer name or greeting
  3. Order number
  4. Relevant delivery details
  5. What to do if the package cannot be found
  6. Customer support information
  7. Optional secondary content

This structure keeps the most important information close to the beginning of the message.

Delivery Confirmation Email Example

The following is a hypothetical example showing how the different elements can work together. It does not represent a real customer, business, or delivery result.

Subject: Your order has been delivered

Hello [Customer Name],

Your order #[Order Number] has been marked as delivered.

Your order
[Product Name]
Quantity: [Quantity]

Delivery date: [Delivery Date]

If you cannot find your package, please check the usual delivery area around your address and with other members of your household or building.

If you still cannot locate your order, please contact [Support Information] and include your order number.

Thank you,
[Business Name]

Delivery Confirmation vs. Shipping Confirmation

These messages belong to different stages of the fulfillment process.

Shipping Confirmation Delivery Confirmation
Communicates that the order has shipped. Communicates that the order has been marked as delivered.
Usually emphasizes tracking and shipment progress. Usually emphasizes delivery status and what happens after delivery.
Answers: “Has my order left the seller?” Answers: “Has my order been delivered?”
Often directs the customer to shipment tracking. Can provide guidance if the delivered package cannot be found.

For the shipping stage itself, see How to Write a Shipping Confirmation Email That Customers Can Understand .

How Delivery Confirmation Fits Into the Customer Journey

Delivery is one stage in the broader customer journey. The communication before and after delivery can influence how easily customers move through the experience.

For example, a simple sequence might include:

  1. Order confirmation
  2. Shipping confirmation
  3. Delivery confirmation
  4. Post-purchase follow-up
  5. Review request or another appropriate customer communication

These messages should have different purposes rather than repeating the same information.

You can learn more about the broader customer journey in Email Marketing and the Customer Journey .

How Automation Can Trigger Delivery Confirmation

Delivery confirmation emails can be connected to the delivery status provided by an ecommerce, fulfillment, or email system.

The automation should use the appropriate event or status from the system. The exact implementation depends on the platform and the tools being used.

A reliable automation should also avoid sending a delivery message when the relevant delivery event has not actually occurred.

Common Delivery Confirmation Email Mistakes

1. Making the Delivery Status Unclear

The customer should not have to interpret vague language to understand whether the order was delivered.

2. Providing Unsupported Delivery Details

Only include delivery information that your system can reliably provide.

3. Forgetting Customers Who Cannot Find the Package

A short support instruction can make the message more useful when the delivery status and the customer's physical experience do not match.

4. Hiding Support Information

Customers should have a clear way to ask for help when something goes wrong.

5. Overloading the Message With Promotions

Promotional content should not distract from the delivery confirmation or make support information difficult to find.

6. Sending the Email at the Wrong Stage

The automation should be connected to the appropriate delivery event rather than an estimated delivery date alone.

7. Using Incorrect Dynamic Data

Test customer names, order numbers, product details, and delivery fields before activating the workflow.

How to Test a Delivery Confirmation Email

Before sending the email through an automated workflow, test both its copy and its dynamic information.

  • Check the subject line.
  • Confirm that the delivery status is clear.
  • Verify the order number.
  • Check product names and quantities.
  • Verify delivery-date information.
  • Test support links and contact information.
  • Check all dynamic fields.
  • Review the email on mobile devices.
  • Check that optional promotional content does not dominate the message.

A test order or test event can help identify problems before customers receive the message.

What to Measure After Launch

Delivery confirmation emails can be evaluated using measurements that reflect their purpose and the capabilities of the email platform.

Depending on your setup, you may review:

  • Email delivery
  • Open activity
  • Clicks on relevant links
  • Support requests related to delivery
  • Customer questions about missing packages

These measurements need context. For example, a customer contacting support is not automatically evidence that the email failed. The customer may be contacting support because a genuine delivery problem occurred.

For broader email measurement concepts, see Email Marketing Analytics .

Delivery Confirmation Email Checklist

  • Does the subject line clearly communicate delivery?
  • Is the delivery status easy to understand?
  • Is the order number visible?
  • Are the relevant products identified?
  • Is the delivery date accurate?
  • Are delivery details supported by reliable data?
  • Does the email explain what to do if the package cannot be found?
  • Is customer support easy to locate?
  • Are unnecessary promotions kept secondary?
  • Have all dynamic fields been tested?
  • Does the email work well on mobile devices?
  • Does the automation trigger at the correct delivery stage?

When Should You Follow Up After Delivery?

The next communication should depend on the customer's needs and the purpose of the next message.

A delivery confirmation does not need to perform the job of every post-purchase email. For example, a separate message may be appropriate for customer feedback or a product review request.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of a delivery confirmation email?

Its primary purpose is to tell the customer that the order has been marked as delivered and provide useful information about what to do next if there is a problem.

When should a delivery confirmation email be sent?

It should normally be triggered when the relevant delivery event is recorded by the business's ecommerce, fulfillment, carrier, or email system.

What should a delivery confirmation email include?

Useful elements include the delivery status, order number, relevant order details, delivery information, guidance for a missing package, and a clear support option.

Should a delivery confirmation email ask for a product review?

A review request can be part of the broader post-delivery communication journey, but it does not have to be combined with the delivery confirmation. Keeping the purposes separate can make each message easier to understand.

Should a delivery confirmation email include a discount?

A business may choose to include secondary promotional content, but the delivery information and customer support path should remain easy to find. Promotional content should not interfere with the primary purpose of the message.

What should I do if a customer says the package was not received?

Provide a clear support path and ask for the relevant order information. The appropriate investigation depends on the carrier, fulfillment process, delivery information, and business policies.

Final Takeaway

A delivery confirmation email has a simple job: tell the customer that the order has been marked as delivered and help them understand what to do next.

Start with a clear delivery status, identify the order, provide accurate delivery information, explain what customers can do if they cannot find the package, and make support easy to reach.

Keep the message focused on the customer's immediate need. When delivery confirmation is treated as its own stage of the customer journey, it becomes a useful communication rather than another generic automated email.
