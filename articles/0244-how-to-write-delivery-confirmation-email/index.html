How to Write a Delivery Confirmation Email After an Order Arrives

A delivery confirmation email tells a customer that an order has been marked as delivered. It also explains what the customer can do if the package cannot be found or the delivery status does not match their experience.

Quick answer

Write a clear delivery update, identify the order, include relevant delivery details, explain how to report a missing package, and provide a reliable support option. Base the message on available delivery information and keep optional promotions secondary.

1. What Is a Delivery Confirmation Email?

A delivery confirmation email is a transactional message sent after the order-management or fulfillment system records the relevant delivery event. It informs the customer that the package has reached the delivery stage according to the information available to the business.

A shipping confirmation and a delivery confirmation have different purposes. Shipping confirmation communicates dispatch or shipment status. Delivery confirmation communicates that the order has been marked as delivered.

These messages should not be treated as interchangeable. A package marked as delivered may still be difficult for a customer to locate, so a useful delivery message should explain how to get assistance if necessary.

2. When Should You Send a Delivery Confirmation Email?

Send the email when your ecommerce, carrier, or fulfillment system records the appropriate delivery event. The exact trigger depends on the integration and the status information available to your business.

Do not trigger a delivery confirmation simply because an estimated delivery date has arrived. An estimate is not proof that delivery occurred.

Before enabling the workflow, check that:

  • The trigger represents a delivery status rather than dispatch.
  • The order or shipment can be identified correctly.
  • The system can handle delayed or missing status updates.
  • Duplicate delivery notifications are avoided where appropriate.

3. Write a Clear Subject Line

The subject line should tell the customer why the email arrived. Use straightforward language and avoid vague wording that could be mistaken for a promotional campaign.

  • Your order has been delivered
  • Your package was marked as delivered
  • Delivery confirmation for order #[Order Number]
  • Your order #[Order Number] has arrived
  • An update about your delivered order

Choose wording that accurately reflects your available delivery information. If the carrier reports a delivery status but the business cannot independently confirm receipt by the customer, avoid implying more certainty than the information supports.

4. Start With the Main Delivery Update

Place the important information at the beginning of the email. Customers should not need to read a long introduction to understand the message.

Example opening

Your order #[Order Number] has been marked as delivered. We hope everything arrived safely. If you have questions about your delivery, the information below can help.

Use “marked as delivered” when that is what the carrier or fulfillment system actually reports. Adapt the wording if your business has more specific, verified delivery information.

5. Identify the Order

Include the order number so customers can distinguish this delivery from previous purchases. This is especially useful when customers have several open or recently completed orders.

Depending on the order and system, useful details include:

  • Order number
  • Relevant product names and quantities
  • Recorded delivery date, when available
  • Carrier or shipment reference, when useful
  • Instructions for accessing the customer's secure order page, if available

Include only information that is relevant and reliably available. Avoid exposing unnecessary personal or sensitive information in the email.

6. Provide Accurate Delivery Details

Delivery details should help the customer understand what the system recorded without suggesting that you know more than you do.

Example delivery summary

Order: #[Order Number]

Status: [Verified Delivery Status]

Delivery date: [Recorded Date, if available]

Items: [Items Associated With This Delivery]

Do not invent a delivery location, recipient name, or delivery time. Display those details only when the underlying system provides them and the business is permitted to share them.

7. Help Customers Who Cannot Find the Package

A carrier's delivered status and a customer's experience do not always match. The package may have been left in an unexpected location, received by another person, or incorrectly marked as delivered.

A helpful email can suggest reasonable initial checks:

  1. Check the usual delivery area and any location specified by the carrier.
  2. Ask other household members or building staff whether they accepted the package.
  3. Review any delivery instructions or verified carrier information.
  4. Contact the business if the package still cannot be located.

Example missing-package guidance

If you cannot find your package, please check the usual delivery area and any delivery information available to you. You may also want to check with other members of your household or building. If the package is still missing, contact our support team with your order number so we can help investigate.

Adjust these instructions to the carrier, location, and business process. Do not tell customers that a package was left with a neighbor or in a particular place unless reliable delivery information supports that claim.

8. Make Customer Support Easy to Reach

Give customers a clear next step if the delivery status appears incorrect, the package is missing, or an item is damaged.

Possible support options include:

  • A working customer support email address
  • A contact page or help center
  • An order-support page
  • A secure account page where the customer can view the order

Use a verified support destination if you add a link to a real email template. Do not publish a placeholder URL or an invented support address.

Example support message

Need help with your delivery? Contact the business through its official customer support channel and include your order number. The support team can review the available order and delivery information.

9. Handle Partial and Multiple Deliveries

Some orders arrive in separate packages. Products may ship from different warehouses, use different carriers, or arrive on different days.

Make sure the message refers only to the items covered by the delivery event. If one package has arrived but the rest of the order is still in transit, do not suggest that the entire order has been delivered.

Example for a partial delivery

One shipment from your order has been marked as delivered. This shipment contains [Delivered Items]. If other items remain outstanding, check the order-status information provided by the retailer or contact its support team for an update.

Use this wording only when your order system can distinguish the delivered shipment from the remaining items. If a secure order-status page is available, the business can provide its verified address in the actual customer email.

10. Personalize the Email Carefully

Personalization should help customers recognize their order or understand the delivery update. Useful fields may include the customer's name, order number, relevant products, and recorded delivery date.

Test the template when optional fields are unavailable. For example, the message should remain readable if the customer's name is missing or the delivery date has not been supplied.

Do not add unnecessary personal information merely to make the message look customized. Relevance, accuracy, and privacy matter more than the number of personalized fields.

11. Use a Simple Email Structure

A practical delivery confirmation email can follow this sequence:

  1. A clear delivery status
  2. Order number and relevant items
  3. Available delivery details
  4. Instructions for a package that cannot be found
  5. Customer support information
  6. Optional secondary content, if appropriate

This order puts the customer's immediate questions ahead of promotional material. It also gives the business a repeatable structure that is easier to test and maintain.

12. Complete Delivery Confirmation Email Template

The following is a hypothetical template. Replace the bracketed fields with accurate information from your order system before using it.

Subject: Your order #[Order Number] has been delivered

Hello [Customer Name],

Your order #[Order Number] has been marked as delivered. We hope everything arrived safely.

Order details

  • Items: [Delivered Items]
  • Delivery date: [Recorded Date, if available]
  • Shipment reference: [Reference, if useful]

If you cannot find your package, please check the usual delivery area and review any delivery information available to you. If you still cannot locate it, contact the business through its official support channel and include your order number.

Thank you for your order,
[Business Name]

13. Delivery Confirmation vs. Shipping Confirmation

Shipping confirmation Delivery confirmation
Reports that an order or package has reached the shipping stage. Reports that the order or package has been marked as delivered.
Usually emphasizes tracking and shipment progress. Emphasizes delivery status and what to do after delivery.
Answers: “Has my order shipped?” Answers: “Has my order been delivered?”
May provide a carrier tracking link. May provide missing-package guidance and support information.

14. Connect Delivery Confirmation to the Customer Journey

Delivery confirmation is one part of the post-purchase communication journey. Other messages may have different purposes and should be planned accordingly.

  1. Order confirmation: acknowledges the purchase.
  2. Shipping confirmation: communicates the shipping status.
  3. Delivery confirmation: communicates the recorded delivery status.
  4. Post-purchase follow-up: provides appropriate next steps or assistance.
  5. Review request: invites feedback under the business's review policy.

Avoid sending several messages that repeat the same information without a clear purpose. Consider the timing and relevance of each communication.

15. Automate Delivery Confirmation Responsibly

Delivery confirmation emails can be triggered by an ecommerce platform, fulfillment system, carrier integration, or other order-management tool. The exact configuration depends on the available events and system capabilities.

Before activating the workflow:

  1. Choose the correct delivery-status event.
  2. Map the order number and relevant delivery fields.
  3. Handle partial deliveries separately when the system supports them.
  4. Prevent unnecessary duplicate notifications.
  5. Test missing or delayed status data.
  6. Confirm that the email matches the actual recorded status.

Do not use an estimated arrival date as the only evidence that delivery occurred. If the system cannot verify a delivery event, choose wording that accurately describes what is known.

16. Common Delivery Confirmation Mistakes

Claiming delivery without reliable status data

Base the notification on the actual event or status available to the business. Do not infer delivery solely from an expected arrival date.

Ignoring missing-package questions

A short explanation and a clear support option can help customers know what to do if they cannot find the package.

Reporting an entire order as delivered when only part arrived

Identify the items or shipment covered by the event and explain when other items remain outstanding.

Hiding customer support information

Customers should be able to find a working support option without searching through unrelated content.

Overloading the email with promotions

Keep the delivery information prominent and optional marketing content secondary.

Failing to test dynamic fields

Check order numbers, product details, delivery dates, and links before activating the automation.

17. Test the Email Before Launch

Review both the email layout and the information supplied by the underlying order system.

  • Does the subject clearly identify the delivery update?
  • Does the message describe the actual recorded status?
  • Is the order number correct?
  • Are the delivered items identified accurately?
  • Are delivery dates based on available information?
  • Are partial deliveries handled correctly?
  • Does each real support link open the intended page?
  • Are missing fields handled gracefully?
  • Is the email readable on mobile devices?
  • Does the automation avoid unnecessary duplicate messages?

Test with sample orders or test events whenever possible. Previewing a template alone may not reveal problems in live order data or triggers.

18. Measure Performance After Launch

Evaluate the email using measures that relate to its purpose and are available in your platform.

  • Successful email delivery and bounce rates
  • Clicks on relevant order or support links
  • Support requests related to delivery
  • Reports of missing packages
  • Incorrect or duplicate notifications

Interpret these measures in context. A support request does not automatically mean that the email failed; the customer may have experienced a genuine delivery problem. Open tracking is also imperfect because privacy features can affect reported activity.

Delivery Confirmation Email Checklist

  • The subject line is clear and accurate.
  • The opening explains the delivery status.
  • The order number is easy to identify.
  • The delivered items are described correctly.
  • Delivery details are supported by available data.
  • Customers receive sensible missing-package guidance.
  • Customer support is easy to contact.
  • Partial deliveries are handled accurately.
  • Promotional content does not obscure essential information.
  • Dynamic fields and real links have been tested.
  • The email works on mobile and desktop.
  • The automation uses the appropriate delivery event.

19. What Should Happen After Delivery?

A delivery confirmation does not need to perform every post-purchase communication task. Depending on the product and customer relationship, the next message might offer assistance, provide product-use information, or request feedback at an appropriate time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of a delivery confirmation email?

Its main purpose is to communicate that an order has been marked as delivered and provide relevant information or support guidance for the customer.

When should a delivery confirmation email be sent?

It should normally be triggered when the appropriate delivery event is recorded by the business's order, fulfillment, or carrier system.

What should a delivery confirmation email include?

Useful details include the delivery status, order number, relevant items, available delivery information, instructions for a missing package, and a clear support option.

What if the customer cannot find the package?

Provide a clear support route and suggest reasonable initial checks, such as reviewing available delivery details and checking with household members or building staff. The appropriate investigation depends on the carrier and business process.

Should I request a product review in the delivery email?

A review request can be sent separately as part of the post-purchase journey. Separating the messages can help keep the delivery notification focused on its primary purpose.

Should the email include a discount?

A business may choose to include secondary promotional content where appropriate, but the delivery update and customer support information should remain easy to find.

Final Takeaway

A delivery confirmation email should explain what the business's system has recorded, identify the relevant order, and help customers understand what to do if something is wrong.

Use accurate status information, account for partial shipments, provide clear support instructions, and test every dynamic field. Keep the message focused on the customer's immediate needs rather than turning it into a generic sales email.
