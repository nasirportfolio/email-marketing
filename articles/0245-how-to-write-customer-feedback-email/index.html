How to Write a Customer Feedback Email After Delivery

Quick answer

A customer feedback email after delivery should make it easy for a customer to share what went well, what was unclear, and what could be improved. Ask a focused question, explain why the feedback matters, provide a simple response method, and avoid turning the message into a sales pitch.

1. What Is a Customer Feedback Email After Delivery?

A customer feedback email after delivery asks a customer to share their experience after receiving an order. It can help a business understand ordering, communication, delivery, packaging, product expectations, or support.

Choose questions based on what you actually want to learn. Avoid asking customers to complete a long list of unrelated questions simply because the information might be interesting.

2. When Should You Send a Customer Feedback Email?

Give the customer enough time to experience the delivery or product while the interaction is still reasonably fresh. There is no single timing that works for every business.

Consider these factors:

  • How quickly the customer can use the product.
  • Whether setup or learning is required.
  • Which part of the customer experience you want evaluated.
  • Whether another post-purchase email is already scheduled.

The goal is to ask when the customer has enough context to provide meaningful feedback, rather than sending the request before the experience can be assessed.

3. Choose a Clear Purpose

A feedback email might help you understand:

  • Whether the ordering process was clear.
  • Whether delivery communication was useful.
  • Whether the product matched expectations.
  • Whether anything caused confusion.
  • What part of the experience could be improved.
  • Whether the customer needs assistance with the order.

A focused objective produces a more useful question than a general request such as “Tell us what you think.”

4. Write a Clear Subject Line

The subject line should make the purpose of the email understandable.

  • How was your experience?
  • We'd like your feedback
  • How did everything go with your order?
  • Tell us about your experience
  • One quick question about your order

Choose wording that matches the actual question inside the email.

5. Start With a Simple Opening

Provide enough context for the customer to understand why you are asking for feedback. A long introduction is rarely necessary.

Example opening

We hope your order arrived safely. We'd like to learn how your experience went so we can understand what worked well and where we can improve.

6. Ask a Specific Feedback Question

Specific questions are generally easier to answer than broad requests. Instead of asking “What do you think about everything?”, try a question related to the experience you want to understand.

Example questions

  • What was the most helpful part of your experience with your order?
  • Was anything about the ordering or delivery process unclear?
  • Was there anything we could have handled better?

The best question depends on the information the business actually needs.

7. Explain Why the Feedback Matters

Customers may be more willing to respond when they understand why their feedback is being requested. Keep the explanation honest and specific.

Your feedback helps us identify parts of the customer experience that may need improvement.

Avoid promising that every response will produce a particular change unless the business can genuinely make that commitment.

8. Make Feedback Easy to Give

If the business needs one piece of information, ask one focused question rather than requiring a long form.

Possible response methods include:

  • A single-question response.
  • A short survey.
  • A rating followed by an optional comment.
  • A simple feedback form.
  • A direct reply to the email.

Choose the simplest method that collects the information you need.

9. Choose the Right Feedback Method

Feedback goalPossible method
Measure a simple satisfaction levelRating or short scale
Understand what went wrongShort open-ended question
Collect feedback on several areasShort survey
Start a personal conversationEmail reply
Collect structured responsesFeedback form

The method should support the question instead of making customers complete a more complicated process than necessary.

10. Use Personalization Carefully

Personalization can make a feedback request more relevant when the customer and order data are accurate.

  • Customer name, when available.
  • Order number.
  • Relevant product name.
  • Delivery date, when recorded.
  • The relevant customer journey stage.

Avoid inserting unnecessary personal information simply because the email system allows it. Test the template when optional fields are missing.

For broader techniques, see Email Personalization .

11. Allow Customers to Report Problems

A feedback request should not assume that every customer had a positive experience. Give customers an appropriate way to describe problems or confusion.

Example question

Was there anything about your experience that we could have handled better?

If a customer reports a problem, have a clear process for reviewing the feedback and responding appropriately.

12. Keep the Feedback Request Focused

A feedback email has a different purpose from a promotional campaign. Placing a large sales message beside the feedback request can distract customers from the question.

If promotional content is appropriate, keep it secondary and make sure customers can complete the feedback request without navigating through unrelated offers.

13. Build a Simple Email Structure

A practical customer feedback email can follow this structure:

  1. Short greeting.
  2. Reference to the recent order or delivery.
  3. Reason for requesting feedback.
  4. One clear primary question.
  5. Simple response method.
  6. Optional additional question.
  7. Thank-you message.
  8. Support information when relevant.

14. Complete Customer Feedback Email Template

This is a hypothetical template. Replace bracketed fields and add a real, tested feedback destination before using it in a live email campaign.

Subject: How was your experience?

Hello [Customer Name],

We hope your order arrived safely.

We'd like to learn how your experience went. Your feedback can help us understand what worked well and where the customer experience could improve.

One quick question:

Was there anything about ordering, delivery, or receiving your product that we could have made easier?

To share your feedback, reply directly to this email or use the verified feedback form provided by the business.

Thank you for taking the time to share your experience.

Best regards,
[Business Name]

The example deliberately uses no fake feedback URL. If you use a form, insert its actual destination and test it before sending.

15. Feedback Email vs. Product Review Request

A feedback email and a product review request can appear similar, but their purposes are different.

Customer feedback email Product review request
Seeks information about the broader customer experience. Usually asks the customer to review a particular product.
Can cover ordering, delivery, support, or product experience. Usually focuses on the customer's opinion of a product.
May be private feedback used for improvement. May be intended for publication as a customer review.
Emphasizes learning and improvement. Emphasizes collecting product-specific reviews.

16. How Automation Can Trigger Feedback Emails

A feedback email can be connected to a post-delivery workflow. The exact trigger depends on the ecommerce platform, fulfillment system, email platform, and type of feedback being collected.

A simple workflow might look like this:

  1. The order is placed.
  2. The order is shipped.
  3. The order is delivered.
  4. The customer receives time to experience the order.
  5. The feedback email is sent.
  6. The response is collected and routed appropriately.

Choose the delay based on the product and the information you want to collect. Avoid duplicate requests and respect applicable consent and email rules.

17. Common Customer Feedback Email Mistakes

Asking too many questions

A long questionnaire can make a simple request feel like a major task. Ask only what is necessary for the current objective.

Asking a vague question

Questions such as “Any thoughts?” may leave customers unsure about what type of response would be useful.

Assuming the customer was satisfied

A useful feedback request should allow customers to describe problems as well as positive experiences.

Making the process difficult

Unnecessary registration, multiple pages, or complicated forms can add friction to a simple request.

Hiding the reason for the request

Briefly explain what the business wants to learn and how the response may be used when appropriate.

Turning the email into a promotion

Keep the primary focus on collecting feedback.

Sending at the wrong time

Sending a request before the customer has enough experience with the product may produce feedback that does not answer the intended question.

Ignoring responses

Decide who will review responses and how reported issues will be handled. Collecting feedback without reviewing it limits its practical value.

18. How to Test a Feedback Email

Before activating the workflow, test both the email and the response process.

  • Check the subject line.
  • Confirm customer and order information is accurate.
  • Test every feedback link and form.
  • Confirm responses reach the correct destination.
  • Check that the question is easy to understand.
  • Review the email on mobile devices.
  • Check the automation timing.
  • Confirm support information is available when appropriate.
  • Check how missing personalization fields appear.

Testing the complete journey is more useful than checking only whether the email template looks correct.

19. What to Measure After Sending

The most useful measurements depend on the purpose of the feedback request. Depending on your email and feedback platforms, you may review:

  • Successful email delivery and bounce rate.
  • Open activity, interpreted cautiously.
  • Feedback-link clicks.
  • Survey completion.
  • Response rate.
  • Types of issues reported.
  • Support requests resulting from feedback.

Avoid treating one metric as the complete picture. A high number of clicks, for example, does not necessarily mean customers completed the feedback process.

For broader measurement concepts, see Email Marketing Analytics .

20. Customer Feedback Email Checklist

  • Is the purpose of the email clear?
  • Is the subject line specific and understandable?
  • Does the opening provide enough context?
  • Is the main feedback question focused?
  • Does the question match what you want to learn?
  • Is the response process simple?
  • Can customers share negative as well as positive feedback?
  • Are customer and order details accurate?
  • Is promotional content secondary?
  • Is the timing appropriate for the product?
  • Have all links and forms been tested?
  • Is there a process for reviewing feedback?
  • Does the email work on mobile devices?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a customer feedback email?

It asks customers to share information about their experience with a business, order, product, delivery, or support process.

When should I send a feedback email after delivery?

Send it after the customer has had enough time to experience the product or delivery process relevant to your question. The appropriate timing varies by product and business.

What should I ask in a customer feedback email?

Ask a focused question connected to what you want to learn, such as the ordering experience, delivery, product expectations, or communication.

How is a feedback email different from a review request?

A feedback email can seek broader information about the customer experience, while a product review request usually focuses on a particular product.

Should feedback emails include discounts?

A business may choose to offer an incentive where appropriate, but it should clearly explain the conditions. Do not make unsupported claims about the effect of an incentive, and do not require positive feedback in exchange.

What should I do with negative feedback?

Route it to the appropriate person or support process, review the issue, and respond according to the business's customer-service procedures.

Final Takeaway

A customer feedback email after delivery should make it easy for customers to explain their experience without requiring unnecessary effort.

Start with a clear purpose, ask a focused question, choose a simple response method, give customers room to report problems, and keep promotional content secondary to the feedback request.

Treat feedback as useful customer information rather than simply another email metric. Review responses to understand where the customer experience works well and where it may need attention.
