How to Write an Order Delay Email to Customers

An order delay email tells a customer that their order will not arrive as originally expected. A good message does more than announce the delay. It explains what happened, gives the customer the most useful information available, and makes the next step clear.

Order delays can happen because of inventory problems, carrier disruptions, production issues, weather, supplier delays, or unexpected operational problems. Whatever the reason, customers generally want a straightforward answer: What happened, when will my order arrive, and what can I do if the new timing does not work for me?

This guide explains how to write an order delay email that is clear, honest, useful, and easy for customers to understand.

Quick Answer: An effective order delay email should acknowledge the delay, identify the affected order, briefly explain the situation, provide the latest expected delivery information, explain available options when necessary, and give customers a clear way to get help.

What Is an Order Delay Email?

An order delay email is a customer communication sent when an order is expected to arrive later than the original delivery estimate.

It is different from a shipping confirmation email. A shipping confirmation normally tells the customer that an order has been shipped and may include tracking information. An order delay email addresses a problem with the expected timeline.

For example, a customer might have been told to expect an order between June 10 and June 12. If the business later discovers that the order will not arrive until June 15, the customer should receive an update rather than being left to discover the delay on their own.

This type of message is part of the broader customer journey. Understanding that journey can help businesses decide what information customers need at different stages. See Email Marketing Customer Journey for more on customer communication across the lifecycle.

When Should You Send an Order Delay Email?

Send an order delay email as soon as you have enough reliable information to tell the customer that the original expectation is no longer realistic.

Do not wait until the customer contacts support if the business already knows that an order will be late.

The timing depends on the situation. You may need to notify customers when:

  • An order cannot leave the warehouse on schedule.
  • An item is temporarily unavailable.
  • A supplier has delayed fulfillment.
  • A carrier disruption affects delivery.
  • A production problem changes the expected shipment date.
  • Weather or another external event affects transportation.
  • The original delivery estimate is no longer accurate.

If the delay is discovered early, an early notification gives customers more time to adjust their plans.

What to Include in an Order Delay Email

A useful order delay email does not need to be long. It needs to contain the information that helps the customer understand the situation.

1. A clear subject line

The subject should make the purpose of the message understandable without forcing the customer to open it just to discover that an order is delayed.

2. The customer's order information

Identify the affected order clearly. Depending on the business, this might include an order number, product name, or another appropriate reference.

3. A direct explanation of the delay

Tell the customer what has changed. Keep the explanation honest and concise.

4. The latest delivery information

If you have a reliable revised delivery date or estimated timeframe, provide it.

5. Available options

If customers can cancel, change an item, choose another delivery option, or contact support, explain those choices clearly.

6. A helpful next step

Tell customers what they should do next, if anything is required from them.

7. A support option

Give customers a straightforward way to ask questions or request assistance.

How to Write an Order Delay Email Step by Step

Step 1: Start with the purpose of the email

Do not make the customer search through several paragraphs to discover why you contacted them.

A simple opening can work well:

We’re writing to let you know that your order will arrive later than originally expected.

This immediately establishes the purpose of the message.

Step 2: Identify the affected order

Give enough information for the customer to recognize the order.

For example:

Order #1847, including the Classic Canvas Backpack, has been affected by a fulfillment delay.

Use the actual order information available in your customer system rather than adding unnecessary personal details.

Step 3: Explain the delay briefly

Customers usually need context, but they do not need an internal operational report.

Instead of writing a long explanation about warehouse processes, explain the relevant reason in one or two sentences.

For example:

Our supplier has experienced an unexpected shipment delay, which has affected the availability of this item.

If the exact reason is not appropriate to share, give a truthful general explanation rather than inventing a specific cause.

Step 4: Give the updated expectation

If you have a reliable new date, state it clearly.

Your order is now expected to arrive by June 15.

If you only have a timeframe, provide the timeframe instead:

We currently expect your order to arrive within the next five to seven business days.

Avoid presenting an uncertain date as a guarantee.

Step 5: Explain what happens next

If the order will continue automatically, say so.

If the customer needs to make a decision, explain the available choices.

For example:

  • You can keep the order and wait for the updated delivery date.
  • You can contact our support team if you would like to discuss your options.
  • If cancellation is available under your policy, explain how the customer can request it.

Step 6: Close with useful support information

End the email by making it easy for the customer to get help.

A simple closing is often more useful than an overly promotional message.

Order Delay Email Subject Lines

The subject line should be specific enough to set the right expectation.

  • Update on Your Order #1847
  • Your Order Delivery Update
  • Important Update About Your Order
  • Your Order Is Taking a Little Longer
  • Updated Delivery Information for Your Order
  • We Have an Update About Your Order
  • Change to Your Expected Delivery Date

Avoid vague subjects such as “Important Message” when the real purpose is simply to communicate an order delay.

Order Delay Email Template

Here is a practical template that can be adapted to different businesses:

Subject: Update on Your Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re writing to let you know that your order #[Order Number] will arrive later than originally expected.

[Brief explanation of the delay.]

Your order is now expected to arrive by [Updated Date/Timeframe]. We’ll continue to keep you updated if anything changes.

[Explain any available customer options, if applicable.]

If you have any questions about your order, please contact [Support Team/Support Channel].

Thank you for your patience and understanding.

Best,
[Company or Team Name]

What to Say When the New Delivery Date Is Uncertain

One of the most difficult situations is a delay without a dependable replacement date.

In that situation, do not create false certainty simply to make the email sound reassuring.

Instead, explain what is known and what is not yet known.

For example:

We’re currently working to confirm a new delivery date. We do not have a reliable date to share yet, but we’ll send you another update as soon as we have confirmed information.

This approach is more useful than giving customers a date that the business may have to change again.

How to Handle a Second or Repeated Delay

A second delay requires particularly clear communication because the customer has already received an expectation that was not met.

Start by acknowledging the new change directly.

Do not simply send the same delay message again with a different date.

A repeated-delay email can include:

  • Acknowledgment that the previous delivery expectation changed.
  • The latest confirmed information.
  • A realistic updated timeframe.
  • Any customer options available under the business's policy.
  • A clear support contact.

Keep the tone respectful. The customer does not need to be blamed for asking about an order that has already been delayed.

Using Email Automation for Order Delay Messages

Order-delay communication can become easier to manage when customer data and order-status information are connected to an email marketing or customer communication system.

Automation can help trigger an appropriate message when a defined order status changes.

For example, a workflow might follow this sequence:

  1. The order is marked as delayed.
  2. The system checks whether a customer notification is required.
  3. The customer receives the appropriate update.
  4. The order status is updated when new information becomes available.
  5. A follow-up message is sent when the order returns to the expected process.

Automation should support accurate communication rather than automatically sending messages based on incomplete or unreliable data. For a broader explanation of automated email workflows, see Email Marketing Automation.

Common Order Delay Email Mistakes

1. Hiding the delay

Do not bury the important information underneath a long introduction.

2. Giving an unrealistic delivery date

If the date is uncertain, do not present it as guaranteed.

3. Overexplaining internal problems

Customers need useful context, not every detail of the company's internal operations.

4. Failing to identify the order

A generic delay message can create confusion when a customer has multiple orders.

5. Ignoring available customer options

If customers can take a meaningful action because of the delay, explain that option clearly.

6. Making the email overly promotional

An order delay is primarily a service communication. Adding aggressive promotional content can distract from the information the customer needs.

7. Sending inconsistent updates

If different messages provide conflicting information, customers may lose confidence in the communication process.

Order Delay Email Checklist

Before sending an order delay email, review the message against this checklist:

  • Does the subject clearly indicate that the message is an order update?
  • Is the affected order easy to identify?
  • Have you clearly explained what changed?
  • Have you avoided unnecessary internal details?
  • Is the revised delivery information accurate?
  • Have you avoided making uncertain information sound guaranteed?
  • Are available customer options explained?
  • Is there a clear support route?
  • Does the message sound respectful and human?
  • Have you checked that personalization fields will display correctly?

If the email is part of a broader customer communication program, review its performance after sending. Metrics such as opens, clicks, replies, support contacts, and customer actions can help identify communication problems. See Email Marketing Analytics for more about measuring email performance.

Final Thoughts

An order delay email should reduce uncertainty, not create more of it.

The most useful approach is simple: tell customers what changed, explain the situation honestly, provide the latest reliable delivery information, and make the next step clear.

You do not need a complicated message. You need accurate information presented at the right time in language customers can understand.

When order updates become part of a consistent customer communication journey, they can also help the business maintain clearer and more predictable communication before, during, and after fulfillment.
