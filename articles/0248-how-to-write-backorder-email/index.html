How to Write an Order Delay Email to Customers

An order delay email tells a customer that an order will arrive later than originally expected. A useful message explains what has changed, shares the latest reliable information, and tells the customer what to expect next.

Delays can result from inventory shortages, supplier problems, production issues, carrier disruptions, severe weather, or unexpected operational problems. Customers should not have to discover a known delay by repeatedly checking their order status or contacting support.

This guide explains when to send an order delay email, what information to include, how to write the message, and how to handle uncertain delivery dates and repeated delays.

Quick answer

An effective order delay email identifies the affected order, acknowledges the delay, briefly explains the reason when known, provides a realistic revised delivery estimate if available, explains relevant customer options, and offers a clear support route.

1. What Is an Order Delay Email?

An order delay email is a service message sent when a business learns that an order is unlikely to arrive within the previously communicated delivery timeframe.

It differs from an order confirmation, which acknowledges a purchase, and a shipping confirmation, which communicates that an order has been dispatched or shipped. An order delay email explains a change to the expected timeline.

For example, a customer may originally expect a package between June 10 and June 12. If the business later determines that delivery is likely to take longer, it should communicate the change and provide an updated estimate when reliable information is available.

This communication is one stage of the customer journey. See Email Marketing and the Customer Journey for related guidance.

2. When Should You Send an Order Delay Email?

Notify customers promptly after the business has reliable information that the original delivery expectation is no longer realistic. Do not wait for a customer to complain if the delay is already known.

Common reasons for sending an update include:

  • An order cannot leave the warehouse on schedule.
  • An item is temporarily out of stock.
  • A supplier has delayed fulfillment.
  • A carrier disruption affects transportation.
  • A production problem changes the expected shipping date.
  • Weather or another external event affects delivery.
  • The original delivery estimate is no longer reliable.

An early, accurate notification gives customers more time to adjust their plans. If the situation is still developing, communicate what is known without making promises about information that has not been confirmed.

3. What to Include in an Order Delay Email

A clear subject line

Make the purpose of the message easy to understand from the inbox. Mention the order update rather than using a vague subject.

The affected order

Include an order number or another suitable reference so customers can identify the purchase. Only include personal information that is necessary.

An honest explanation

Explain the reason briefly if it is known and appropriate to share. If the exact cause is not confirmed, do not invent one.

The latest delivery estimate

Share a revised date or timeframe only when there is a reasonable basis for it. Make clear when an estimate is not guaranteed.

Available customer options

Depending on the business and its policies, customers may be able to keep waiting, ask for assistance, change an order, or request cancellation. Explain only the options that are actually available.

A clear support route

Tell customers how to get help. Use a verified contact address or official support page when providing a link in a real customer email. Never publish a guessed support URL.

4. How to Write an Order Delay Email Step by Step

Step 1: State the delay immediately

Begin with the main point rather than a long introduction.

Example

We’re writing to let you know that your order will arrive later than originally expected.

Step 2: Identify the order

Give the customer enough information to recognize the affected purchase.

Example

Your order #[Order Number], containing [Product Name], has been affected by a fulfillment delay.

Use actual order information from the business system. Examples in this article are illustrative and should be replaced before sending.

Step 3: Explain what happened

A concise explanation helps customers understand the situation without overwhelming them with internal operational details.

Example

Our supplier has experienced an unexpected shipment delay, which has affected the availability of this item.

Use that explanation only if it accurately describes the actual reason. Otherwise, state what is known without speculating.

Step 4: Give a realistic delivery estimate

If a revised date has been confirmed, state it clearly. If only a timeframe is available, use that instead.

Example with a revised date

Your order is currently expected to arrive by [Confirmed Date].

Example with a timeframe

We currently expect your order to arrive within [Estimated Timeframe].

Avoid presenting an uncertain estimate as a guarantee. If the business has no dependable new date, explain that clearly.

Step 5: Explain what happens next

Tell the customer whether the order will continue through the normal process or whether the customer needs to make a decision.

  • Explain whether the order will remain active.
  • Describe any verified alternatives that are available.
  • Explain how to request assistance.
  • Describe cancellation or refund options only in accordance with the applicable policy.

Step 6: Close with a helpful next step

End with a brief, respectful closing. Keep the focus on the customer's order rather than turning a service message into a sales pitch.

5. Order Delay Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject that accurately describes the update and is consistent with the information inside the email.

  • Update on Your Order #[Order Number]
  • Your Order Delivery Update
  • Important Update About Your Order
  • Updated Delivery Information for Your Order
  • A Change to Your Expected Delivery Date
  • We Have an Update About Your Order
  • Your Order Is Taking Longer Than Expected

Avoid vague subject lines that hide the purpose of the message. If the customer has several orders, including the relevant order number can help distinguish the update.

6. Order Delay Email Templates

Template A: A delay with a confirmed revised date

Subject: Update on Your Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re writing to let you know that your order #[Order Number] will arrive later than originally expected.

[Brief, accurate explanation of the delay.]

Your order is currently expected to arrive by [Confirmed Revised Date]. We’ll let you know if important delivery information changes.

If you have questions, please contact us through [Verified Support Channel].

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
[Company or Team Name]

Use this template only when the revised date is supported by reliable information. Replace every bracketed field before sending.

Template B: A delay without a confirmed new date

Subject: An Update About Your Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry, but your order #[Order Number] will take longer than originally expected.

We’re still working to confirm a reliable delivery date and do not want to give you an estimate that may be incorrect.

We’ll share another update when we have confirmed information. If you need assistance in the meantime, please contact us through [Verified Support Channel].

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Company or Team Name]

This version is suitable when the business knows there is a delay but cannot yet provide a dependable replacement date.

Template C: A second delay

Subject: Further Update on Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry that your order has been delayed again. We understand that another change to the expected timing is frustrating.

The latest information we have is: [Verified Status or Explanation]. [Provide a revised estimate only if one is reasonably supported.]

If the revised timing does not work for you, please contact us through [Verified Support Channel] to discuss the options available under our policy.

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
[Company or Team Name]

Do not reuse a previous delivery date if it is no longer realistic. Acknowledge the additional delay and clearly distinguish confirmed information from estimates.

7. What to Say When the New Delivery Date Is Uncertain

Sometimes a business knows an order is delayed but cannot confidently predict when it will arrive. In that situation, an honest update is more useful than an invented date.

A message can explain three things:

  • The original delivery expectation has changed.
  • A new date has not yet been confirmed.
  • The business will provide another update when reliable information is available.
Example wording

We’re working to confirm a new delivery date. We do not have a reliable date to share yet, but we’ll send you another update when we have confirmed information.

Avoid sending repeated messages that provide no new information. If the delay continues, give customers a meaningful update when the status, expected timing, or available options change.

8. How to Handle a Second or Repeated Delay

A repeated delay needs careful communication because the previous expectation was already missed. A new email should acknowledge the change rather than simply repeat the earlier message.

Include the following where relevant:

  • A direct acknowledgment of the additional delay.
  • The latest confirmed order status.
  • A realistic revised estimate, if available.
  • Relevant customer options under the business's policy.
  • A clear support route.

Avoid blaming customers for asking about late orders. Use a respectful tone, keep the facts consistent, and ensure that support staff can access the same order information.

9. Using Email Automation for Order Delay Messages

An ecommerce platform, order-management system, or customer communication tool may automate delay notifications when reliable order-status data is available.

A basic workflow may follow these steps:

  1. The order system records a delay or a relevant status change.
  2. The workflow checks whether a customer notification is required.
  3. The message uses the correct order information.
  4. The customer receives the appropriate update.
  5. A later message is sent when meaningful new information becomes available.

Before activating an automated workflow, verify that:

  • The trigger represents the intended order status.
  • Order numbers and customer details map to the correct fields.
  • Duplicate notifications are controlled.
  • Unconfirmed delivery estimates are not presented as guarantees.
  • Support information and any links have been tested.
  • Customers receive an update when a significant change requires one.

Automation should support accurate communication rather than send messages based on incomplete data. Read Email Marketing Automation for related guidance.

10. Common Order Delay Email Mistakes

Hiding the delay

Put the main update near the beginning so the customer understands the purpose immediately.

Giving an unrealistic delivery date

Use a revised date only when supported by reasonable evidence. Otherwise, communicate the uncertainty.

Overexplaining internal problems

Give enough context to explain the delay without overwhelming the reader with operational details that do not help them make a decision.

Failing to identify the order

A generic message can create confusion when customers have several recent purchases.

Ignoring customer options

If a delay changes what a customer can reasonably do, explain the available options and the correct way to request help.

Using unverified links

A support link that leads to an unrelated or nonexistent page creates frustration. Verify every destination before publishing or sending it.

Making the message overly promotional

An order delay is primarily a service communication. Keep essential information more prominent than optional marketing content.

Sending inconsistent updates

Make sure the email, order page, and support team use consistent, up-to-date information.

11. Order Delay Email Checklist

Before sending the message, review this checklist:

  • The subject clearly identifies the order update.
  • The affected order is easy to recognize.
  • The delay is explained honestly.
  • The message contains no invented reason for the delay.
  • The delivery estimate is supported by available information.
  • Uncertain dates are not presented as guarantees.
  • Relevant customer options are explained accurately.
  • The support channel is valid and appropriate.
  • Personalization fields display correctly.
  • Any links have been tested.
  • The message is readable on mobile devices.
  • The automation does not send unnecessary duplicate updates.

12. Measuring the Effectiveness of Order Delay Emails

Measurement should focus on whether the communication reaches customers and helps them understand what is happening. Depending on the email platform and business process, useful indicators include:

  • Email delivery and bounce rates.
  • Clicks on relevant order-status or support links.
  • Replies and support requests about the delay.
  • Repeated customer questions caused by unclear information.
  • Duplicate notifications or incorrect order details.

Interpret these metrics in context. A support request does not automatically mean the email failed; a customer may have a genuine question about the delay. Open tracking is also imperfect because privacy features can affect reported activity.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I send an order delay email?

Send it promptly after the business has reliable information that the original delivery expectation is no longer realistic. Do not wait for the customer to ask if the delay is already known.

Should I explain the exact reason for the delay?

Explain the reason when it is known and appropriate to share. If the cause is uncertain, communicate what is known without inventing details.

What if I do not know the new delivery date?

State that the date has not been confirmed. Explain when customers can expect another update only if the business can reasonably meet that communication commitment.

Should I offer a discount for a delayed order?

That depends on the business's policies, the circumstances, and the available customer options. A discount is not a substitute for accurate information and appropriate support.

Can order delay emails be automated?

Yes, when the order system provides reliable status information and the workflow has been tested. Automation should prevent duplicate messages and use accurate customer and order data.

What should I do if an order is delayed more than once?

Acknowledge the additional delay, share the latest confirmed information, explain any available options, and provide a clear support route. Do not repeat an outdated delivery estimate.

Final Thoughts

An order delay email should reduce uncertainty rather than create more of it. Customers need a clear explanation, accurate order information, a realistic delivery estimate when available, and a useful next step.

Keep the message direct and respectful. If the situation changes again, communicate meaningful new information rather than sending repetitive updates with no practical value.

A well-designed order communication process combines reliable order data, tested automation, consistent support information, and clear writing. These practices help businesses communicate delays more effectively without promising what they cannot guarantee.
