How to Write a Backorder Email to Customers

A backorder email tells a customer that an item is temporarily unavailable for immediate shipment but may be supplied when inventory is replenished. The message should make the situation clear before the customer has to ask, explain what is known about restocking, and describe the choices available under the store's policy.

A backorder is not the same as a confirmed shipment delay. With a backorder, the item may not yet be available to dispatch. That distinction matters: customers need an honest estimate, not a delivery promise based on an unconfirmed supplier date.

Quick answer

A useful backorder email identifies the affected item and order, explains that stock is unavailable, shares a realistic restock or dispatch estimate only when supported, explains available alternatives or cancellation options, and states when the customer will receive the next update.

1. What Is a Backorder Email?

A backorder email is an order-related service message sent when a customer has ordered an item that is not currently available to ship. The store may expect to fulfill the order after new inventory arrives, but the timing depends on the actual supply situation.

For example, a customer orders a desk lamp listed as available. During fulfillment, the store discovers that the remaining units have been allocated to earlier orders. If the store can still fulfill the purchase after replenishment, it should explain the backorder and give the customer accurate information about the next step.

Keep the distinction clear across customer communications:

  • Order confirmation: acknowledges the purchase.
  • Backorder notification: explains that an item is awaiting replenishment before it can be fulfilled.
  • Shipping confirmation: confirms that the order or item has actually been dispatched.

2. When Should You Send a Backorder Email?

Send the notification as soon as the business confirms that an ordered item cannot be shipped within the previously communicated timeframe. Prompt communication gives customers a chance to decide whether waiting still works for them.

Common triggers include:

  • Inventory falls below the quantity required to fulfill paid orders.
  • A supplier confirms that replenishment will arrive later than expected.
  • A stock-count discrepancy is discovered during fulfillment.
  • Demand exceeds available inventory and new stock is pending.
  • A product batch is temporarily unavailable for a verified operational reason.

Do not describe an item as backordered merely because tracking information has not updated after dispatch. First confirm the order's actual status in the inventory or order-management system.

3. What to Include in the Message

Identify the order and item

Include the order number and the affected product name. If an order contains several items, specify which item is backordered and, where useful, whether the remaining items will ship separately.

Explain the current status plainly

Use direct wording such as “The blue desk lamp in your order is temporarily out of stock.” Avoid vague phrases that could make the customer think the order has already shipped.

Give an evidence-based estimate

If a supplier has confirmed a restock date and the store has a reasonable basis for its fulfillment estimate, share it as an estimate. Distinguish the expected restock date from the expected dispatch or arrival date; these are not necessarily the same.

Explain the next step and available choices

State whether the order will remain active automatically and describe the options actually offered by the business, such as waiting, selecting an available substitute, splitting the shipment, or requesting cancellation. Do not promise an option that the store cannot honor.

Set a follow-up expectation

If the date is uncertain, say when the customer can expect the next update only if the business can meet that commitment. A specific, realistic communication plan is more useful than a confident but unsupported delivery promise.

4. A Practical Decision Process Before Sending

Use this short process to decide what the email should say. It helps keep the message aligned with the inventory facts instead of relying on a generic template.

  1. Verify the item status. Confirm that the item is genuinely awaiting replenishment and identify the affected quantity.
  2. Check the supply evidence. Review the latest supplier or warehouse information. Do not treat an old estimate as a confirmed date.
  3. Determine fulfillment options. Check whether other items can ship now and whether substitutions or cancellation are supported by store policy.
  4. Choose the correct message. Use a confirmed-estimate template only when the date is reasonably supported; otherwise, use the uncertain-date version.
  5. Record the next action. Set a follow-up task or workflow condition so the order is reviewed when inventory or supplier information changes.
Example decision

A customer orders three products, but one item is backordered. If the other two products are available and the store supports split shipments, the email should name the delayed item, explain whether the available items will ship separately, and describe any additional shipping implications accurately. If split shipment is not available, explain the actual fulfillment plan instead of implying that the other items will arrive first.

5. Backorder Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject line that accurately signals an order update. Personalize it only with information that has been verified.

  • Update on Order #[Order Number]
  • Important Update About Your [Product Name]
  • Your Order Is Awaiting New Stock
  • Backorder Notice for Order #[Order Number]
  • An Update on the Availability of Your Item
  • Your Order: Restock Timing and Next Steps

Avoid subject lines such as “Your order has shipped” unless dispatch has actually occurred. A misleading subject can create confusion even if the email body explains the situation correctly.

6. Backorder Email Templates

Template A: Expected restock timing is available

Subject: Update on Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re writing to let you know that [Product Name] in order #[Order Number] is temporarily out of stock and is currently on backorder.

Based on the latest information available to us, we expect new stock around [Expected Restock Date]. After stock arrives and is processed, we currently expect to dispatch your item within [Processing Estimate]. These dates are estimates and may change if the supply situation changes.

[Explain whether the rest of the order will ship separately, if applicable.] You can [describe verified options available under your store policy].

We’ll send another update if there is a meaningful change to the expected timing. If you need help, contact us through [Verified Support Channel].

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
[Company or Support Team]

Use this version only when the dates and fulfillment details are supported by current information. Replace every bracketed field and remove any option that is not available.

Template B: The restock date is not confirmed

Subject: An Update About Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry, but [Product Name] in order #[Order Number] is temporarily unavailable and cannot be dispatched yet.

We are checking the latest replenishment information, but we do not have a reliable restock date to share at this time. We do not want to give you a date that may prove inaccurate.

Your current options are [list only the options available under your policy]. We’ll contact you again when we have meaningful new information, or by [Next Update Date] if that date is a commitment your team can meet.

If you have questions, please contact us through [Verified Support Channel].

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Company or Support Team]

Template C: Only one item in a multi-item order is backordered

Subject: Availability Update for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

Most items in your order are available, but [Backordered Product] is awaiting replenishment.

[Explain accurately whether available items will ship separately or whether the order will be held until all items are ready.] The current estimate for the backordered item is [Verified Estimate / Not Yet Confirmed].

You can review the options available to you here: [Verified Order or Support Link]. Please contact us if you need help understanding the fulfillment plan.

Thank you for choosing [Company Name].

Best regards,
[Company or Support Team]

These templates are starting points, not substitutes for checking the actual order, stock status, delivery terms, and customer options.

7. What to Say When Restock Timing Is Unknown

Sometimes a supplier cannot provide a dependable date. In that situation, separate confirmed facts from uncertainty. Tell the customer the item is not ready to ship, state that the restock date is unconfirmed, and explain what the business will do next.

A useful update answers three questions:

  • What is happening to the item now?
  • What is known—and not known—about replenishment?
  • What can the customer do, and when will the business communicate again?

Do not keep sending automated messages that repeat “still delayed” without a meaningful update. If there is no new supply information, a planned check-in can still be useful, but it should explain the current status honestly and avoid implying progress that has not occurred.

8. How to Explain Customer Options

Customer options depend on the store's actual policies, product availability, payment arrangement, and applicable consumer rules. Explain only choices the business can support.

Possible option What the email should clarify
Wait for replenishment Whether the order stays active and how updates will be provided.
Choose a substitute Which alternatives are available and how the customer can confirm a change.
Ship available items first Whether split shipment is supported and whether any extra charge applies.
Cancel the affected item or order The valid cancellation process and the applicable refund terms.
Contact support A tested contact method and any order reference the customer should provide.

Do not assume that every customer has the same choices. If a choice depends on stock, geography, or order status, verify eligibility before offering it.

9. Automating Backorder Updates Responsibly

An ecommerce platform or email automation tool can help send backorder notifications when it receives dependable inventory and order-status data. Automation should reduce missed updates, not turn uncertain inventory information into false promises.

  1. Trigger the workflow when the order system confirms a backorder status.
  2. Check whether the customer has already received the same notification.
  3. Insert the correct order number, affected product, and verified status.
  4. Select the confirmed-date or unknown-date message based on current data.
  5. Set a follow-up condition for a meaningful stock or status change.
  6. Pause or route the message for human review if required fields are missing or conflicting.

Before launch, test the workflow with sample orders, including a multi-item order, a cancelled item, an unknown restock date, and a stock update that occurs after the first notification. Confirm that customers do not receive duplicate or contradictory messages.

10. Backorder Email Quality-Control Checklist

  • The email names the correct order and affected product.
  • The inventory status has been verified before sending.
  • Restock, dispatch, and delivery estimates are not confused.
  • No unconfirmed date is presented as a guarantee.
  • The email explains what happens to the other items in the order.
  • Customer options match the actual policy and order status.
  • Every support or order link has been tested.
  • Personalization fields have safe fallbacks if data is missing.
  • Duplicate notifications are prevented.
  • A follow-up owner or workflow condition is defined.
  • The email is readable on mobile devices and uses a clear subject.

11. How to Evaluate Backorder Emails

Assess both message delivery and the operational outcome. Depending on the systems available, useful measures include:

  • Delivery and bounce rates: whether the messages reach valid addresses.
  • Customer replies and support contacts: whether recipients need clarification or help.
  • Cancellation and substitution requests: whether customers are choosing alternatives after receiving the update.
  • Duplicate or incorrect notifications: whether workflow logic and data mapping are reliable.
  • Time to meaningful update: how long customers wait after a confirmed inventory change before receiving the next communication.

Interpret the measures in context. A cancellation is not automatically evidence of a poor email; the customer may reasonably decide not to wait. The purpose of the message is to communicate accurately and help the customer make an informed decision, not to hide a supply problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a backorder and an out-of-stock item?

An out-of-stock label describes current availability. A backorder generally means an order has been accepted for an item that is not currently available to fulfill and is expected to be supplied later. A store should explain its own process clearly rather than assume customers know the distinction.

Should I provide a restock date if the supplier has not confirmed one?

No. Explain that the date is not confirmed, share only reliable information, and describe the next communication step. An invented date can damage trust and lead customers to make plans based on incorrect information.

Should I send the available items separately?

That depends on the store's fulfillment capabilities, shipping terms, and policy. If split shipment is possible, explain what will ship first and whether any additional charge applies. If it is not available, describe the actual plan.

Can backorder notifications be automated?

Yes, when the order system provides accurate stock and status data. Test triggers, personalization, duplicate prevention, and follow-up conditions before activating the workflow.

How often should I send updates?

Send an update when meaningful information changes, and make a specific follow-up commitment only when the business can meet it. Avoid frequent messages that simply repeat the same information.

Should I offer a discount or compensation?

Only if the business chooses to offer it under its policies and applicable rules. A discount does not replace a clear explanation, accurate timing, or a transparent account of the customer's available options.

Final Thoughts

A good backorder email makes an uncertain fulfillment situation easier to understand. It identifies the affected item, distinguishes confirmed facts from estimates, explains realistic choices, and gives the customer a dependable next step.

The strongest process combines accurate inventory data, carefully tested automation, and honest customer communication. Review the message whenever stock status changes, and never let a template promise more than the business can deliver.
