How to Write a Shipping Delay Email to Customers

A shipping delay email informs a customer that a shipment is taking longer than expected. Its purpose is to explain the verified situation, provide the latest reliable information, and make the next step clear. A useful message reduces uncertainty instead of making promises the business cannot keep.

Before writing, distinguish between an order that has not been dispatched and a package already handed to a carrier. The first may involve a fulfillment delay; the second may involve a carrier delay. The wording should reflect the actual status.

Quick answer

Identify the order, explain what has changed, share a reliable delivery estimate if available, provide a working tracking link when useful, explain what happens next, and offer a support contact. If the delivery date is unknown, say so honestly.

1. What Is a Shipping Delay Email?

A shipping delay email is an order-related service message sent when shipping or delivery takes longer than the customer was told to expect. It should state the known status, explain the effect on the expected timeline, and tell the customer whether any action is required.

It differs from an order delay email, which may describe a problem before fulfillment begins. A shipping delay email focuses on dispatch, carrier movement, or delivery. If the product is unavailable because stock has not arrived, a backorder email may be more accurate.

For related guidance, read How to Write an Order Delay Email to Customers and How to Write a Backorder Email to Customers.

2. When Should You Send a Shipping Delay Email?

Send an update when reliable information shows that the expected shipping or delivery timeline has materially changed. If the business already knows about a significant delay, customers should not have to discover it by repeatedly checking tracking.

Appropriate triggers may include:

  • A fulfillment center confirms that dispatch will occur later than planned.
  • A carrier reports a service disruption affecting the package.
  • A verified shipment exception changes the delivery expectation.
  • A confirmed weather event affects the shipping route.
  • The promised delivery window has passed and the shipment needs investigation.

A routine tracking scan does not always justify another email. Send an update when information changes the customer's expectations or requires a decision.

3. Check the Shipment Before Writing

Use the verified order and shipment status to choose the right message. Do not force every problem into the same template.

Situation What to communicate
Package has not been dispatched Explain the fulfillment delay and give a supported dispatch estimate if available.
Carrier reports a delay Share the verified carrier status and revised delivery estimate, if available.
Tracking has not updated Check the shipment status before claiming that the package is lost.
No reliable new date State that the date is unconfirmed and explain the next communication step.
One package in a multi-package order is delayed Identify the affected package and clarify the status of the remaining shipments.

4. Shipping Delay Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject line that clearly describes the update without creating unnecessary alarm.

  • Shipping Update for Order #[Order Number]
  • Important Update About Your Delivery
  • Your Order Delivery Has Been Delayed
  • New Delivery Estimate for Order #[Order Number]
  • Update: Your Package Is Taking Longer Than Expected
  • We Have an Update About Your Shipment

Use an order number when it helps customers recognize the purchase. Avoid vague subjects such as “Important Information” that do not explain the message's purpose.

5. Shipping Delay Email Templates

Template A: A new delivery estimate is available

Subject: Shipping Update for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We are writing to let you know that your shipment for order #[Order Number] is taking longer than expected.

Based on the latest available shipping information, your package is currently expected to arrive around [Revised Date]. This is an estimate and may change as the shipment progresses.

You can check the latest status here: [Verified Tracking Link].

We apologize for the inconvenience. If you have questions, please contact us at [Support Email].

Thank you for your patience,
[Business Name] Customer Support

Template B: No confirmed delivery date is available

Subject: Update on Your Delayed Shipment

Hi [Customer Name],

Your order #[Order Number] is experiencing a shipping delay. We do not yet have a confirmed new delivery date.

We are checking the shipment status and will share another update when reliable information becomes available. You can also review the current status here: [Verified Tracking Link], if tracking is available.

We understand that waiting is frustrating and apologize for the inconvenience. If you need help, contact [Support Email].

Thank you,
[Business Name] Customer Support

Template C: The package has not left the fulfillment center

Subject: Fulfillment Update for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We wanted to update you about order #[Order Number]. Your package has not yet been dispatched, so it will take longer than originally expected to arrive.

Our current dispatch estimate is [Date], if this estimate has been confirmed. We will update you if the timeline changes.

We are sorry for the inconvenience. Please contact [Support Email] if you have questions about your order.

Best regards,
[Business Name] Customer Support

Replace every bracketed field before sending. Verify order numbers, dates, tracking links, and support details. Never send an example order number or placeholder to a real customer.

6. How to Explain a New Delivery Estimate

Make the difference between an estimate and a guarantee clear. If a carrier provides the date, attribute it to the carrier's latest information rather than presenting it as a promise from the store.

When a carrier estimate is available

“Based on the latest carrier update, your package is currently expected to arrive by [Date]. This is the carrier's current estimate and may change.”

When no reliable estimate is available

“We do not have a confirmed delivery date yet. We are checking the shipment status and will share another update when reliable information becomes available.”

Do not reuse an old date just to make the email appear complete. An honest explanation of uncertainty is better than a specific date without a reliable basis.

7. Using Tracking Information Helpfully

A tracking link can help customers check their shipment without contacting support. Before including it, confirm that it works and belongs to the correct package.

  • Place the tracking link close to the shipment update.
  • Use descriptive link text such as “View shipment tracking.”
  • Do not expose another customer's order details or tracking data.
  • Explain confusing carrier status terms in plain language.
  • If tracking has not updated, verify the situation before claiming the package is lost.

8. Automating Shipping Delay Updates Responsibly

An ecommerce or shipping system can trigger a message when a verified shipment exception materially affects the customer's delivery expectations. Automation should use current data and avoid sending an email for every routine scan.

  1. Trigger the workflow from a meaningful, verified shipping status.
  2. Confirm the order number, recipient, and affected package are mapped correctly.
  3. Check whether the same delay notification has already been sent.
  4. Use a revised estimate only when current data supports it.
  5. Route missing or conflicting data for human review.
  6. Test tracking links and mobile rendering before activation.

Check its current features and integrations against your needs; an email marketing platform may not replace the ecommerce or carrier system that supplies shipment status.

9. Common Shipping Delay Email Mistakes

Being vague about the status

State what is delayed and identify the affected order. Avoid generic wording that forces customers to contact support for basic facts.

Promising an unconfirmed date

Do not convert a carrier estimate into a guaranteed delivery promise. Explain uncertainty when the date is not dependable.

Blaming another party without evidence

Describe the verified cause when it is appropriate to share. Avoid unsupported accusations about a carrier or supplier.

Sending repetitive updates

Send messages when information materially changes or when a promised check-in is due. Repeating the same status without useful context can frustrate customers.

Using broken links

Test tracking and support links and confirm that each destination is correct.

Ignoring the customer's next step

Explain whether the customer needs to act, can monitor the shipment, or should contact support.

10. Shipping Delay Email Quality Checklist

  • The message identifies the correct order and affected package.
  • The shipment status has been verified.
  • The reason for the delay is accurate or clearly described as unknown.
  • The delivery estimate is based on current information.
  • Unconfirmed dates are not presented as guarantees.
  • The tracking link works and belongs to the correct shipment.
  • The next step and support route are clear.
  • Personalization fields display correctly.
  • Duplicate notifications are prevented.
  • The email is readable on mobile devices.
  • The subject line and email body communicate the same status.
  • The workflow has been tested with missing-data scenarios.

11. How to Evaluate Shipping Delay Emails

Review whether the message reaches customers and provides useful information. Depending on your systems, examine:

  • Delivery and bounce rates: whether the email reaches valid addresses.
  • Shipment-status support contacts: whether customers still need clarification.
  • Tracking-link clicks: whether customers use the supplied status page.
  • Incorrect or duplicate notifications: whether data mapping and workflow rules are reliable.
  • Time to communicate: how quickly customers are informed after reliable delay information arrives.

Interpret these measures in context. A support request does not automatically mean the email failed; the customer may have a legitimate question. Email open tracking is also imperfect because privacy features can affect reported activity.

12. Frequently Asked Questions

When should I send a shipping delay email?

Send it when reliable information shows that the shipping or delivery expectation has materially changed. Notify customers promptly rather than waiting for them to discover a known problem.

What if the carrier has not provided a new delivery date?

Say that the date is not confirmed, share the verified status, and explain when another update will be provided only if you can meet that commitment.

Should I include a tracking link?

Include one when it is available, works correctly, and helps the customer check the shipment. Make sure it does not expose another customer's information.

Should I blame the shipping carrier?

Explain the verified cause if it helps the customer understand the delay. Keep the tone factual and avoid assigning blame when the cause is uncertain.

Can shipping delay emails be automated?

Yes, if the workflow uses accurate shipment data, meaningful triggers, duplicate prevention, and tested personalization. Missing or conflicting data should be reviewed instead of turned into an unsupported claim.

How often should I send an update?

Send an update when the status changes meaningfully or when a promised check-in is due. Avoid repeated messages that provide no new information.

Final Thoughts

A useful shipping delay email reduces uncertainty. It tells customers what changed, identifies the affected shipment, provides the latest reliable information, and explains what happens next.

Verify the shipment before writing, use estimates carefully, test all links, and make automation follow meaningful status changes. Accurate communication is more valuable than a confident promise that later needs to be corrected.
