How to Write a Delivery Delay Email to Customers

A delivery delay email tells a customer that an order is unlikely to arrive within the previously communicated delivery window. The message should explain what is known, set realistic expectations, and show the customer how to get help. A clear update is more useful than a vague apology or an unsupported promise.

Delivery delays can happen because of carrier disruptions, severe weather, incomplete address details, customs processing, high seasonal demand, or fulfillment problems. Only mention a cause when it has been verified. If the cause is unknown, explain what you can confirm and what will happen next.

Quick answer

A good delivery delay email identifies the order, states that delivery is delayed, provides the latest reliable estimate or clearly says no date is confirmed, links to valid tracking information when available, and gives a useful support option.

1. Delivery Delay vs. Shipping Delay

The terms are related, but they do not always describe the same event. A shipping delay often refers to dispatch or movement through the carrier network. A delivery delay focuses on the order not arriving when the customer expected it to arrive. Use the term that best describes the verified status.

Situation Best communication focus
Order has not left the warehouse Explain the fulfillment or dispatch delay and the current dispatch estimate.
Carrier has the parcel but delivery is later than expected Explain the delivery delay and share the latest carrier estimate if available.
Tracking has stopped updating Verify the status before stating that the parcel is lost.
Delivery failed because nobody was available Explain the carrier's next attempt or collection options, if confirmed.
Product is not in stock Consider an order delay or backorder message instead.

For a more shipment-focused message, see How to Write a Shipping Delay Email to Customers. If the order itself has not been prepared, see How to Write an Order Delay Email to Customers.

2. When Should You Send a Delivery Delay Email?

Send the email when you have reliable information that the delivery window has materially changed. Communicate early enough to help the customer plan, but do not send a speculative warning based only on an unverified assumption.

  • The carrier reports a delay that affects the expected arrival date.
  • A verified delivery exception changes the original expectation.
  • A weather or service disruption affects the relevant route.
  • The promised delivery window has passed and the order needs investigation.
  • A revised estimate is available and the customer needs to know about it.

If no reliable update is available, explain that the date is not confirmed and give a next step only when your team can reasonably follow through. Avoid sending repeated emails that merely restate the same status.

3. Delivery Delay Email Subject Lines

Use a subject line that makes the purpose clear and helps the customer identify the relevant purchase.

  • Delivery Update for Order #[Order Number]
  • Important Update About Your Delivery
  • Your Order Is Taking Longer Than Expected
  • Revised Delivery Estimate for Order #[Order Number]
  • An Update About Your Upcoming Delivery
  • We Have an Update on Your Order

Avoid misleading urgency, vague subjects, or language that implies a parcel is lost when that has not been confirmed.

4. Delivery Delay Email Templates

These examples are starting points. Replace every bracketed field with accurate information and follow your store's published policies.

Template A: A revised delivery estimate is available

Subject: Delivery Update for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We are contacting you with an update about order #[Order Number]. Your delivery is taking longer than originally expected.

Based on the latest available information, your package is currently expected to arrive around [Revised Date]. This is an estimate and may change as the shipment progresses.

You can check the latest status here: [Verified Tracking Link].

We apologize for the inconvenience. If you need assistance, contact us at [Support Email].

Thank you for your patience,
[Business Name] Customer Support

Template B: The new delivery date is not confirmed

Subject: An Update About Your Delayed Delivery

Hi [Customer Name],

Your order #[Order Number] has been delayed, and we do not yet have a confirmed new delivery date.

We are checking the latest shipment information. We will contact you again when we have a reliable update. If tracking is available, you can review the current status here: [Verified Tracking Link].

We understand that delays can be frustrating and are sorry for the inconvenience. Please contact [Support Email] if you have questions.

Best regards,
[Business Name] Customer Support

Template C: Delivery requires customer action

Subject: Action May Be Needed for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We have an update about delivery for order #[Order Number]. The latest verified status indicates that [briefly describe the delivery issue].

[Explain the specific action required, such as checking the carrier's delivery options, only if that action is confirmed and applicable.] You can review the shipment here: [Verified Tracking Link].

If you need help, contact us at [Support Email].

Thank you,
[Business Name] Customer Support

5. How to Communicate a Revised Delivery Date

State the revised date in a way that matches the evidence available. If the carrier provides an estimate, attribute it to the carrier. Do not describe an estimate as a guarantee unless the business genuinely offers and can honor that guarantee.

When an estimate is available

“According to the latest carrier update, your package is currently expected to arrive by [Date]. This is the current estimate and may change.”

When no estimate is available

“We do not have a confirmed delivery date yet. We are checking the shipment status and will provide another update when reliable information becomes available.”

Before sending, confirm the date, time zone if relevant, destination, and whether the message refers to the whole order or only one package. Do not reuse a previous date that has already passed.

6. Explain Customer Options and Support

A delay message should answer the customer's most immediate question: “What should I do now?” The answer depends on your shipping arrangements, applicable law, and published business policies.

  • Provide a working tracking page when it is available.
  • Explain whether the customer needs to update address details or arrange a new delivery, but only when confirmed.
  • Offer a clear support contact for questions or unresolved issues.
  • Explain cancellation, refund, or alternative-delivery options only when they are actually available and accurately described.
  • If only one parcel is delayed, clarify the status of the other parcels in the order.

Do not invent a refund entitlement or promise compensation that your policy does not support. If the customer's options depend on local rules or the specific purchase terms, direct the customer to the appropriate policy or support team.

7. Automating Delivery Updates Responsibly

Automation can help a store send timely updates, but it should be built around accurate order and carrier data. A marketing email platform alone may not know the actual status of a shipment; confirm that the required ecommerce or shipping integration supplies the right event.

  1. Choose a meaningful, verified delay event as the trigger.
  2. Map the correct customer, order number, shipment, and delivery estimate.
  3. Prevent duplicate notifications for the same event.
  4. Use fallback wording when a reliable date is unavailable.
  5. Route missing or conflicting data to a person for review.
  6. Test the message on mobile devices and test every link before launch.

For broader workflow ideas, read Email Marketing Automation. If you are comparing email marketing platforms for appropriate follow-up workflows, you can explore GetResponse. Check its current features and integrations against your requirements; do not assume it replaces a carrier or order-management system.

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Promising a date without evidence

False certainty can create a second problem if the package misses the new date. Share a supported estimate or explain that the date is unknown.

Using a generic apology without information

An apology can show consideration, but it should accompany the order status, useful details, and next step.

Blaming a carrier without verification

Describe confirmed facts rather than speculating about the cause.

Leaving out the order number

Identify the affected order so the customer can connect the email to the right purchase.

Sending repeated messages with no new value

Send another update when the status changes meaningfully or when a promised check-in is due.

Making customer options unclear

Explain whether action is needed and where the customer can get help. Do not list options that are not actually available.

9. Pre-Send Quality Checklist

  • The order and affected shipment are correctly identified.
  • The delay status has been verified.
  • The stated cause is accurate or omitted if unknown.
  • The delivery date is current and supported by reliable information.
  • Estimates are not incorrectly presented as guarantees.
  • Tracking and support links work.
  • Customer actions and options are accurate.
  • Personalization fields have been tested.
  • Duplicate messages are prevented.
  • The email is readable on mobile devices.
  • The subject line accurately describes the message.
  • The message follows the store's policies and applicable requirements.

10. How to Evaluate Delivery Delay Emails

Evaluate both the reliability of the workflow and the usefulness of the message. Depending on the data your systems collect, review:

  • Delivery and bounce rates: whether the message reaches valid email addresses.
  • Support contacts about delivery status: whether customers still need basic clarification.
  • Tracking-link clicks: whether recipients use the status page provided.
  • Duplicate or incorrect notifications: whether the trigger and data mapping are reliable.
  • Time to notify: how quickly customers are informed after a meaningful delay is confirmed.

Interpret metrics in context. A customer support request may be legitimate even when the email is clear. Open tracking is also imperfect because privacy features can affect reported opens.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

What should a delivery delay email include?

Include the order number, a clear description of the delay, the latest reliable delivery estimate if available, tracking information when useful, the next step, and a support contact.

When should a customer be notified?

Notify the customer when reliable information shows that the expected delivery window has materially changed. Do not rely on an unverified assumption.

What if there is no new delivery date?

Say that the date is unconfirmed, share what is known, and explain the next update step only if your team can follow through.

Should I offer a refund in the email?

Only describe refund or cancellation options that are genuinely available under the applicable policy and terms. Do not make unsupported promises.

Can delivery delay emails be automated?

Yes, when reliable shipment data, meaningful triggers, duplicate prevention, and appropriate error handling are in place.

How often should I send delivery updates?

Send updates when the status materially changes or a promised follow-up is due. Avoid repetitive messages that do not help the customer.

Final Thoughts

A good delivery delay email combines accuracy, empathy, and a useful next step. Tell customers what has changed, distinguish confirmed facts from estimates, provide a working route to shipment information, and make support easy to reach.

Before automating the process, verify the source data and test the workflow for missing dates, duplicate events, and partially delivered orders. Clear communication helps customers make informed decisions and helps businesses avoid preventable confusion.
