How to Write a Delivery Delay Email to Customers

A delivery delay email tells a customer that their package will arrive later than originally expected. The most useful message explains what changed, provides the latest reliable delivery information, and tells the customer what to expect next.

Quick Answer:

To write a delivery delay email, clearly state that the delivery is delayed, identify the order, provide the latest reliable delivery estimate if available, explain what happens next, and give the customer a way to get help. If there is no confirmed new date, say so rather than making an uncertain promise.

Delivery Delay Email Template

If you need a customer-ready message quickly, adapt this template with accurate order and delivery information.

Subject: Update on your delivery for order #1847

Hi [Customer Name],

We wanted to let you know that your order #1847 is experiencing a delivery delay.

Your order was originally expected to arrive on [Original Date]. Based on the latest available information, delivery is now expected around [New Date].

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience. We will provide another update if the delivery information changes.

If you have questions about your order, please contact us at [Support Email].

Thank you,
[Business Name] Customer Support

What Is a Delivery Delay Email?

A delivery delay email is a customer communication sent when an order is expected to arrive later than the original delivery estimate.

The delay may result from carrier problems, transportation disruptions, weather, fulfillment issues, address problems, or other circumstances. The email should describe only what the business can reasonably confirm.

A delivery delay message is focused on the customer's expected arrival. This makes it different from a general order delay or a message about a product being on backorder.

When Should You Send a Delivery Delay Email?

Send the email when the business has reliable information that the expected delivery date has changed or the shipment is unlikely to arrive on time.

If the original delivery date has already passed, communicate the delay as soon as practical rather than waiting for the customer to contact support.

You do not need to send a separate message for every minor tracking event. The goal is to communicate meaningful changes that affect the customer's expectations.

What to Include in the Email

A useful delivery delay email should answer the customer's main questions quickly.

  • Which order is affected?
  • What has changed?
  • When is delivery now expected?
  • Is the shipment still moving?
  • Will another update be sent?
  • How can the customer get help?

Depending on the situation, you may also include a tracking link, carrier information, or instructions for contacting customer support.

How to Write a Delivery Delay Email Step by Step

1. State the delay immediately

The first sentence should make the situation clear.

Example:

Your order #1847 is experiencing a delivery delay and will arrive later than originally expected.

2. Identify the order

Include the order number and, when useful, the affected product. This helps customers quickly understand which purchase the email refers to.

3. Explain what changed

Give a short explanation if you have reliable information about the cause. Customers generally need a useful explanation rather than a detailed account of internal operations.

4. Provide the latest delivery information

If a new delivery date or timeframe is available, include it clearly.

5. Explain what happens next

Tell customers whether the shipment is still moving and whether another update will be provided.

6. Make support easy to reach

Give customers a clear way to ask questions if the delay affects their plans.

How to Explain a New Delivery Date

When a reliable new delivery date is available, make it easy to find.

Your package was originally expected to arrive on October 5. Based on the latest carrier update, it is now expected to arrive around October 8.

If the date comes from a carrier estimate, make that clear. A carrier estimate should not automatically be presented as a guaranteed promise from the store.

What to Say When There Is No Confirmed Date

Sometimes the business does not have a reliable new delivery date. In that situation, avoid inventing a date simply to make the email sound complete.

Example:

We do not have a confirmed delivery date yet. We are monitoring the shipment and will send another update when reliable information becomes available.

Honest uncertainty is better than a specific estimate that the business may have to correct later.

Delivery Delay Email Subject Lines

A good subject line tells the customer why the email matters.

  • Update on your delivery for order #1847
  • Your delivery has been delayed
  • Important update about your delivery
  • New delivery update for your order
  • Update: Your order will arrive later than expected
  • Delivery update for order #1847

Avoid vague subjects such as "An Update About Your Order" when the customer needs to know immediately that delivery has been delayed.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a U.S. customer orders a $74.99 product from an online store. The original delivery estimate is October 5, but a carrier update indicates that the package is now expected around October 8.

A useful email would clearly state the delay, identify the order, provide the updated estimate, and explain that the date is based on the latest available shipping information.

The exact message should reflect the store's actual shipping policy and the information available for that shipment.

How to Handle Tracking Information

If tracking information is available, include it when it helps the customer understand the shipment's current status.

Keep the tracking link easy to find. Customers should not have to search through a long message to locate it.

Make sure the tracking destination is the correct shipment page. Do not use broken, placeholder, or unrelated links.

Customer Support and Next Steps

A delivery delay can matter more to one customer than another. Someone may be waiting for a routine purchase, while another customer may need the package for a specific event or deadline.

Make the next step clear. Depending on the business and its policies, that may mean checking tracking information, contacting support, changing an order, or simply waiting for the next update.

Avoid offering cancellation, refunds, replacements, or other options unless those options are actually available under the business's policies.

Automating Delivery Delay Emails

Delivery delay messages can be automated when an order or shipping system receives a meaningful status change.

Automation can help a business communicate consistently, but the trigger should use reliable data. Sending a customer a delay notice because of a temporary or incorrect tracking status can create unnecessary confusion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Being vague

Customers should not have to guess whether their package is delayed. State the delivery problem clearly.

Promising an uncertain date

Do not turn an estimate into a guarantee when the delivery is outside the business's direct control.

Giving too much internal detail

A long explanation of warehouse, transportation, or carrier operations usually does not help the customer decide what to do next.

Forgetting the order number

The customer should be able to identify the affected order immediately.

Sending repeated messages without useful updates

Communication should be meaningful. Avoid sending multiple emails that provide no new information.

Failing to update the customer again

If the first email says another update will follow, make sure the business actually provides it when new reliable information becomes available.

Delivery Delay Email Checklist

  • Clearly state that delivery is delayed.
  • Include the correct order number.
  • Identify the affected product or shipment.
  • Explain the relevant reason when reliable information exists.
  • Provide the latest delivery estimate when available.
  • Clearly communicate uncertainty when there is no confirmed date.
  • Include tracking information when useful.
  • Explain what happens next.
  • Use a clear subject line.
  • Provide a customer support contact.
  • Verify order and shipment information before sending.
  • Use reliable triggers for automated messages.

Final Thoughts

A delivery delay email should make the situation easier to understand, not more confusing. Tell customers what changed, identify their order, provide the latest reliable information, and explain what happens next.

If there is no confirmed delivery date, communicate that honestly. A useful delay email does not need to be long; it needs to answer the customer's most important questions clearly and at the right time.
