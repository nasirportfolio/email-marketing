How to Write a Package Lost Email to Customers

When a customer's package appears to be lost, silence can create more frustration than the shipping problem itself. A clear email should acknowledge the situation, explain what is known, and give the customer a practical next step.

The goal is not to make the shipping problem sound smaller than it is. The goal is to communicate honestly while making the customer's options easy to understand.

Quick Answer: A package lost email should briefly acknowledge the problem, identify the affected order, explain the current tracking situation, apologize for the inconvenience, and clearly tell the customer what happens next. If your business can offer a replacement, refund, investigation, or other resolution, explain the available option without making promises you cannot keep.

Package Lost Email Template

Here is a practical template you can adapt for an ecommerce store or other business that ships physical products.

Subject: An Update About Your Order [Order Number]

Hello [Customer Name],

We are sorry to let you know that there appears to be a problem with the delivery of your order [Order Number].

The tracking information for your package has not updated as expected, and we are currently treating the shipment as potentially lost.

We understand how frustrating this can be, especially when you are waiting for an order. We are reviewing the shipment and will help you with the next step.

What happens next:
[Explain the available resolution, such as a replacement, refund, carrier investigation, or additional delivery instructions.]

You can review your order details here: View Your Order

If you have any questions, please contact us at [Support Email].

Thank you for your patience,
[Company Name] Customer Support

What Is a Package Lost Email?

A package lost email is a customer communication sent when an order appears to have been lost during shipping or when the available delivery information strongly suggests that the shipment may no longer be moving toward the customer.

The exact situation can vary. A carrier may show no recent tracking movement, the package may be delayed beyond the expected delivery period, or a shipping investigation may indicate that the shipment cannot currently be located.

Because the status may not always be confirmed immediately, use precise language. If you only know that the package is suspected to be lost, do not state that the carrier has definitely lost it unless that has been confirmed.

When Should You Send It?

Timing depends on your shipping process, carrier information, expected delivery window, and customer-support policy.

You should generally communicate when the available information gives you a reasonable basis to believe that the package requires attention rather than continuing to wait without explanation.

Before sending the message, verify the order number, tracking information, shipping address, latest carrier update, and any relevant support case.

Do Not Send Too Early

A shipment can sometimes appear inactive while still moving through the carrier network. If the package is still within a normal delivery window, a lost-package message may create unnecessary concern.

Do Not Wait Without Communicating

If the situation has clearly become unusual and the customer is likely to be wondering where the order is, an informative update can be better than leaving the customer to repeatedly check tracking information.

What to Include in the Email

A useful package lost email should answer the customer's most immediate questions.

  • Which order is affected?
  • What is happening with the shipment?
  • What does the current tracking information show?
  • Has the shipment been confirmed as lost or is it still under investigation?
  • What will the business do next?
  • What options are available to the customer?
  • How can the customer contact support?

Keep the explanation factual. Customers usually need clarity more than a long description of the internal shipping process.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Start With a Clear Update

Tell the customer why you are contacting them. Avoid vague openings such as “We wanted to reach out regarding your order.”

A stronger opening identifies the issue directly:

“We are contacting you because the tracking information for your order has not updated as expected.”

2. Identify the Order

Include the order number so the customer immediately knows which purchase is affected.

If appropriate, you can also include the product name or a link to the order-status page.

3. Explain What You Know

Explain the current status without exaggerating certainty.

For example, if a carrier investigation is still in progress, say that rather than telling the customer the package is permanently lost.

4. Acknowledge the Inconvenience

A short, genuine apology can make the message feel more human.

Avoid excessive apologies or emotional language. One clear acknowledgment is usually enough.

5. Explain the Resolution

The most important part of the email is what happens next.

Depending on your business policy, this could involve a replacement, refund, carrier investigation, or another appropriate resolution.

6. Give the Customer a Way to Get Help

Provide a working support channel. If the customer needs to reply to the email, say so clearly.

How to Explain Tracking Information

Tracking information can be confusing because customers may see technical carrier statuses that do not clearly explain what is happening.

Your email should translate the relevant information into plain language.

Instead of copying several tracking events into the email, summarize the important point and provide the tracking link when it is useful.

For example:

“The carrier's tracking record has not shown a new movement since [Date]. We are reviewing the shipment and will update you once we have more information.”

This approach gives the customer useful context without overwhelming them with technical tracking history.

Replacement or Refund Options

If your business offers a replacement or refund, explain the option clearly and according to your actual policy.

Do not promise a replacement, refund, or delivery date if your business has not approved that resolution.

Replacement

If you can send another item, tell the customer what they need to do and what happens after the replacement is approved.

Refund

If a refund is available, explain the applicable process and avoid promising an exact arrival time unless your payment process supports that information.

Investigation

If the carrier investigation is still underway, tell the customer what stage the process has reached and when they can expect another update if you have a defined support process.

Package Lost Email Subject Lines

The subject line should make the purpose of the email clear without using unnecessary alarm.

  • Update About Your Order [Order Number]
  • Important Update About Your Shipment
  • An Update on Your Package
  • We Are Looking Into Your Shipment
  • Shipping Update for Order [Order Number]
  • Your Order Requires a Delivery Update

Choose the subject line that accurately reflects the situation. If the package has not been officially confirmed as lost, avoid wording that presents the situation as certain.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Consider a hypothetical U.S. online store that shipped a $59.99 product to a customer. The carrier tracking page has not shown movement for several days beyond the store's expected delivery window.

Instead of simply telling the customer to contact the carrier, the store could explain that it has noticed the tracking issue and is taking responsibility for helping resolve it.

Subject: An Update About Your Shipment

Hello Jamie,

We are sorry that your order has not arrived as expected.

The tracking information for your shipment has not updated since [Date], so we are reviewing the delivery with the carrier.

We understand that you were expecting your order and appreciate your patience while we work through the issue.

We will [explain the actual next step according to your store policy].

If you have questions, simply reply to this email and our support team will help.

Thank you,
[Store Name] Customer Support

This is a hypothetical example. The actual resolution should always follow the store's policies and the facts of the shipment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Saying the Package Is Definitely Lost Too Early

If the shipment is only suspected to be lost, use accurate wording such as “appears to be delayed” or “we are investigating the shipment.”

2. Making the Customer Do All the Work

Simply telling the customer to contact the carrier can create frustration. Explain what your business is doing as well.

3. Providing No Resolution

A message that only says “your package may be lost” leaves the customer wondering what to do next. Include the next step whenever possible.

4. Using Excessive Technical Language

Internal carrier terminology may not help customers understand the situation. Translate tracking information into plain language.

5. Promising an Uncertain Delivery Date

Do not provide a new delivery date unless you have reliable information supporting it.

6. Ignoring the Customer's Previous Experience

If the customer has already contacted support about the same order, review that conversation before sending an automated message. Repeating information the customer already provided can make the experience worse.

Package Lost Email Checklist

Before sending the email, check that you have:

  • Confirmed the correct order number.
  • Checked the latest tracking information.
  • Used accurate language about the shipment status.
  • Explained the problem clearly.
  • Acknowledged the inconvenience.
  • Explained what your business is doing next.
  • Provided an available resolution when appropriate.
  • Included a working order or tracking link when useful.
  • Provided a clear support contact.
  • Avoided unsupported promises.
  • Checked that the message works well on mobile devices.

Final Thoughts

A package lost email should make a difficult situation easier to understand. Customers need an honest explanation, useful order information, and a clear idea of what happens next.

Keep the message concise, avoid unsupported promises, and take responsibility for explaining the available resolution. When the situation is uncertain, communicate what you know rather than pretending to know more.

Good shipping communication does not remove every delivery problem, but it can make the customer experience more transparent and easier to manage.
