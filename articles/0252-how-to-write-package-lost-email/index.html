How to Write a Package Lost Email to Customers

When a package appears to be lost, customers need more than a tracking link. They need an honest explanation of what is known, what the business is doing, and what will happen next. A useful email acknowledges the inconvenience, identifies the order, and gives a realistic next step without making promises the business cannot keep.

Quick answer:

A package-lost email should identify the order, explain the current tracking status, distinguish a suspected loss from a confirmed loss, acknowledge the inconvenience, and outline the next step. Depending on the facts and store policy, that step may be a carrier investigation, a later update, a replacement, or a refund.

Package Lost Email Template

Use this template when the shipment needs investigation. Replace every bracketed field with verified information before sending.

Subject: An update about order [Order Number]

Hello [Customer Name],

We are sorry that your order [Order Number] has not arrived as expected. The latest tracking information shows [Brief, Verified Status], and we are checking the shipment with the carrier.

You can review the current tracking details here: [Verified Tracking Link]. We will [Describe the actual next step and, if your process supports it, when the customer can expect another update].

If you have questions, please contact us at [Support Contact] and include your order number so we can locate the shipment.

Thank you for your patience,
[Business Name] Customer Support

What Is a Package-Lost Email?

A package-lost email is a customer-service message used when a shipment cannot be located or its tracking history suggests that it may be lost. A stalled tracking page alone does not always prove a package is lost: scans can be delayed, and shipments may move between facilities without an immediate public update.

Use language that matches the evidence. If an investigation is underway, say that the shipment is being investigated. State that a package is confirmed lost only when the carrier or your own process has established that status.

When Should You Send It?

Send an update when the delivery is outside the expected window or the shipment status gives you a reasonable basis to investigate. Follow your carrier's procedures and your store's customer-service policy; there is no single waiting period that applies to every carrier, service, or destination.

Before contacting the customer, check the order number, shipment tracking, expected delivery window, latest carrier event, and any existing support conversation. This avoids sending a premature alert or asking customers to repeat information they have already supplied.

Communicate while investigating

If the investigation takes time, tell the customer what is happening and what they can reasonably expect next. Give a follow-up date only if your team can meet it. A short, accurate update is more useful than silence or a speculative delivery promise.

What Information Should It Include?

  • The order number and affected shipment.
  • The current, verified tracking status.
  • Whether the package is suspected to be lost or confirmed lost.
  • A brief acknowledgment of the inconvenience.
  • The action the business is taking.
  • The available resolution or the process for deciding one.
  • A working tracking link and support contact, when useful.
  • A realistic follow-up expectation, if known.

Do not include internal carrier jargon, unnecessary personal details, or a long list of tracking events. Summarize the relevant information in plain language.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. State the issue directly

Use a clear opening such as: “We are contacting you because your order has not arrived within the expected delivery window.” Avoid vague phrases that make customers guess why you are writing.

2. Identify the order

Include the order number and, where useful, the product or shipment reference. Check the details before sending so the customer does not receive information about another order.

3. Explain what is known

Describe the latest verified status. If the carrier has not confirmed a loss, avoid saying the package is definitely lost. Be transparent about uncertainty without making the message sound evasive.

4. Acknowledge the inconvenience

A short, sincere apology recognizes the impact on the customer. Do not overstate what happened or blame the carrier, customer, or warehouse before the facts are clear.

5. Explain the next step

Tell the customer whether you are opening a carrier investigation, checking fulfillment records, reviewing a replacement, or processing an approved refund. Only mention options that your business can actually provide.

6. Make it easy to get help

Give a working support channel and explain which details to include. If the customer can reply directly to the email, say so only when replies are monitored.

How to Explain Tracking Status

Tracking information may show a delay, an exception, or no recent scan. Translate the status into ordinary language rather than copying technical carrier messages without explanation.

Example:

“The latest tracking update was recorded on [Date]. We have not yet received confirmation of the package's location, so we are checking with the carrier. We will share another update when we have verified information.”

Use this wording only if it accurately describes the shipment. Link to the real tracking page and test the link before sending. Never send a placeholder tracking URL to a customer.

Replacement, Refund, or Investigation

The right resolution depends on the shipment facts, the customer's circumstances, and the store's stated policy. Explain the process clearly and avoid suggesting that a particular outcome has been approved when it has not.

Carrier investigation

If the shipment is still being traced, explain that the investigation is in progress and describe the next communication step. Do not promise a result or completion date unless it is supported by your process.

Replacement

If a replacement is approved, tell the customer what will happen next. Share a new dispatch estimate only when the fulfillment team can support it, and distinguish dispatch from delivery.

Refund

If a refund is available and approved, explain how it will be initiated and where the customer can find relevant status information. Avoid guaranteeing an exact time for funds to appear unless your payment provider and policy support that claim.

Let the customer know what choices exist

If your policy allows the customer to choose between a replacement and a refund, state the available choices and how to request one. Do not offer a choice that is unavailable for the order.

Package Lost Email Subject Line Examples

  • Update about order [Order Number]
  • An update on your shipment
  • We are checking the status of your package
  • Shipping update for order [Order Number]
  • Next steps for your delayed shipment
  • Resolution update for order [Order Number]

Choose a subject that reflects the actual status. Avoid alarming wording when a package is merely delayed or under investigation, and do not imply that the customer must act if no action is required.

Worked Ecommerce Example

Imagine an online store receives no new tracking scan for a customer's order after the expected delivery window. The store checks its order records, confirms the shipment has not been marked delivered, and contacts the carrier for clarification. The store should not immediately claim that the package is permanently lost.

Subject: An update about your shipment

Hello [Customer Name],

We are sorry your order [Order Number] has not arrived as expected. The tracking page has not shown a new update since [Verified Date], so we have contacted the carrier to check the shipment's status.

You can review the latest tracking information here: [Verified Tracking Link]. We will contact you again when we have a meaningful update. If our investigation confirms that a resolution is needed, we will explain the options available under our policy.

If you have questions, reply to this email or contact [Support Contact].

Thank you,
[Business Name] Customer Support

This is an illustrative example, not a promise that every store can offer the same process. Adjust the message to match your actual tracking data and customer-service policy.

Automating Package-Loss Updates

Email automation can help stores send consistent shipping updates, but a message about a potentially lost package should be triggered by reliable data and appropriate business rules. A single missing scan should not automatically be treated as proof of loss.

Consider these safeguards:

  • Use confirmed carrier events or a defined exception rule.
  • Check that the order has not already been delivered or resolved.
  • Prevent duplicate alerts for the same shipment event.
  • Use accurate order and tracking data.
  • Route uncertain cases to a human support review.
  • Record when the next customer update is due, if your workflow supports it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Declaring a package lost too early

A lack of recent tracking movement may require investigation but does not always prove loss. Describe the status accurately.

Making the customer do all the work

Do not simply tell the customer to contact the carrier if your business can reasonably investigate or explain the next step.

Offering an unapproved resolution

Do not promise a replacement, refund, or delivery date before confirming that it is available and approved.

Using technical or vague language

Explain the important status in plain English and identify what the customer should expect next.

Ignoring earlier support messages

Review the customer's previous contact before sending an automated response. Repeating questions or instructions can make a frustrating experience worse.

Forgetting to follow up

If you tell the customer that you will provide another update, assign responsibility for doing so. Do not promise a specific date unless you can reasonably meet it.

Package Lost Email Pre-Send Checklist

  • Confirm the correct order and shipment.
  • Review the latest tracking information.
  • Distinguish suspected loss from confirmed loss.
  • Explain the issue in plain language.
  • Acknowledge the inconvenience.
  • Describe the action your business is taking.
  • Offer only resolutions permitted by your policy.
  • Test tracking and support links.
  • Remove every sample value and placeholder.
  • Check mobile readability and email formatting.
  • Prevent duplicate automated messages.
  • Assign ownership for any promised follow-up.

Final Thoughts

A package-lost email should help the customer understand the situation and the next step. Identify the order, explain verified information, acknowledge the inconvenience, and make the available resolution process clear.

Accuracy matters more than dramatic wording. When a shipment is still under investigation, communicate what is known, explain what your business is doing, and follow through on any update you promise.
