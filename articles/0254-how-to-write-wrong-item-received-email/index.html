How to Write a Wrong Item Received Email to Customers

Receiving the wrong product can frustrate a customer, especially when the item was purchased for a particular occasion or purpose. A helpful email should acknowledge the mistake, confirm which order is affected, and explain the next step without making the customer navigate an unclear support process.

Whether the customer received the wrong size, color, model, or completely different product, your message should be accurate, empathetic, and consistent with your store's actual replacement, return, and refund policies.

Quick answer: A wrong-item email should apologize, identify the order and incorrect product, explain whether additional information is needed, and state what happens next. Promise a replacement, return, or refund only when that option is available and authorized.

Wrong Item Received Email Template

Use this template when a customer reports receiving an item different from what they ordered. Adapt the resolution to your actual policy.

Subject: We’re Sorry You Received the Wrong Item

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that you received the wrong item in order [Order Number]. I understand how inconvenient this must be, and we appreciate you letting us know.

We’re reviewing the order details so we can help resolve the issue. If possible, please reply with the name of the item you received. If our process requires it, you may also include a photo of the item and its packaging.

We’ll review the information and explain the next step under our store policy, which may include a replacement, return, or refund where applicable.

Thank you for your patience. We’re sorry for the inconvenience.

Best regards,
[Support Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is a Wrong Item Received Email?

A wrong-item received email is a customer-service message used when the product delivered does not match the customer's order. The discrepancy might involve a different model, size, color, quantity, or product altogether.

The message can be a reply to a customer's complaint or an update sent after the business discovers a fulfillment error. In either case, its purpose is to make the next steps clear and reduce unnecessary back-and-forth.

What to Do Before Replying

Before sending a template, check the order record and the customer's message. A few quick checks can prevent the support team from asking for information it already has or offering a solution that cannot be fulfilled.

  • Confirm the order number and products purchased.
  • Compare the ordered product with the item reported as received.
  • Check whether the customer has already supplied photographs or other details.
  • Review inventory and the applicable return, replacement, and refund policy.
  • Check whether a previous support agent has already approved a resolution.

If the facts are unclear, ask one specific question rather than sending a long list of requests.

What to Include in the Email

A useful response usually includes these elements:

  • A relevant subject line: Make the purpose of the message easy to understand.
  • Order identification: Include the order number or another appropriate identifier.
  • A clear acknowledgment: State that the customer received an item different from what they ordered.
  • An apology: Recognize the inconvenience without blaming the customer.
  • A focused request: Ask for missing information only when it is genuinely needed.
  • A realistic resolution: Explain the available next step based on verified details.
  • A support option: Tell the customer how to reply or contact your team.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Acknowledge the Problem

Start with a direct statement such as, “I’m sorry you received an item different from the one you ordered.” This immediately addresses the customer's concern.

2. Identify the Order

Use the order number and product details to reduce confusion. Avoid including unnecessary personal information in the email.

3. Show Empathy

A short acknowledgment of the inconvenience is enough. The customer usually needs a clear solution more than a lengthy explanation of the company's internal process.

4. Request Only Necessary Information

If the support team cannot identify the incorrect product from the existing record, ask the customer to describe it. Request a photograph only if it will help verify the issue or is required by the applicable process.

5. Explain the Available Resolution

State whether the next step is a replacement, return, refund, or further review. Confirm availability and authorization before making a firm commitment.

6. Give a Clear Next Step

Tell the customer whether to reply, wait for an update, or follow a return instruction. If a timeframe is known, give a realistic estimate rather than an unsupported guarantee.

Email Templates for Different Situations

Template 1: Wrong Color or Size

Subject: We’re Sorry About the Incorrect Item in Your Order

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m sorry the [Product Name] you received was the wrong [color/size]. We understand that this is not what you expected.

We’re checking the order details and available options. We’ll explain the appropriate next step under our store policy as soon as the review is complete.

Thank you for bringing this to our attention.

Best,
[Support Representative]

Template 2: Completely Different Product

Subject: Help With the Wrong Item in Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry you received a different product from the one listed in your order. We understand how inconvenient this is.

We’re reviewing the order and fulfillment details. If you have not already shared this information, please tell us the name of the item you received. We’ll let you know if any further information is needed.

Once we confirm the details, we’ll explain the available resolution and next steps.

Thank you for your patience,
[Company Support Team]

Template 3: Replacement Approved

Subject: Replacement Update for Order #[Order Number]

Hello [Customer Name],

Thank you for giving us the details about the incorrect item. We’ve approved a replacement for [Product Name].

[Include the confirmed processing or shipping information here.] We’ll send the relevant tracking details when they become available.

We apologize for the original fulfillment error and appreciate your patience.

Best regards,
[Support Representative]

Template 4: Return or Refund Review Required

Subject: Next Steps for Your Incorrect Item

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that the item delivered did not match your order. We’re reviewing the details so we can determine the appropriate resolution under our policy.

If a return is required, we’ll explain the instructions and any applicable shipping arrangements before you send the item back.

We’ll update you when the next step has been confirmed.

Thank you,
[Customer Support Team]

Wrong Item Received Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject line that matches the actual stage of the case.

  • We’re Sorry You Received the Wrong Item
  • Help With the Item in Order #[Order Number]
  • Next Steps for Your Incorrect Item
  • Update About Your Recent Order
  • Replacement Update for Order #[Order Number]
  • We’ve Received Your Wrong-Item Report
  • Resolution for Your Incorrect Order Item

Keep the subject line specific and professional. Avoid implying that a replacement has shipped or a refund has been issued unless that action has actually occurred.

Requesting Photos or Details

Photos can help verify a fulfillment error, but the business should not request them automatically if the order record already provides sufficient evidence. Explain what is needed and why.

Example request:

“To help us verify the issue, could you please reply with the name of the item you received? If convenient, a photo of the item or its product label may also help us identify the discrepancy. Thank you for helping us review this.”

Privacy reminder: Request only information relevant to the case. Customers should not be asked to share payment-card details, passwords, or other sensitive information to resolve an ordinary order discrepancy.

Replacement, Return, or Refund

The right resolution depends on the customer's circumstances, your inventory, and your actual store policy. Avoid presenting one option as guaranteed before the case has been reviewed.

Replacement

Check stock and fulfillment availability before promising a replacement. Explain the expected processing or shipping timing only when you have reliable information.

Return

If the incorrect item must be returned, provide clear instructions, including the return method and who is responsible for shipping costs under your policy. Do not leave the customer guessing about whether they should ship the item before receiving instructions.

Refund

If a refund is approved, explain when it will be initiated and distinguish the business's processing time from the time the payment provider or bank takes to post the funds.

When the Correct Item Is Out of Stock

Be transparent about availability. Explain the alternatives that are actually available, such as waiting for restock, selecting an eligible alternative, or requesting a refund where permitted by policy. Do not offer a substitute without the customer's agreement when their choice is required.

Customer-Service Automation

Automation can help organize wrong-item reports and follow-up messages. It should support the service team rather than send inaccurate or contradictory updates.

  1. Record the customer's report and order number.
  2. Check whether the reported discrepancy is already documented.
  3. Request missing information only if needed.
  4. Route the case to the appropriate support workflow.
  5. Send a resolution message after the action has been confirmed.
  6. Follow up if the case remains open and an update is due.

Mistakes to Avoid

Blaming the Customer

Do not assume the customer selected the wrong item. Review the order and fulfillment details first.

Asking for Repeated Information

Read the original message and inspect the available order record before requesting details again.

Making an Unverified Promise

Do not guarantee a delivery date, refund date, or replacement until the relevant action and timing have been confirmed.

Using a Vague Response

“We are looking into it” is incomplete without a clear next step. Explain what is being reviewed and how the customer will receive an update.

Ignoring the Case After the First Reply

Assign responsibility for follow-up so that the customer is not left waiting after supplying the requested information.

Wrong Item Email Pre-Send Checklist

  • Confirm the customer's order and the incorrect item.
  • Acknowledge the inconvenience and apologize appropriately.
  • Check the conversation history for information already supplied.
  • Request only necessary details or photographs.
  • Verify replacement availability and resolution approval.
  • Explain return instructions if a return is required.
  • Provide a realistic timeframe where known.
  • Tell the customer what happens next.
  • Use a clear, relevant subject line.
  • Proofread the message and check mobile readability.

Final Thoughts

A good wrong-item received email combines empathy with a practical resolution process. Confirm what happened, ask only for information that is still needed, and explain the next step in plain language.

Use templates to maintain consistency, but personalize every response to the customer's order and actual situation. Clear communication can reduce repeat messages and help customers understand what to expect.
