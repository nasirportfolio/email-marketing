How to Write a Missing Item in Order Email to Customers

When a customer receives an order and discovers that one of the items is missing, they need a clear answer quickly. A useful customer service email should acknowledge the problem, confirm the order details, explain what happened when known, and tell the customer exactly what will happen next.

Quick Answer: A missing-item email should identify the missing product, apologize for the incomplete order, confirm the order details, explain the replacement or refund process, and provide realistic next steps. Keep the message specific to the customer's actual order instead of sending a generic response.

Missing Item in Order Email Template

Subject: We’re Sorry About the Missing Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the [Missing Product]. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.

We checked your order and confirmed that the missing item was included in your original purchase.

We’ll arrange to send the missing item to you [insert the actual next step or timing].

Once the replacement shipment is processed, we’ll provide [tracking information / the next update], according to our normal process.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience while we correct the issue.

If you have any questions, please reply to this email and our support team will be happy to help.

Best,
[Customer Service Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is a Missing Item Email?

A missing item email is a customer service message used when an order arrives without one or more products that the customer purchased.

For example, a customer may order three products but receive only two. The missing product might have been left out during packing, separated into another shipment, or affected by another fulfillment issue.

The email should not assume the cause until the order and fulfillment records have been checked. The priority is to confirm the situation and give the customer a clear resolution.

Current ecommerce customer-service guidance recommends gathering the relevant order information, personalizing the response, acknowledging the customer's frustration, and clearly explaining what the business can do next. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

When to Send the Email

Send the response after reviewing the customer's report and checking the order information. If the missing item is confirmed, explain the next action as soon as practical.

If the order may have been split into multiple shipments, check the shipment details before promising a replacement. A customer may receive different parts of an order at different times.

If you still need information from the customer, ask for only what is necessary to investigate the issue. Avoid making the customer repeat information already available in the order record.

What to Check Before Responding

Before sending the email, review the order carefully. Useful details to verify include:

  • Order number
  • Customer name
  • Products originally purchased
  • Quantity ordered for each product
  • Items marked as fulfilled
  • Whether the order was split into multiple shipments
  • Available tracking information
  • Any previous customer-service communication
  • Your actual replacement and refund policy

An itemized order record is especially useful because it lets a business compare what was ordered with what was actually fulfilled. Packing slips can also contain itemized product and quantity information that helps with order verification. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Practical tip: Do not immediately assume the item was forgotten during packing. First check whether it was shipped separately or whether the fulfillment record shows a different status.

What to Include in the Email

A useful missing-item email should normally include:

  • The customer's name
  • The order number
  • The missing product
  • The quantity missing, when relevant
  • A clear apology
  • A brief explanation when the cause is confirmed
  • The replacement, refund, or other available resolution
  • Shipping or tracking information when available
  • Return instructions if they are actually required
  • A clear way to contact support if the issue continues

The customer should finish reading the message knowing what happened, what the business is doing, and whether they need to do anything themselves.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Acknowledge the Missing Item

Start by identifying the problem directly. Avoid opening with a long explanation about your fulfillment process.

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the Wireless Charging Stand. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.

2. Confirm the Order

Tell the customer that you checked the order when you have actually verified it. Mention the missing product and, when useful, the order number.

We checked order [Order Number] and confirmed that the [Product Name] was included in your original purchase.

3. Explain What Happens Next

Give the customer a specific next step. If the missing product will be shipped separately, say so. If your team needs additional information first, explain what you need.

We’ll arrange to send the missing item and will provide an update once the shipment has been processed.

4. Avoid Unsupported Promises

Do not promise overnight shipping, a guaranteed delivery date, a free upgrade, or an immediate refund unless the business has confirmed that option.

A realistic statement is better than a specific promise that may later change.

Explain the Replacement Process

If the missing item will be sent, explain how the replacement process works. The customer should know whether the business is arranging the shipment automatically or whether the customer needs to confirm anything first.

Replacement example

We’ve confirmed the missing item and will arrange for the [Product Name] to be shipped separately. Once the shipment is processed, we’ll send the available tracking information.

If the product is temporarily unavailable, explain the actual options your business offers, such as waiting for restock, selecting an approved alternative, or receiving a refund.

When a Refund May Be Used

A refund may be appropriate when the missing product cannot be shipped or when your store's policy provides a refund for an incomplete order.

The email should explain the actual refund process rather than inventing a processing time.

We’re unable to send the missing item at this time, so we can process a refund for that item according to our refund policy. We’ll confirm once the refund has been submitted.

If the business offers both replacement and refund options, explain the available choices clearly and tell the customer how to select one.

Explain Shipping and Tracking Clearly

If the missing item is being shipped separately, provide tracking information when it is available. If tracking is not available yet, say that clearly rather than inserting a placeholder link into the finished customer email.

For stores using Shopify, order and shipping emails can include order-status information, depending on how the store's notification templates are configured. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Your replacement shipment has been processed. The tracking information is available through your order update email.

Replace this wording with your store's actual tracking process before using the template.

Missing Item Email Subject Lines

Here are practical subject-line options:

  • We’re Sorry About the Missing Item in Your Order
  • We’re Fixing the Missing Item in Your Order
  • Update About Your Order
  • Your Missing Item — Next Steps
  • We’ve Confirmed the Missing Item
  • Replacement Update for Your Order
  • About Your Incomplete Order

Choose the subject that accurately reflects the situation. Avoid creating unnecessary urgency or making the customer think a promotional message is being sent.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a customer in the United States ordered three products for $89.99, but the package arrived with only two of the products. The store checks the order and confirms that the third item was part of the original purchase.

Subject: We’re Fixing the Missing Item in Your Order

Hi James,

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the USB-C Charging Hub. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.

We checked your order and confirmed that the charging hub was included in your original purchase.

We’ll arrange to send the missing item separately. Once the shipment is processed, we’ll provide the available tracking information.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience while we correct the issue.

If you have any questions, please reply to this email and our support team will be happy to help.

Best,
Customer Support
[Company Name]

This is a hypothetical example. A real business should replace the product, price, order information, shipping process, and customer details with accurate information from the actual order.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending a generic apology

“Sorry for the inconvenience” is not enough by itself. Tell the customer which item is missing and what you are doing about it.

2. Failing to check for split shipments

Some orders may be fulfilled in more than one shipment. Check the order and shipment records before deciding that an item was actually omitted.

3. Asking for information you already have

If the customer's order number and product information are already available, use them. Avoid making the customer repeat information unnecessarily.

4. Blaming the warehouse or shipping carrier

Customers usually need a solution rather than an internal explanation of who made the mistake. Keep internal blame out of the message unless a specific explanation is genuinely useful.

5. Making promises you cannot guarantee

Avoid unsupported promises about delivery dates, refund timing, free upgrades, or replacement shipping. Use confirmed information only.

6. Forgetting the customer's next step

If the customer needs to confirm an address, choose an option, return an item, or provide information, say exactly what they need to do.

7. Failing to personalize the template

A template should save time without making the customer feel like they received an automated response. Include the customer's name, order details, missing product, and actual resolution whenever possible. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Missing Item Email Checklist

  • Confirm the customer's order.
  • Check whether the order was split into multiple shipments.
  • Confirm which product and quantity are missing.
  • Use the customer's name.
  • Acknowledge the incomplete order.
  • Apologize clearly.
  • Explain the confirmed situation briefly.
  • Tell the customer what happens next.
  • Explain replacement or refund options accurately.
  • Provide tracking information when available.
  • Give only realistic timeframes.
  • Explain anything the customer needs to do.
  • Provide a clear way to contact support.
  • Follow up after the issue is resolved when appropriate.

Final Thoughts

A missing-item email should make the customer's situation easier, not create another support problem. Start by checking the order, confirm what is missing, acknowledge the inconvenience, and explain the actual solution.

A reusable template can save customer-service teams time, but the strongest responses still use customer-specific information. Personalization, empathy, a clear recap, and a defined next step make a template more useful than a generic apology. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

Before sending the message, verify the fulfillment status, replacement availability, refund policy, and shipping information. This keeps the email accurate and helps the customer understand exactly what to expect.
