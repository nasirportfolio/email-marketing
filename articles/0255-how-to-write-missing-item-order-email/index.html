How to Write a Missing Item in an Order Email to Customers

When a customer opens a delivery and discovers that an item is missing, they need more than a general apology. They need to know whether the item was shipped separately, whether the order has been checked, and what the business will do next.

A well-written missing-item email acknowledges the incomplete order, identifies the product, explains the verified situation, and gives the customer a realistic next step. The message should be based on the actual order record rather than assumptions about what went wrong.

Quick answer: Check the order and shipment records first. Then apologize, identify the missing product, explain whether it is being shipped separately or needs further investigation, and outline the available replacement or refund process. Do not promise a solution or delivery date until it has been confirmed.

Missing Item in Order Email Template

Use this template when a customer reports an incomplete order. Adjust the wording to match the facts, your store policy, and the action your team has actually approved.

Subject: We’re Sorry About the Missing Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the [Missing Product]. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.

We’re checking the order and shipment details to confirm what happened and determine the appropriate next step.

[Insert the verified action here, such as confirming a separate shipment, arranging an approved replacement, or explaining the refund process.]

We’ll provide an update when the next step has been confirmed. We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

If you have any questions, please reply to this email and our support team will be happy to help.

Best regards,
[Customer Service Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is a Missing Item Email?

A missing-item email is a customer-service message used when an order arrives without one or more products that the customer purchased. For example, a customer may order three items but find only two in the delivered package.

The cause is not always an item being forgotten during packing. The product may have been shipped separately, marked incorrectly in a fulfillment system, or delayed by an inventory issue. The first response should therefore focus on verification rather than blame.

The purpose of the email is to do three things:

  • Acknowledge the customer's incomplete order.
  • Explain what the business knows and what still needs checking.
  • Give the customer a clear, realistic next step.

When to Send the Email

Respond after reviewing the customer's report and the information available in the order system. If the missing item is confirmed, explain the next action as soon as practical.

If the order may have been split into multiple shipments, check the shipment details before promising a replacement. Some stores fulfill products from different locations, so items from one order may arrive in separate packages.

If the situation remains unclear, tell the customer that you are checking the details. Avoid making them wait without an update when your team can provide useful information.

What to Check Before Responding

A brief verification process can prevent an unnecessary replacement, a duplicate shipment, or an inaccurate promise. Review these details before replying:

  • Order number: Make sure you are reviewing the correct purchase.
  • Product and quantity: Confirm what was ordered and how many units were purchased.
  • Fulfillment record: Check which items were marked as packed or fulfilled.
  • Split shipments: Determine whether the missing product has a separate shipment or tracking number.
  • Tracking status: Review the latest available shipping information.
  • Previous communication: Check whether the customer has already supplied details or received a response.
  • Store policy: Verify the available replacement, return, and refund options.
Practical tip: Do not say that the item was forgotten during packing until the fulfillment record supports that conclusion. If you have not confirmed the cause, explain that you are investigating the discrepancy.

What to Include in the Email

A useful missing-item email should give the customer enough information to understand the case without overwhelming them.

  • The customer's name and order number.
  • The product and quantity reported missing.
  • A direct acknowledgment and appropriate apology.
  • A brief explanation of the verified situation, if known.
  • The next action being taken.
  • Replacement or refund details when approved.
  • Tracking information when it is available.
  • Any action the customer must take.
  • A working support contact or reply option.

The customer should finish reading the message knowing what the business is doing and whether they need to do anything themselves.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Acknowledge the Missing Item

Begin with a direct sentence that names the problem. For example:

“I’m sorry that your order arrived without the [Product Name]. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.”

2. Confirm the Order Details

Reference the order number and product when you have verified them. Only say that the item was included in the original order if the order record confirms it.

“We checked order [Order Number] and confirmed that the [Product Name] was included in your original purchase.”

3. Explain What Happens Next

Tell the customer whether the team is checking tracking information, arranging a replacement, or reviewing the available resolution.

4. Avoid Unsupported Promises

Do not promise overnight shipping, a guaranteed delivery date, or an immediate refund unless the relevant action has been approved and the timing is reliable.

5. Make the Next Step Clear

If the customer needs to confirm an address, choose between available options, or provide additional information, state that request clearly. If no action is needed, say so.

Template 4: A Refund Is the Appropriate Resolution

Subject: Update About the Missing Item in Your Order

Hello [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the [Product Name]. We’re unable to provide the missing item, so we can offer the following resolution under our policy: [Insert the approved refund or other available option].

We’ll confirm the next step and provide an update once the action has been submitted.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your understanding.

Best regards,
[Customer Support Team]

Template 5: More Information Is Needed

Subject: A Quick Question About Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry to hear that an item may be missing from your order. We’re reviewing the order details and need one additional piece of information to help us investigate.

Could you please confirm whether the package contained [specific product or quantity]? We’ll use your response to check the fulfillment details and determine the appropriate next step.

Thank you for helping us resolve this.

Best,
[Support Representative]

Explain the Replacement Process

If the missing item will be sent separately, explain whether the shipment is already arranged or still needs to be processed. The distinction matters: a planned replacement is not the same as a dispatched package.

Before confirming a replacement, check product availability, fulfillment requirements, the shipping address where relevant, and any required approval. Once the shipment has actually been processed, provide the tracking information according to your store's normal procedure.

If the item is temporarily unavailable, explain the real options. These might include waiting for restock, choosing an eligible alternative, or receiving a refund where permitted by policy. Do not imply that a substitute has been selected without the customer's agreement when their choice is required.

When a Refund May Be Appropriate

A refund may be suitable when the missing product cannot be supplied or when the applicable store policy provides a refund for an incomplete order.

Explain the process accurately. If the refund has only been approved but not submitted, do not say that it has already been issued. If it has been submitted, distinguish the business's processing step from the time the payment provider or bank takes to post the funds.

If the customer can choose between a replacement and a refund, explain the available options clearly and give them a simple way to respond.

Shipping and Tracking Information

If the missing item is being shipped separately, provide its tracking information when it is available. If a tracking number has not yet been generated, say that you will provide an update when the shipment is processed.

Never insert a made-up tracking link or tell the customer that a shipment is on its way before the carrier or fulfillment system confirms that status. Clear, accurate updates help prevent repeated support requests.

Missing Item Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject line that reflects the actual stage of the case.

  • We’re Sorry About the Missing Item in Your Order
  • Update About Your Incomplete Order
  • We’re Checking the Status of Your Missing Item
  • Your Missing Item — Next Steps
  • We’ve Confirmed the Missing Item
  • Replacement Update for Order #[Order Number]
  • About the Missing Product in Your Order
  • Refund Update for Your Missing Item

Use a subject line such as “Replacement Update” only when the message really concerns a replacement. Avoid creating unnecessary urgency or suggesting that a resolution is complete when it is still being reviewed.

Practical Ecommerce Example

Imagine a customer orders three products for $89.99, but the package arrives with only two. The store checks the order and confirms that the third product was included in the original purchase. The following example shows how to communicate the next step without making unsupported promises.

Subject: We’re Fixing the Missing Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry that your order arrived without the [Product Name]. I understand how frustrating it is to receive an incomplete order.

We checked your order and confirmed that the product was included in your original purchase. We’re arranging the appropriate next step and will update you when the action is confirmed.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience while we correct the issue.

If you have any questions, please reply to this email and our support team will be happy to help.

Best regards,
Customer Support
[Company Name]

This is a hypothetical example. Replace the product, price, order details, and resolution with accurate information from the real order before sending a customer email.

Using Automation and Follow-Up

Email automation can help support teams handle routine order issues consistently. However, automation should use verified order data and should not send a replacement confirmation before a replacement has actually been approved or processed.

A practical workflow might follow these steps:

  1. Record the customer's report and order number.
  2. Check the order and fulfillment records.
  3. Determine whether the item was shipped separately.
  4. Request missing information only when necessary.
  5. Route the case for the appropriate review or approval.
  6. Send the confirmed resolution and any available tracking details.
  7. Follow up if the case remains unresolved and an update is due.
  8. Close the case when the agreed resolution is complete.

For broader workflow ideas, read Email Marketing Automation and Email Marketing Customer Journey.

Follow up after the issue is resolved

Once the missing item has arrived or the agreed resolution has been completed, a short follow-up can confirm the outcome and let the customer ask for further help if needed. Keep the follow-up relevant to the case rather than turning a service message into an unrelated promotional email.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending a Generic Apology

An apology alone does not resolve the issue. Identify the missing product and explain what the business is doing next.

2. Failing to Check for Split Shipments

Some orders are fulfilled in multiple packages. Check the shipment records before deciding that an item was omitted.

3. Asking for Information Already Available

Review the original report and order details before asking the customer to repeat information.

4. Blaming the Warehouse or Carrier

Customers need a clear solution more than an internal explanation of who made the mistake. Keep the focus on resolving their order.

5. Making Promises You Cannot Guarantee

Avoid unsupported claims about delivery dates, refunds, or replacement shipping. Communicate confirmed actions and realistic timeframes.

6. Forgetting the Customer's Next Step

If the customer needs to confirm an address, choose an option, or provide details, explain exactly what is needed. If no action is required, make that clear.

7. Failing to Personalize the Template

A template should save time without making the response feel careless. Include the customer's actual order details and the verified resolution.

Missing Item Email Checklist

  • Confirm the correct customer and order.
  • Check whether the order was split into multiple shipments.
  • Confirm which product and quantity are missing.
  • Acknowledge the incomplete order and apologize appropriately.
  • Explain the verified situation without guessing the cause.
  • Request only information that is still needed.
  • Confirm replacement or refund availability before promising it.
  • Provide tracking information only when available.
  • Use realistic timeframes.
  • Explain any action the customer needs to take.
  • Provide a clear support contact.
  • Proofread the message and check mobile readability.
  • Follow up if the case remains unresolved.

Final Thoughts

A missing-item email should make the customer's situation easier, not create another support problem. Start by checking the order, confirm what is missing, acknowledge the inconvenience, and explain the actual next step.

A reusable template can save time, but it should never replace checking the facts. Personalization, accurate order information, and a clear resolution make a template more useful than a generic apology.

Before sending the message, verify the fulfillment status, replacement availability, refund policy, and shipping information. This helps the customer understand what to expect and reduces avoidable follow-up questions.
