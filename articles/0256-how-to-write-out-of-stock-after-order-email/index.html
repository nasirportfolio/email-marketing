How to Write an Out-of-Stock After Order Email to Customers

An out-of-stock problem is especially difficult when the customer has already placed an order. They expected the product to be available, so discovering that it cannot be fulfilled can create frustration and uncertainty.

Quick Answer: An out-of-stock after order email should clearly explain that the purchased item is unavailable, acknowledge the inconvenience, and tell the customer what options are actually available. Depending on the business and its policies, those options may include waiting for restock, choosing an alternative product, receiving a refund, or canceling the affected item.

Out-of-Stock After Order Email Template

Subject: An Update About Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

I’m sorry to let you know that the [Product Name] you ordered is currently unavailable.

We discovered an inventory issue while processing your order, and we apologize that we cannot fulfill this item as originally expected.

We can offer the following option: [insert the actual available option].

If you would prefer another resolution, please [explain the actual process].

We’re sorry for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

Best,
[Customer Service Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is an Out-of-Stock After Order Email?

An out-of-stock after order email is a customer service message sent when a product becomes unavailable after the customer has already purchased it.

This is different from a normal out-of-stock product page. In a normal stockout, the customer may simply be unable to purchase the item. In this situation, the customer has already placed an order and expects the business to fulfill it.

Inventory systems can have different rules for what happens when stock reaches zero. For example, Shopify can be configured to stop selling when an item is out of stock or, in certain situations, continue selling it for preorders or other fulfillment models. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The customer email should focus less on internal inventory terminology and more on the practical question: What happens to my order now?

Why This Email Matters

An unexpected stock problem can affect the customer's delivery plans, budget, and confidence in the store. A vague message can create additional questions, while a clear message gives the customer a reasonable path forward.

A useful email should answer four basic questions:

  • Which item is affected?
  • Why can’t it be fulfilled as expected?
  • What options are available?
  • What does the customer need to do next?

The goal is not to hide the inventory problem. The goal is to communicate it accurately and make the resolution easy to understand.

When to Send the Email

Send the email once the stock problem has been confirmed and the business knows what it can realistically offer the customer.

If you already know that an item cannot be fulfilled, avoid waiting until the expected shipping date to communicate the problem.

Earlier communication gives the customer time to decide whether to wait, choose another option, or request an available refund or cancellation.

However, do not send a premature stockout message based on a single inventory number. Check whether another fulfillment location has available inventory and whether the order can still be completed. Shopify notes that fulfillment locations can affect whether an item is considered available for online orders. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

What to Check Before Sending It

A customer should not receive an out-of-stock notice until the order and inventory information have been checked.

  • Order number
  • Customer name
  • Product name
  • Product variant, such as size or color
  • Quantity ordered
  • Current inventory
  • Inventory at relevant fulfillment locations
  • Whether the item is already being prepared or shipped
  • Whether new inventory is expected
  • Whether an alternative product is actually available
  • Actual refund and cancellation options
Important: Do not assume that a zero quantity at one location means the entire order cannot be fulfilled. Review the actual fulfillment situation before contacting the customer.

What to Include in the Email

A useful out-of-stock-after-order email should normally include:

  • The customer's name
  • The affected order number
  • The unavailable product and variant
  • A clear explanation of the stock problem
  • A brief and sincere apology
  • The available resolution options
  • Expected timing when it is genuinely known
  • Instructions for choosing an option, if necessary
  • Refund or cancellation information when applicable
  • A clear customer-support contact method

Avoid filling the email with internal warehouse or inventory terminology that does not help the customer understand the situation.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Tell the Customer What Happened

Put the main problem near the beginning of the email.

I’m sorry to let you know that the Classic Canvas Backpack in Black you ordered is currently unavailable.

2. Acknowledge the Inconvenience

The customer has already made a purchase, so the message should recognize that the situation may be disappointing.

We understand that you ordered this item expecting it to be available, and we’re sorry that we cannot fulfill it as originally expected.

3. Explain the Situation Without Making Excuses

If the reason is confirmed and useful, explain it briefly. If you do not know the exact cause, do not invent one.

For example:

We discovered an inventory discrepancy while processing your order and confirmed that the item is not currently available.

4. Give the Customer a Clear Choice

The customer should not have to reply simply to discover what options exist. Explain the available choices in the same email whenever possible.

Depending on the actual business policy, options may include waiting for a restock, selecting another available product, receiving a refund for the affected item, or canceling the affected portion of the order.

How to Explain a Backorder

A backorder is different from a simple discontinued or permanently unavailable product. A backordered item is temporarily unavailable but is expected to be fulfilled later. Shopify describes backorders as orders for products that are temporarily unavailable and fulfilled at a later date. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

If your business can genuinely offer a backorder, explain the expected timing clearly. Do not present an uncertain date as a guarantee.

Backorder example:

The item is currently unavailable, but we expect additional inventory around [estimated timeframe]. If you would like to keep the item on your order, please [explain the actual confirmation process].

If there is no reliable restock estimate, say that clearly instead of giving the customer a date that may later prove incorrect.

How to Offer an Alternative Product

Offering an alternative can be useful when the original item is unavailable, but the customer should understand that it is an option rather than an automatic substitution unless the business has an established process allowing that substitution.

If you prefer not to wait for the original item, we can help you review the currently available alternatives. Please reply to this email and our support team will help you choose an available option.

Avoid calling an alternative product “better,” “equivalent,” or “the same” unless the business can support that description.

How to Explain Refund Options

If the unavailable product cannot be supplied, a refund may be one of the available resolutions. The exact process depends on the store's policies and payment setup.

Shopify supports full and partial refunds for orders, including refunds for selected items. Shopify also provides separate order-cancellation workflows that can include refunds. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Refund example:

Since the [Product Name] is unavailable, we can process a refund for that item according to our refund policy. We’ll confirm once the refund has been submitted.

Do not promise an exact date for the funds to appear in the customer's account unless your payment process supports that timeframe.

When the Order Has Multiple Items

A stock problem is more complicated when an order contains several products. The customer needs to know whether the other items will still ship.

If the other items can ship

The other items in your order can continue through fulfillment. The [Product Name] is currently unavailable, so we’d like to confirm how you would like us to handle that item.

If the unavailable item affects the entire order

The [Product Name] is currently unavailable, and this affects the fulfillment of your order. We’re sorry for the inconvenience. Your available options are [actual options].

Be explicit about which products are affected. Do not make the customer guess whether the entire order has been delayed.

Out-of-Stock Email Subject Lines

Here are practical subject-line options:

  • An Update About Your Order
  • Important Update About Your Order
  • We’re Sorry — An Item in Your Order Is Unavailable
  • Update About [Product Name]
  • We Need to Update Your Order
  • Your Order — Next Steps
  • Availability Update for Your Order

Choose a subject that accurately describes the message. Avoid unnecessary urgency or promotional language.

U.S. Ecommerce Example

Imagine a customer in the United States ordered a $79.99 backpack in black. During fulfillment, the store discovers that the selected color is unavailable. The business expects more inventory but does not have a confirmed delivery date.

Subject: An Update About Your Backpack Order

Hi Emily,

I’m sorry to let you know that the black backpack you ordered is currently unavailable.

We discovered an inventory issue while processing your order and confirmed that we cannot ship the selected color right now.

We expect additional inventory around [estimated timeframe]. If you would like to wait for the black backpack, we can keep you updated as inventory becomes available.

If you prefer another option, please reply to this email and our support team can help you review the available alternatives.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

Best,
Customer Support
[Company Name]

This is a hypothetical example. Replace the product, price, availability information, and resolution options with accurate details from the actual order.

Follow up after the customer chooses an option

Once the customer selects a resolution, send a confirmation after the action has actually been completed.

Follow-up example:

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re following up to confirm that your selected resolution for [Product Name] has been processed.

[Insert the actual confirmation or next step.]

Thank you for your patience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Waiting too long to communicate

If you already know that the product cannot be fulfilled, communicate the problem once the facts and available options have been confirmed.

2. Giving an uncertain restock date as a guarantee

If the supplier or inventory estimate is uncertain, explain that it is an estimate. Do not turn an estimate into a promise.

3. Automatically substituting another product

Do not replace the purchased item with another product unless the business's actual process permits it and the customer has agreed when required.

4. Blaming internal teams

Customers usually need a solution rather than an explanation of which employee, warehouse, or supplier caused the problem.

5. Ignoring the rest of the order

If other products can ship normally, explain that. If the stock problem affects the whole order, explain that instead.

6. Offering unsupported alternatives

Only offer products that are actually available and appropriate for the customer's situation.

7. Promising a refund without verifying the order

Confirm the affected item, refund amount, and actual refund process before making the promise. Shopify supports full and partial refunds, but the exact customer-facing resolution depends on the order and store circumstances. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Out-of-Stock Email Checklist

  • Confirm the customer's order.
  • Verify the exact product and variant.
  • Check relevant inventory locations.
  • Confirm that the item really cannot be fulfilled as ordered.
  • Check whether a restock is expected.
  • Determine the actual customer options.
  • Explain the problem clearly.
  • Apologize without making excuses.
  • Explain backorder timing when applicable.
  • Explain alternative-product options when available.
  • Explain refund or cancellation options accurately.
  • Clarify what happens to other items in the order.
  • Give only realistic timeframes.
  • Tell the customer what action is required.
  • Follow up after the selected resolution is completed.

Final Thoughts

An out-of-stock-after-order email should be honest, specific, and focused on the customer's next step. Tell the customer which item is affected, explain the situation briefly, and provide the options that the business can actually deliver.

If the product can be restocked, explain the expected timing carefully. If an alternative is available, make it a clear choice rather than an automatic substitution. If the item cannot be fulfilled, explain the applicable refund or cancellation process.

Most importantly, verify the order and inventory information before sending the message. A good template saves time, but accurate and personalized information should come first.
