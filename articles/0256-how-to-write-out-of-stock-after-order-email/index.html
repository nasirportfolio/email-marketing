How to Write an Out-of-Stock After Order Email to Customers

Discovering that a product is unavailable after a customer has placed an order creates a difficult customer-service situation. The customer may have already paid, planned around the expected delivery date, or purchased the item for a specific occasion. A vague notification can create more frustration than the stock problem itself.

A good out-of-stock after-order email explains what is unavailable, acknowledges the inconvenience, and presents the options the business can genuinely offer. Depending on inventory and store policy, those options might include waiting for a restock, choosing an alternative, receiving a partial shipment, or cancelling the affected item with an appropriate refund.

Quick answer: Tell the customer which item is unavailable, apologize, explain what is known about restocking, and clearly describe the available choices. Do not invent a restock date, assume the customer wants a substitute, or claim that a refund has been processed before it has been submitted.

Out-of-Stock After Order Email Template

Use this template when an item becomes unavailable after an order is placed. Adapt the choices and timing to your actual inventory and store policy.

Subject: An Update About Your Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

Thank you for your order. We’re sorry to let you know that [Product Name] is currently out of stock.

We understand that this may affect your plans, and we apologize for the inconvenience.

Here are the options currently available: [insert verified restock information, an eligible alternative, partial shipment, or cancellation and refund options according to your policy].

Please let us know which available option you prefer by replying to this email. We’ll confirm the next step once we receive your response.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Customer Support Representative]
[Company Name]

What Is an Out-of-Stock After-Order Email?

An out-of-stock after-order email is a transactional or customer-service message sent when a product a customer ordered is no longer available to fulfill as expected.

This situation differs from a standard out-of-stock notice displayed before purchase. The customer has already submitted an order, so the business must explain how the existing order will be handled rather than simply inviting the visitor to shop later.

The email should answer four questions:

  • Which item is unavailable?
  • Why is the order affected, if the reason is known?
  • What realistic options can the customer choose?
  • What happens next, and when will the customer receive another update?

Why Items Become Unavailable After Ordering

Several operational problems can cause an item to become unavailable after an order is placed. Identifying the actual cause helps the business give a more accurate explanation.

  • Inventory synchronization delay: The online store may not immediately reflect stock changes from another sales channel.
  • Simultaneous purchases: Several customers may attempt to buy the final units around the same time.
  • Inventory discrepancy: The recorded quantity may differ from the stock physically available.
  • Supplier delay: A supplier may not deliver the expected replenishment on schedule.
  • Quality-control issue: Remaining units may need to be withheld because they do not meet the required standard.
  • Fulfillment error: The order may have been accepted before the warehouse confirmed that the item could be picked and packed.

Do not speculate about the cause in the customer email. If the reason has not been confirmed, explain the stock issue and focus on the available resolution.

What to Check Before Sending

Before notifying a customer, verify the order and stock information. This reduces the risk of sending a false alert or offering an option that cannot be fulfilled.

  • Confirm the order number and affected product.
  • Check the quantity ordered and the quantity available.
  • Confirm whether the item is unavailable or merely delayed in the inventory system.
  • Check whether the remaining items can ship separately.
  • Verify any supplier or restock estimate before communicating it.
  • Review the applicable cancellation, replacement, and refund policy.
  • Determine which alternatives are genuinely available.
  • Check whether another support message has already been sent.
Important: A stock notification should not automatically cancel an entire order. Check whether only one product is affected and explain how the remaining items will be handled.

What to Include in the Email

A clear message should contain the information the customer needs to make a decision.

  • Order identification: Include the order number and affected product.
  • Direct explanation: State that the item is currently unavailable.
  • Apology: Acknowledge the inconvenience without making excuses.
  • Restock information: Give a date or estimate only if it is supported by reliable information.
  • Available options: Explain the choices the business can actually provide.
  • Payment and refund information: Describe the applicable process accurately.
  • Next step: Tell the customer whether to reply, choose an option, or wait for a confirmed update.
  • Support contact: Provide a clear way to ask questions.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

1. Use a Clear Subject Line

Make the purpose of the message clear without using alarming or promotional language. “An Update About Your Order” or “An Item in Your Order Is Currently Unavailable” are suitable starting points.

2. Identify the Affected Product

Use the product name and order number so the customer does not have to guess which purchase is affected. If the order contains multiple products, identify the unavailable item precisely.

3. Acknowledge the Inconvenience

Apologize briefly and sincerely. Avoid lengthy explanations about internal stock management unless a specific explanation will help the customer.

4. Present Realistic Options

Depending on the circumstances and policy, options may include waiting for restock, selecting an alternative, receiving the available products first, or cancelling the affected item. Do not offer every option automatically if your business cannot support it.

5. Explain the Next Step

If the customer must choose an option, explain how to respond. If the business will take the next action automatically, state what will happen and when the customer can expect an update, provided that timing is known.

Email Templates for Different Situations

Template 1: The Item Is Out of Stock and the Restock Date Is Unknown

Subject: An Item in Your Order Is Currently Unavailable

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry, but [Product Name] is currently out of stock. We understand that you expected your order to be fulfilled as planned, and we apologize for the inconvenience.

We do not yet have a confirmed restock date, so we do not want to give you an inaccurate estimate.

The available options are [insert the options your store can provide]. Please reply with your preferred option, and we’ll confirm the next step.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Support Representative]
[Company Name]

Template 2: A Restock Estimate Is Confirmed

Subject: Restock Update for Your Order #[Order Number]

Hello [Customer Name],

We’re sorry that [Product Name] is not currently available for shipment.

Our latest supplier information indicates that the item is expected to be available around [Date or Date Range]. This is an estimate, and the actual fulfillment date may change.

Please let us know whether you would prefer to wait or choose another option available under our policy. We’ll confirm your selection and the next step.

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
[Customer Support Team]

Template 3: An Alternative Product Is Available

Subject: An Alternative for the Unavailable Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry to let you know that [Original Product] is currently out of stock.

We have [Alternative Product] available as a possible substitute. Before we make any change to your order, we’d like to confirm whether this alternative suits your needs.

[Explain any price difference and applicable terms accurately.] If you would prefer not to substitute the item, please let us know so we can explain the other options available.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best,
[Support Representative]

Template 4: The Customer Can Choose Between Waiting and Cancellation

Subject: Please Choose an Option for Order #[Order Number]

Hello [Customer Name],

We’re sorry that [Product Name] is unavailable at the moment.

You may choose to wait for a restock if the available estimate suits you, or you may choose another option offered under our store policy.

Please reply with your preferred choice. We’ll confirm the next steps and any applicable refund or shipping details before proceeding.

We apologize for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience.

Best regards,
[Company Name]

Template 5: The Unavailable Item Is Cancelled and a Refund Has Been Submitted

Subject: Update on the Unavailable Item in Your Order

Hi [Customer Name],

We’re sorry that we could not fulfill [Product Name] in your order.

As confirmed, the affected item has been cancelled and a refund of [Amount] was submitted to [Payment Method] on [Date]. The time it takes for the funds to appear may depend on your payment provider or bank.

[Explain what will happen to any remaining items in the order.] If you have questions about the update, please reply to this email.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Support Representative]

Use carefully: The cancellation and refund template above is only for cases where both actions have actually occurred. If the refund has not been submitted, change the wording to describe the approved or planned next step accurately.

How to Communicate Restock Timing

Restock information is useful only when the business has a reasonable basis for providing it. Supplier estimates, inbound inventory records, and warehouse processing can help establish a timeframe, but they do not always guarantee the date the item will be ready to ship.

  • Confirmed estimate: Give the expected date or range and make clear if it is an estimate.
  • Unknown date: Say that the date has not been confirmed instead of guessing.
  • Repeated delay: Send an update when the previous estimate changes.
  • Item restocked: Confirm availability before promising shipment, then explain the next step.

A restock estimate is not the same as a delivery estimate. If the item is expected to return to inventory on a particular date, additional time may still be needed for order processing and shipping.

Offering an Alternative Product

An alternative can be useful when the original product is unavailable, but the substitute should meet the customer's needs. Do not assume that a similar product is automatically acceptable.

Before proposing a substitute, check:

  • Whether the alternative serves the same purpose.
  • Whether the size, model, specifications, or other important features differ.
  • Whether the price is different and how that difference will be handled.
  • Whether the alternative is actually in stock.
  • Whether the customer must approve the change before it is made.

Present the substitute as a choice where customer consent is needed. If the customer declines it, explain the other options that are available under your policy.

Cancellation and Refund Communication

If the original item cannot be supplied, explain the cancellation and refund options clearly. The appropriate process depends on the store's policy, applicable requirements, the payment method, and the customer's circumstances.

Keep these stages distinct:

  • Option offered: The business has explained that cancellation or a refund may be available.
  • Cancellation approved: The business has confirmed that the item or order will be cancelled.
  • Refund submitted: The refund transaction has actually been initiated.
  • Funds posted: The payment provider or bank has completed its part of the process.

Do not describe a refund as completed merely because it has been approved. State the actual status and avoid promising a precise posting date unless it is supported by reliable information.

Out-of-Stock Email Subject Lines

Choose a subject line that matches the purpose of the message.

  • An Update About Your Order #[Order Number]
  • An Item in Your Order Is Currently Unavailable
  • Restock Update for Your Order
  • Please Choose an Option for Your Order
  • An Alternative for Your Unavailable Item
  • Update on the Cancellation of Your Item
  • Refund Update for Order #[Order Number]
  • Your Item Is Back in Stock

Use “Your Item Is Back in Stock” only after availability has been verified and the message is relevant to the customer. Avoid misleading urgency or subject lines that disguise an important order update as a promotion.

Inventory Alerts and Follow-Up Automation

Inventory and email systems can help identify affected orders and organize follow-up messages. However, an automated workflow should use accurate inventory data and distinguish between an unavailable item, a planned restock, and an order that is ready to ship.

A practical workflow may include these steps:

  1. Detect or confirm the stock discrepancy.
  2. Identify affected orders and products.
  3. Verify availability and any restock estimate.
  4. Notify the customer with the appropriate message.
  5. Record the customer's selected option when a choice is required.
  6. Route cancellation, replacement, or refund actions through the correct process.
  7. Send an update when the status changes.
  8. Close the case when the order has been resolved.

Follow up without sending contradictory messages

If a customer has chosen to wait for restock, suppress irrelevant cancellation prompts unless the circumstances change and the customer needs a new choice. If a refund has been submitted, do not send a message that implies the order is still awaiting a decision. Keep the order status and email workflow synchronized.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Not Explaining Which Item Is Unavailable

Identify the product clearly, especially when the order contains several items.

2. Inventing a Restock Date

An unsupported estimate can create another broken expectation. State what is known and be honest about uncertainty.

3. Cancelling the Entire Order Without Checking

If only one product is affected, explain how the remaining products will be handled and follow the applicable order policy.

4. Substituting a Product Without Required Consent

Do not assume that a similar product is acceptable. Explain the difference and obtain approval when necessary.

5. Confusing Refund Approval With Refund Submission

Use accurate wording that reflects the real transaction status.

6. Sending Repeated or Irrelevant Messages

Check the customer's selected option and the current order status before sending automated follow-ups.

7. Omitting the Next Step

Tell the customer whether to reply, wait for an update, or review the available choices. A notification without a useful next step leaves the customer uncertain.

Out-of-Stock Email Pre-Send Checklist

  • Verify the correct customer, order, and affected product.
  • Confirm the current inventory status.
  • Check whether other products in the order can still ship.
  • Use a clear and accurate subject line.
  • Acknowledge the inconvenience and apologize appropriately.
  • Provide a restock estimate only when supported by reliable information.
  • Explain the actual available options.
  • Obtain customer approval for a substitute when required.
  • Describe cancellation and refund status accurately.
  • Explain the customer's next step.
  • Check the message for mobile readability and spelling.
  • Ensure follow-up messages reflect the current order status.

Final Thoughts

An out-of-stock after-order email should help the customer make an informed decision. Explain the unavailable item, share only reliable restock information, and present the choices the business can actually provide.

Use templates to save time, but personalize each message to the order and current inventory situation. Keep restock estimates, substitutions, cancellations, and refunds accurate, and make the next step clear.
