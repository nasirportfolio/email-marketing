How to Write a Backorder Update Email to Customers

Customer service email templates · Practical writing guide

When a customer orders an item that is temporarily unavailable, silence can create uncertainty. A useful backorder update email explains what has changed, gives the best available delivery estimate, and tells the customer what they can do next.

The goal is not simply to announce a delay. It is to help customers understand their order status and make an informed decision without having to contact your support team for basic information.

Quick Answer: What Should a Backorder Update Email Include?

A good backorder update email should include:

  • The customer's order number and affected product.
  • A clear explanation that the item remains on backorder.
  • The latest estimated shipping or delivery date, if known.
  • An honest explanation when the date is not yet confirmed.
  • Available options, such as waiting, choosing an alternative, or requesting cancellation where permitted.
  • A support contact and a clear next step.

Keep the message direct, considerate, and specific to the customer's order. Never promise a shipping date that your team cannot reasonably support.

Backorder Update Email Template

Copy and customize this example

Subject: Update on Your Backordered Item — Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We are writing to share an update about [Product Name] in your order #[Order Number]. This item is currently on backorder, and we are waiting for new stock to become available.

Our latest estimate is that the item will be available to ship around [Estimated Date]. This is an estimate and may change if our stock delivery is delayed.

You can keep your order open and wait for the item, contact us to discuss an available alternative, or ask about cancellation and refund options under our order policy.

We understand that waiting is inconvenient, and we appreciate your patience. If you have questions or would like to discuss your options, contact us at [Support Email] or [Support Phone].

Thank you for shopping with [Company Name].

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Customer Support Team]
[Company Name]

Replace every bracketed field before sending. If you do not have a reliable estimated date, say so clearly and explain when the customer should expect another update instead of inserting a guessed date.

1. What Is a Backorder Update Email?

A backorder update email informs a customer that an item they ordered has not yet shipped because stock is unavailable, and provides the latest information about what happens next.

A backorder is not necessarily a cancelled order. Depending on the retailer's process and the customer's choice, the order may remain active while the business waits for inventory to arrive.

A backorder update is different from an initial out-of-stock notification. The customer has already placed an order, so the message should focus on the existing order, current status, timing, and available choices.

It is also different from a shipping delay notice for a package that has already left the warehouse. In a backorder situation, the product may still be waiting for replenishment before it can ship.

2. When Should You Send a Backorder Update?

Send the initial update as soon as you know that the order will not ship as expected. Do not wait for customers to ask where their products are.

Useful times to send an update include:

  • When the delay is identified: Explain that the item is on backorder.
  • When a new estimate becomes available: Share the revised expected shipping date.
  • When an estimate changes: Explain the change and provide the latest information.
  • When stock arrives: Tell the customer when the order is being prepared or shipped, as appropriate.
  • When there is no new date: Send an update if you previously promised one, even if the date is still unconfirmed.
Practical rule: If you promise another update by a particular date, send it on time—even if you have no new delivery estimate. Keeping that promise helps customers know that their order has not been forgotten.

3. Backorder Update Email Subject Line Examples

Choose a subject line that accurately describes the order update.

  • Update on Your Backordered Item — Order #[Order Number]
  • Your [Product Name] Order: Latest Availability Update
  • An Update About Your Order #[Order Number]
  • Revised Shipping Estimate for Your Order
  • Your Backordered Item: What Happens Next
  • We Have an Update About [Product Name]
  • Your Order Status: Waiting for New Stock
  • New Availability Information for Your Order

Avoid misleading urgency, vague phrases such as “Important News,” or promises like “Your Order Ships Tomorrow” unless that statement is accurate. Include the order number when it helps customers recognize the transaction.

4. Backorder Update Email When the Delivery Date Is Unknown

Sometimes a supplier has not confirmed a replenishment date. In that situation, be transparent. An honest explanation is more useful than an invented estimate that may disappoint the customer later.

Template: No confirmed date

Subject: An Update on Your Backordered Item

Hi [Customer Name],

We wanted to update you about [Product Name] in order #[Order Number]. The item is still on backorder, and we do not yet have a confirmed date for when new stock will arrive.

We are checking with our supplier and will share another update by [Next Update Date]. We do not want to give you a shipping estimate that we cannot confirm.

If you prefer not to wait, please contact us at [Support Contact] to discuss the options available under our order policy.

We apologize for the uncertainty and appreciate your patience.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Company Name]

The most important detail in this version is the next update date. It gives the customer a clear expectation even when the final shipping date remains unknown.

5. Email Template When the Backorder Date Changes

If a supplier postpones delivery or new stock arrives later than expected, tell affected customers promptly. Acknowledge the previous estimate and state the new information clearly.

Template: Revised estimate

Subject: Revised Availability Estimate for Order #[Order Number]

Hi [Customer Name],

We are contacting you with an update about [Product Name] in order #[Order Number]. We previously estimated that the item would be available around [Previous Estimate], but that estimate has changed.

Our latest information indicates that the item may be ready to ship around [New Estimated Date]. Please note that this is an estimate, not a confirmed delivery date.

We understand that a change in timing can be frustrating. You can contact us at [Support Contact] to discuss the options available for your order.

We will send another update by [Next Update Date] if the order has not shipped before then.

Thank you for your understanding.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Company Name]

Do not hide the fact that the original estimate changed. Explain the latest situation in plain language and avoid blaming a supplier unless you have a factual reason to mention that detail.

6. How to Explain the Customer's Options

A delay message should not leave the customer wondering what to do. Explain only the choices your business can actually offer.

Option A: Keep the order open

Explain that the customer can continue waiting for replenishment. If you have a reliable estimate, provide it with an appropriate qualification. If not, give a date for the next status update.

Option B: Choose an alternative

If similar products are available, offer a relevant alternative. Include meaningful differences such as size, model, color, or price. Do not substitute an item without the customer's consent when consent is required.

Option C: Ask about cancellation or a refund

Explain the cancellation and refund options that apply to the order. These may depend on the store's policy, payment status, and applicable consumer-protection rules. Do not promise an outcome or processing time unless you can support it.

Present choices neutrally. The purpose of the email is to help the customer decide, not to pressure them into keeping an order they no longer want.

7. How to Personalize a Backorder Update Email

Personalization means providing information that helps the customer understand their own order. Adding a first name alone is not enough.

  • Use the order number: Help the customer identify the affected purchase.
  • Name the product: Avoid making the customer guess which item is delayed.
  • Give a relevant estimate: Use the latest information available for that item.
  • Explain what happens next: State whether another update or a shipping notification will follow.
  • Provide a support route: Make it easy to ask a question or discuss options.

Example for a U.S. online store

Example: Specific and customer-focused

Subject: Update on Order #US10482 — Blue Desk Lamp

Hi Jordan,

We wanted to let you know that the blue desk lamp in order #US10482 is still waiting for new stock. Our current estimate is that it may be ready to ship during the week of November 9.

This timing is an estimate and is not yet confirmed. We will send another update by November 5 if we do not have a confirmed shipping date sooner.

If you would rather discuss an alternative or ask about cancellation options, reply to this email or contact our support team at [Support Email].

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
Customer Support Team
[Company Name]

This is an illustrative example, not a real customer order or a promise about inventory. Use actual order details and dates in your own emails.

8. How to Automate Backorder Update Emails

Automation can help an online store send timely updates without requiring support staff to write every message manually. The automation should use accurate order and inventory data, not a fixed sequence that ignores changes in stock availability.

A practical workflow

  1. Identify affected orders. Use your order system to identify items that cannot ship as expected.
  2. Send the initial notification. Include the affected product, order number, current status, and available choices.
  3. Store the next update date. Set a follow-up date when the delivery estimate is unknown or uncertain.
  4. Check for inventory changes. Update the message when new stock information becomes available.
  5. Stop obsolete reminders. If the item ships, is cancelled, or the customer chooses an alternative, prevent irrelevant backorder emails from continuing.
  6. Measure the results. Review support requests, cancellations, delivery-related complaints, and customer responses.

Useful automation safeguards

  • Do not send an outdated estimated date after the system has changed it.
  • Do not mark an item as shipped merely because stock has arrived.
  • Keep a record of the status and time of each customer update.
  • Give customers a clear route to contact support.
  • Respect applicable email, privacy, and customer communication requirements.

A basic workflow can be built around order-management software and an email service provider. Before activating it, test scenarios such as a revised date, a cancelled order, a partial shipment, and stock arriving earlier than expected.

9. Common Backorder Email Mistakes to Avoid

1. Giving an unverified delivery promise

A date that sounds reassuring but later proves wrong can damage trust. Label estimates clearly and distinguish them from confirmed shipping dates.

2. Sending a vague message

“Your order is delayed” does not explain which product is affected or what happens next. Include the product name, order number, and current status.

3. Waiting for the customer to complain

Proactive updates can reduce uncertainty and avoid unnecessary support contacts. Communicate when you learn that the expected timing has changed.

4. Failing to explain available choices

Customers may want to wait, select another product, or discuss cancellation. Tell them which options are available instead of leaving them uncertain.

5. Sending repetitive emails without new information

Do not send frequent messages that simply repeat the same vague apology. If a follow-up is due and there is no new estimate, explain what has been checked and when the next update will arrive.

6. Forgetting to update the automation

A customer should not continue receiving backorder reminders after the item has shipped or the order has been cancelled. Connect email triggers to the current order status.

7. Using an impersonal or defensive tone

Acknowledge the inconvenience without making excuses. A concise explanation, a clear next step, and a helpful support contact are more useful than a long defensive message.

10. Backorder Update Email Checklist

Before sending, confirm that your message meets these requirements:

  • The subject line clearly identifies the order update.
  • The customer's name and order number are correct.
  • The affected product is identified accurately.
  • The backorder status is explained in plain language.
  • The estimated date is based on the latest available information.
  • An uncertain date is clearly described as an estimate.
  • A next update date is included when appropriate.
  • Available alternatives or cancellation options are explained accurately.
  • The support contact details work.
  • The email is readable on mobile devices.
  • The automation will stop or change when the order status changes.
  • The message contains no unsupported promises or misleading claims.

Final Thoughts

A useful backorder update email answers three questions: What is happening with my order? When might the item ship? What can I do if I do not want to wait?

Give customers the latest reliable information, acknowledge uncertainty when it exists, and explain the next step. If you promise another update, send it on time even when the delivery estimate has not changed.

These practices help customers make informed decisions and give your support team a consistent way to communicate during inventory delays.
