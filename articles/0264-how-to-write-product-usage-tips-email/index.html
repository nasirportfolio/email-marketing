How to Write a Product Usage Tips Email

A product usage tips email gives customers practical advice for getting more value from something they have already purchased. Instead of sending a general promotional message, the email focuses on a specific way the customer can use the product more effectively.

Quick Answer

A good product usage tips email should provide one or a small number of relevant tips that customers can understand and use immediately. Start with a clear reason for the email, explain the tip in simple language, show the customer what to do, and include help when necessary.

The best tip is usually the one that solves a real customer question rather than the one that gives the business another opportunity to promote a product.

Product Usage Tips Email Template

Subject: 3 useful tips for getting more from your [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

Now that you've started using your [Product Name], here are a few simple tips that may help:

  1. [Tip 1]: [Short explanation]
  2. [Tip 2]: [Short explanation]
  3. [Tip 3]: [Short explanation]

Start with [recommended first action].

If you have any questions, [contact support / visit the help resource].

We hope these tips help you get more from your purchase.

Best,
[Brand Name] Customer Team

This format works particularly well when the tips are short, specific, and directly connected to the product the customer purchased.

Why Send a Product Usage Tips Email?

Customers often need more than a receipt and delivery notification. After receiving a product, they may still have questions about setup, usage, maintenance, features, or common mistakes.

A well-written usage tips email can answer those questions in a convenient format. It can also help the customer discover useful parts of the product that they might otherwise overlook.

For example, a clothing retailer could explain how to care for a particular fabric. A kitchen-product company could share a preparation or cleaning tip. A software business could explain a feature that makes a common task easier.

The important distinction is that the email should be genuinely useful to someone who already owns the product.

How to Choose Useful Product Tips

Not every product fact deserves its own email. Choose tips based on what customers are likely to need.

Start With Common Questions

Customer support questions are a useful source of ideas. If customers repeatedly ask how to perform the same task, that question may be a strong subject for a usage tip.

Focus on Practical Actions

A useful tip should tell the customer something they can actually do. "This product has several features" is less actionable than explaining how to use one of those features.

Address Avoidable Mistakes

If there is a common mistake that can affect the customer's experience, explain how to avoid it clearly and without creating unnecessary fear.

Highlight Underused Features

For products with multiple features, introduce one useful feature at a time instead of presenting a long list.

Keep the Customer's Experience in Mind

A beginner may need basic instructions, while an experienced customer may benefit from an advanced tip. The content should match the customer's likely level of experience.

When to Send the Email

Timing matters because a useful tip can become less useful when it arrives at the wrong stage.

After the Customer Receives the Product

This timing works well for basic first-use instructions and simple setup recommendations.

After the Customer Has Used the Product

More advanced tips may be more appropriate after the customer has had time to become familiar with the product.

Before a Seasonal or Relevant Use Case

A usage tip can be particularly useful when it relates to an upcoming event, season, or common customer situation.

After a Support Question

When appropriate, a business can use a customer's previous question or support interaction to provide relevant educational content.

What to Include in a Product Usage Tips Email

1. A Specific Subject Line

Tell the customer what they will learn. If the email contains three tips, saying so can make the purpose immediately clear.

2. A Short Introduction

Explain why the tips may be useful. Keep this section brief so the practical information appears quickly.

3. Clear Tips

Each tip should contain an understandable action or recommendation.

4. Supporting Explanation

Add enough explanation to make the recommendation useful, but avoid turning every tip into a long paragraph.

5. One Primary Next Step

If there is a recommended action, make it obvious.

6. Support Information

Provide a way to get assistance when customers may need additional help.

Product Usage Tips Subject Lines

Here are practical subject-line formats you can adapt to different products:

  • 3 useful tips for your [Product Name]
  • Get more from your [Product Name]
  • 3 things to try with your [Product Name]
  • A few useful [Product Name] tips
  • How to use your [Product Name] more effectively
  • One useful [Product Name] tip
  • Getting started with [Product Name]
  • Have you tried these [Product Name] features?

Choose a subject line that accurately reflects the content. If the email contains one tip, do not make it sound like a comprehensive product guide.

How to Write the Email Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the Product

Start with the exact product or feature the customer purchased or is using.

Step 2: Identify the Customer's Likely Need

Think about what the customer may want to accomplish. The best tip usually helps the customer complete a specific task.

Step 3: Select One to Three Tips

Keep the number of tips manageable. Too many tips can make the email feel like a manual rather than a quick customer-help message.

Step 4: Write Each Tip as an Action

Use direct language. Explain what the customer should do instead of simply describing the product.

Step 5: Add Context

Briefly explain when or why the customer should use the tip.

Step 6: Add a Help Option

If the customer needs more information, provide an appropriate support route.

Step 7: Review the Email on Mobile

Make sure the subject line, paragraphs, lists, buttons, and links are easy to read on a small screen.

Product Usage Tips Email Examples

Example 1: Skincare Product

Subject: 3 simple tips for using your moisturizer

Hi Taylor,

Now that you've received your moisturizer, here are three simple ways to make it easier to use as part of your routine:

  1. Apply a small amount first rather than using too much.
  2. Use it consistently according to the product instructions.
  3. Keep the container closed when you're finished.

Start with the amount recommended on the product packaging.

If you have questions about the product, please contact our customer support team.

Best,
The Customer Team

This is a hypothetical example. The actual usage instructions should always follow the product's manufacturer guidance.

Example 2: Kitchen Product

Subject: 3 useful tips for your new kitchen tool

Hi Jordan,

Ready to start using your new kitchen tool? Here are three simple tips:

  1. Read the product instructions before the first use.
  2. Use the recommended settings for the task you're performing.
  3. Clean and store the product according to the care instructions.

Starting with these basic steps can make the first few uses easier.

Best,
The Customer Team

Example 3: Software Product

Subject: 3 ways to get more from your account

Hi Alex,

Here are three features worth trying as you get familiar with your account:

  1. Save frequently used workflows.
  2. Customize the settings you use most often.
  3. Review the available help resources when you need guidance.

Start with the feature that matches the task you perform most frequently.

Best,
The Customer Success Team

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Giving Generic Advice

"Use your product regularly" is not very useful unless the customer knows exactly what action to take. Make tips specific.

Including Too Many Tips

A long list can overwhelm customers. Start with the most useful information.

Repeating the Product Description

The customer already purchased the product. The email should help them use it rather than repeat the product listing.

Ignoring Product Instructions

Usage emails should not contradict official product instructions, safety guidance, or manufacturer recommendations.

Making Unsupported Claims

Avoid promising results that the business cannot reasonably support. A usage tip should be practical and accurate.

Turning Every Tip Into a Sales Pitch

If every tip ends with another product recommendation, the educational purpose of the email becomes less convincing.

How to Personalize Usage Tips

Personalization does not have to mean adding the customer's first name. More useful personalization can come from understanding what the customer actually purchased or how they have interacted with the product.

Personalize by Product

Send tips that match the exact product rather than sending the same message to every customer.

Personalize by Experience Level

A new customer may need basic setup guidance, while an experienced customer may benefit from a more advanced feature tip.

Personalize by Customer Activity

Where appropriate, customer activity can help determine which educational message is most relevant.

Personalize by Timing

A first-use tip and a long-term maintenance tip serve different purposes. Match the message to the stage of the customer journey.

Practical Tip

Before sending a usage tips email, ask: "Will this tip help the customer do something better or easier?"

If the answer is no, the tip probably needs to be more specific.

Final Checklist

  • Does the email relate directly to the customer's product?
  • Are the tips practical?
  • Can the customer understand each tip quickly?
  • Does each tip explain an actual action?
  • Have you avoided unnecessary product promotion?
  • Does the email match the customer's experience level?
  • Are the instructions consistent with official product guidance?
  • Are unsupported claims avoided?
  • Is the email easy to read on mobile?
  • Is there a clear way to get help if needed?
  • Have all links been checked before publishing?

Final Thoughts

A product usage tips email should make the customer's next step easier. The message does not need to be long or complicated. A few specific, well-timed tips can be more useful than a large amount of general information.

Focus on what the customer can actually do with the information. Use clear instructions, match the tips to the product and customer stage, and keep promotional content secondary to the educational purpose.
