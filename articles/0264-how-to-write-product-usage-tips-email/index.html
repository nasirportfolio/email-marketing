Advanced Product Usage Tips Email: Examples and Templates

Customer education · Product engagement · Email marketing

Once customers understand the basics of a product, they may benefit from learning a more useful feature, a faster workflow, or a practical way to adapt the product to their needs. An advanced product usage tips email introduces that next step without overwhelming the reader.

Unlike a getting-started email, an advanced usage email assumes the recipient has some familiarity with the product. Its purpose is to help the customer discover additional value through one relevant, actionable lesson. The best message is specific, accurate, and matched to the customer's experience.

What Is an Advanced Product Usage Tips Email?

An advanced product usage tips email teaches customers how to use a feature or method beyond the basic setup process. It might explain a time-saving shortcut in software, a less familiar product function, a more efficient workflow, or a way to use an existing feature for a particular goal.

For example, a project management platform might show customers how to create a reusable project template after they have completed their first project. An email marketing platform might explain how to segment an audience using a supported feature. A physical product company might direct experienced owners to an additional function described in the official product manual.

The tip must match the product and the customer's likely experience. Advanced does not mean complicated: it means the information offers something useful beyond the first steps.

Who Should Receive Advanced Usage Tips?

These emails work best when the recipient has had a reasonable opportunity to learn the basics. Sending advanced instructions too early may create confusion rather than value.

Customers who completed onboarding

If a customer has completed a basic setup step, you may introduce a related feature that builds on that knowledge.

Customers who regularly use the product

Experienced users may appreciate shortcuts, workflow improvements, or lesser-known features that solve a practical problem.

Customers with a specific goal

Where reliable preference or usage information is available, choose tips that support the customer's goal rather than sending a generic feature announcement to everyone.

Customers who may benefit from a next step

If a customer has already completed the introductory task, a follow-up lesson can help them progress. Do not assume that a customer is ready for advanced guidance without a reasonable basis.

How to Choose a Useful Advanced Tip

Start with a customer problem, not with a list of features you want to promote. The tip should help the reader do something better, faster, or more confidently.

  1. Identify a real task: Find a common question or a useful next step in the customer journey.
  2. Check that it is advanced enough: Avoid repeating the basic setup instructions customers already received.
  3. Verify the method: Confirm the feature exists and the instructions match the current product version.
  4. Explain the benefit: Tell the customer why the tip may be useful.
  5. Keep it manageable: Teach one technique and link to a fuller resource when needed.
Example: Instead of sending a general announcement about every feature in a software platform, teach one relevant workflow, show the steps, and link to a verified tutorial.

How to Structure an Advanced Usage Tips Email

1. Use a benefit-focused subject line

Explain what the reader may learn. For example, “A Faster Way to Organize Projects in [Product Name]” or “Try This Advanced Feature in [Software Name].” Avoid promising a result the feature cannot reliably deliver.

2. Establish why the tip matters

Connect the lesson to a task or problem the customer may recognize. Keep the opening brief so readers can quickly understand the benefit.

3. Teach one technique

Give clear steps in the correct order. If the feature is complex, use a short explanation in the email and link to a detailed tutorial.

4. Provide a relevant example

Show how the technique applies to a realistic task. Use examples that accurately reflect the product and do not imply unsupported capabilities.

5. Include one useful call to action

Use a direct action such as “Explore the feature” or “View the advanced tutorial.” The linked page should match the promise in the email.

Advanced Product Usage Tips Email Templates

Use these templates as starting points. Replace placeholders, verify product details, and make sure the instructions match your actual product before sending.

Template 1: Introduce an Advanced Feature

Subject: Have You Tried This Feature in [Product Name]?

Hi [First Name],

Now that you are familiar with the basics of [Product Name], here is another feature you may find useful: [Feature Name].

This feature can help with [Specific Task or Use Case]. To try it:

  1. Open [Relevant Section].
  2. Select [Feature or Setting].
  3. Follow the instructions to complete [Specific Task].

You can see the full walkthrough here: [Advanced Feature Guide].

Best regards,
[Company Name]

Best for: Products with features customers may not discover during initial setup.

Template 2: Show a More Efficient Workflow

Subject: A Simpler Workflow for [Customer Task]

Hi [First Name],

If you regularly use [Product Name] for [Task], this workflow may help you organize the process more efficiently.

Start by [Verified Step One], then [Verified Step Two]. Once those steps are complete, use [Relevant Feature] to [Specific Outcome].

We have prepared a detailed guide here: [Workflow Guide URL].

Try the process when it fits your work, and contact [Support Contact] if you need help.

Best,
[Company Name]

Best for: Products where users repeat the same task and could benefit from a documented workflow.

Template 3: Help Customers Discover a Lesser-Known Feature

Subject: One Useful [Product Name] Feature to Explore

Hi [First Name],

Many products include features that are easy to overlook. One you may want to explore is [Feature Name].

It is designed to help users [Accurate Feature Purpose]. You can learn how it works and when to use it in our guide.

Explore the feature tutorial

If the feature is not relevant to your current work, you can keep using the parts of [Product Name] that suit your needs.

Best regards,
[Company Name]

Best for: Educating customers without suggesting that everyone must use every feature.

Template 4: Advanced Tips for Existing Customers

Subject: Your Next Step With [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

If you have already mastered the basics of [Product Name], you may be ready to explore [Advanced Task or Feature].

This approach is useful when you want to [Relevant Customer Goal]. Our guide explains the process, the prerequisites, and the steps to follow.

Read the advanced guide

If you would prefer help with the basics first, you can revisit [Beginner Guide URL].

Best,
[Company Name]

Best for: Customers with different experience levels who need an appropriate next step.

Template 5: Share a Practical Tip Based on Customer Goals

Subject: A [Product Name] Tip for [Relevant Goal]

Hi [First Name],

If your goal is to [Customer Goal], consider trying [Relevant Feature or Method] in [Product Name].

The method works by [Brief, Accurate Explanation]. Follow the instructions in the guide to see whether it fits your workflow.

View the step-by-step guide

Best regards,
[Company Name]

Best for: Segments where the customer's goal is known from a reliable preference or onboarding response.

How to Choose the Right Template

  • Use the feature introduction when the customer may not know a useful capability exists.
  • Use the workflow template when the customer performs a recurring task.
  • Use the lesser-known feature template when discovery is the main goal.
  • Use the next-step template when customers have different levels of experience.
  • Use the goal-based template when you have reliable information about the customer's needs.

How to Personalize Advanced Usage Emails

Personalization should help select the right lesson, not just insert a first name into a generic message.

  • Match experience level: Avoid sending advanced instructions before customers have a reasonable chance to learn the basics.
  • Use relevant product data: Confirm that the feature applies to the product version or plan the customer uses.
  • Focus on a real task: Choose tips that relate to an expressed preference or a reliable customer need.
  • Offer an alternative: Include a beginner guide or help resource when the advanced lesson may not be appropriate.
  • Provide fallback content: Make sure missing personalization fields do not create confusing sentences.
  • Respect preferences: Follow applicable email policies and communication requirements.

How to Automate Advanced Usage Tips Emails

An automated sequence can deliver relevant lessons over time. The goal is not to send the maximum number of emails, but to offer the next useful lesson when the customer is likely to benefit from it.

  1. Define the audience: Identify customers for whom the tip is relevant.
  2. Choose a valid trigger: Use an onboarding milestone, confirmed product event, or suitable customer segment when available.
  3. Set a sensible delay: Allow enough time for customers to learn the basics before introducing more complex material.
  4. Select accurate content: Match the email to the correct product, version, and experience level.
  5. Limit repetition: Suppress lessons that have already been sent when your data can reliably identify them.
  6. Test the sequence: Verify links, conditions, fallback fields, mobile display, and email preferences.
  7. Review results: Use meaningful engagement and customer outcomes to decide what to improve.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Sending advanced tips too early

Customers who are still trying to complete basic setup may find advanced instructions confusing. Sequence lessons according to the customer journey rather than sending them all at once.

Teaching too many features in one email

A long feature list makes it difficult to identify what to try first. Focus on one technique and link to additional resources for readers who want to explore further.

Making unsupported product claims

Do not invent capabilities, shortcuts, or results. Verify instructions against current documentation and test them before sending.

Using generic examples

A tip becomes more useful when it demonstrates a real task. Replace vague statements with accurate steps, a relevant use case, and a working tutorial link.

Assuming every customer has the same needs

Advanced features are not equally useful to everyone. Use relevant customer information where available, and do not imply that a feature is necessary for all customers.

Measuring opens as the only success metric

An open does not prove that a customer learned the technique or benefited from it. Measure clicks, meaningful feature use, task completion, or feedback where reliable data is available.

How to Measure Advanced Usage Email Performance

Decide what the email is supposed to help the customer accomplish before selecting metrics.

  • Click-through rate: Shows whether readers visit the tutorial or feature guide.
  • Feature adoption: Measures use of the feature when reliable product analytics are available.
  • Task completion: Indicates whether customers complete the workflow the email teaches.
  • Customer feedback: Reveals whether the lesson was clear and relevant.
  • Unsubscribe and complaint rates: Help identify content that is poorly targeted or too frequent.

Open rates can be affected by privacy protections and automated activity. Treat them as an imperfect signal. Compare similar campaigns over a suitable period and avoid claiming that the email caused a result unless you have evidence that supports that conclusion.

Advanced Usage Email Pre-Send Checklist

  • The tip provides value beyond basic onboarding.
  • The feature exists in the customer's product version or plan.
  • The instructions have been checked for accuracy.
  • The email teaches one clear technique.
  • The subject line reflects the actual content.
  • The tutorial link works and matches the promised topic.
  • The message is appropriate for the intended audience.
  • Personalization fields and fallback content have been tested.
  • The email is readable on mobile devices.
  • Tracking measures a meaningful outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an advanced product usage tips email?

It teaches an existing customer a useful technique, feature, or workflow beyond the basic setup process.

How is it different from a getting-started email?

A getting-started email helps customers begin using a product. An advanced usage email assumes some familiarity and introduces a more specific next step.

How many tips should one email include?

One main tip is a useful starting point. If several steps are required to explain it, keep the email focused and link to a complete tutorial.

When should advanced usage emails be sent?

Send them when customers have had a reasonable opportunity to learn the basics and the lesson is relevant to their needs. The ideal timing varies by product and audience.

Can advanced usage emails be automated?

Yes. You can use appropriate onboarding milestones or reliable product events to select the next lesson. Test your conditions and make sure the content is still relevant when the message is sent.

Should every customer receive advanced tips?

Not necessarily. Some customers may only need basic functionality, while others may benefit from more advanced guidance. Use relevant information to target lessons and provide alternatives when appropriate.
