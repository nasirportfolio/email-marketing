How to Write a Product Care Reminder Email: Examples and Templates

A practical guide to post-purchase email communication

Customers may appreciate a product after buying it but still forget important care tasks. Depending on the item, they might need to review cleaning instructions, check a recommended maintenance schedule, or prepare a product for storage. A product care reminder email brings a relevant task back to the customer's attention at an appropriate time.

The purpose is not to send repeated messages simply to stay visible. A good reminder explains why the task may matter, tells the customer what to do next, and points to reliable instructions. For general post-purchase care communication, see our product care email guide. For detailed directions, use a product care instructions email.

Quick answer

A product care reminder email prompts a customer to review or perform an appropriate care task. Identify the task, choose a relevant time, use a clear subject line, provide a simple next step, and link to the manufacturer's instructions or a verified care guide.

1. Why Send Product Care Reminders?

A reminder helps customers remember a relevant task after purchase. It can be useful when care instructions are easy to overlook or when the product has a legitimate recurring care requirement.

A business might remind a customer to review seasonal storage guidance, check a documented maintenance schedule, or revisit instructions before using an item after storage. For broader post-purchase communication, see Article 0261: How to Write a Product Care Email.

Useful reminders make product information easier to find. They should not claim that a reminder guarantees better performance, prevents damage, or extends the life of every product.

For detailed advice rather than a prompt, see our product care tips email guide. That article focuses on the care advice itself; this one focuses on prompting customers at the right time.

2. Product Care Reminders Versus Care Tips

These email types are related but have different purposes.

3. How to Choose the Right Timing

Timing depends on the product, the care task, and reliable information available to the business. Do not choose a fixed interval unless it is supported by manufacturer instructions or a clear customer need.

After delivery or first use

A reminder may be appropriate when customers need to review important care information after receiving or beginning to use a product.

Before a seasonal change

Some products have relevant seasonal storage or preparation requirements. Send a reminder before the information is likely to be needed, where the product and instructions support that timing.

At a recommended maintenance interval

If official documentation specifies a maintenance interval, use it accurately. For messages focused on ongoing upkeep, see Article 0269: Product Care Maintenance Email.

After a customer request

If a customer asks for care guidance, a relevant follow-up can direct them to the correct instructions. Respect their email preferences.

A reminder should not replace a usage check-in. If the purpose is to ask how a customer is getting on with a product, read our product usage follow-up email guide.

4. What to Include in a Product Care Reminder

  1. Clear subject: Identify the product or care task.
  2. Short opening: Explain why the reminder is relevant.
  3. One main task: State the action the customer should consider.
  4. Verified guidance: Link to the correct care instructions.
  5. Clear call to action: Make the next step obvious.
  6. Support option: Explain where the customer can get help.

Keep the email concise and readable on mobile. If the care task is complicated, direct readers to a complete product care guide or the relevant product care instructions.

5. Product Care Reminder Subject Lines

  • A care reminder for your [Product Name]
  • Time to review your [Product Name] care guide
  • A helpful reminder for your [Product Name]
  • Preparing your [Product Name] for seasonal storage
  • Have you reviewed the care instructions for [Product Name]?
  • Your [Product Name] maintenance information
  • A quick reminder about caring for your recent purchase

Do not imply that maintenance is overdue unless you have reliable information to support that statement. Avoid false urgency and exaggerated claims.

6. Product Care Reminder Email Templates

These templates are examples. Replace placeholders with accurate product details and verify the task before scheduling a reminder.

Template 1: General care reminder

Subject: A care reminder for your [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

We're sharing a quick reminder to review the care instructions for your [Product Name].

If a care task is relevant at this stage, check the official guidance for your product and follow the recommended steps.

Next step: Review the care instructions here: [Verified Care Guide Link].

Best regards,
[Brand Name] Customer Care

Template 2: Seasonal reminder

Subject: Preparing your [Product Name] for seasonal storage

Hi [First Name],

If you plan to store your [Product Name] during the coming season, now may be a useful time to review its official storage instructions.

Follow the recommendations for your specific product and avoid any cleaning or storage method that is not approved.

Read the guide: [Verified Care Guide Link].

Best regards,
[Brand Name] Team

Template 3: Maintenance reminder

Subject: A maintenance reminder for your [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

We are sharing a reminder to review the maintenance information supplied with your [Product Name].

Please check the official instructions to confirm whether a task is due for your model.

View the maintenance information: [Verified Link].

Need help? Contact [Support Contact].

Best regards,
[Brand Name] Customer Care

7. How to Personalize Reminders

Use the purchased product

Send reminders for the product or model the customer actually bought. Avoid instructions that may not apply to their item.

Use reliable purchase information

A verified purchase or delivery date may help schedule a relevant message. Do not assume that a product has been used or maintained simply because time has passed.

Respect customer preferences

Allow customers to manage their email preferences where appropriate. Avoid excessive reminders and respect unsubscribe requests.

Match the customer's situation

A seasonal storage reminder should differ from a message about a recommended maintenance task. Give every email a clear purpose.

8. Building a Product Care Reminder Workflow

Automation can help send relevant reminders consistently. Before launching a workflow, confirm that the product needs the reminder and that the timing is justified.

  1. Identify the product: Determine which purchased item the message concerns.
  2. Confirm the task: Use official instructions or verified guidance.
  3. Choose a trigger: Use a relevant date or reliable signal.
  4. Check eligibility: Respect consent and unsubscribe requirements.
  5. Send a useful reminder: Explain the task and link to the correct instructions.
  6. Prevent unnecessary repeats: Use frequency limits and suppress irrelevant messages.
  7. Review results: Monitor clicks, feedback, customer questions, and unsubscribes.

If your follow-up is about how the customer is using an item rather than a care task, see Article 0263: Product Usage Follow-Up Email.

9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Inventing a maintenance schedule

Do not tell customers that a task is due at a specific interval unless the timing has a reliable basis.

Sending reminders too frequently

Repeated messages without a clear purpose can annoy customers. Use frequency limits.

Using vague instructions

“Take care of your product” does not explain what the customer should do. Identify the task or link to exact instructions.

Creating unnecessary concern

Avoid suggesting that a customer will damage a product unless there is a specific, verified reason for urgency.

Ignoring product differences

Requirements can differ between models and materials. Use the appropriate instructions.

Making the reminder too promotional

Keep the care task central. An offer should not obscure the reason for the message.

10. How to Measure Reminder Email Performance

MetricWhat it helps you understand
Click-through rateWhether recipients visit the linked care information.
Care guide visitsWhich resources customers seek after a reminder.
Customer questionsWhether the message leaves information unclear.
Unsubscribe rateWhether frequency or relevance needs improvement.
Customer feedbackWhether the reminder was timely and useful.

Do not treat opens or clicks as proof that a customer completed the care task. Review customer feedback and improve the message when the data suggests a problem.

11. Product Care Reminder Checklist

  • The task is relevant to the customer's product.
  • The timing has a clear and defensible reason.
  • Instructions and intervals are verified.
  • The subject accurately describes the message.
  • The next step is clear and practical.
  • The care guide and support links work.
  • The email is easy to read on mobile.
  • Frequency limits prevent unnecessary repeats.
  • Customer preferences and unsubscribes are respected.
  • The message avoids unsupported claims and false urgency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a product care reminder email?

It prompts a customer to review or perform an appropriate care task related to a purchased product.

When should a reminder be sent?

Send it when the task is likely to be relevant, using verified product guidance, a meaningful date, or another reliable signal.

How is a reminder different from a care tips email?

A reminder prompts an action. A care tips email explains useful care practices in more detail.

Can reminders be automated?

Yes, provided the timing is justified, the content is accurate, and customer preferences are respected.

What if a customer needs detailed instructions?

Link to the manufacturer's instructions, a verified care guide, or the appropriate support team instead of trying to fit a complicated procedure into a short reminder.

What if the customer needs a usage check-in?

Use a product usage follow-up email when the purpose is to ask about the customer's experience rather than prompt a care task.
