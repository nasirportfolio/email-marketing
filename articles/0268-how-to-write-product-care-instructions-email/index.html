How to Write a Product Care Instructions Email

A product care instructions email tells customers exactly how to look after a product after they receive it. Depending on the product, the message may explain cleaning, washing, storage, handling, charging, maintenance, or other important care requirements.

The key is clarity. Customers should be able to open the email and quickly understand what they should do, what they should avoid, and where they can find more detailed information.

Quick Answer

To write a product care instructions email, identify the purchased product, provide the most important care actions in a logical order, clearly explain anything the customer should avoid, and include a support or documentation link when additional information is needed.

Keep the instructions accurate and product-specific. Do not guess about cleaning methods, materials, temperature limits, charging requirements, or other technical details.

Product Care Instructions Email Template

Subject: Important care instructions for your [Product Name]

Hi [First Name],

Thanks again for purchasing your [Product Name].

Here are the key care instructions to help you look after your product:

1. Cleaning
[Explain the approved cleaning method.]

2. Storage
[Explain the recommended storage conditions.]

3. Handling
[Explain any important handling instructions.]

4. Avoid
[Explain anything that should not be done to the product.]

5. Maintenance
[Explain any required or recommended maintenance.]

For complete product instructions, visit [Care Guide URL].

If you have any questions, contact us at [Support Email].

Best,
[Brand Name] Customer Care

What Is a Product Care Instructions Email?

A product care instructions email is a post-purchase message that explains how a customer should care for a product they purchased.

The instructions can be very simple or more detailed depending on the product. A shirt may need washing and drying guidance, while an electronic product may require information about charging, cleaning, storage, and handling.

Product-use and care education is a recognized part of post-purchase communication. Shopify's current guidance describes onboarding and post-purchase emails as opportunities to help customers use products more effectively and includes care instructions as an example. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The email should complement, not replace, official product documentation when the product has detailed safety or technical requirements.

Why Clear Care Instructions Matter

1. Customers need practical information

Buying a product does not automatically mean a customer knows how to maintain it. A clear email can answer common questions at the point when the information is useful.

2. It makes post-purchase communication more helpful

A care email gives customers something useful rather than simply asking them to make another purchase.

3. It can reduce avoidable confusion

Customers may not know whether a product can be washed, submerged, exposed to heat, cleaned with a particular substance, or stored in a particular way. Clear instructions can direct them to the correct method.

4. It supports product education

Product education is an important part of the post-purchase journey. Shopify specifically recommends helpful product information and care instructions as post-purchase content. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

5. It gives customers an easy path to support

When the email includes a relevant help page or support contact, customers know where to go when the instructions do not answer their question.

What to Include in the Email

The exact content depends on the product. Include only instructions that are relevant and verified.

Product identification

Start by identifying the product. If your store sells several versions or materials, make the relevant model or variation clear.

Cleaning instructions

Explain the appropriate cleaning method when cleaning is part of normal product care.

Storage instructions

Explain how the product should be stored if storage conditions affect its care.

Handling instructions

Include any important handling information customers need for normal use.

Things to avoid

Clearly identify actions or conditions that should be avoided when those warnings are supported by the product documentation.

Maintenance instructions

If the product needs periodic maintenance, explain what the customer should do and when.

Additional documentation

Link to a complete care guide, manual, FAQ, or support page if the email cannot reasonably contain all the required information.

Support contact

Give customers a clear way to ask questions if they need additional assistance.

How to Write the Instructions Step by Step

Step 1: Identify the exact product

Start with the product the customer purchased. The instructions should match the actual product rather than a similar item.

Step 2: Verify the care information

Use official manufacturer documentation, approved product information, or instructions your business has verified.

This step is especially important for products involving electrical components, batteries, chemicals, heat, specialized materials, or other safety considerations.

Step 3: Put the most important action first

Customers often scan email rather than reading every line. Put the most important care instruction near the beginning.

Step 4: Use numbered steps when order matters

If the customer needs to complete several actions in sequence, use numbered instructions.

Step 5: Use bullets for independent tips

If the instructions do not need to be completed in a particular order, bullets can make them easier to scan.

Step 6: Separate actions from warnings

Make it obvious what customers should do and what they should avoid.

Step 7: Provide the next resource

Finish with a relevant link to the complete documentation or a customer support channel.

Product Care Instructions Email Examples

Example 1: Clothing

Subject: How to care for your new sweater

Hi Sarah,

We hope you're enjoying your new sweater. Here are the key care instructions:

  1. Check the garment care label before washing.
  2. Use the washing method specified on the label.
  3. Follow the recommended drying instructions.
  4. Store the sweater according to the garment's care requirements.

Please follow the care label for product-specific instructions.

If you have questions, contact our customer care team.

Example 2: Kitchen Product

Subject: Care instructions for your new kitchen product

Hi James,

Thanks again for your purchase. To care for your new product:

  • Clean it using the recommended cleaning method.
  • Dry it properly when required.
  • Avoid cleaning products that are not suitable for the material.
  • Store it in an appropriate location.
  • Follow the supplied product instructions for additional care requirements.

For complete instructions, please see the product care guide.

Example 3: Electronic Product

Subject: Important care instructions for your new device

Hi Alex,

Here are a few important care instructions for your device:

  • Use the charging equipment or method recommended by the manufacturer.
  • Keep the device in suitable storage conditions.
  • Clean the device according to the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Keep ports and openings free from debris using the recommended method.
  • Follow the complete safety instructions supplied with the product.

If you need technical assistance, please contact our support team.

Example 4: Furniture

Subject: Care instructions for your new furniture

Hi Taylor,

We hope you're enjoying your new furniture. To care for the product:

  • Use cleaning products appropriate for the material.
  • Follow the manufacturer's cleaning recommendations.
  • Avoid products that are not approved for the finish.
  • Follow the supplied instructions for ongoing maintenance.

Contact us if you need help with product-specific care.

Product Care Instructions Subject Line Examples

The subject line should clearly communicate that the email contains useful product information.

  • Important care instructions for your [Product Name]
  • How to care for your [Product Name]
  • Your [Product Name] care instructions
  • Keep your [Product Name] in good condition
  • Care instructions for your new [Product Name]
  • Please save these [Product Name] care instructions
  • Your quick [Product Name] care guide
  • How to clean and care for your [Product Name]

Choose a subject line that accurately describes the content. Avoid creating artificial urgency when the email is simply educational.

When to Send Care Instructions

The timing should reflect when the customer is most likely to use the information.

After delivery

For physical products, care instructions may be useful after the customer receives the item and is ready to use it.

After initial setup

Some products require setup or first-use instructions before ongoing care becomes relevant. In that case, send care information after the customer has completed the initial stage.

Before maintenance is needed

If a product has a predictable maintenance cycle, the instructions can be sent before the customer is expected to perform that task.

As part of a post-purchase sequence

Care instructions can form one stage of a broader post-purchase sequence that includes delivery updates, product education, support, and later feedback requests. How to Personalize Product Care Instructions

Personalize by product

The most useful personalization is matching the instructions to the product the customer actually purchased.

Personalize by product category

If your store sells many products, create different care instruction emails for meaningful product categories.

Personalize by customer stage

A first-time customer may benefit from more basic instructions, while a returning customer may need a shorter reminder.

Use purchase data appropriately

Purchase history can help determine which instructions should be sent. Segmentation makes the content more relevant than sending one generic care email to everyone. Shopify's current customer-engagement guidance recommends using customer profiles and purchase history to personalize post-purchase communication. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Keep personalization useful

Personalization should improve relevance. It should not add unnecessary personal details or make the message feel intrusive.

How to Automate Product Care Instructions Emails

Product care instructions can be included in an automated post-purchase flow when your ecommerce and email systems provide the necessary order and product information.

Step 1: Choose the trigger

The trigger might be purchase, fulfillment, delivery, or another relevant customer event.

Step 2: Identify the product

Use product or category information to determine which care instructions the customer should receive.

Step 3: Set the delay

Choose a delay based on when the customer is likely to need the information. The right timing can differ between product categories.

Step 4: Add the instructions

Keep the main email concise and link to the complete documentation when necessary.

Step 5: Add exclusion rules

Consider excluding orders that have been cancelled, refunded, or returned when the care message is no longer relevant.

Step 6: Test the flow

Test different products and customer paths to make sure the correct instructions are sent.

Step 7: Review customer questions

Customer-support questions can reveal missing or confusing instructions. Use that feedback to improve the email.

Automated post-purchase flows can include product education and care information as customers move from purchase to product use. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Guessing care instructions

Never invent technical or safety information. Verify the instructions before publishing them.

2. Using the wrong instructions

Similar-looking products can have different care requirements. Match the email to the actual product.

3. Burying important information

Do not hide an important warning or required action inside a long paragraph. Make it easy to find.

4. Making the email too long

Include the most useful instructions and link to the complete documentation when the product requires more detail.

5. Using complicated language

Customers should not need specialist knowledge to understand ordinary care instructions. Use direct, plain language.

6. Making it overly promotional

The primary purpose of the email should be helping the customer care for the product.

7. Sending it at the wrong time

A useful instruction can become less useful if it arrives long before or long after the customer needs it.

8. Forgetting to test links

Check every link to a care guide, product manual, support page, or FAQ before activating the automation.

Product Care Instructions Email Checklist

Before sending your email, check:

  • ☐ The correct product is identified.
  • ☐ Every instruction has been verified.
  • ☐ The most important instruction appears early.
  • ☐ Cleaning information is included when relevant.
  • ☐ Storage information is included when relevant.
  • ☐ Handling information is clear.
  • ☐ Important things to avoid are clearly stated.
  • ☐ Maintenance requirements are explained when necessary.
  • ☐ Additional documentation is linked when needed.
  • ☐ Customer support information is easy to find.
  • ☐ The timing matches the customer's product journey.
  • ☐ Returned or cancelled orders are handled appropriately.
  • ☐ Every link has been tested.
  • ☐ The email is readable on mobile devices.
  • ☐ The message is useful rather than unnecessarily promotional.

Final Thoughts

A product care instructions email gives customers practical information they can use after a purchase. The strongest messages are not complicated. They identify the product, explain the important actions clearly, warn customers about relevant things to avoid, and provide a path to additional help.

Accuracy matters more than length. Use verified product information, avoid assumptions, and make the most important instructions easy to find.

When care instructions are delivered at the right point in the customer journey, they become a useful part of post-purchase communication rather than another generic marketing email.
