How to Write a Product Value Tips Email

Buying a product is only the beginning of the customer experience. Once customers have completed their first use, they may still have questions about features, shortcuts, better techniques, or ways to get more value from what they purchased. A product value tips email gives them practical guidance without overwhelming them.

Quick Answer: A product value tips email should teach customers a few useful ways to get more value from a product they already purchased. Choose tips that are relevant to the specific product, explain why each tip matters, and give the customer an easy next step. Keep the message educational first rather than turning it into a sales pitch.

The Simple Structure of a Product Value Tips Email

A practical product value tips email can follow this structure:

  1. Recognize the customer's purchase.
  2. Explain what the tips will help them accomplish.
  3. Give three to five useful product-specific tips.
  4. Explain when each tip is useful.
  5. Link to a detailed resource when appropriate.
  6. Offer support if the customer needs help.
  7. End with one simple next step.

The key is usefulness. A customer should be able to read the email and immediately understand at least one practical way to improve their experience with the product.

What Is a Product Value Tips Email?

A product value tips email is a post-purchase educational email that helps customers use a product more effectively, discover useful features, or apply practical techniques they may not have noticed when they first received it. It is different from an order confirmation, a setup email, or a promotional offer because its main purpose is to help customers benefit from their existing purchase.

For example, a software company might explain a useful shortcut, a kitchenware brand could share a care technique, and a fitness equipment store might explain a safe way to adjust a product for different exercises. The tips should be accurate for the specific product and should not make unsupported performance claims.

Why Product Value Tips Matter

Helpful product education can make the customer experience clearer and more rewarding.

  • Encourages product adoption: Customers learn how to use features that might otherwise go unnoticed.
  • Reduces avoidable confusion: Clear tips can answer common questions before customers need to contact support.
  • Builds trust: Useful guidance demonstrates that the brand cares about the customer experience after the sale.
  • Supports retention: Customers who understand a product may be better positioned to continue using it.
  • Creates useful engagement: Links to guides or demonstrations give customers a relevant next step.

These are possible benefits, not guaranteed outcomes. Measure performance in your own customer journey to find out which messages are genuinely helpful.

When Should You Send a Product Value Tips Email?

Send the email after customers have had a reasonable opportunity to set up and try their purchase. The right timing depends on the product's complexity, delivery time, and normal usage pattern.

  • Simple products: A few days after delivery may be enough.
  • Products requiring setup: Send after the setup guidance, allowing time for the first use.
  • Complex products: Consider a sequence of short, progressive educational emails.
  • Consumable products: Share storage, care, or usage advice at a relevant point in the product lifecycle.
Avoid sending advanced tips before the customer has received the product or completed essential setup steps. Use delivery or behavioral data when it is available and reliable.

What to Include in the Email

1. A relevant opening

Acknowledge the product the customer purchased and explain what they will learn. Keep the introduction brief and avoid pretending to know how much the customer has used the product unless you have reliable data.

2. A small number of actionable tips

Choose three to five tips that customers can understand and try. Each tip should explain what to do and why it is useful.

3. A clear explanation

Use plain language. If a tip requires several steps, link to a more detailed guide rather than packing the entire tutorial into the email.

4. One primary call to action

Invite the customer to view a guide, try a feature, watch a demonstration, or contact support. A single clear action is often easier to follow than several competing links.

5. A support option

Give customers a genuine way to ask for help if the instructions do not match their product or situation. Link to your real support or contact page.

How to Choose Useful Product Tips

Start with questions customers frequently ask, features they often overlook, and simple improvements that can be demonstrated accurately. Support tickets, product documentation, customer feedback, and usability testing can help identify these needs.

  • Choose tips that relate directly to the purchased product.
  • Prioritize the questions customers are most likely to have at this stage.
  • Make every tip specific enough to act on.
  • Check the instructions against current product documentation.
  • Separate basic instructions from advanced techniques.
  • Link to authoritative help material for details or safety instructions.

Product Value Tips Email Examples

Example 1: Software product

Subject: Three ways to get more from your new software

Hello [First Name],

Now that you have access to [Product Name], here are three features that may help simplify your workflow:

  1. Save a reusable template: Use a template for tasks you repeat often.
  2. Explore the keyboard shortcuts: Check the official shortcut list for actions you perform regularly.
  3. Review your notifications: Adjust alerts so important updates are easier to notice.

See the full guide here: [Link to the relevant product guide]

Need help? Visit our support page: [Support Link]

Best wishes,
[Brand Team]

Example 2: Home product

Subject: Helpful care tips for your [Product Name]

Hello [First Name],

A few simple habits can help you care for your [Product Name]. Always follow the instructions supplied with your particular model.

  • Use the cleaning method recommended in the product manual.
  • Store the item in the conditions specified by the manufacturer.
  • Check the care guide before using unfamiliar cleaning products.

Read the product care guide: [Care Guide Link]

Thank you for choosing us,
[Brand Team]

Example 3: Online learning product

Subject: Make your next learning session more useful

Hello [First Name],

Here are three practical ways to make better use of your learning resources:

  • Set a realistic goal for each study session.
  • Write down one key idea after completing a lesson.
  • Return to difficult topics and practise them again.

Continue learning here: [Course Link]

Best wishes,
[Brand Team]

  • Three ways to get more from your [Product Name]
  • Helpful tips for your new [Product Name]
  • Make more of the features you already have
  • Have you tried these [Product Name] features?
  • A quick guide to getting more value from your purchase
  • Simple ways to improve your [Product Name] experience
  • Your next step with [Product Name]
  • Useful ideas for using [Product Name]
  • Tips selected for your [Product Name]
  • Get started with these practical product tips

Test subject lines with a suitable audience and evaluate them alongside clicks, useful actions, unsubscribes, and other relevant engagement measures.

How to Personalize the Email

Personalization should make the information more relevant, not simply insert the customer's name. Depending on your consent, data quality, and email platform, you may be able to use:

  • Purchased product or model
  • Customer experience level
  • Purchase history
  • Relevant product usage behavior
  • Previously viewed educational content

Example of product-based personalization

A customer who purchased a beginner camera may receive basic composition tips, while a customer who purchased an advanced camera could receive information about features relevant to more experienced users.

Use behavioral data only when it is collected and handled appropriately. If you do not know a customer's experience level, send broadly useful advice rather than making assumptions.

How to Automate the Email

A product value tips email can be one stage in a broader post-purchase sequence. Plan each message around the customer's likely needs at that point in the journey.

  1. Purchase: Send the order confirmation.
  2. Shipping: Provide delivery information.
  3. Delivery: Confirm or acknowledge delivery when appropriate.
  4. Setup: Help the customer prepare the product.
  5. First use: Help the customer complete the first meaningful use.
  6. Value tips: Teach useful ways to get more from the product.
  7. Feedback: Ask for feedback after sufficient product experience.

Your product setup email can prepare the customer, while the product first-use email can help with the first practical experience. This article can then introduce useful techniques that go beyond the basic first use.

Use behavioral conditions when possible

If your marketing platform supports behavioral triggers, avoid sending advanced tips before the customer has completed the basic stages. Customers who have not completed setup may need setup help rather than advanced product tips.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Giving generic advice

A value tips email should be connected to the customer's actual product. Generic statements provide little reason to keep reading.

2. Including too many tips

A long list can make the email difficult to act on. Prioritize the tips that are genuinely useful.

3. Repeating the setup email

If the customer has already received setup instructions, the value tips email should add something new.

4. Making unsupported claims

Do not claim that a tip guarantees better performance, saves a specific amount of money, or produces a particular result without reliable evidence.

5. Turning the message into an upsell

Related-product recommendations may be appropriate later, but the main purpose of a value tips email should remain customer education.

6. Sending advanced information too early

Customers should receive information that matches their experience level and stage.

7. Forgetting the support path

If a customer has difficulty applying a tip, provide an obvious place to get help.

What to Measure

Measure whether the email helps customers engage with useful product education, rather than assuming that a purchase must immediately follow every educational email.

  • Open rate: Indicates whether customers are opening the message, subject to email privacy limitations.
  • Click-through rate: Shows whether customers are clicking linked resources.
  • Guide clicks: Identifies interest in deeper product education.
  • Feature or resource usage: Useful when customer actions can be tracked reliably.
  • Support contacts: Can reveal whether the tips are clear or confusing.
  • Unsubscribe rate: Helps identify potentially irrelevant or excessive messaging.
  • Repeat purchase behavior: A longer-term outcome that should not automatically be attributed to one email.

Compare the email with other messages in the same customer journey rather than relying on one metric in isolation.

Product Value Tips Email Checklist

  • ☐ The email is clearly connected to the purchased product.
  • ☐ The purpose of the email is obvious.
  • ☐ The tips provide practical value.
  • ☐ The tips match the customer's experience level.
  • ☐ Each tip explains why it is useful.
  • ☐ The content adds something beyond the setup email.
  • ☐ The number of tips is manageable.
  • ☐ Product-specific information is used where possible.
  • ☐ Claims are accurate and supportable.
  • ☐ A detailed resource is available when needed.
  • ☐ A real support option is provided.
  • ☐ The email is easy to scan on mobile.
  • ☐ The primary action is clear.
  • ☐ The email does not become an unnecessary sales pitch.
  • ☐ Placeholder links have been replaced with real URLs.

Final Thoughts

A product value tips email is an opportunity to continue helping customers after the basic setup and first-use stages are complete. Instead of sending another promotional message, give customers information that makes their existing purchase more useful.

Start with a small number of practical tips. Explain why each one matters, make the instructions easy to follow, and provide a clear path to more detailed information when necessary.

Product education works best when it follows the customer's actual journey. Setup, first use, advanced tips, support, and feedback can each have their own purpose rather than being combined into one overloaded email.

The goal is simple: help the customer get more value from something they already chose to buy.
