How to Write a Product Reorder Reminder Email

A product reorder reminder email helps customers remember when it may be time to purchase a product again. The most useful messages are timely, specific, and easy to act on without making the customer feel pressured.

Quick Answer

To write a product reorder reminder email, identify the product the customer previously purchased, estimate an appropriate reorder window, explain why the reminder is relevant, provide a clear reorder option, and keep the message short. Personalize the email with product information when appropriate and avoid sending reminders too early or too frequently.

A Simple Product Reorder Email Structure

A reorder reminder does not need to be complicated. A practical message can follow this structure:

  1. Remind the customer about the product.
  2. Explain why you are contacting them now.
  3. Give them a simple way to reorder.
  4. Add one useful product-related detail if appropriate.
  5. End with a clear call to action.

Simple Example

Subject: Ready for another [Product Name]?

Hi [First Name],

You purchased [Product Name] a while ago, so we wanted to give you a quick reminder in case you are getting close to needing another one.

If you are ready to reorder, you can find the product here:

Reorder [Product Name]

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

What Is a Product Reorder Reminder Email?

A product reorder reminder email is a message sent to a previous customer when it may be useful to purchase the same or a related product again.

These emails are particularly useful for products that customers regularly replace, consume, replenish, or purchase again. Examples can include household supplies, personal-care products, food products, pet supplies, office supplies, and other items with a predictable or semi-predictable purchasing cycle.

The goal is not simply to tell customers to buy again. The goal is to make the next purchase easier when the timing is genuinely relevant.

Reorder reminders are also connected to the broader goal of keeping existing customers engaged. For a broader explanation of this topic, see Email Marketing Customer Retention .

When Should You Send a Reorder Reminder?

Timing is one of the most important parts of a reorder campaign. A reminder sent too soon may be irrelevant. A reminder sent too late may arrive after the customer has already purchased elsewhere.

There is no single reorder interval that works for every product. Consider the customer's previous purchase, the type of product, expected usage, and your own customer data.

Use the Previous Purchase as a Starting Point

If a customer bought a product on a particular date, you can use that purchase as the starting point for a future reminder.

For example, a business selling a frequently replenished product might test a reminder several weeks after purchase. A product used less frequently may require a much longer interval.

Test Rather Than Assume

Start with a reasonable interval and evaluate the results. If customers consistently reorder earlier or later than expected, use that information to improve future timing.

Timing should also fit into the wider customer journey. Understanding how different email messages support different stages can help you plan reorder communication more deliberately. See Email Marketing Customer Journey for the broader framework.

Use the Customer's Purchase Context

A useful reorder email should make it obvious why the customer is receiving the message.

Instead of writing a generic message such as "Buy again today," mention the product the customer previously purchased.

For example:

"You previously purchased our Daily Moisturizer. If you are getting close to finishing your current bottle, you can reorder it here."

This gives the customer context without requiring them to remember why the business contacted them.

Product Reorder Email Subject Lines

The subject line should clearly communicate the purpose of the email without making an unsupported claim about the customer's product usage.

Simple Subject Lines

  • Ready to reorder [Product Name]?
  • Need another [Product Name]?
  • A quick reminder about [Product Name]
  • Time to restock [Product Name]?
  • Running low on [Product Name]?

Friendly Subject Lines

  • Your [Product Name] reminder
  • Need another one?
  • Ready for your next [Product Name]?
  • Your next order is easy

Test different subject lines based on your audience rather than assuming one style will work for every business.

How to Structure the Email Body

A reorder reminder should usually answer three questions quickly:

  1. What product am I being reminded about?
  2. Why am I receiving this reminder now?
  3. What should I do if I want to reorder?

Start With Context

Mention the customer's previous product or purchase context early in the email.

Keep the Explanation Short

Customers do not need a long explanation about why they may need the product again. A concise reminder is often easier to understand.

Make the Next Step Obvious

The customer should not have to search through your website to find the product again. Direct them to the relevant product or reorder page when your system supports it.

Create a Clear Reorder CTA

The call to action should describe the action the customer can take.

Useful examples include:

  • Reorder [Product Name]
  • Shop [Product Name]
  • Order Again
  • Restock Now
  • Get Another [Product Name]

Avoid using a vague CTA when a more descriptive option can tell the customer exactly what will happen after the click.

Personalization Ideas

Personalization can make a reorder reminder more useful when the underlying customer data is accurate.

Product Name

Referencing the product the customer actually purchased can make the reminder immediately understandable.

Customer Name

If you have permission to use the customer's name and the data is reliable, a simple greeting can make the message feel more relevant.

Previous Purchase Information

You can use information such as a previous product, purchase date, or relevant variant when it helps the customer understand the reminder.

Relevant Product Options

If the product has legitimate variations, you can show the option that matches the customer's previous purchase while allowing them to choose another option if appropriate.

Personalization should improve clarity rather than make the email feel intrusive.

Automating Reorder Reminder Emails

Reorder reminders are well suited to automation because the trigger can be connected to a customer's previous purchase.

A basic automated workflow can look like this:

  1. Customer purchases a product.
  2. The purchase is recorded in the store or email platform.
  3. A waiting period begins.
  4. The customer receives a reorder reminder.
  5. The system checks whether the customer has purchased again.
  6. Customers who reorder can be removed from the reminder sequence.

Use Different Timing for Different Products

Avoid using one universal delay when your catalog contains products with very different purchasing cycles.

For example, a consumable product and a durable product should not necessarily receive the same reorder reminder schedule.

Stop the Reminder After a New Purchase

If the customer has already reordered, continuing to send the same reminder can make the automation feel poorly coordinated.

Build an appropriate purchase condition into the workflow whenever your email platform supports it.

Automation should be part of a broader email plan rather than an isolated tactic. For foundational guidance on planning email campaigns, see Email Marketing Strategy .

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending the Reminder Too Early

A customer may still have plenty of the product. Test your timing and adjust it according to actual customer behavior.

2. Sending Too Many Reminders

Repeated reminders can become annoying. Give customers enough space between messages and consider stopping the sequence after a reasonable number of attempts.

3. Making Unsupported Assumptions

Avoid stating that a customer definitely has finished a product unless your data actually supports that conclusion.

"You may be ready for another" is different from "You have definitely run out."

4. Making Customers Search for the Product

If the purpose of the email is to encourage a reorder, make the relevant product easy to reach.

5. Using a Vague CTA

A button such as "Click Here" gives less information than "Reorder [Product Name]."

6. Ignoring Mobile Readers

Many customers read email on mobile devices. Keep the message concise, make links easy to tap, and ensure the destination page works well on smaller screens.

[Brand Name]

How to Measure Performance

A reorder reminder should be evaluated based on the actions that matter to the business, not just opens.

Reorder Rate

Track how many recipients make another purchase after receiving the reminder.

Click-Through Rate

Measure how often recipients click the reorder link or button.

Conversion Rate

Compare the number of recipients who complete the intended purchase with the number who received the email.

Revenue Per Recipient

When your analytics system supports it, evaluate how much revenue the campaign generates relative to the number of recipients.

Unsubscribe Rate

Monitor unsubscribes as well. A campaign that generates purchases but also creates excessive negative engagement may need changes to its timing, frequency, or targeting.

Product Reorder Email Checklist

  • ☐ The email clearly identifies the relevant product.
  • ☐ The reminder timing makes sense for the product.
  • ☐ The message explains why the customer is receiving it.
  • ☐ The email does not make unsupported assumptions.
  • ☐ The subject line is clear and relevant.
  • ☐ The reorder CTA is specific.
  • ☐ The customer can easily reach the relevant product.
  • ☐ Personalization data is accurate.
  • ☐ The automation stops or changes after a new purchase when appropriate.
  • ☐ The email is easy to read on mobile devices.
  • ☐ The campaign is measured using meaningful business metrics.
  • ☐ Customers can manage their email preferences appropriately.

Final Thoughts

A product reorder reminder email works best when it arrives at a genuinely useful time and makes the next purchase simple.

Start with the customer's previous purchase, choose a sensible reminder window, keep the message focused, and provide a clear reorder path. Then use campaign results to improve timing and messaging instead of relying entirely on assumptions.

The strongest reorder emails do not pressure customers to buy. They provide a timely reminder when another purchase may actually be useful.
