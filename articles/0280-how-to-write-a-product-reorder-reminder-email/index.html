How to Write a Product Reorder Reminder Email

A product reorder reminder email helps a previous customer find and buy a product again when they may need it. It is most useful for items that are consumed, replaced, or purchased repeatedly. A good reminder makes the next step convenient without pretending to know exactly what remains in the customer's home or business.

Quick answer: Send a reorder reminder based on a reasonable product-use interval or the customer's purchase history. Name the item, explain why you are contacting them, provide a direct and working reorder link, and make it easy to update preferences or unsubscribe. Start with helpful timing and accurate information rather than pressure or artificial urgency.

What Is a Product Reorder Reminder Email?

A product reorder reminder is a marketing email sent to an existing customer to make it easier to purchase a previously bought item again. It can be useful for products such as coffee, printer supplies, pet food, skincare products, cleaning supplies, or business consumables—provided the product is appropriate for repeat purchasing and the customer has agreed to receive marketing messages.

The message should answer three questions quickly: which product is being referenced, why the reminder may be useful now, and how the customer can reorder. If you cannot reliably estimate when the product will run out, use cautious wording such as “Ready for another order?” rather than “You must be running out.”

Reorder Reminder vs. Replenishment Reminder

These terms overlap, but they can emphasize slightly different things. A replenishment reminder usually focuses on replacing a product that may be used up. A reorder reminder focuses on making another purchase of an item the customer has bought before. The same email can do both, so the practical distinction is the message's intent and timing—not a strict technical rule.

When Should You Send a Product Reorder Reminder?

Choose timing from the product's normal usage cycle and your own order data. A reminder sent too early can feel irrelevant; one sent too late may arrive after the customer has already bought elsewhere. There is no universal interval that works for every product or audience.

  • Review purchase history: Look for typical gaps between repeat orders for the same product or product category.
  • Account for variation: Household size, frequency of use, package size, and seasonality can affect when a customer needs more.
  • Test a small timing range: Compare a few reasonable send intervals while keeping the audience and message as consistent as practical.
  • Suppress recent purchasers: Do not send a reminder if the customer has already reordered the item.
  • Respect product-specific rules: Avoid unsupported claims about health, safety, expiry, or dosage. Follow applicable regulations for regulated products.
Practical tip: Treat predicted reorder dates as estimates, not facts. Give customers control over reminders and stop the sequence when a new order is recorded.

Product Reorder Reminder Email Subject Line Examples

Keep the subject line clear and specific. Use the product name only when it is accurate and appropriate to disclose in the recipient's inbox.

  • Ready to reorder your [Product Name]?
  • Need another [Product Name]?
  • Your previous [Product Name] is easy to reorder
  • Would you like to restock [Product Name]?
  • Reorder your essentials in a few clicks
  • Need more supplies for your next project?
  • A quick reminder about your last order
  • Make your next [Product Name] order easier

Use a preheader to add context, such as “Find your previous item and review the current options.” Avoid fake countdowns, invented scarcity, or claims that an item is nearly gone when you cannot know that.

How to Write the Email

1. Identify the item clearly

Use the correct product name and, when helpful, a small product image. Confirm that the item is still available and that the link leads to the right product or a relevant reorder page.

2. Explain the reminder in a helpful way

Briefly connect the message to the customer's previous purchase. For example: “You ordered [Product Name] recently, so we wanted to make reordering easy if you need it again.” This wording does not assume the customer has run out.

3. Make the next step simple

Use one clear primary action, such as “Reorder [Product Name].” If the product has variants, let customers review size, quantity, price, and availability before checkout. Do not silently add products to a cart or submit an order on the customer's behalf.

4. Provide useful details

Where relevant, show the last purchased item, package size, current price, delivery information, or a way to choose a substitute. Verify dynamic information before sending so customers are not shown stale prices or unavailable products.

5. Respect customer choice

Include a working unsubscribe link and a route to manage email preferences. If customers can choose reminder frequency or pause reminders, explain how. Send marketing reminders only where you have the required permission and legal basis.

Example: A concise reorder reminder

Subject: Ready to reorder your coffee?

Preheader: Find your previous coffee blend and review the current options.

Body:

Hello [First Name],

You previously ordered [Coffee Blend]. If it is time to stock up, you can find the product and choose the quantity that works for you.

Button: View and Reorder

Not ready yet? No problem. You can return whenever you need another order.

Thank you,
[Brand Name]

Replace every bracketed field before sending. The CTA should be a real link styled as a button in your email platform; the template above is copy for the email message, not a ready-made web form.

Personalization and Automation

Personalization should improve relevance rather than simply insert a first name. Useful data may include the last product purchased, product variant, purchase date, reorder history, and whether the item is still available. Use only data your business is permitted to process, and avoid exposing sensitive purchase details unnecessarily.

A basic automation can follow this sequence:

  1. Record a completed order and identify products suitable for repeat purchase.
  2. Estimate a reminder date using observed order intervals or a documented product-use assumption.
  3. Before sending, check for a newer order, a refund, an out-of-stock item, or an unsubscribe.
  4. Send the reminder with a product-specific destination and accurate details.
  5. Stop or reset the reminder sequence when another order is completed.
  6. Review complaints, unsubscribes, and customer feedback as well as sales.

An email marketing platform such as GetResponse may help manage consent-based segments, automated workflows, and campaign reporting. Check the features and plan limits before choosing a platform; the workflow also depends on whether your store can pass reliable purchase events and product data to it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sending too early: Base timing on product use and observed buying patterns, then test.
  • Assuming the product is gone: Use conditional, respectful language instead of asserting something you cannot know.
  • Linking to the wrong item: Test product URLs and variant selection before the campaign goes live.
  • Ignoring recent orders: Suppress customers who have already reordered.
  • Using false urgency: Do not invent low-stock claims, deadlines, or discounts.
  • Over-emailing customers: Set frequency limits and consider the person's other marketing messages.
  • Forgetting consent and preferences: Follow applicable email marketing laws and make unsubscribe options clear.
  • Measuring clicks alone: Evaluate completed orders and customer experience, not only email engagement.

How to Measure Performance

Measure the campaign against its purpose: helping customers place relevant repeat orders. Track delivered emails, clicks to the correct product, completed reorder rate, revenue attributed under your chosen method, unsubscribes, spam complaints, and returns or cancellations where available.

Compare similar customer groups and time periods. If you test subject lines or timing, change one major variable at a time where possible. A high click rate with few completed orders may point to a broken link, unexpected checkout costs, unavailable inventory, or a mismatch between the email and product page. Do not assume the email subject line is the only cause.

Pre-Send Checklist

  • Correct customer and product information
  • Reasonable timing and no recent duplicate order
  • Accurate price, availability, and product destination
  • One clear primary call to action
  • Mobile-friendly layout and readable text
  • Working preference and unsubscribe links
  • Consent, suppression, and frequency rules checked
  • Tracking links tested and campaign metrics defined

Final Recommendation

A useful product reorder reminder makes a repeat purchase easier without pressuring the customer. Start with a relevant product, choose a sensible timing rule, verify order and inventory data before sending, and give the recipient control over future reminders. Improve the sequence using completed-order data and customer feedback rather than relying on opens or clicks alone.
