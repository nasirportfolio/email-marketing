How to Write a Product Reorder Reminder Email for Subscription Customers

Subscription customers already have an established buying relationship with your business. They may receive the same product every few weeks or months, but that does not mean every reorder reminder should be automatic, repetitive, or overly promotional.

A useful subscription reorder reminder helps the customer understand what is coming, when action may be needed, and how to manage the next purchase. The message should feel like a helpful reminder rather than pressure to buy.

Quick Answer

To write a product reorder reminder email for subscription customers, identify the customer's expected reorder timing, mention the product clearly, explain the upcoming action or billing date when relevant, provide an easy way to review or change the order, and keep the message concise. Personalization should reflect the customer's actual subscription information rather than simply adding a first name.

What Is a Subscription Reorder Reminder Email?

A subscription reorder reminder email is a message sent to a customer who regularly receives or purchases a product on a recurring schedule.

The purpose is to help the customer prepare for an upcoming reorder or recurring purchase. Depending on the business model, the email may remind the customer that an upcoming order is approaching, explain an upcoming charge, or provide an opportunity to review the next shipment.

The message should reflect the actual subscription arrangement. If the customer has a monthly subscription, the reminder should not suggest a random purchase interval that does not match the customer's schedule.

For a broader look at ordinary reorder reminders, see How to Write a Product Reorder Reminder Email .

How It Differs From a General Reorder Reminder

A general reorder reminder is usually directed at a customer who previously bought a product and may be ready to purchase it again.

A subscription customer is different because there is already a recurring purchase relationship. The customer may not need a message asking whether they want the product again. Instead, the email can focus on the upcoming subscription event.

This distinction matters because the customer experience changes when an order is already scheduled.

  • General reorder reminder: encourages a possible future purchase.
  • Subscription reminder: prepares the customer for an upcoming recurring order.
  • Reorder confirmation: confirms that a new order has already been received.

For the confirmation stage, see How to Write a Product Reorder Confirmation Email .

When Should You Send the Reminder?

Timing should be based on the customer's actual subscription schedule and the amount of notice that is useful for the customer.

For example, a business might send a reminder several days before a recurring order is scheduled. The exact timing should depend on the product, fulfillment process, billing process, and customer expectations.

Consider the Product's Usage Cycle

A consumable product that is normally used within a month may require different reminder timing from a product that customers receive every few months.

Consider the Customer's Subscription Date

Use the actual subscription schedule when available. Avoid sending reminders based only on an average customer purchase interval when the customer's individual subscription data is available.

Consider What the Customer Needs to Change

If customers can change quantities, skip a shipment, update an address, or change the subscription date, give them enough time to make those changes.

What Details Should the Email Include?

A subscription reorder reminder should answer the questions a customer is most likely to have before the upcoming order.

  • What product is scheduled?
  • How many units are included?
  • When is the next order expected?
  • What price or billing information is relevant?
  • What address will be used, when appropriate?
  • Can the customer change the quantity?
  • Can the customer skip or postpone the order?
  • Where can the customer manage the subscription?
  • How can the customer contact support?

Only include information that your store or subscription system can provide accurately. Do not invent delivery dates, prices, savings, or subscription terms.

Subscription Reorder Reminder Subject Lines

The subject line should make the purpose of the email clear without creating unnecessary urgency.

Clear and Direct

  • Your next [Product Name] order is coming up
  • Your [Product Name] subscription is scheduled for [Date]
  • A reminder about your next [Product Name] order
  • Your next subscription order is coming soon
  • Review your upcoming [Product Name] order

Friendly

  • Ready for your next [Product Name]?
  • Your next [Product Name] is almost due
  • A quick reminder about your next order
  • Your next [Product Name] is coming up

Avoid subject lines that imply an order has been placed when the message is only a reminder. Accuracy is especially important for subscription communications.

How to Structure the Email

A simple structure keeps the message useful and easy to scan.

  1. Greet the customer.
  2. Explain why they are receiving the message.
  3. Identify the upcoming product and order details.
  4. State the relevant date or timing.
  5. Explain what the customer can change.
  6. Provide a clear subscription-management option.
  7. Offer support if necessary.

The main information should appear near the beginning. Customers should not have to read several promotional paragraphs before finding the actual subscription details.

Personalization Ideas

Useful personalization goes beyond inserting the customer's first name.

Product Personalization

Mention the actual product or subscription item when the system has reliable product data.

Quantity Personalization

If the customer's subscription includes a known quantity, display it clearly.

Date Personalization

When the schedule is known, show the relevant upcoming date. Make sure the date and time information comes from the subscription system rather than a generic assumption.

Account-Based Personalization

Where supported, give the customer a route to manage their actual subscription rather than sending them to a generic product page.

Personalization should make the message more useful. It should not expose unnecessary customer information or make unsupported assumptions.

Give Customers Control

Subscription customers may want to change their plans even when they still like the product. A reminder can reduce frustration when it makes available options easy to find.

Depending on your subscription system, useful options may include:

  • Review upcoming order
  • Change quantity
  • Change delivery frequency
  • Skip an upcoming order
  • Change the delivery date
  • Update shipping information
  • Manage the subscription
  • Contact customer support

Only include controls that your business actually supports. Do not create buttons that lead to unavailable or unrelated pages.

Subscription Reorder Email Examples

Example 1: Simple Subscription Reminder

Subject: Your next [Product Name] order is coming up

Hi [First Name],

This is a quick reminder that your next [Product Name] subscription order is scheduled for [Date].

Product: [Product Name]
Quantity: [Quantity]
Scheduled date: [Date]

If everything looks right, no action is needed. If you need to make an available change, you can review your subscription before the next order is processed.

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

Example 2: Reminder With Customer Flexibility

Subject: Review your upcoming [Product Name] subscription

Hi [First Name],

Your next [Product Name] order is scheduled for [Date].

You're currently scheduled to receive [Quantity]. If you need to change the quantity, timing, or another available subscription option, please review your subscription before the upcoming order.

If everything is correct, you don't need to do anything.

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

How to Automate the Reminder

Subscription reorder reminders are well suited to automation because the trigger can be connected to the customer's subscription schedule.

Use the Subscription Date as the Trigger

Instead of sending every customer the same campaign on the same day, use the upcoming subscription event as the trigger when your email platform supports this workflow.

Use Customer Data Carefully

The automation should pull current product, quantity, timing, and subscription-status information from the appropriate system.

Prevent Duplicate Messages

Make sure the workflow has sensible conditions so customers do not receive several identical reminders for the same upcoming order.

Separate Reminder and Confirmation Messages

A reminder should not replace the transactional confirmation that follows an actual order. These messages have different purposes and should be written accordingly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Treating Subscription Customers Like New Buyers

A subscription customer already knows the product. Repeating a long product introduction can distract from the purpose of the reminder.

2. Sending the Reminder at the Wrong Time

A reminder that arrives too early may be forgotten. One that arrives too late may not give the customer enough time to make a change.

3. Hiding the Important Information

Product, quantity, date, and subscription-management information should be easy to find.

4. Making Unsupported Claims

Do not promise a delivery date, discount, inventory status, or subscription benefit unless your system and business terms support the statement.

5. Using Excessive Promotional Content

The primary purpose of the reminder is to help the customer understand an upcoming subscription event. Promotional content should not make the important information difficult to find.

6. Making It Difficult to Manage the Subscription

If your business offers legitimate subscription-management options, make those options easy to locate.

Product Reorder Reminder Email Template

Subject: Your next [Product Name] order is coming up

Hi [First Name],

This is a quick reminder that your next [Product Name] subscription order is scheduled for [Date].

Product: [Product Name]
Quantity: [Quantity]
Scheduled date: [Date]
Price: [Price, if appropriate]

If everything looks correct, no action is needed.

If your subscription includes available options to change, skip, or reschedule the next order, you can review your subscription before the scheduled order date.

If you need help, contact [Support Contact].

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

Subscription Reorder Reminder Checklist

  • Is the message clearly identified as a reminder?
  • Is the correct product shown?
  • Is the quantity accurate?
  • Is the relevant subscription date accurate?
  • Are pricing details accurate when included?
  • Can the customer understand what happens next?
  • Are available subscription-management options easy to find?
  • Is customer support information available?
  • Does the email avoid unnecessary promotional content?
  • Does the automation prevent duplicate reminders?
  • Does the email work well on mobile devices?
  • Have all personalization fields been tested?

Final Thoughts

A product reorder reminder for subscription customers should make an upcoming recurring purchase easier to understand and manage.

The most useful messages are specific about the product and timing, accurate about the customer's subscription, and clear about what the customer can do. They do not need to be long or heavily promotional.

If you are building a broader post-purchase email sequence, keep the reminder connected to the other stages of the customer journey. For example, product usage follow-up messages can provide useful guidance after a purchase. See How to Write a Product Usage Follow-Up Email .
