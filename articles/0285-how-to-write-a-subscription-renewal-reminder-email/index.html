How to Write a Subscription Renewal Reminder Email

A subscription renewal reminder gives customers advance notice that their subscription is approaching a renewal point. The message can help them understand what will happen next, review important details, and make any available changes before the renewal.

The most useful renewal reminders are clear and specific. They do not make customers search through an account to discover when they will be charged, what they are renewing, or where they can manage their subscription.

Quick Answer

To write a subscription renewal reminder email, clearly explain that the subscription is approaching renewal, provide the relevant renewal date and product or service details, explain any important billing information, and give customers an easy way to review or manage their subscription. Keep the reminder factual, concise, and consistent with the actual subscription terms.

What Is a Subscription Renewal Reminder Email?

A subscription renewal reminder email is a message sent before an existing subscription reaches a renewal point.

Depending on the business model, the renewal may happen automatically or may require the customer to take an action. The email should make that distinction clear.

For example, a customer with an automatically renewing subscription may need to know the upcoming renewal date and expected charge. A customer whose subscription requires manual renewal may need a clear explanation of how to renew.

This makes a renewal reminder different from a general reorder reminder. A reorder reminder encourages a possible new purchase, while a renewal reminder communicates about an existing subscription relationship.

If your business also sends product reorder reminders, see How to Write a Product Reorder Reminder Email .

What Should the Reminder Accomplish?

A good renewal reminder should reduce uncertainty before the renewal event. Customers should be able to understand the important information without reading a long promotional message.

The email should normally help the customer answer questions such as:

  • What subscription is renewing?
  • When will it renew?
  • Will the subscription renew automatically?
  • What amount will be charged, if known?
  • Which payment method or account will be used, when appropriate?
  • What will happen after renewal?
  • Can the customer change or cancel the subscription?
  • Where can the customer get help?

The exact information depends on the subscription terms and the capabilities of your billing or subscription-management system.

When Should You Send a Renewal Reminder?

Renewal timing should give customers enough notice to understand the upcoming event and make any available changes.

There is no single reminder schedule that is appropriate for every subscription. The right timing depends on factors such as the renewal frequency, price, product or service type, cancellation process, and customer expectations.

Shorter Subscription Periods

A subscription that renews frequently may require a shorter reminder window because customers encounter renewal events more often.

Longer Subscription Periods

Annual or longer-term subscriptions may benefit from earlier notice because the renewal amount can be larger and the customer may need more time to review the subscription.

Use the Actual Renewal Date

When your system provides the customer's renewal date, use that information rather than relying on a generic campaign calendar.

The reminder should arrive before the renewal event, not after it has already happened.

What Details Should You Include?

The renewal email should contain enough information for the customer to understand the upcoming event.

Detail Why It Matters
Subscription name Helps the customer identify what is renewing.
Renewal date Shows when the renewal is scheduled.
Renewal amount Helps the customer understand the expected charge when the amount is known.
Billing frequency Clarifies whether the subscription renews monthly, annually, or on another schedule.
Management option Shows where the customer can review or change the subscription.
Support information Provides a path for questions or problems.

Avoid including information that your system cannot reliably verify. Accuracy is more important than adding extra personalization.

Subscription Renewal Reminder Subject Lines

A renewal reminder subject line should make the purpose of the email immediately understandable.

Clear Subject Lines

  • Your [Subscription Name] renews on [Date]
  • Reminder: Your subscription renews soon
  • Your [Subscription Name] renewal is coming up
  • Your subscription renewal is scheduled for [Date]
  • Review your upcoming [Subscription Name] renewal

Friendly Subject Lines

  • A quick reminder about your upcoming renewal
  • Your next subscription renewal is coming up
  • Before your [Subscription Name] renews
  • Your [Subscription Name] is almost ready to renew

Avoid subject lines that imply a payment has already been processed when the email is actually a pre-renewal reminder.

How to Structure the Email

A simple structure can keep the reminder useful and easy to scan.

  1. Greet the customer.
  2. Explain that the subscription is approaching renewal.
  3. Show the renewal date.
  4. Show the relevant subscription and billing details.
  5. Explain what will happen next.
  6. Provide a subscription-management option.
  7. Provide customer support information.

The renewal information should appear near the beginning of the message. Do not make customers read several promotional paragraphs before reaching the important account details.

How to Explain Renewal and Billing Information

Billing information should be written carefully because customers may use the email to understand an upcoming financial transaction.

State the Renewal Date Clearly

Use a clear date format and avoid ambiguous wording. If the exact time matters, provide the relevant time zone when appropriate.

State the Amount Only When It Is Accurate

If the renewal amount is available from the billing system, show it clearly. If taxes, discounts, usage-based charges, or other factors can change the final amount, explain that accurately rather than presenting an uncertain amount as final.

Explain Automatic Renewal Clearly

If the subscription renews automatically, say so plainly. Customers should not have to infer whether they need to take action.

Do Not Overpromise

Do not claim that a customer will be charged a particular amount or on a particular date unless the subscription system supports that information.

Personalization Ideas

Subscription renewal emails can be personalized using information that genuinely helps the customer understand their account.

Subscription Name

Identify the actual subscription instead of using a vague phrase such as "your account."

Renewal Date

Show the customer's actual upcoming renewal date when it is available.

Plan or Product

If the customer has a specific plan, product, or service level, identify it accurately.

Customer Preferences

If your system supports relevant subscription preferences, show them only when doing so helps the customer understand the upcoming renewal.

Good personalization is useful and accurate. Adding a customer's name while showing incorrect subscription information does not create a better experience.

Make Subscription Management Easy

A renewal reminder should give customers a clear path to manage the subscription when management options are available.

Depending on the subscription system, customers may be able to:

  • Review the current subscription
  • Change a plan
  • Update payment information
  • Change billing details
  • Change delivery or service preferences
  • Pause a subscription
  • Cancel a subscription
  • Contact customer support

Only mention options that your business actually provides. Every link or button should lead to the appropriate destination.

Subscription Renewal Email Examples

Example 1: Automatic Renewal

Subject: Your [Subscription Name] renews on [Date]

Hi [First Name],

This is a reminder that your [Subscription Name] subscription is scheduled to renew on [Date].

Subscription: [Subscription Name]
Renewal date: [Date]
Renewal amount: [Amount]
Billing frequency: [Monthly/Annual/etc.]

Your subscription is set to renew automatically. If your details need to be changed, please review your subscription before the renewal date.

If you have any questions, contact [Support Contact].

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

Example 2: Renewal Requiring Customer Action

Subject: Your [Subscription Name] is ready for renewal

Hi [First Name],

Your [Subscription Name] subscription is approaching its renewal date.

Renewal date: [Date]
Plan: [Plan Name]
Renewal price: [Price, if applicable]

If you would like to continue, review the renewal details and complete the available renewal steps before [Date].

If you need help, contact [Support Contact].

Thanks,
[Brand Name]

How to Automate Renewal Reminders

Subscription renewal reminders are suitable for automation because the renewal date can serve as the basis for a customer-specific workflow.

Use the Renewal Date as the Trigger

The workflow can be designed around the customer's actual renewal date rather than sending the same reminder to every subscriber at the same time.

Check Subscription Status

Before sending the reminder, confirm that the subscription is still active and that the customer has not already changed or cancelled it.

Use Current Billing Data

Renewal date, plan, price, and other account information should come from the current subscription or billing data whenever possible.

Prevent Duplicate Reminders

Build appropriate workflow conditions so the same customer does not receive duplicate renewal messages for the same renewal event.

If your business also uses reorder automation, keep renewal reminders separate from product reorder reminders. The customer intent is different.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending the Reminder Too Late

Customers need reasonable notice if they may want to review or change their subscription.

2. Hiding the Renewal Date

The renewal date should be easy to find rather than buried inside a long paragraph.

3. Being Unclear About Automatic Renewal

If the subscription renews automatically, say so clearly. If customer action is required, explain that instead.

4. Showing Incorrect Pricing

Incorrect billing information can create confusion and unnecessary support requests. Use current subscription data whenever possible.

5. Making the Email Too Promotional

A renewal reminder has a specific customer-service purpose. Excessive promotions can distract from the information the customer actually needs.

6. Making Cancellation or Management Difficult to Find

If customers have legitimate subscription-management options, make those options reasonably easy to locate.

7. Confusing Renewal With Reorder Confirmation

A pre-renewal reminder should not say that a renewal has already been completed. Keep reminder and confirmation messages distinct.

For the confirmation stage after a reorder, see How to Write a Product Reorder Confirmation Email .

Renewal Reminder Checklist

  • The email clearly says it is a renewal reminder.
  • The correct subscription or plan is identified.
  • The renewal date is accurate.
  • The renewal amount is accurate when included.
  • Automatic renewal or required customer action is explained clearly.
  • The billing frequency is clear when relevant.
  • Personalized information comes from current subscription data.
  • The customer can find the subscription-management option.
  • Customer support information is available.
  • The email does not make unsupported claims.
  • The message is not overloaded with unrelated promotions.
  • The automation checks subscription status before sending.
  • Duplicate reminders are prevented.
  • The email works well on mobile devices.
  • All personalization fields have been tested.

Final Thoughts

A subscription renewal reminder should make an upcoming renewal easier to understand, not more confusing.

The strongest messages clearly explain what is renewing, when the renewal will occur, what the customer can expect, and how to manage the subscription when appropriate.

Keep the message focused on the customer's actual subscription. Accurate dates, billing information, and management options are more valuable than unnecessary promotional copy.

If you are building a complete post-purchase communication sequence, keep renewal reminders separate from reorder reminders, replenishment reminders, and order confirmations. Each message should have one clear purpose.
