How to Write a Subscription Payment Retry Email

A subscription payment retry email tells a customer that a previous payment attempt was unsuccessful and that another payment attempt is being made or needs to be completed.

The message should reduce confusion rather than create pressure. The customer should understand what happened, what will happen next, and what action is available if the payment does not succeed.

Quick Answer

To write a subscription payment retry email, explain the payment status clearly, identify the subscription or billing period, provide the relevant next step, explain what happens after the retry, and make customer support easy to find. Keep the message short, factual, helpful, and appropriate to the customer's billing situation.

A payment retry email is different from the initial failed-payment notification. The initial message explains that the payment failed. A retry message focuses on the next recovery attempt and what the customer should know or do.

What Is a Subscription Payment Retry Email?

A subscription payment retry email is a customer communication associated with another attempt to collect a recurring subscription payment after an earlier payment attempt did not succeed.

Depending on the billing system, the payment may be retried automatically or the customer may need to update their payment method before another successful attempt can occur.

The email should therefore describe the actual billing state rather than suggesting that every failed payment will automatically succeed on the next attempt.

Payment retry and dunning workflows can include automated retries and customer messages. Stripe's current guidance describes failed-payment emails and payment retries as parts of subscription payment recovery.

What Should the Email Accomplish?

A useful retry email should answer the customer's immediate questions without making the customer search for information.

  1. What happened? Explain that the previous payment attempt was unsuccessful or requires another attempt.
  2. What is happening now? Explain whether another payment attempt is scheduled or already being processed.
  3. Is action required? Tell the customer if they need to update payment information or take another step.
  4. What happens next? Explain the next relevant billing or account step.
  5. Where can they get help? Provide a clear support option.

The goal is not to make the customer feel pressured. The goal is to make the billing situation understandable and easy to resolve.

Payment Retry vs. Payment Failed Email

These messages can be closely related, but they serve different communication purposes.

Email Type Primary Purpose Typical Customer Question
Payment failed email Explain the unsuccessful payment attempt Why did my payment fail?
Payment retry email Explain the next recovery attempt What happens now?
Payment update email Ask the customer to update billing information How do I fix my payment method?
Payment success email Confirm successful collection Did my payment go through?

Keeping these messages conceptually separate helps prevent customers from receiving confusing or contradictory billing information.

The previous-stage message is covered in Article 0286: Subscription Payment Failed Email .

Essential Details to Include

The exact information depends on the billing system and the customer's account, but a practical retry email may include the following:

  • Customer name
  • Subscription or plan name
  • Payment status
  • Relevant billing date
  • Amount due, when appropriate
  • Last four digits of the payment method, when appropriate
  • Next retry date or expected next step, when known
  • Payment-update or billing-management link, when action is required
  • Customer support information
Important:

Do not include sensitive payment information such as a complete card number, security code, password, or other confidential authentication information in an ordinary customer email.

Subscription Payment Retry Subject Lines

A retry email subject line should tell the customer why the message matters without creating unnecessary alarm.

  • We’ll retry your subscription payment
  • Your subscription payment is being retried
  • We’re trying your subscription payment again
  • Your payment needs another attempt
  • Update your payment method before the next attempt
  • Your subscription payment needs attention
  • Payment retry for your [Plan Name] subscription
  • Action needed for your subscription payment

Choose the subject line based on the actual customer state. If no customer action is required, avoid implying that the customer must immediately do something.

How to Structure the Email

A practical subscription payment retry email can follow this structure:

  1. Greeting: Use the customer's name when appropriate.
  2. Payment status: Explain what happened in one or two sentences.
  3. Retry information: Explain the next attempt if the timing is known.
  4. Customer action: Provide a clear action if one is required.
  5. Account impact: Explain any relevant subscription-status consequence accurately.
  6. Support: Tell the customer where to get help.
  7. Closing: End politely and clearly.

Simple Structure Example

Subject: We’ll retry your subscription payment

Hi [First Name],

We weren't able to process the latest payment for your [Plan Name] subscription.

We'll try the payment again on [Retry Date]. If your payment information needs to be updated, you can review it through your billing account.

If you need help, please contact [Support Contact].

Thanks,
[Brand Name] Billing Team

How to Explain the Payment Status

Payment language should be precise. A customer should not have to guess whether the payment is failed, pending, scheduled for retry, or successfully recovered.

When the Payment Failed

Use direct wording such as:

“We couldn't process your latest subscription payment.”

When a Retry Is Scheduled

Use wording such as:

“We'll try to process the payment again on [Date].”

When Customer Action Is Required

Use wording such as:

“Please update your payment method before the next payment attempt.”

When the Payment Has Already Been Recovered

Do not continue sending a retry message after the payment has successfully recovered. The automation should check the current payment status before sending another message.

What Action Should the Customer Take?

Not every retry email requires a customer action.

If the billing system is going to retry the payment automatically, the email may simply explain what will happen next.

If the payment method must be updated, the customer should receive a clear route to the appropriate billing or payment-management page.

Use action-oriented wording such as:

  • Update payment method
  • Review billing information
  • Complete payment
  • Review your subscription
  • Contact support

The action should match the actual billing workflow. Do not tell customers to “retry payment” manually if the system is already handling the retry automatically.

When Should a Retry Email Be Sent?

There is no single timing schedule that fits every subscription business. The appropriate timing depends on the billing system, payment failure, retry configuration, customer experience, and potential account consequences.

A simple workflow might look like this:

Stage Possible Communication Purpose
Payment attempt fails Failed-payment email Explain the problem
Retry scheduled Retry information email Explain what happens next
Payment still unresolved Follow-up reminder Encourage appropriate action
Payment recovered Payment confirmation Confirm successful recovery
Recovery unsuccessful Final billing-status communication Explain the next account status

How to Build a Retry Email Sequence

A retry email usually works best as part of a state-based billing workflow rather than as an isolated broadcast.

Step 1: Detect the Failed Payment

The workflow should receive a reliable payment-status event from the billing system.

Step 2: Check the Current Subscription Status

Determine whether the subscription is active, past due, paused, cancelled, or in another billing state supported by the platform.

Step 3: Determine Whether a Retry Will Occur

If the system has an automatic retry process, identify the relevant next attempt when that information is available.

Step 4: Send the Appropriate Message

Send the retry communication only when the customer's current state matches the workflow conditions.

Step 5: Stop the Sequence After Recovery

Once the payment succeeds, remove the customer from unresolved-payment reminders so they do not receive an outdated warning.

Step 6: Escalate Only When Appropriate

If the payment remains unresolved, the business can move the customer into the next appropriate billing communication according to its subscription terms and billing process.

Personalization Ideas

Useful personalization should improve clarity rather than simply insert a customer's first name.

Use the Subscription Name

Mention the relevant plan when doing so helps the customer identify the affected subscription.

Use the Billing Date

When appropriate, show the date associated with the unsuccessful payment attempt.

Use the Amount

If the amount is useful and appropriate for the communication, show the amount associated with the payment.

Use the Retry Date

If a retry has been scheduled and the date is reliable, showing it can reduce uncertainty.

Use the Customer's Current Status

Do not send the same message to an account that has already recovered and an account that still requires action.

Automating Subscription Payment Retry Emails

Automation is particularly useful for subscription billing because payment events can occur continuously.

A basic workflow can be connected to payment events and subscription status changes.

  1. Payment attempt fails.
  2. Billing system records the failure.
  3. Workflow checks the subscription status.
  4. Retry rules determine the next payment attempt.
  5. Appropriate email is sent.
  6. Workflow checks whether payment succeeds.
  7. Retry communication stops after successful recovery.

For a broader introduction to automated email workflows, see Email Marketing Automation .

The automation should always use current billing data. Sending an outdated payment-warning email after a successful payment can create unnecessary customer confusion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Treating Every Failed Payment the Same

Different payment situations may require different customer communication. Use the information available from the billing system.

2. Promising That the Retry Will Succeed

A retry is another payment attempt, not a guarantee of successful collection. Avoid promises that the business cannot support.

3. Hiding the Important Information

Customers should not have to read several paragraphs to discover that their payment failed or when the next attempt is expected.

4. Using Excessive Urgency

Billing messages can be serious without becoming threatening. Explain actual consequences accurately and avoid artificial pressure.

5. Sending Messages After Payment Recovery

This is one of the most damaging automation mistakes because it makes the business appear unaware of the customer's current account state.

6. Making the Customer Search for Help

If the customer needs to update billing information or contact support, make that next step easy to identify.

7. Using an Unclear Call to Action

“Click here” is less useful than a specific action such as “Update payment method” or “Review billing information.”

How to Measure Performance

Payment retry emails should be measured against the actual goal of the billing workflow.

Metric What It Can Tell You
Email delivery rate Whether messages are reaching the intended recipients
Open rate Whether recipients are opening the message
Click-through rate Whether recipients are using the relevant billing action
Payment recovery rate How many affected payments are successfully recovered
Time to recovery How long it takes for an unresolved payment to become successfully paid
Unsubscribe rate Whether the communication is creating unwanted email engagement

Email metrics should be interpreted together with actual payment outcomes. A high click rate does not necessarily mean that a billing problem was successfully resolved.

For broader measurement concepts, see Email Marketing Analytics .

Subscription Payment Retry Email Checklist

  • The customer can immediately understand the payment status.
  • The subscription or plan is clearly identified.
  • The relevant payment date is included when useful.
  • The amount is shown when appropriate.
  • The retry date is included when reliably known.
  • The email clearly states whether customer action is required.
  • The call to action matches the actual billing workflow.
  • The message does not promise successful payment recovery.
  • Sensitive payment information is not exposed.
  • The tone is clear, professional, and respectful.
  • Support information is easy to find.
  • The automation stops after successful payment recovery.
  • The email is tested on mobile devices.

Final Thoughts

A subscription payment retry email should make a billing situation easier to understand, not more stressful.

Start with the facts: explain what happened, describe the next payment attempt when it is known, and tell the customer whether action is required.

The strongest retry workflows also use current billing information. When payment succeeds, the recovery sequence should stop. When payment remains unresolved, the customer should receive the next appropriate communication rather than a repeated copy of the same message.

This approach keeps the email useful while connecting customer communication with the actual subscription billing process.
