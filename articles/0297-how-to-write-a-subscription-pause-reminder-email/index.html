How to Write a Subscription Pause Reminder Email

Quick Answer: A subscription pause reminder email reminds a customer that an already scheduled subscription pause is approaching. It should clearly state the pause date, explain what will happen, mention any relevant billing or service changes, and give the customer an appropriate way to manage the subscription before the pause takes effect.

Customers sometimes schedule a subscription pause in advance rather than pausing immediately. In that situation, a reminder can help them remember what they requested and understand what will happen next.

A good reminder should not create uncertainty about whether the pause has already happened. It should clearly distinguish between an upcoming pause and a completed pause.

The message should also avoid unnecessary sales pressure. Its primary purpose is to help the customer understand and manage an upcoming subscription change.

This guide explains how to write a practical subscription pause reminder email, what information to include, when to send it, and how to automate the reminder responsibly.

What Is a Subscription Pause Reminder Email?

A subscription pause reminder email is a message sent before a scheduled subscription pause takes effect.

The reminder is different from a pause confirmation. A confirmation tells the customer that the pause has already been successfully processed. A reminder tells the customer that the scheduled pause is approaching.

For example, a customer may schedule a pause to begin on October 15. A business could send a reminder shortly before that date explaining the upcoming change.

The exact timing depends on the subscription model, customer expectations, and the amount of advance notice that is useful for the particular service.

What Is the Purpose of the Reminder?

The main purpose is to reduce uncertainty before the scheduled subscription change occurs.

A useful reminder can help the customer:

  • Remember that a pause is scheduled.
  • Understand when the pause begins.
  • Understand what happens during the pause.
  • Review relevant billing information.
  • Change the subscription if their plans have changed.
  • Contact support if something is unclear.

The reminder should be helpful rather than alarmist. There is usually no need to make a routine subscription event sound like an urgent problem.

Reminder vs. Confirmation Email

These two messages serve different purposes and should use different wording.

Pause reminder: "Your subscription pause is scheduled to begin on October 15."

Pause confirmation: "Your subscription pause has been successfully processed."

The distinction is important because customers should never be left wondering whether their subscription is currently active or already paused.

If the pause has not started yet, use future-oriented language such as "scheduled," "will begin," or "upcoming."

If the pause has already been processed, use confirmation language that accurately reflects the current account status.

What Information Should the Email Include?

A subscription pause reminder should contain enough information for the customer to understand the upcoming change without making the message unnecessarily long.

Essential Information

  • Customer name when reliable personalization data is available.
  • Subscription or plan name.
  • The scheduled pause date.
  • What will happen when the pause begins.
  • Relevant billing information.
  • The expected restart date when one exists.
  • A way to manage the subscription when available.
  • A support option.

Information That May Be Relevant

  • Upcoming shipment information.
  • Account-access changes.
  • Credits or benefits affected by the pause.
  • How to change the pause date.
  • How to resume the subscription earlier.
  • Plan-specific pause conditions.

Only include information that is accurate for the customer's actual subscription.

When Should You Send the Reminder?

The right reminder timing depends on how much advance notice customers need and what action they may need to take.

A business might send a reminder a few days before the scheduled pause, for example, if customers need time to review their upcoming subscription status.

However, there is no universal number of days that works for every subscription business.

Consider These Factors

  • How frequently the subscription renews.
  • Whether a physical product is being delivered.
  • Whether the customer can change the pause date.
  • Whether billing will change.
  • How much advance notice is already provided during the pause process.
  • How much time the customer needs to take action.

The reminder should be early enough to be useful but not so frequent that customers feel overwhelmed by repeated messages about the same subscription event.

Subscription Pause Reminder Subject Lines

The subject line should make the upcoming event clear without implying that the pause has already occurred.

  • Your subscription pause is coming up
  • Your subscription pause starts soon
  • A reminder about your upcoming subscription pause
  • Your subscription pause begins on [Date]
  • Reminder: Your subscription pause is scheduled for [Date]
  • Your [Plan Name] pause starts soon
  • Your subscription pause is approaching
  • Upcoming change to your subscription

Avoid a subject such as "Your subscription has been paused" if the pause has not yet started.

How to Structure the Email

A simple structure helps customers find the most important information quickly.

1. Start With the Upcoming Event

Tell the customer immediately that the subscription pause is scheduled.

2. State the Date

Show the date clearly rather than making the customer search through the email.

3. Explain What Happens Next

Explain the practical effect of the pause.

4. Explain Billing

If the pause affects recurring billing, explain the applicable arrangement accurately.

5. Give the Customer Control

If the customer can change, cancel, extend, or resume the subscription, explain how to do so.

6. Provide Support

Give customers a clear support option if they have questions.

7. Close Briefly

End the message without adding unnecessary promotional content.

Explain the Upcoming Pause Date

The pause date should be one of the most visible pieces of information in the email.

Scheduled pause date: October 15, 2026

Expected restart date: November 15, 2026

If the subscription will remain paused until the customer manually resumes it, say so clearly instead of displaying an artificial restart date.

For example:

"Your subscription is scheduled to pause on October 15 and will remain paused until you choose to resume it."

Use the date format that is clear to the audience and consistent with the rest of the website and customer communications.

Explain Billing Clearly

Billing information can be particularly important when a customer has scheduled a pause.

The email should explain what will happen according to the actual subscription terms and billing system.

If Billing Will Pause

If the customer's recurring billing is scheduled to stop during the pause, explain that clearly.

Example:

"Your recurring subscription billing will be paused while your subscription is paused, according to your current plan terms."

If Billing Will Continue

If the customer will still be charged during some part of the pause period, explain that before the pause begins.

If Billing Depends on the Plan

Use the billing rule that applies to the customer's actual plan.

Avoid generic promises such as "you won't be charged" unless the business can accurately guarantee that outcome.

Tell Customers What They Can Do

A reminder is more useful when it tells customers whether they can change their plans before the pause takes effect.

Depending on the subscription system, customers may be able to:

  • Keep the scheduled pause.
  • Change the pause date.
  • Cancel the pause.
  • Resume the subscription early.
  • Extend the pause.
  • Change their subscription plan.
  • Contact customer support.

If an account-management page exists, link to the real destination used by the business.

Do not publish placeholder links such as a fake "#" destination. Replace template placeholders with the actual customer-management URL before using the email.

Personalize the Reminder

Personalization can make a reminder more useful when the data is accurate and relevant.

Useful fields may include:

  • Customer name.
  • Subscription name.
  • Plan name.
  • Pause date.
  • Restart date.
  • Next billing date.
  • Relevant product or service.

For more information about using customer information appropriately in email, see What Is Email Personalization?.

Personalization should never be added simply for appearance. Incorrect dates, plan names, or customer details can create confusion and reduce trust.

Automate the Reminder

A subscription pause reminder is often a good candidate for email automation because the message can be triggered by a scheduled subscription event.

A basic workflow might look like this:

  1. Customer schedules a subscription pause.
  2. The subscription system records the future pause date.
  3. The system calculates the appropriate reminder time.
  4. The reminder workflow checks that the pause is still scheduled.
  5. The reminder email is sent.
  6. The workflow stops or changes if the customer modifies the subscription.

The final check is important. If a customer cancels the scheduled pause before the reminder is sent, the system should not continue sending a message that says the pause is still scheduled.

For a broader explanation of triggers, conditions, actions, and automated workflows, see What Is Email Marketing Automation?.

Example Automation Logic

Trigger: Subscription pause is scheduled.

Wait: Until the configured reminder time.

Condition: Pause is still scheduled.

Action: Send pause reminder.

Alternative: If the pause was cancelled or changed, update the workflow instead of sending outdated information.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Saying the Pause Has Already Happened

If the pause is scheduled for the future, do not describe it as completed.

2. Hiding the Pause Date

Customers should be able to find the important date quickly.

3. Giving Incorrect Billing Information

Billing statements must match the customer's actual subscription terms.

4. Sending the Reminder After the Pause Has Changed

Automation should check the current subscription status before sending the message.

5. Sending Too Many Reminders

Repeated reminders about the same event can become annoying. Use a reasonable communication schedule.

6. Making the Email Too Promotional

The primary purpose is to communicate an upcoming subscription event. Promotional content should not distract from that purpose.

7. Using Inaccurate Personalization

Dynamic fields should be tested before the automation is activated.

8. Using Fake or Untested Links

Any account-management link should lead to the intended destination and should be tested before launch.

9. Ignoring Mobile Readers

Dates, billing information, and important actions should remain easy to read on a mobile device.

10. Measuring Only Email Engagement

A reminder should also be evaluated in the context of the subscription journey and customer actions.

Subscription Pause Reminder Email Template

Use the following as a starting template. Replace all bracketed fields with accurate information before sending.

Subject: Your subscription pause starts on [Pause Date]

Hi [First Name],

This is a reminder that your [Subscription/Plan Name] subscription is scheduled to pause on [Pause Date].

Pause date: [Pause Date]
Expected restart date: [Restart Date]

During the pause, [briefly explain what happens to billing, deliveries, account access, or service availability].

If you need to change your subscription before the pause begins, you can manage your subscription through your account.

If you have any questions, please contact [Support Team/Support Channel].

Thanks,
[Company Name] Customer Support

Practical Examples

Example 1: Digital Subscription

Subject: Your subscription pause starts soon

Hi Alex,

Just a reminder that your monthly subscription is scheduled to pause on October 15, 2026.

Your subscription is expected to resume on November 15, 2026.

Your current subscription settings will remain in place unless you make a change before the pause begins.

If you need to change your subscription, please manage it through your account.

Best,
Customer Support

Example 2: Pause With Customer-Controlled Restart

Subject: Reminder: Your subscription pause begins October 15

Hi Jordan,

Your subscription is scheduled to pause on October 15, 2026.

Once the pause begins, your subscription will remain paused until you choose to resume it.

If your plans have changed, you can manage your subscription before the pause begins.

Please contact our support team if you need help.

Thanks,
Customer Support

Example 3: Product Subscription

Subject: Reminder: Your product subscription will pause soon

Hi Taylor,

We're reminding you that your product subscription is scheduled to pause on October 20, 2026.

Your upcoming subscription shipment will be affected according to the pause settings on your account.

If you need to change the scheduled pause before it begins, please manage your subscription through your account.

If you have questions about an upcoming shipment, our support team can help.

Thank you,
Customer Support

What to Measure

The purpose of a pause reminder is communication, so measurement should focus on whether the reminder supports the subscription experience.

Depending on the email platform and business model, useful measurements may include:

  • Delivery rate.
  • Bounce rate.
  • Clicks on subscription-management links.
  • Changes made after the reminder.
  • Pause cancellations before the effective date.
  • Successful subscription pauses.
  • Support requests related to the scheduled pause.
  • Subscription resumption after the pause.

Do not assume that a high click rate automatically means the reminder was successful. A customer may click because they are confused rather than because the email performed well.

Use analytics alongside the actual customer journey and business objective.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a subscription pause reminder email?

It is an email sent before a scheduled subscription pause takes effect. It reminds the customer about the upcoming change and explains important dates and next steps.

What is the difference between a pause reminder and a pause confirmation?

A pause reminder is sent before the scheduled pause occurs. A pause confirmation is sent after the pause has been successfully processed.

When should I send a subscription pause reminder?

The appropriate timing depends on the subscription model and how much notice customers need. The reminder should arrive early enough to be useful without creating unnecessary communication.

Should the reminder include the pause date?

Yes. The scheduled pause date is usually one of the most important pieces of information in the message.

Should a pause reminder include the restart date?

If the subscription has a known restart date, including it can help customers understand the complete schedule. If the customer controls when the subscription resumes, explain that instead.

Should the email explain billing?

If the pause changes billing, the reminder should explain the applicable billing arrangement accurately.

Can a subscription pause reminder be automated?

Yes. An automation can use the scheduled pause date to determine when the reminder should be sent. The workflow should check the current subscription status before sending.

What should happen if the customer cancels the pause?

The reminder should normally not be sent if the pause has been cancelled before the reminder trigger. The automation should check the current subscription status before sending.

Should a pause reminder contain a discount?

A discount is not necessary for a reminder. The primary purpose should be to communicate the upcoming subscription change clearly. If a business chooses to include an offer, it should not obscure the important subscription information.

What should I measure after sending the reminder?

Depending on the business, useful measurements can include delivery, clicks, subscription-management actions, changes to the scheduled pause, support requests, and later subscription activity.

Final Thoughts

A subscription pause reminder email helps customers understand an upcoming change before it takes effect.

The strongest reminders are simple and specific. They clearly state that the pause is scheduled, show the relevant date, explain billing or service changes, and give customers a way to manage the subscription when appropriate.

The most important distinction is between a reminder and a confirmation. If the pause has not started, do not write as though it has already happened.

Automation can make these reminders consistent, but the workflow should always check the current subscription status before sending. If the customer has changed or cancelled the pause, the outdated reminder should not be sent.

Ultimately, the goal is not to send more email. The goal is to make subscription management clearer and more predictable for customers.
