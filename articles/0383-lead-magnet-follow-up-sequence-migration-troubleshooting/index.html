How to Troubleshoot a Lead Magnet Follow-Up Sequence After Migration

A lead magnet follow-up sequence may work correctly before migration but develop problems after being moved to a new email marketing platform. Subscribers may fail to enter the workflow, messages may arrive at the wrong time, personalization may break, or multiple automations may send competing emails.

The fastest way to resolve these problems is to diagnose the actual cause before changing settings. This guide provides a structured troubleshooting process for identifying symptoms, examining evidence, correcting configuration errors, and retesting the subscriber journey.

1. Start With a Structured Troubleshooting Process

Before changing the automation, define what went wrong and compare the observed behavior with the intended behavior documented during migration.

  1. Describe the symptom: Record exactly what happened and what should have happened.
  2. Identify the affected step: Determine whether the problem involves entry, a delay, a message, a condition, or an exit rule.
  3. Inspect available evidence: Review automation history, contact records, sending logs, and relevant settings.
  4. Form a possible explanation: Identify the most likely configuration or data issue.
  5. Test the explanation: Use a controlled test contact or supported testing tool.
  6. Apply a focused correction: Change the setting responsible for the failure rather than unrelated parts of the workflow.
  7. Retest: Confirm that the original problem is resolved and related behavior remains correct.

This approach reduces guesswork and makes it easier to understand whether a change actually fixed the problem.

2. Troubleshoot Common Migration Problems

Symptom Possible cause First action
Subscriber does not enter Trigger, eligibility rule, or form connection is incorrect Inspect the signup event and entry history
Subscriber enters twice Repeated trigger or missing entry restriction Review repeat-entry rules and active workflows
Email is missing Sending failure, incorrect branch, or unexpected exit Trace the contact's automation history
Email arrives too early or late Incorrect delay, timezone, or condition Compare the actual settings with the documented schedule
Personalization is broken Missing field or incompatible merge syntax Check field mapping and fallback content
Link does not work Outdated destination or malformed URL Test the destination independently
Subscriber receives unwanted messages Suppression, exit, or conflicting automation rules are incorrect Contain the problem and inspect the sending workflow

These are starting points, not definitive diagnoses. Confirm the cause using the logs and configuration available in your particular platform.

3. Fix Subscribers Who Do Not Enter the Sequence

When an eligible subscriber fails to enter the automation, inspect the path from signup to workflow entry.

Check the trigger

Confirm that the correct form, tag, event, or signup action is connected to the intended automation. A trigger copied from the old platform may not exist or behave identically in the new system.

Check eligibility conditions

Review required tags, fields, subscription status, and other conditions. A contact may have submitted the form but failed a condition that determines whether the automation can start.

Check contact records and event history

Verify that the signup was recorded and that the contact's current status permits the intended communication. If the event is missing, investigate the form or integration before changing the automation itself.

Example: A subscriber submits a lead magnet form but does not receive the first email.

Diagnosis: The contact record exists, but the expected signup tag is missing.

Correction: Investigate the form-to-tag connection, correct the mapping, and submit a new controlled test signup.

Verification: Confirm that the test contact receives the intended tag and enters the correct workflow once.

4. Fix Duplicate Entries and Repeated Emails

Duplicate messages can result from repeated form submissions, overlapping triggers, re-entry settings, or an old automation that remains active after migration.

Investigate the following:

  • Whether the contact entered the same workflow more than once.
  • Whether the form or integration generated duplicate events.
  • Whether both the old and new platforms remain capable of sending messages.
  • Whether another automation sends the same or a similar email.
  • Whether the workflow intentionally permits re-entry.

Do not assume that deleting a duplicate contact record will resolve the cause. Correct the trigger or workflow coordination rule responsible for the repeated sending.

If unwanted messages are still being sent, pause the affected automation when necessary and safe to do so. Determine which subscribers and messages may be affected before restarting the workflow.

5. Investigate Missing or Skipped Emails

If a subscriber receives the first message but not a later one, trace that contact through the workflow one step at a time.

  1. Confirm that the first email was processed as expected.
  2. Inspect the waiting period before the missing email.
  3. Check whether a condition prevented the contact from proceeding.
  4. Look for an exit rule, suppression status, or failed automation action.
  5. Review sending logs to determine whether the email was scheduled, attempted, or rejected.
  6. Check whether another workflow changed the contact's tags or fields.

Distinguish between an email that was never scheduled and one that was scheduled but failed during sending. These problems require different corrective actions.

Do not manually resend every missing email without reviewing the contact's history. A delayed message may still be queued, and an unplanned resend could create duplicates.

6. Correct Timing and Sequence-Order Errors

Timing errors can arise when delays, sending windows, or timezones are configured differently in the destination platform.

Check:

  • The configured wait before each email.
  • The event from which the delay begins.
  • The account or contact timezone used by the platform.
  • Any allowed sending windows or weekday restrictions.
  • Conditions that must be met before the next step runs.
  • Whether a message is waiting in a sending queue.

Compare the actual workflow settings with the intended schedule. Do not assume that a delay is measured from the same event across different platforms.

After correcting a timing setting, test the affected step and at least one subsequent step to ensure the sequence still follows the intended order.

7. Resolve Personalization and Data-Mapping Problems

Personalization can break when the new platform uses different field names, merge syntax, or data formats.

Common symptoms include an empty name field, an incorrect custom value, or a message displaying an unprocessed personalization token.

Use this process:

  1. Identify the exact field used by the affected email.
  2. Confirm that the destination platform contains the expected field.
  3. Check that imported data maps to the correct destination field.
  4. Review the platform's supported personalization syntax.
  5. Configure a suitable fallback for missing optional data.
  6. Send a test message with both populated and missing-field scenarios.

Never rely on a preview using only a fully populated test contact. Test the situations in which optional subscriber data is unavailable.

8. Repair Broken Links and Tracking

A migrated email may look correct while a download link, button, or tracking URL still points to an outdated destination.

Check each important link individually:

  • Confirm that the destination URL is correct.
  • Open the destination and verify that it loads successfully.
  • Check whether the lead magnet file is accessible to the intended audience.
  • Review redirects and tracking parameters where applicable.
  • Test buttons and text links on desktop and mobile layouts.
  • Verify unsubscribe and preference-management links.

If a tracked link fails, compare it with the original destination and the new platform's link-tracking settings. Correct the underlying URL or tracking configuration instead of replacing links randomly.

9. Investigate Delivery Failures

If an email was scheduled but not delivered, use the platform's sending status and error details to determine what happened.

Possible issues include invalid addresses, hard or soft bounces, sending restrictions, authentication problems, or platform-level errors.

Recommended steps:

  1. Check whether the message was actually submitted for sending.
  2. Review the available error or bounce category.
  3. Determine whether the issue affects one contact or many contacts.
  4. Inspect relevant domain authentication and sending settings if indicated by the error.
  5. Follow the email platform's guidance for resolving the specific problem.
  6. Use a controlled test to verify the correction before normal sending resumes.

A workflow error and a delivery problem are not necessarily the same issue. Confirm the point of failure before changing automation logic.

10. Protect Unsubscribed and Suppressed Contacts

Problems involving subscriber permissions require particular care. A migration or import should not unintentionally reactivate contacts who opted out or were otherwise suppressed.

If a suppressed contact appears to have received a marketing email:

  1. Contain the affected sending process where appropriate.
  2. Review the contact's status and relevant event history.
  3. Check the import mapping and suppression rules.
  4. Inspect whether another workflow bypassed the expected exclusion.
  5. Correct the cause and test the exclusion using supported test procedures.
  6. Review whether other contacts may have been affected.

Do not send a marketing email to an unsubscribed person to test whether the correction works. Use the platform's supported testing tools and follow applicable requirements.

11. Know When to Pause the Workflow

Not every problem requires a full stop. A broken link in one email may need a focused correction, while unauthorized sending or widespread duplicate messages may require immediate containment.

Situation Suggested response
Confirmed unauthorized marketing send Contain the affected sending process and investigate immediately
Repeated duplicate emails Pause the affected workflow if needed and locate the duplicate trigger
Incorrect subscriber eligibility Review entry conditions and suppression handling before resuming
One broken content link Correct and retest the affected email; assess whether sending should be paused
Small, unexplained metric fluctuation Investigate against the baseline before deciding whether to intervene

Choose the response according to severity, scope, and subscriber risk. Before restarting a paused sequence, verify the status of queued messages and contacts who may have been affected during the incident.

12. Retest After Every Significant Correction

Changing one setting can affect other parts of the workflow. After fixing a problem, repeat the failed test and any related tests that could be influenced by the correction.

For example, changing an entry trigger should be followed by tests for both eligible entry and duplicate entry. Changing a contact field should be followed by checks of personalization and conditional branches that use that field.

Record the original symptom, the likely cause, the change made, and the retest result. This creates a useful troubleshooting history and helps prevent the same problem from recurring.

Practical Troubleshooting Record

Problem: A test subscriber enters the new sequence but does not receive the expected second email.

Evidence: The automation history shows that the first step completed, but the next step is waiting.

Investigation: Check the waiting period, the next-step conditions, and any contact-field requirements.

Correction: Fix the confirmed configuration issue without changing unrelated steps.

Retest: Verify that the test contact advances correctly and that the following step still behaves as intended.

Record: Save the incident details, correction, and final test result.

Migration Troubleshooting Checklist

  • Describe the observed problem and expected behavior.
  • Identify the exact workflow step where behavior diverges.
  • Inspect contact records, automation history, and sending logs.
  • Check triggers, conditions, delays, and exit rules.
  • Investigate duplicate workflows and repeated entry events.
  • Verify personalization fields, links, and tracking settings.
  • Check delivery errors and subscriber suppression status.
  • Contain serious problems before they affect more subscribers.
  • Make a focused correction and retest the affected path.
  • Document the cause, action taken, and final result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I check first when a migrated sequence fails?

Start by identifying the exact symptom and the first workflow step where the actual behavior differs from the expected behavior. Then inspect the relevant contact record, automation history, and sending logs before changing settings.

Should I rebuild the entire sequence when one email fails?

Usually not. Identify the cause and correct the affected component first. Retest related steps afterward to make sure the correction does not create a new problem.

Why might an email be missing even when the automation is active?

The contact may not have qualified for the next step, may be waiting for a condition, may have exited the workflow, or may have encountered a sending failure. Inspect the contact's history to distinguish these possibilities.

When should I pause a migrated automation?

Consider pausing the affected workflow when continued sending could cause unauthorized messages, repeated emails, or widespread subscriber harm. Less serious issues may be handled through a focused correction, depending on their scope and risk.

How can I prevent the same problem from happening again?

Document the root cause, maintain a repeatable test checklist, review the relevant settings after changes, and monitor the workflow for recurring symptoms.

Conclusion

Troubleshooting a migrated lead magnet follow-up sequence is most effective when it follows a structured process: identify the symptom, gather evidence, locate the cause, make a focused correction, and retest the affected workflow.

Pay particular attention to subscriber entry, duplicate sending, timing, personalization, delivery errors, and suppression rules. By documenting each incident and verifying each correction, you can make the sequence more reliable without making unnecessary changes to the entire automation.
