How to Rebuild Email Automation Time Zone Settings After Migration

Moving an email automation workflow to another platform can change more than its triggers and actions. A schedule that previously sent messages at a convenient local time may use an account time zone, a contact-specific time zone, or a different scheduling rule after migration.

This guide explains how to identify the scheduling model, map the old settings to the new platform, and test the resulting send times before activating a workflow. The objective is to prevent messages from arriving earlier or later than intended—not to assume that two platforms interpret time settings identically.

1. Why Time Zone Settings Need a Separate Migration Check

Time zones affect when an automation becomes eligible to send a message. The same clock time can represent different moments in different locations, and a platform may use one time reference for the workflow while another setting controls the recipient's delivery time.

Common causes of scheduling problems include:

  • Account-level defaults: The new account may use a different default time zone from the old account.
  • Contact-level time zones: The destination platform may not preserve a contact's local-time information or may not support the same feature.
  • Fixed-time scheduling: A workflow may follow one account time zone rather than the recipient's local clock.
  • Delayed steps: A delay of a fixed duration is not necessarily the same as waiting until a particular local time.
  • Daylight-saving changes: Some locations change their clocks seasonally, while others do not.
  • Missing or invalid location data: A platform may be unable to determine a subscriber's intended local time.

These issues should be investigated individually. Changing the account's time zone without checking the workflow and recipient settings can fix one schedule while disrupting another.

2. Identify Which Clock Controls Each Automation

Before rebuilding a workflow, determine what its schedule means. Do not rely on the label alone; review the platform's documentation and the actual settings available in your account.

Scheduling model What it means What to validate after migration
Account time zone The schedule follows the time zone configured for the account or workspace. Confirm the destination account's time zone and the intended send time.
Recipient local time The platform attempts to schedule delivery according to each recipient's local time. Confirm feature availability, location data requirements, and behavior when a time zone is unknown.
Fixed delay A step waits for a defined duration after a preceding event. Check the delay duration and whether the following action uses a separate clock-based schedule.
Specific date and time A message or event is scheduled for a particular date and clock time. Confirm the date, time zone, and treatment of ambiguous or nonexistent local times.
Business-hour window An action is restricted to an allowed time window, if supported by the platform. Verify the window, applicable time zone, and what happens outside the permitted hours.

A fixed delay and a recipient-local send schedule solve different problems. For example, waiting 24 hours after a signup is not the same as sending at 10:00 a.m. in the subscriber's local time the next day.

3. Create a Time Zone Mapping Record

Document the existing behavior before recreating it. This record gives you a clear way to compare the old and new workflow settings and makes unexpected differences easier to investigate.

For each affected automation, record:

  1. The workflow name and the step that schedules the message.
  2. The old platform's time zone setting and scheduling model.
  3. The intended recipient or account time zone.
  4. The scheduled clock time and any permitted delivery window.
  5. The destination platform's equivalent setting, if one exists.
  6. The fallback behavior when the recipient's time zone is missing.
  7. The expected result of a test and the observed result.

Do not invent a direct mapping if the destination platform has different capabilities. Record the difference and choose an explicit alternative rather than assuming the migration tool has preserved the original behavior.

4. Check Account and Contact Time Zones Separately

An account's default time zone does not necessarily tell you which local time a particular subscriber should receive a message. Check both settings where the platform supports them.

Account-level checks

  • Confirm the time zone selected in the destination account.
  • Review any workflow-specific scheduling setting that overrides the account default.
  • Check whether scheduled campaign times are interpreted in the account's time zone.
  • Document any account-wide setting that affects multiple workflows.

Contact-level checks

  • Determine whether the destination platform stores a time zone for each contact.
  • Check how the value is populated, updated, and validated.
  • Confirm whether the platform derives local time from a location field or requires an explicit time zone value.
  • Identify what happens when a contact's location is unknown or the stored value is invalid.

A country field alone may not identify a single time zone. Countries can span multiple time zones, and location information may be incomplete. Avoid assigning a precise time zone from insufficient evidence.

5. Account for Daylight-Saving Time

Some regions move their clocks forward or backward during the year. Other regions maintain the same offset from UTC. A migrated workflow can therefore behave differently if it relies on a fixed UTC offset instead of a location-based time zone that follows the relevant rules.

For recurring schedules, check the following:

  • Whether the platform supports location-based time zone identifiers.
  • Whether a recurring schedule follows local clock time or a fixed UTC time.
  • How the platform handles a local time that does not exist when clocks move forward.
  • How it handles a local time that occurs twice when clocks move backward.
  • Whether its documented scheduling behavior changes around clock transitions.
Practical rule: If the business requirement is to send at the same local clock time throughout the year, verify that the chosen scheduling method supports that behavior. A fixed UTC offset may not preserve the same local send time after a daylight-saving transition.

Do not assume that every platform resolves ambiguous times in the same way. Use its documentation and test environment to establish the expected result.

6. Handle Missing or Uncertain Time Zone Data

Migration can expose contacts whose time zone information was never collected, was stored in a different format, or could not be mapped to a supported destination value. These contacts need an explicit handling rule.

Choose a fallback based on the purpose of the message and the available data:

  • Use an approved account time zone: Suitable only when sending according to the account's clock is acceptable for the workflow.
  • Delay enrollment or sending: Consider this when accurate local timing is essential and the missing value must be resolved first.
  • Use a broad, permitted delivery window: This may be an option when the platform supports it and the business requirement allows it.
  • Exclude uncertain records from a time-sensitive send: Use a documented rule when an incorrect send time would create a meaningful risk.

These are possible approaches, not universal platform features. Confirm that your destination system supports the selected method before configuring it.

For related data-quality considerations, see Article 0397: Contact Field Validation and Article 0398: Invalid Data Fallback Rules.

7. Test Scheduling Before Activating the Workflow

A workflow can look correct in the editor while still using an unexpected time zone at runtime. Test the schedule with controlled records before enabling it for the full audience.

  1. Use a test contact: Choose a record with a known, valid time zone when testing recipient-local delivery.
  2. Set an identifiable trigger: Record when the test contact enters the workflow.
  3. Calculate the expected result: Write down the intended local date and time, including the relevant time zone.
  4. Inspect the workflow configuration: Confirm that no account default or workflow-level rule contradicts the intended schedule.
  5. Observe the actual result: Use the platform's available execution history, scheduled-action view, or test logs.
  6. Repeat for a missing time zone: Confirm the documented fallback works as intended.
  7. Check transition cases: Where relevant, review the platform's documented behavior around daylight-saving changes.
  8. Approve the workflow only after review: Keep the production audience out of the test until the results are understood.

Some systems do not allow an immediate test of future dates or daylight-saving transitions. In that case, use the platform's documented preview or test features and record what remains unverified.

8. Diagnose Common Time Zone Problems

Observed problem Possible cause Next action
Messages arrive at the same wrong hour for many contacts Account or workflow time zone differs from the intended setting Inspect account defaults and workflow-level scheduling rules.
Only some contacts receive messages at unexpected times Missing, incorrect, or differently interpreted contact time zone data Compare affected contacts' values and the platform's fallback behavior.
Send times shift during part of the year Fixed UTC offset or a daylight-saving rule difference Check the time zone representation and recurring schedule behavior.
A follow-up occurs at an unexpected interval A fixed delay was confused with a local-time scheduling step Review the delay and the next action separately.
A workflow cannot reproduce the old schedule The destination platform may not support an equivalent scheduling feature Document the difference and select a tested alternative.
Two messages become eligible at an unexpected time Multiple scheduling rules or workflows may be interacting Review each workflow's schedule and enrollment history before changing settings.

Change one relevant setting at a time where practical. Record the original configuration, the change made, and the observed outcome. This makes it easier to reverse an unsuccessful adjustment without introducing additional uncertainty.

9. Prevent Duplicate Sends During Schedule Corrections

Correcting a time zone should not automatically mean restarting every contact in a workflow. A contact may already have received a message or completed a step even if the schedule displayed an unexpected time.

Before re-enrolling contacts or replaying workflow steps:

  • Check which messages were sent and which actions completed.
  • Identify whether the platform supports safe editing of an active workflow.
  • Determine whether changing a schedule affects contacts already waiting in the workflow.
  • Use the platform's documented process for rescheduling existing contacts.
  • Test the correction on a limited group before applying it broadly.
  • Keep a record of any contacts excluded from replay to prevent duplicate messages.

Do not assume that editing a workflow changes the timing of every contact already enrolled. Behavior can differ by platform and by the workflow's current state.

If the issue involves a failed workflow action rather than its clock settings, consult Article 0399: Automation Error Handling.

10. Document the Final Configuration

Once the schedule has been validated, keep a short record that another administrator can use to maintain it. This is especially important when the business serves subscribers in multiple regions.

The record should include:

  • Workflow name and purpose.
  • Scheduling model and the time zone it uses.
  • Intended delivery time or allowed window.
  • Rules for missing or invalid time zone data.
  • Relevant daylight-saving behavior, where applicable.
  • Test cases, expected outcomes, and observed results.
  • Known limitations and unresolved questions.
  • The person responsible for future schedule changes.

Update the record whenever the account, workflow, contact-data structure, or scheduling requirements change. Documentation is most useful when it describes the current configuration rather than an earlier migration plan.

Pre-Launch Checklist

  • Identify whether each workflow uses account time, recipient-local time, a fixed delay, or a specific scheduled time.
  • Confirm the destination account's time zone.
  • Review workflow-level overrides.
  • Validate contact time zone data where relevant.
  • Define behavior for missing or invalid time zone information.
  • Review daylight-saving implications for recurring local-time schedules.
  • Test expected send times with controlled records.
  • Review the handling of already-enrolled contacts.
  • Check for potential duplicate sends before replaying actions.
  • Document test results, limitations, and the approved configuration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does changing the account time zone fix every automation?

No. Some workflows may use recipient-local time, workflow-specific settings, or fixed delays. Inspect each scheduling model before changing an account-wide setting.

Is a 24-hour delay the same as sending at the same local time tomorrow?

Not necessarily. A fixed duration measures elapsed time, while a local-time schedule targets a clock time. The result can differ around daylight-saving transitions or when other scheduling rules apply.

Can I use a contact's country to determine the correct time zone?

Not reliably in every case. Some countries have multiple time zones, and a country value may not identify the recipient's location precisely enough. Use validated data and a documented fallback.

Should I restart all contacts after correcting the schedule?

Not automatically. First determine which actions have already completed and how the platform applies changes to enrolled contacts. A full restart could send duplicate messages or repeat actions unnecessarily.

What if the destination platform cannot reproduce the original schedule?

Document the capability difference, identify an alternative that meets the business requirement, and test it before launch. Do not describe the migration as equivalent until the relevant behavior has been verified.

Conclusion

Rebuilding email automation time zone settings requires more than copying a clock time from one platform to another. The account default, recipient data, scheduling model, daylight-saving behavior, and treatment of existing workflow participants can all affect the result.

Identify the original behavior, map the destination settings, define safe fallbacks, and test representative cases before activating the migrated workflow. Keep the final configuration and test results documented so that future changes can be made without repeating the same investigation.
