How to Validate Email Marketing Attribution Models After Platform Migration

Moving email campaigns to a new marketing platform can change how conversions are recorded, assigned to channels, and presented in reports. A campaign may appear to generate more or fewer conversions even when customer behavior has not changed significantly. Before interpreting those results, marketers need to determine whether the old and new platforms are measuring attribution in comparable ways.

This guide explains how to inspect attribution settings, create controlled comparisons, investigate reporting differences, and document the results. It focuses on measurement validation rather than assuming that one platform's figures are automatically more accurate than another's.

1. Understand What an Attribution Model Does

An attribution model determines how credit for a conversion is assigned to one or more marketing interactions. Depending on the system, these interactions may include email clicks, paid advertisements, organic search, direct visits, and other recorded touchpoints.

Different models can assign different amounts of credit to the same customer journey. Therefore, a difference between two reports does not necessarily mean that a tracking system is broken.

  • Last-click attribution: Assigns conversion credit to the last eligible click before the conversion, according to the system's rules.
  • First-click attribution: Assigns credit to the first eligible click in the measured journey.
  • Linear attribution: Distributes credit evenly across eligible interactions included in the model.
  • Position-based attribution: Gives different weights to selected positions, such as the first and last interactions.
  • Data-driven attribution: Uses a platform's methodology and available data to assign credit. Its availability and calculation rules vary.

Not every email marketing platform supports every model. Some report email revenue using their own campaign attribution rules, while analytics systems may use separate channel attribution settings.

2. Separate Attribution Models from Attribution Windows

An attribution model and an attribution window answer different questions. The model determines how credit is distributed among eligible interactions. The window determines which interactions can qualify based on how long before a conversion they occurred.

For example, one system might credit an email click that occurred several days before a purchase, while another system may exclude that click because its configured window is shorter. Even if both systems use last-click attribution, their reports may differ.

Validation rule: Check the attribution model and the attribution window separately. For a focused review of window settings, see Article 0408: Email Marketing Migration Attribution Window Validation.

3. Record the Old and New Platform Settings

Before comparing totals, create a record of the settings used in each platform. Use the actual configuration screens and documentation instead of relying on memory or assumptions.

Setting What to record Why it matters
Attribution model The selected model and its documented rules Different models may assign different credit.
Attribution window Eligible time period and interaction types Older interactions may be excluded.
Conversion definition Purchase, completed form, subscription, or another goal Different goals produce different totals.
Time zone Reporting time zone in each system Events may fall on different reporting dates.
Revenue rules Currency, order value, refunds, and adjustments Revenue totals may use different calculations.
Channel classification Rules for email, direct, paid, and other traffic The same visit may be grouped differently.

4. Choose a Stable Comparison Period

Select a period for which the required source data is available in both systems. Avoid comparing an incomplete current day with a fully processed historical day. Also account for reporting delays, order processing, cancellations, refunds, and delayed conversion events where applicable.

Record the start date, end date, reporting time zone, and extraction time. If one platform updates historical attribution after additional interactions are processed, note when the report was retrieved.

A useful comparison can include a historical period before migration and a carefully selected period after migration. However, these periods answer different questions: the first helps establish the old system's behavior, while the second helps assess the new system after launch.

5. Test Known Customer Journeys

Aggregate totals can conceal problems. Where permitted and supported by the available data, inspect a small set of test journeys or documented conversion examples whose events and timestamps are known.

  1. Record the initial marketing interaction.
  2. Record subsequent eligible interactions, such as an email click or website visit.
  3. Record the conversion event and its timestamp.
  4. Check whether each platform received the expected events.
  5. Apply each platform's documented attribution rules to the available journey.
  6. Compare the assigned credit and explain any difference.

Use test transactions or approved test environments when possible. Do not create artificial purchases, manipulate production analytics, or send unsolicited campaigns simply to manufacture attribution data.

6. Compare Conversion Counts and Revenue Separately

Conversion counts and revenue totals are related but distinct measurements. Two platforms may record the same number of purchases while reporting different attributed revenue because of currency handling, order values, refunds, or attribution rules.

Build a comparison table using clearly defined metrics. For example:

Metric Old platform New platform Follow-up
Recorded conversions Enter verified result Enter verified result Investigate missing or duplicate events.
Email-attributed conversions Enter verified result Enter verified result Compare model and eligibility rules.
Total recorded revenue Enter verified result Enter verified result Check order records and currency.
Email-attributed revenue Enter verified result Enter verified result Check attribution and revenue rules.

These are comparison fields, not actual performance results. Fill them with verified figures from your own reports. Do not assume that email-attributed conversions should equal all website conversions.

7. Investigate Differences Systematically

When a difference appears, investigate likely causes one at a time. Changing multiple settings simultaneously makes it harder to identify the source.

Missing or duplicated events

Check whether purchase or lead events are missing, duplicated, or recorded with inconsistent identifiers. Compare analytics events with the relevant order or lead records where access and privacy rules permit.

Inconsistent campaign tagging

Review campaign URLs and tracking parameters. Inconsistent source, medium, or campaign values can split traffic into unexpected reporting categories. Standardize naming conventions for future campaigns and document historical inconsistencies.

Different channel rules

An analytics system and an email platform may classify the same visit differently. A customer could click an email and later return directly. Which interaction receives credit depends on the reporting system's attribution and channel rules.

Time-zone and processing differences

Confirm that reports use comparable time zones and sufficiently processed data. Check whether one system includes refunds or late-arriving conversions that the other has not yet incorporated.

Consent and tracking restrictions

Consent choices, browser restrictions, blocked tracking, and differences in identity resolution may prevent some interactions from being connected. Validate only the data you are authorized to process, and do not attempt to bypass consent or privacy controls.

8. Establish a Practical Acceptance Rule

There is no universal percentage difference that proves attribution is correct or incorrect. An appropriate tolerance depends on the business, event volume, data quality, reporting delays, and the purpose of the measurement.

Define acceptance rules before evaluating the results. For example, require known test events to appear as expected, investigate unexplained duplicates, verify that revenue uses the intended currency, and document any differences caused by incompatible model definitions.

If the two systems use fundamentally different attribution methodologies, their attributed totals may never match exactly. In that case, validate each system against its own documented rules and use a consistent reporting method for comparisons over time.

9. Document the Validation Results

Keep a migration validation record that another team member can understand without repeating the entire investigation. Include:

  • The old and new platform names and reporting dates.
  • The model, attribution window, conversion definition, and time zone in each system.
  • The source reports or exports used for comparison.
  • Known test journeys and their expected outcomes.
  • Conversion-count and revenue differences that remain unexplained.
  • Corrections made, their dates, and the person responsible.
  • Outstanding limitations and the next review date.

Keep records in an access-controlled location and retain only the personal data needed for the documented purpose. Use aggregated or pseudonymized information when individual-level details are unnecessary.

10. Use This Final Validation Checklist

  • Attribution models have been identified and documented.
  • Attribution windows have been checked separately.
  • Conversion definitions and revenue rules are understood.
  • Reporting dates and time zones are comparable.
  • Known test journeys have been checked where feasible.
  • Missing and duplicated events have been investigated.
  • Campaign tagging and channel classification have been reviewed.
  • Conversion counts and revenue have been compared separately.
  • Remaining differences have documented explanations or follow-up actions.
  • Reports, settings, and decisions have been saved for future audits.

Conclusion

Validating attribution after an email marketing platform migration requires more than comparing two headline numbers. Marketers must understand how each system defines eligible interactions, applies attribution rules, records conversions, and calculates revenue.

A documented comparison of settings, known journeys, event records, and reporting differences helps distinguish measurement problems from legitimate differences between attribution methods. Once the limitations are understood, teams can make more consistent decisions and evaluate future email campaigns using a clearly defined measurement approach.
