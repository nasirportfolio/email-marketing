ARTICLE 0409

How to Validate Email Marketing Attribution Models After Platform Migration

After an email marketing platform migration, campaign reports may assign conversion credit differently even when the same customer journey and purchase occurred. One reason is that the old and new platforms may use different attribution models or implement apparently similar rules in different ways.

An attribution model defines how credit is assigned to eligible marketing interactions when a conversion occurs. Depending on the model, credit may go to one interaction or be distributed among several interactions. A reliable migration therefore requires more than checking whether the attribution setting has the same label in both systems.

Scope of this guide: Article 0408 focuses on attribution-window validation—whether an interaction qualifies within a defined period. This article focuses on attribution-model validation—how credit is assigned among eligible interactions.

1. Understand What an Attribution Model Does

An attribution model applies a defined rule to decide how recorded marketing interactions receive credit for a conversion. The model is separate from the underlying transaction and from the time window used to determine whether an interaction is eligible.

Imagine a subscriber who clicks an email, later visits from another marketing source, and eventually purchases. Depending on the configured model and eligibility rules, the email may receive all, some, or none of the reported credit.

Attribution credit is a measurement convention. It does not, by itself, prove that an interaction caused an incremental purchase. The model should be documented so report users understand what the numbers mean.

2. Document the Existing Model Before Migration

Before retiring the old platform, record how important reports assign credit. Save available settings, reporting definitions, and examples of representative conversion journeys.

Record the following items:

  • The name and definition of the current attribution model.
  • The eligible interaction types included in the report.
  • The rules used when multiple interactions qualify.
  • The conversion event or transaction being measured.
  • The attribution window and its documented boundary rules.
  • Any campaign, channel, or source exclusions.
  • The reporting time zone and date basis.
  • Whether credit is fractional, whole-conversion, or represented in another way.

Do not rely only on a screenshot of a model name. Record the behavior the setting is intended to produce. If the old platform's logic is undocumented or cannot be exported, note that limitation and avoid claiming exact equivalence in the new system.

3. Compare Model Definitions, Not Just Labels

Two platforms can use the same model name while differing in eligible touchpoints, tie-breaking rules, data availability, or reporting implementation. Compare their definitions and supported capabilities before deciding that the configuration has been reproduced.

Model or rule General behavior Validation question
First-touch Assigns credit to the first eligible interaction under the defined rules. Does the system identify the same earliest eligible interaction?
Last-touch Assigns credit to the last eligible interaction under the defined rules. Does the system use the same interaction eligibility and ordering rules?
Linear multi-touch Distributes credit evenly among the interactions included by the model. Are the same eligible interactions included in the allocation?
Position-based Assigns credit according to specified positions, often including the first and last interactions. Are the position rules and allocation weights consistent?
Rule-based referral attribution Uses explicit program rules to determine which referral receives credit. Are referral eligibility, conflict handling, and priority rules reproduced?

These are general descriptions, not a claim that every platform supports each model. Verify the capabilities and definitions of the specific old and new systems.

4. Define the Test Scenarios

Build a small set of controlled test journeys that cover the situations most likely to reveal differences. Use test records or supported testing procedures rather than manipulating real customer records without authorization.

Scenario A: One eligible interaction

Create a test journey with one eligible marketing interaction followed by a conversion. Confirm that the system records the interaction and applies the documented attribution rule.

Scenario B: Multiple eligible interactions

Use a test journey with two or more eligible interactions before a conversion. Check which interactions receive credit and whether the result matches the documented model.

Scenario C: An ineligible interaction

Include an interaction that should not qualify under the documented rules. Confirm that the system does not treat it as eligible merely because it appears in the event history.

Scenario D: Repeated or tied interactions

Where the platform's rules make this relevant, test repeated interactions or interactions with equal timestamps. Confirm how ordering, duplicate handling, and tie-breaking are defined. Do not assume an undocumented tie-breaking rule.

Scenario E: Missing interaction data

Test a journey where one expected interaction is unavailable or cannot be associated with the conversion. Record the platform's actual behavior and any limitations in the available data.

5. Compare Expected and Observed Credit

For every test, write down the expected outcome before reviewing the result. This reduces the risk of changing the explanation after seeing the report.

Test record Expected behavior Observed behavior Result
Single eligible interaction Credit follows the documented single-touch rule. Record the actual result. Pass, fail, or blocked
Multiple eligible interactions Credit follows the selected model's allocation rule. Record the actual allocation. Pass, fail, or blocked
Ineligible interaction Ineligible event is excluded from model credit. Record whether it was excluded. Pass, fail, or blocked
Missing or duplicated event Behavior matches documented data-quality rules. Record the actual result and limitation. Pass, fail, or blocked

Use Blocked when the test cannot be performed because a required feature, test environment, or data source is unavailable. A blocked test is not evidence of successful validation.

6. Keep Attribution Windows Separate From Model Rules

Attribution-window rules determine which interactions are eligible based on timing and other configured criteria. Attribution-model rules determine how credit is assigned after eligibility has been established.

For example, a customer may interact with two emails and then make a purchase. If the first email falls outside the configured window, it may not be eligible. The model is then applied to the remaining eligible interactions according to the platform's documented rules.

Validate the window separately where possible, then validate the model using a known set of eligible interactions. If both settings change at the same time, a change in reported credit may have more than one explanation.

7. Check Conversion Definitions and Event Quality

An attribution model cannot produce a meaningful comparison if the old and new platforms measure different conversion events. One system may count completed purchases while another report counts checkout starts or a custom event.

Confirm that the conversion definition is equivalent, or document the difference. Check event identifiers, duplicate events, missing interaction records, and the relationship between the conversion and the underlying transaction.

Keep source event records where appropriate and retain a traceable explanation for any corrections. Do not overwrite event history simply to make the reports agree.

8. Investigate Differences in Attributed Conversions

The new platform gives more credit to email

Check whether the model assigns credit differently, includes additional eligible interactions, uses different channel rules, or has a different conversion definition. Also check for duplicate events and differences in the reporting period.

The new platform gives less credit to email

Review the model definition, eligible interactions, missing events, source classification, and any exclusions. A lower attributed total does not automatically mean the campaign itself performed worse.

The same conversion receives different source credit

Compare the interaction history and model rules. Check which events were eligible and how each system orders or allocates credit among them.

The results cannot be reproduced

Record the settings, event data, report filters, and any undocumented platform behavior. If exact reproduction is impossible, explain the limitation and establish a new baseline rather than asserting equivalence.

9. Reconcile Model Output With Business Records

Attribution reports and transaction records answer different questions. A transaction system confirms that a business event occurred; an attribution model determines how reporting credit is assigned to eligible marketing interactions.

Compare the reports using the same conversion definition and period. Where available, use transaction identifiers to confirm that the underlying purchase exists and has not been duplicated, cancelled, or refunded.

Do not expect attributed revenue across different platforms to match automatically. Differences can be legitimate consequences of different models, data availability, eligibility rules, or reporting definitions. Document the reason when it is known and investigate unexplained differences.

10. Document and Approve the Model Configuration

Create a record containing the old and new model definitions, supported settings, test cases, expected results, observed results, and unresolved differences.

For each test, record:

  • The test identifier and purpose.
  • The eligible interactions and conversion used.
  • The model and attribution-window settings.
  • The expected credit allocation.
  • The observed result and supporting evidence.
  • The validation status and any unresolved issue.
  • The person responsible for review or approval.

Protect access credentials and customer data in validation records. Store only the information necessary to explain and reproduce the test, following the organization's privacy and retention requirements.

11. Monitor the Model After Migration

Validation should not end when the migration launches. Model settings, tracking integrations, event definitions, and reporting configurations may change later.

Assign responsibility for reviewing important configuration changes and investigating unexpected shifts in attributed conversions. Repeat relevant tests after a material change to the model, event collection, or campaign measurement setup.

When the new platform cannot reproduce the old model exactly, label the reporting transition clearly. Compare trends only when the underlying definitions are sufficiently consistent, and avoid presenting a measurement change as proof of a change in marketing effectiveness.

12. Attribution-Model Validation Checklist

  • The old attribution model and its definitions have been documented.
  • The new platform's supported models and limitations have been reviewed.
  • Eligible interaction types are documented.
  • The attribution window has been recorded and tested separately.
  • The conversion event is equivalent or differences are documented.
  • Test journeys cover single and multiple eligible interactions.
  • Ineligible, missing, duplicated, or tied events are considered where relevant.
  • Expected results were documented before reviewing observed results.
  • Differences in credit allocation have been investigated.
  • Validation results and limitations have been recorded.
  • Post-launch monitoring responsibility has been assigned.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an attribution model in email marketing?

An attribution model is a rule for assigning conversion credit to eligible marketing interactions. Depending on the model, credit may be assigned to one interaction or distributed among several.

How is an attribution model different from an attribution window?

The window helps determine which interactions qualify based on timing and configured eligibility rules. The model determines how credit is assigned among eligible interactions.

Why might the same conversion receive different attribution after migration?

The new platform may use a different model, different eligible interactions, different event data, or a different conversion definition. Compare the rules and source records before concluding that the campaign's performance changed.

Should the new platform always reproduce the old attribution model?

Exact reproduction may not be possible when the new platform has different capabilities or definitions. Document the differences, validate the new rules, and establish a clearly labelled reporting baseline.

Does attribution credit prove that an email caused a purchase?

No. Attribution allocates reporting credit according to a defined rule. Estimating incremental impact generally requires a separate measurement approach, such as an appropriate experiment or holdout comparison.

Conclusion

Validating attribution models after migration means checking how the new platform assigns credit, not simply comparing a setting label or total revenue figure. Document the model, verify the eligible interactions, test representative journeys, and reconcile differences against conversion records.

When the old and new systems cannot produce identical results, document why and establish a transparent baseline. This makes post-migration reporting more interpretable and reduces the risk of treating a change in measurement rules as a change in business performance.
