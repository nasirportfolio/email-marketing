Email Marketing Subject Lines: How to Write Better Subject Lines

Learn practical strategies for writing clear, relevant, and engaging email marketing subject lines that help subscribers understand what your email is about.

What Is an Email Marketing Subject Line?

An email subject line is the short text that appears in an email inbox before a subscriber opens a message. It is one of the first things people see when they receive an email.

A good subject line should clearly communicate the purpose of the email and give the recipient a useful reason to open it.

Why Are Email Subject Lines Important?

Subscribers receive many emails every day. A clear and relevant subject line can help an email stand out and make its purpose easier to understand.

Subject lines are important because they can influence how subscribers respond to an email campaign. However, a subject line should never make promises that the email does not deliver.

1. Keep Your Subject Line Clear

The reader should have a reasonable idea of what the email contains. Avoid making the subject line unnecessarily complicated.

Example:
Your Guide to Better Email Campaigns

A clear subject line sets an appropriate expectation before the subscriber opens the email.

2. Keep It Concise

Shorter subject lines can be easier to read, particularly on mobile devices where screen space is limited.

Focus on the most important information instead of trying to explain the entire email in the subject line.

3. Make the Subject Line Relevant

The subject line should match the content of the email. Relevance helps establish trust between the sender and the subscriber.

Example:
5 Email Marketing Tips for Beginners

4. Use Personalization Carefully

Personalization can make a subject line more relevant when the information is accurate and appropriate.

Example:
Nasir, Here Are 5 Email Marketing Tips

Personalization should provide value rather than being added simply to make a subject line look different.

5. Create Curiosity Without Misleading Readers

A subject line can encourage curiosity by highlighting an interesting benefit or question.

Example:
Are You Making These Email Marketing Mistakes?

Avoid misleading clickbait. The email should deliver what the subject line promises.

6. Highlight a Useful Benefit

Tell readers what they can learn, discover, or accomplish by opening the email.

Example:
Learn How to Improve Your Email Campaigns

7. Use Urgency Responsibly

Urgency can be useful when there is a genuine deadline or limited-time opportunity.

Example:
Registration Ends Friday

Do not create false urgency simply to encourage people to open an email.

8. Ask a Relevant Question

A relevant question can encourage subscribers to think about a problem or topic related to the email.

Example:
Is Your Email List Ready for Your Next Campaign?

9. Use Numbers When They Add Clarity

Numbers can make the structure or benefit of an email easy to understand.

Example:
7 Email Marketing Tips Every Beginner Should Know

10. Avoid Excessive Capitalization and Symbols

Avoid writing subject lines entirely in capital letters or filling them with unnecessary punctuation and symbols.

A professional and natural subject line is usually easier for subscribers to read and understand.

Common Email Subject Line Mistakes

  • Writing misleading subject lines
  • Making the subject line unnecessarily long
  • Using too many capital letters
  • Using excessive punctuation
  • Making unrealistic promises
  • Ignoring the email's actual content
  • Using personalization incorrectly
  • Failing to test different approaches

A/B Testing Email Subject Lines

A/B testing allows marketers to compare different versions of an email element to learn which approach performs better with their audience.

For example, a marketer might test two different subject lines while keeping the rest of the campaign similar.

Testing should be based on a clear goal and enough meaningful data to make a useful comparison.

Subject Lines and Mobile Devices

Many subscribers read emails on smartphones. This makes concise and clear subject lines especially useful.

Before sending a campaign, preview the email and consider how the subject line and preheader may appear on smaller screens.

How Beginners Can Write Better Subject Lines

  1. Identify the main purpose of your email.
  2. Choose the most important benefit or information.
  3. Write a short and clear subject line.
  4. Make sure it accurately represents the email.
  5. Consider personalization when appropriate.
  6. Check how it looks on mobile devices.
  7. Test different approaches when possible.
  8. Review campaign performance and improve future emails.

Conclusion

Writing effective email marketing subject lines starts with clarity, relevance, and honesty. A strong subject line should give subscribers a clear reason to consider opening the email without making misleading promises.

Beginners can improve their subject lines by keeping them concise, focusing on benefits, using personalization carefully, testing different approaches, and reviewing campaign performance.
