Email Marketing A/B Testing: A Beginner's Guide

Learn how email marketing A/B testing can help you compare different email versions and improve campaign performance.

What Is Email Marketing A/B Testing?

A/B testing, also called split testing, is a method of comparing two versions of an email element to understand which version performs better with an audience.

For example, a marketer can test two different subject lines while keeping the rest of the email similar. The results can provide useful information for future email campaigns.

Why Is A/B Testing Important?

A/B testing can help marketers make decisions based on campaign data rather than assumptions.

  • Improve email engagement
  • Test different subject lines
  • Compare calls to action
  • Understand subscriber preferences
  • Improve email content
  • Increase conversions
  • Improve future campaigns

What Can You A/B Test?

There are several elements that marketers can test in an email campaign.

  • Subject lines
  • Preheader text
  • Email content
  • Call-to-action text
  • Call-to-action placement
  • Images
  • Email layout
  • Personalization
  • Sending time
Beginner Tip: Start by testing one important element at a time. This makes it easier to understand what caused a difference in performance.

1. Test Email Subject Lines

Subject lines are one of the most common elements to test. Different wording can influence how subscribers respond to an email.

3. Test Email Content

Different approaches to email content can also be tested. One version might use a short introduction while another version begins directly with the main benefit.

Keep the primary purpose of the email consistent so that the comparison remains meaningful.

4. Test Images and Visual Elements

Images can be tested when they are relevant to the campaign. For example, marketers can compare an email containing a main image with a similar email without that image.

The purpose is to determine whether the visual element contributes to the campaign objective.

5. Test Sending Times

Some email marketing platforms allow marketers to compare different sending times.

  • Morning
  • Afternoon
  • Evening

The best sending time can vary depending on the audience, location, industry, and type of email.

How to Perform an A/B Test

  1. Define the goal of the test.
  2. Choose one email element to test.
  3. Create Version A.
  4. Create Version B.
  5. Send the versions to appropriate audience groups.
  6. Collect meaningful campaign data.
  7. Compare the results.
  8. Identify the better-performing approach.
  9. Apply the learning to future campaigns.

Choose One Variable at a Time

One of the most important principles of A/B testing is to keep the experiment focused.

If you change the subject line, email design, image, and call to action at the same time, it may be difficult to determine which change influenced the result.

For beginners, testing one major variable at a time is a practical way to understand campaign performance.

Define Your Testing Goal

Before starting an A/B test, decide what you want to learn. The goal should determine which measurement is most important.

  • Improve opens
  • Increase clicks
  • Increase registrations
  • Increase downloads
  • Improve sales
  • Increase engagement

Common A/B Testing Mistakes

  • Testing too many elements at once
  • Changing the goal during the test
  • Making decisions from very little data
  • Ignoring the audience being tested
  • Testing irrelevant differences
  • Focusing on only one measurement
  • Stopping a test too quickly
  • Assuming one result applies to every campaign

A/B Testing and Email Analytics

A/B testing works together with email analytics. After a campaign, marketers can review relevant measurements and compare the performance of different versions.

The most useful measurement depends on the campaign objective. For example, if the goal is to encourage visitors to reach a landing page, click performance may be particularly relevant.

How Beginners Can Start A/B Testing

  1. Choose one important email campaign.
  2. Test two subject lines.
  3. Keep the rest of the email similar.
  4. Define the main measurement before sending.
  5. Review the results.
  6. Record what you learned.
  7. Use the learning in your next campaign.

Email Marketing A/B Testing Best Practices

  • Set a clear testing objective.
  • Test one major variable at a time.
  • Keep the audience groups comparable.
  • Use meaningful campaign data.
  • Choose measurements that match your goal.
  • Document your test results.
  • Use successful insights in future campaigns.
  • Continue testing instead of relying on one experiment.
