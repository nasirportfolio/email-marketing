Email Marketing Email Sequence: A Complete Guide

Learn how to plan, create, automate, and improve an effective email marketing sequence for subscribers and customers.

What Is an Email Marketing Sequence?

An email marketing sequence is a planned series of emails designed to be sent to a subscriber or customer in a particular order. Each email has a specific purpose and contributes to the overall goal of the sequence.

Email sequences can be sent manually or automatically through an email marketing platform. Automated sequences are commonly triggered by actions such as subscribing to a list, downloading a resource, making a purchase, or joining a specific audience.

Why Are Email Sequences Important?

Sending a planned sequence helps marketers communicate consistently instead of deciding what to send every time a subscriber needs information.

  • Build relationships with subscribers
  • Educate potential customers
  • Deliver useful resources
  • Guide subscribers through a customer journey
  • Automate repetitive communication
  • Promote relevant products or services
  • Improve the consistency of email marketing

How Does an Email Sequence Work?

  1. A subscriber performs a defined action.
  2. The action triggers the email sequence.
  3. The first email is delivered.
  4. A defined delay or condition determines the next step.
  5. Additional emails are delivered in sequence.
  6. The subscriber exits or completes the sequence.
Example:

A visitor downloads a free email marketing checklist. The first email delivers the checklist. A later email provides an educational lesson, another shares a useful resource, and a later message introduces a relevant email marketing tool.

Email Sequence vs. Email Campaign

An email campaign can refer to a specific email sent to a selected audience at a planned time. An email sequence is a series of related emails designed to work together.

The terms can sometimes overlap, but the important difference is that a sequence emphasizes the relationship and order between multiple messages.

Common Types of Email Sequences

  • Welcome sequence
  • Lead nurturing sequence
  • Educational sequence
  • Onboarding sequence
  • Product promotion sequence
  • Post-purchase sequence
  • Re-engagement sequence
  • Event or webinar sequence

1. Welcome Email Sequence

A welcome sequence introduces new subscribers to a business, website, brand, or topic.

It can explain what subscribers can expect and provide useful information during the early stage of the relationship.

2. Lead Nurturing Sequence

A lead nurturing sequence helps educate potential customers who have shown interest but may not yet be ready to take a buying action.

The sequence can answer questions, provide useful information, and gradually introduce relevant solutions.

3. Educational Email Sequence

An educational sequence delivers lessons or useful information over several emails. It can be useful for tutorials, mini-courses, training programs, and educational resources.

4. Customer Onboarding Sequence

An onboarding sequence helps new customers or users understand how to use a product or service.

Each message can focus on one important feature, task, or next step.

5. Re-Engagement Sequence

A re-engagement sequence is designed for subscribers who have become less active.

The emails can provide useful content, remind subscribers of the value of remaining on the list, or allow them to manage their preferences.

How to Plan an Email Sequence

Step 1: Define the Goal

Start with one clear objective. The goal may be to welcome new subscribers, educate leads, onboard customers, promote a resource, or support another stage of the customer journey.

Step 2: Identify the Audience

Define who should receive the sequence and why the content is relevant to them.

Step 3: Choose the Trigger

The trigger determines when the sequence starts. Examples include joining a list, completing a form, downloading a lead magnet, making a purchase, or taking another defined action.

Step 4: Decide the Number of Emails

There is no universal number of emails for every sequence. Choose the number needed to accomplish the goal without adding unnecessary messages.

Step 5: Give Every Email a Purpose

Each email should contribute something useful. Avoid creating multiple messages that repeat the same information.

Step 6: Plan the Timing

Decide when each message should be sent. Timing should reflect the audience, purpose, and amount of information being delivered.

Example of a Five-Email Sequence

Email 1 — Welcome

Thank the subscriber and explain what they can expect.

Email 2 — Education

Provide a useful lesson related to the subscriber's interest.

Email 3 — Practical Resource

Share a checklist, guide, template, or other useful resource.

Email 4 — Relevant Solution

Introduce a relevant product, service, or recommended tool.

Email 5 — Next Step

Invite the subscriber to continue learning or take an appropriate next action.

Use Segmentation in Email Sequences

Segmentation allows marketers to create different sequences for subscribers with different interests, characteristics, or actions.

For example, subscribers interested in email automation can receive different educational content from subscribers interested in email design.

Use Personalization Carefully

Personalization can make an email sequence more relevant when the subscriber information being used is accurate and appropriate.

Simple personalization may include a subscriber's first name. More advanced personalization can use preferences or behavior.

Email Sequence Calls to Action

Each email should have a clear purpose and an appropriate call to action.

  • Read an article
  • Download a resource
  • Watch a tutorial
  • Complete a setup step
  • Explore a product
  • Learn more

Automation for Email Sequences

Email automation allows a sequence to run according to predefined triggers, delays, conditions, and subscriber actions.

Automation reduces repetitive manual work and helps ensure that subscribers receive the appropriate messages at the planned time.

Common Email Sequence Mistakes

  • Creating a sequence without a clear goal
  • Sending too many emails
  • Repeating the same information
  • Making every email promotional
  • Using irrelevant content
  • Ignoring subscriber behavior
  • Using unclear calls to action
  • Failing to test automation
  • Ignoring mobile email users
  • Not measuring results

Email Sequence Best Practices

  1. Define a clear objective.
  2. Target the appropriate audience.
  3. Use a relevant trigger.
  4. Give every email a specific purpose.
  5. Keep the content useful and relevant.
  6. Use reasonable delays between emails.
  7. Segment subscribers when appropriate.
  8. Personalize carefully.
  9. Use clear calls to action.
  10. Test the entire automation.
  11. Monitor performance.
  12. Improve the sequence over time.

How to Measure an Email Sequence

The performance of an email sequence can be measured using metrics such as delivery rate, open rate, click rate, conversion rate, unsubscribe rate, and other measurements related to the campaign goal.

The most useful metrics depend on the purpose of the sequence. A lead nurturing sequence, for example, may use engagement and conversion measurements, while an onboarding sequence may focus on activation or completion of important steps.
