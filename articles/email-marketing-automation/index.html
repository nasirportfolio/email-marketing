What Is Email Marketing Automation? A Beginner's Guide

Learn what email marketing automation is, how automated emails work, common workflows, benefits, and how beginners can get started.

What Is Email Marketing Automation?

Email marketing automation is the use of software to send emails automatically when specific triggers, conditions, or actions occur.

Instead of manually sending every email, marketers can create automated workflows that communicate with subscribers at the appropriate time.

How Does Email Automation Work?

An automation usually contains a trigger, one or more conditions, actions, and email messages.

  1. A subscriber performs a specific action.
  2. The automation recognizes the trigger.
  3. The subscriber enters an automated workflow.
  4. The system sends the appropriate email or emails.
  5. The workflow may wait, check conditions, or perform additional actions.

Common Email Automation Workflows

Welcome Email Automation

A welcome workflow can automatically send a message when someone subscribes to an email list.

A business can use a welcome sequence to introduce its brand, provide useful information, and explain what subscribers can expect from future emails.

Lead Nurturing Automation

Lead nurturing workflows can provide educational content over time to people who have shown interest in a business.

Abandoned Cart Automation

Ecommerce businesses can use abandoned cart workflows to remind customers about products they added to a shopping cart but did not purchase.

Post-Purchase Automation

Post-purchase workflows can send order-related information, educational content, product guidance, review requests, or other relevant follow-up messages.

Re-engagement Automation

Re-engagement workflows can target subscribers who have not interacted with emails for a defined period.

Benefits of Email Marketing Automation

Saves Time

Automated workflows can handle repetitive communication without requiring marketers to send every message manually.

Consistent Communication

Automation helps businesses deliver planned messages consistently according to defined triggers and timing.

Better Customer Journeys

Automated workflows can provide different messages based on a subscriber's actions, interests, or stage in the customer journey.

Scalable Marketing

A well-designed workflow can communicate with many subscribers without requiring the marketer to manually manage every email.

Email Campaigns vs Automation

A campaign is generally a planned email sent to a selected audience at a particular time.

Automation is designed to respond automatically to triggers, conditions, or subscriber behavior.

For example, a monthly newsletter may be a campaign, while a welcome sequence that begins after signup is an automation.

Important Parts of an Automation Workflow

Trigger

The trigger determines when a subscriber enters the workflow. Examples include joining a list, completing a purchase, or meeting a defined condition.

Delay

A delay allows the workflow to wait for a specified amount of time before performing the next action.

Condition

Conditions can help determine which subscribers should receive particular messages based on available information or behavior.

Action

An action can include sending an email, updating subscriber information, adding someone to a group, or moving them through another part of a workflow.

How Beginners Can Create Their First Automation

Step 1: Choose One Goal

Start with a simple goal, such as welcoming new subscribers.

Step 2: Choose the Trigger

Decide what action should start the workflow.

Step 3: Create the Email

Write a useful and relevant message that matches the purpose of the automation.

Step 4: Set the Timing

Decide when the email should be sent and whether additional messages should follow.

Step 5: Test the Workflow

Test the automation before activating it to make sure triggers, links, timing, and messages work as expected.

Step 6: Monitor Results

Review available performance data and improve the workflow based on subscriber engagement and business results.

Common Email Automation Mistakes

  • Creating complicated workflows before understanding the basics
  • Using unclear triggers
  • Sending too many automated emails
  • Ignoring subscriber preferences
  • Failing to test workflows
  • Not monitoring performance
  • Sending irrelevant automated content

Conclusion

Email marketing automation allows businesses to communicate with subscribers automatically based on defined triggers and conditions.

Beginners should start with a simple workflow, such as a welcome sequence, and gradually learn more advanced automation techniques.

With proper planning, testing, segmentation, and measurement, automation can become an important part of an effective email marketing strategy.
