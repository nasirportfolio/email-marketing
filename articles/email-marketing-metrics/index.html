Email Marketing Metrics: A Beginner's Guide to Measuring Campaign Performance

Learn the most important email marketing metrics and understand how to measure, analyze, and improve your email campaigns.

What Are Email Marketing Metrics?

Email marketing metrics are measurements that help marketers understand how subscribers receive, open, interact with, and respond to email campaigns.

By reviewing these measurements, marketers can identify what is working, discover problems, and make better decisions for future campaigns.

Why Are Email Marketing Metrics Important?

Sending an email campaign is only one part of email marketing. Measuring the results helps you understand whether the campaign achieved its intended purpose.

For example, a campaign may have strong delivery results but receive very few clicks. This information can help a marketer investigate the subject line, email content, offer, or call to action.

Important Email Marketing Metrics

1. Delivery Rate

Delivery rate indicates how successfully an email campaign was delivered to recipients' mail servers.

A strong delivery result is important because subscribers cannot interact with an email that cannot be delivered.

What to watch:

Monitor delivery problems and investigate unusual changes in delivery performance.

2. Open Rate

Open rate measures the percentage of delivered emails that were reported as opened according to the email platform's measurement system.

Open-related data can provide useful insight into subscriber engagement, although modern privacy features can affect how opens are measured.

3. Click-Through Rate

Click-through rate, commonly called CTR, measures the proportion of recipients who clicked a link in an email according to the platform's calculation method.

CTR can help marketers understand whether the email content, links, offer, and call to action encouraged subscribers to take another step.

4. Conversion Rate

Conversion rate measures how many recipients completed a desired action after interacting with a campaign.

A conversion could be a purchase, registration, form submission, download, booking, or another action defined by the business.

5. Bounce Rate

Bounce rate refers to emails that could not be delivered to the intended recipient.

Bounces can be temporary or permanent depending on the reason the message could not be delivered.

6. Unsubscribe Rate

Unsubscribe rate measures the percentage of recipients who unsubscribe after receiving an email campaign.

An increasing unsubscribe rate can indicate that content, frequency, targeting, or audience expectations need review.

7. Click-to-Open Rate

Click-to-open rate compares the number of unique clicks with the number of unique opens according to the platform's measurement system.

This metric can provide additional insight into how effectively email content encourages people who opened the message to click.

Which Email Metrics Should Beginners Track?

Beginners do not need to track every available measurement. Start with metrics that directly relate to the goal of the campaign.

  • Delivery results
  • Open-related engagement data
  • Click-through rate
  • Conversion rate
  • Bounce rate
  • Unsubscribe rate

Email Metrics and Campaign Goals

Different campaigns can have different goals, so the most useful metrics may also differ.

Newsletter

A newsletter may focus on engagement, clicks, and content interaction.

Promotional Campaign

A promotional campaign may focus more strongly on clicks, conversions, revenue, or other business outcomes.

Lead Generation Campaign

A lead generation campaign may measure form submissions, registrations, downloads, or other lead-related actions.

How to Improve Email Campaign Performance

Improve Audience Targeting

Use relevant audience segments so that subscribers receive content that matches their interests or needs.

Write Clear Subject Lines

Create subject lines that accurately communicate the purpose of the email without misleading subscribers.

Create Useful Content

Provide information, resources, offers, or solutions that are genuinely useful to the intended audience.

Use a Clear Call to Action

Make the next step easy to understand. A clear call to action can help subscribers know exactly what they should do.

Test Different Approaches

Where your email platform supports it, testing different subject lines, content, layouts, or calls to action can help identify better-performing approaches.

How Beginners Can Measure an Email Campaign

  1. Define the main goal of the campaign.
  2. Choose the metrics that match that goal.
  3. Send the campaign to the appropriate audience.
  4. Allow enough time for meaningful results to develop.
  5. Review the available performance data.
  6. Compare results with previous campaigns when appropriate.
  7. Use the findings to improve the next campaign.

Email Marketing Metrics and Automation

Email automation workflows can also be measured using relevant performance data.

For example, marketers can review the performance of a welcome sequence, abandoned-cart workflow, post-purchase emails, or re-engagement campaign.

Measuring automated emails helps marketers identify which parts of a workflow need improvement.

Common Email Analytics Mistakes

  • Tracking metrics without a clear campaign goal
  • Focusing on one metric only
  • Ignoring conversions and business outcomes
  • Comparing campaigns with completely different goals
  • Making decisions from very small amounts of data
  • Ignoring changes in measurement technology and privacy features
  • Failing to test and improve future campaigns

Email Marketing Metrics: A Simple Beginner Framework

A simple way to analyze an email campaign is to think about the customer journey from delivery to action.

  1. Was the email delivered?
  2. Did subscribers engage with the message?
  3. Did they click the intended link?
  4. Did they complete the desired action?
  5. Did the campaign produce the intended business result?

This simple framework helps beginners avoid becoming overwhelmed by large amounts of analytics data.

Conclusion

Email marketing metrics help marketers understand how campaigns perform and where improvements may be needed.

Beginners should focus on metrics that match their campaign goals, such as delivery results, clicks, conversions, bounces, and unsubscribes.

By consistently measuring results, testing different approaches, and learning from campaign data, marketers can gradually improve their email marketing strategy.
