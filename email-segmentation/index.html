What Is Email Segmentation? A Beginner's Guide

Learn what email segmentation is, why it matters, common segmentation methods, and how beginners can create useful audience segments.

What Is Email Segmentation?

Email segmentation is the practice of dividing an email list into smaller groups based on shared characteristics, interests, actions, or other relevant information.

Instead of sending exactly the same message to every subscriber, marketers can use segmentation to send more relevant content to different groups of people.

Why Is Email Segmentation Important?

Subscribers can have different interests, needs, purchasing behavior, and levels of engagement.

Segmentation helps marketers organize their audience and create messages that are more relevant to each group.

Common Types of Email Segmentation

Demographic Segmentation

Demographic segmentation groups subscribers using information such as age, gender, occupation, or other demographic data when that information is relevant and appropriately collected.

Geographic Segmentation

Geographic segmentation groups subscribers according to location. This can be useful for businesses with location-specific products, services, events, or offers.

Interest-Based Segmentation

Interest-based segmentation groups subscribers according to topics, products, or content they have shown interest in.

Behavioral Segmentation

Behavioral segmentation uses subscriber actions, such as email interactions, website activity, purchases, or other relevant behavior.

Engagement Segmentation

Engagement segmentation can separate highly active subscribers from subscribers who have not interacted with emails recently.

Examples of Email Segmentation

Imagine an online store that sells clothing, shoes, and accessories. Instead of sending every subscriber the same promotion, the business could create different segments based on product interests.

  • Subscribers interested in shoes
  • Subscribers interested in clothing
  • Recent customers
  • Subscribers who have not purchased
  • Highly engaged subscribers

Email Segmentation and Personalization

Segmentation and personalization are related but different. Segmentation divides an audience into groups, while personalization can customize parts of a message for an individual subscriber.

Marketers can use segmentation as a foundation for creating more relevant and personalized email experiences.

How to Create an Email Segment

Step 1: Define the Purpose

Decide why you need the segment and what type of message you want to send to that audience.

Step 2: Choose Relevant Data

Select information that is genuinely useful for creating the segment, such as interests, actions, location, or engagement.

Step 3: Create the Segment

Use your email marketing platform to define the conditions that subscribers must meet to enter the segment.

Step 4: Create Relevant Content

Develop an email that matches the needs and interests of the selected audience.

Step 5: Measure the Results

Review campaign performance and use the results to improve future segmentation and email campaigns.

Benefits of Email Segmentation

  • More relevant email content
  • Better audience organization
  • More targeted campaigns
  • Improved customer communication
  • Better use of subscriber data
  • More opportunities for personalized marketing

Email Segmentation and Automation

Segmentation can work together with email automation. For example, an automated workflow can send different messages depending on the segment a subscriber belongs to.

This allows marketers to create different customer journeys for different groups while reducing manual work.

Common Segmentation Mistakes

  • Creating too many unnecessary segments
  • Using irrelevant data
  • Sending content that does not match the segment
  • Failing to keep subscriber information accurate
  • Ignoring subscriber preferences
  • Making segments unnecessarily complicated

How Beginners Can Start

Beginners should start with a small number of useful segments. For example, you could separate subscribers by their interests or engagement level.

Start with simple rules, send relevant content, measure the results, and gradually develop more advanced segmentation strategies as your experience grows.
